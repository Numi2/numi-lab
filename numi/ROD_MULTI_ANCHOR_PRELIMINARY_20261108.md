# Multi-anchor axial rod: partial physical gates passed, precision failure retained

The isolated candidate implements the [declared hard-anchor axial chain domain](ROD_MULTI_ANCHOR_PLAN_20261108.md). On the Apple M6 it resolves the original all-pinned three-node witness with relative force error 3.28e-8, rather than accepting the original incorrect force or rejecting under the stretch-only guard. A separate dense FP64 reference passed for 3 nodes/3 anchors, 7 nodes/3 anchors and 17 nodes/5 anchors with varying edge stiffness and node masses, moving targets and axial gravity. Their maximum position, velocity and support-force errors were 4.28e-9 m, 7.09e-6 m/s and 8.15e-7 N. Repeating each from the same initial input produced identical checked positions, velocities and support forces.

The two-endpoint symmetric, asymmetric and moving cases, the pinned two-node witness and the 41-step two-node force/work series passed their unchanged gates. The nine-node bent/contact probe passed its own gates on the inherited stretch-guard fallback; this does not independently qualify its force accuracy.

The rounded-input 128-node case at a 16 m offset still publishes success while missing the 1% support-force gate by approximately 0.00248206 N. This is the previously documented representation failure and blocks promotion. The reversed-target test rejected without publishing and preserved the input host arrays; it is not a complete resident-state rollback test. Duplicate anchors, safe unsupported-domain behavior, multi-step restart and independently qualified bending/contact remain to be tested. Passing exact axial tests does not qualify a suture, contact, or the full surgical workflow.

The [individual outputs and pinned identities](measurements/evidence/rod-multi-anchor-20261108) retain the passing and failing results. The source and dense reference are at commit 930db6091; earlier failures and the precision analysis remain in ancestor evidence. This is not a performance benchmark.
