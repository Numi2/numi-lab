# Stretch consistency: containment, not a general rod repair

The [candidate](../src/metal/DiscreteElasticRod.metal) implements the preregistered [stretch residual check](ROD_STRETCH_CERTIFICATE_PLAN_20261108.md). On the two- and three-node fully pinned witnesses and the symmetric free-middle case, the original Metal shader published success with inaccurate reactions (and, for the free-middle case, inaccurate motion). The candidate instead reported status 4, did not publish, and exhausted its existing 128-iteration budget. The asymmetric and moving free-middle cases were rejected by both. This prevents those specific false publications, but it does not resolve the valid axial motion, and rejection is not the completion criterion. The existing status interface reports net correction but not this added residual, so its failure message can misleadingly show zero correction; meaningful failure reporting is also unfinished.

The existing nine-node probe passed its own gates under both shaders. Its reported maximum iterations changed from 17 to 78, maximum after-energy from 2.64074e-5 to 2.20663e-6, and maximum anchor force from 0.00107438 to 0.00220606 N. This is a material change in accepted results; that probe does not independently qualify its general reactions and motion, so its pass cannot establish the candidate's physical correctness. The candidate is unpromoted. Timing printed by that probe is not a controlled benchmark.

The candidate checks only the XPBD stretch equation at the final position and multiplier; a separate momentum balance and consistent bending, twist, attachments, contact, work, rollback and moving cases are still required. Passing stretch consistency alone must not be interpreted as full physical certification. A useful repair needs a better coupled solve, not rejection of the valid examples.

The [raw baseline and candidate logs, fixture binary hashes, and input hashes](measurements/evidence/rod-stretch-certificate-20261108) retain the original outcomes.
