# Full-horizon skin-wound traction: collector repair

The first full-horizon registration completed its zero-force native trajectory
in 316.2 seconds, but its collector incorrectly required a summary prefix the
probe does not emit. Its immutable notebook is stopped and analyzed as
inconclusive; the raw 60-step output and failed collector receipt remain bound
as evidence. The native process itself exited successfully and wrote all 60
samples.

The corrected collector is checked against that exact retained native output
and a deliberately corrupted copy that must fail the unchanged `1e-4` volume
residual gate. A new notebook reparses the archived control without rerunning
or counting it as another experimental replicate, then performs one fresh
10 mN-per-bite loaded trajectory. The originally registered prediction and
acceptance limits remain fixed. This is one matched simulation comparison on
one deterministic coupon, not population evidence.

The coupon is an uncalibrated homogeneous synthetic material. The probe has no
needle, suture thread, robot arm, or biological tissue. The 60 ms simulated
horizon took 316.2 s wall time on the Mac mini for the zero-force arm; this is
measured throughput for this one run, not a general benchmark or a 100x
performance result. Any new rendering must be labeled simulation and must not
replace the current site video unless it adds a more informative accepted
trajectory.
