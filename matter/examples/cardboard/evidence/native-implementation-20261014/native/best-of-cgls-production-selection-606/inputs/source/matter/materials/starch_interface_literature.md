# Published starch-interface data: calibration boundary

A bounded literature search found useful board-level failure tests, but no
complete, grade-matched local traction–separation parameter set for the
current corrugated recipe. This is a search result, not proof that no such data
exist. The project uses published data and does not require new physical tests.

[Johnson and Popil (2015), DOI 10.1016/j.ijadhadh.2015.02.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijadhadh.2015.02.003)
is the strongest direct lead: C-flute board made with 205 g/m² unbleached Kraft
liner and 122 g/m² NSSC medium, tested in pin adhesion. Its 50 × 160 mm specimens
were loaded at 12.5 mm/min. The reported curves combine board deformation and
several adhesive, interface and paper failure loci. Specimen-specific cured
bond thickness, recipe and mode-I/mode-II fracture parameters are missing.
The accepted manuscript also gives differing strength ranges in its discussion
and figure; reconcile definitions and the final typeset source before adopting
absolute benchmarks. Its adhesive-modulus sweep supplies elastic model inputs,
not a local fracture law.

[Schaepe, Watanabe and Nanko (2004)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/283995734_An_investigation_of_bonding_mechanisms_on_the_doublebacker)
examines starch green-bond development during double-backing. It informs cure
history and process dependence, but does not provide the required post-cure
local cohesive law.

[Rabbi, Jain and Joodaky (2026), DOI 10.1016/j.tws.2026.114575](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tws.2026.114575)
reports C-flute peel response after vibration. The accessible text offers a
board-level degradation observable, not sufficient local normal/shear
traction, fracture-energy and bondline information for this recipe.

These sources can support separate, source-matched pin-adhesion or peel
instruments. Inferring a local law requires an explicit inverse problem,
identifiability and uncertainty checks, and independent published validation
conditions. Whole-board peak force cannot directly become cohesive traction or
fracture energy. Paper ply-delamination and carton seam adhesives remain
different material systems. See the [bulk damage and interface assessment](robertsson2023_bulk_damage_architecture.md).
