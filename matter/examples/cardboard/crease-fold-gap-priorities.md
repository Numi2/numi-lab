# Corrugated-board crease and fold evidence: executable comparison and gaps

## Strongest sources

The most practical near-term post-crease bending comparison for a single-wall C-flute coupon is Aduke, Venter, and Coetzee (2024), [*Numerical Modelling of Corrugated Paperboard Boxes*](https://doi.org/10.3390/mca29040070), printed pp. 3–5 and 14–16. It reports 10 physical 400 × 100 mm coupons with a central crease, fluting oriented in CD, tested in four-point bending under ISO 5628:1990. Bottom anvils were 200 mm apart, top anvils 340 mm apart (70 mm from each outer support to loading point), crosshead speed 12.7 mm/min, and an LVDT measured center deflection. The board was C-flute 250KL/150SC/250KL and conditioned 24 h at 23 °C / 50% RH. The physical force–displacement curve is shown in Fig. 15. The authors state that the crease reduced bending stiffness by 63%, which supports a rounded dimensionless creased-to-uncreased ratio of 0.37. Table 5 prints the uncreased CD value as 6.88 N/m, but Eq. (1) on printed p. 5 is `S_b = (1/16)(P/Y)(L^3/b)(a/L)`. With P a force and Y, L, a, and b lengths, this equation has dimensions force × length: in SI, `(P/Y)` is N/m, `(L^3/b)` is m², and `(a/L)` is dimensionless. The printed Table 5 unit N/m therefore conflicts with the printed equation. The source does not resolve the convention. Preserve 6.88 and the caption's N/m as published, but do not admit 6.88 or the derived 2.55 as absolute quantitative targets until the authors or the cited Eq. (1) source clarify it. The 0.37 ratio is unitless and remains the defensible rounded comparison. The 2.55 value is only `6.88 × (1 − 0.63)`, not a directly tabulated observation.

This is a runnable **bending-fixture reference after a crease already exists**, not a reproducible crease-making recipe. The article does not specify the physical creasing rule/channel dimensions, score gap/depth, score speed/load, moisture at scoring, or score alignment to a flute. Its inverse-calibrated reduced crease-region constants (Table 2 and Table 4) are model parameters for this paper grade and setup, not direct measurements of a local paper damage law. Raw curve points are not included in the article; its data availability statement says data are available on request.

For direct corrugated-board score processing, the strongest open source found is Nagasawa and Komiyama (2012), [*Effect of Scoring Condition on Creasing Characteristics of Double-Wall Corrugated Board*](https://doi.org/10.1299/jamdsm.6.1263), printed pp. 1264–1269. It runs a prototype rotary pre-creaser followed by a main creaser on AB double-wall board, with separate gap, feed-speed, humidity, and moisture-content studies. Tool geometry, sheet grades/calipers, sample layout, conditioning, and inside-liner failure criterion are reported. It also measures post-score moment versus fold angle and gives a peak-moment/crease-position relation. Its board is AB double-wall (210/160/160/180/210 g/m²), not the current single-wall 205/126 g/m² Haj-Ali recipe; use it as a separate source preset or a process-mechanism reference, never as validation of the current grade.

## Practical next comparison

For the current Matter instrument, the first useful folded-board comparison is the **Aduke 2024 four-point crease-bend protocol**, with an uncreased control and a central, preformed crease. This exercises the same C-flute, liner/core/liner architecture and gives an exact fixture plus a reported 63% stiffness loss. To call the comparison source-matched, the model needs the 250KL/150SC/250KL material preset; using the current 2009 material preset is cross-grade. Because the publication omits how the physical crease was made, the executable slice should state that it begins from an already creased initial geometry/region and should compare the post-crease bend only.

For an eventual **actual score-and-fold simulation**, make a separate Nagasawa 2012 AB-double-wall preset. Two different conditions in that paper must stay distinct:

- The report's nominal/normal scoring setup is pre-creaser gap 1.2–1.4 mm, main-creaser gap 0.7 mm, and feed 2.92 m/s. The local minimum in inside-liner failure frequency occurs around pre-gap / board-height 0.15–0.25.
- The plotted moment-angle curve in Fig. 9 is for a different pre-gap of 3.0 mm (0.38 of 7.91 mm board height), main gap 0.7 mm (0.09), and feed 2.92 m/s. Do not label it as the nominal-gap response.

The source reports Fig. 9 as a plot for 10 pieces from two blanks, but not as raw angle–moment values. Eq. 1 is raw published numerical evidence: `ΔM_peak = 2.21 ΔX + 0.34 N·m·m⁻¹`, where `ΔX = e/λ` is the crease offset from the flute apex normalized by pitch. No curve points were digitized in this package. A later comparison should digitize the plot with plotted-series identity and estimated pixel-to-axis uncertainty or obtain the authors' raw data; it must label those values as digitized estimates.

## Manufacturing sequence and simulation boundary

The physical production chain to represent is **fiber furnish → liner and fluting paper → flute formation in the single-facer → starch application at flute tips and liner joining in the double-backer → heat/pressure setting and drying → scoring, slotting, and cutting into blanks → folding and adhesive closure**. Haj-Ali et al. (2009, §2 and Tables 1–5) identify the paper grades and assembled flute/liner structure but do not model fiber preparation or papermaking. Popil et al. (2007, pp. 5–6) document the double-backing sequence directly: preheat the liner and medium, meter adhesive with a doctored gravure roll, bring the single-face board together with liner, and press over a heated plate. It reports the adhesive bath and applied mass for its simulator; its doctor gap is not the cured glue thickness, and the report does not provide a transferable drying, cure, or residual-moisture law. Popil (2017, p. 123) separately describes pilot single-facer corrugating and manual starch double-backing without numerical industrial operating settings. Nagasawa and Komiyama (2012, pp. 1264–1265) give rotary pre-creaser/main-creaser tooling and cut scored blanks into test pieces. Aduke et al. (2024, pp. 3–5) report tests on already-creased samples folded to 90°, not the crease manufacturing settings. A patent describing a flexo-folder-gluer documents slitting/scoring/slotting followed by folding and adhesive closure; it establishes a converting sequence, not material calibration or a validated mechanics law.

The current Matter board is **authored as an assembled initial geometry**: a sinusoidal medium, two liner surfaces, and finite glue bridges are placed directly into the mesh and assigned their material models. It begins stress-free and preconditioned. Those features describe a manufactured board's shape and component arrangement; they do not mean the solver has simulated how that board was manufactured. The current run does not start from fibers or paper webs and does not model corrugator-roll forming, web tension, wet adhesive metering, joining pressure, heating/drying/cure, moisture loss, residual stress, scoring/slotting/cutting, folding, or box-joint gluing. The near-term source-supported crease work starts from an already assembled and scored board state. Claiming process simulation requires those operations, material state changes, and their order to be explicit model steps, with source-supported parameters and observables.

Primary process references: [Popil et al. (2007), *Adhesive level effect on corrugated board strength*](https://research.gatech.edu/sites/default/files/rbi/pdfs/Adhesive%20level%20effect%20on%20corrugated%20board%20strength%20PPP%20Miami%20U%20final%20ver%203.pdf); [Popil (2017), *The Physical Testing of Paper*, p. 123](https://repository.gatech.edu/bitstreams/e4749f61-7780-41d3-93c7-d51cf5127e3e/download); [Nagasawa and Komiyama (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1299/jamdsm.6.1263); [Aduke et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.3390/mca29040070); [US Patent 5,690,601, slitting/scoring and folder-gluer process](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5690601A/en). The local constitutive path diagnostic is preserved in [`evidence/release-and-resolution/constitutive-path/README.md`](evidence/release-and-resolution/constitutive-path/README.md).

## Gaps that still block physical crease qualification

The sources support useful process and output checks, but leave the constitutive loop incomplete:

1. **Crease damage and paper fracture.** Nagasawa et al. observe inside-liner failures as a probability/frequency metric; Aduke et al. fit reduced crease-region properties. Neither supplies a grade-matched local crack-initiation stress, delamination strength, fracture energy, damage-softening curve, or unload/reload law for the creased sheets. The current compression-calibrated Hill ideal-plastic model has no failure evolution and cannot predict those events.
2. **Adhesive.** The crease studies do not identify bondline thickness/width, starch solids/cure state, density, cohesive tensile/shear strengths, mode-I/II fracture energies, or failure locus. Aduke's resolved model uses shared nodes at flute tips (a tied interface) and explicitly notes that starch-bond flexibility could lower stiffness. The earlier Popil 2007 report provides fully dried starch E = 400 MPa and assumed ν = 0.3, but no matching-grade glue density or cohesive calibration. A perfect-bond bridge can be an executable assumption, not a validated debonding model.
3. **Moisture and condition history.** Nagasawa et al. report that increasing board water content from 5.3% to 6.9% lowered inside-liner failure occurrence by roughly 20–40%; they do not provide a moisture-dependent orthotropic stress–strain/damage law. Aduke et al. condition the bending-board samples at 23 °C / 50% RH for 24 h but do not report water content at crease formation. Moisture state must therefore be a source-bound test condition, not an inferred material calibration.
4. **Creaser phase and machine history.** The score's offset relative to the flute apex measurably changes peak-moment spread. A source-matched model needs the exact score/flute phase, roller gaps, roller profile, feed speed, pre-crush, and humidity. Nagasawa reports these for its AB board; the 2024 single-wall crease source does not report them.
5. **Manufacturing and residual state.** Neither source identifies enough roll pressure/temperature, bond application, starch gelation/drying, web tension, or post-process moisture to simulate board manufacture and predict residual stress. Both are compatible with starting from an already assembled/preformed, conditioned board; neither supports claiming a forming-and-cure simulation.
6. **Box response.** Aduke's paper includes a 400 × 400 mm box compression experiment, but its successful box-load comparison is for its own 250KL/150SC/250KL preset and calibrated region properties. It is not a validation of the current 2009-based model or a prediction of folded box assembly from crease physics.

The laminated-sheet work by Beex and Peerlings (2009), [*An experimental and computational study of laminated paperboard creasing and folding*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijsolstr.2009.08.012), offers detailed ply-delamination mechanisms and score/fold measurements, but it is solid laminated paperboard without a corrugated medium and flute-tip glue joints. It can inform candidate mechanisms; it cannot be substituted for corrugated-board crease validation.
