# Slewed contact-triggered placement: rejected

All eight candidate trials failed the physical and toe-delay gates. The toe-loading delay was 0.90–1.07 times the same-lane no-placement reference, so no trial achieved the required 15% reduction. The slower contact ramp reduced both-feet-unloaded substeps compared with the corresponding abrupt drive in all eight matched trials: 59–116 substeps became 0–11. Right-foot sampled contact-speed ratios improved in seven of eight comparisons, but several remained over the limit. Some trials lost required recovery coverage or knee timing; no setting is promoted. The delay comparison is a repeated-event proxy, not a biological target or integrated material slip.

The 3-bit contact ramp obeyed its one-level-per-step update on all 99 checked transitions per recorded candidate-side window. Unchanged lanes 0–3 matched the earlier no-placement state, toe-contact and q/v samples across both used seeds, and pre-intervention state/contact records matched the original controls. The ramp is nevertheless research-only, uses one-step-old contact data and persists in a changed checkpoint schema. No production rollback or checkpoint migration has been qualified. These short, very slow correlated trials do not establish natural gait, grip, takeoff, flight, landing or autonomous navigation.
