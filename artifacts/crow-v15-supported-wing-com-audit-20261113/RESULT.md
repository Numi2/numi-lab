# Supported-wing transition: whole-body momentum audit

A native forward-kinematics observer reconstructed the mass-weighted center of mass from accepted q/v in the matched supported-wing trial. Its uniform-translation sanity check differed by at most 5.6e-17 m/s. Prior model reconstruction and replay validations are not full sensor calibration. Runtime gravity is assumed -9.81 m/s², and recorded control interval 0.02 s and 16 substeps imply 0.00125 s per substep; those and applied-force logger equivalence remain assumptions.

Although two push lanes had root rises above 0.05 m, the largest reconstructed COM rise in any test lane relative to tick 350 through tick 380 was about 0.0493 m. The higher root value therefore does not establish an equal whole-body elevation; articulated body motion changes their relationship. First three-interval release COM vertical velocity varied from about 0.05 to 0.34 m/s across push lanes, and all lanes later recontacted. Early push-associated positive foot impulses do not prove sustained leg-powered flight.

For ticks 348–365, modeled endpoint COM momentum minus logged foot impulse and assumed gravity left a residual of about 0.405–0.742 N s in the push lanes. Integrated recorded vertical aerodynamic force yielded about 0.401–0.751 N s, leaving a difference between approximately -0.0092 and +0.0038 N s. Similar approximate agreement occurs in the hold arms. This supports a force-balance consistency check but does not prove that every force or numerical term is captured or validate the aerodynamic model. One used training seed and correlated lanes cannot qualify takeoff, flight, landing or navigation. Future launch gates should inspect whole-body COM alongside root height and contact continuity.
