# Phase-lead compensation for physical target interpolation

The full-interval position-target ramp sharply reduced the measured joint motion proxies but cut forward travel. One possible engineering explanation is the approximately half-control-interval delay introduced by interpolation. The walking carrier is analytic, so test adding a bounded 5, 10 or 20 ms phase lead only to the walking oscillator while retaining the 16-substep physical target ramp. This is an anticipatory controller input, not animation and not a measured crow phase. The contact gating, onset/end timing, other action channels, solver, initial state, and retained sham are unchanged.

Use the same used-seed teacher-off eight-lane two-second development window. Check exact sampled pre-gait parity, complete contact and joint observations, all-lane 90%-of-control travel, supported bilateral clearance, no new nonfoot contact or toe caps, no increase in total unsupported substeps, and all four boundary proxies at least 20% lower. Report full-substep mean, 95th percentile and maximum as post-hoc checks; worsening there precludes claiming uniformly smoother movement. Any pass is still a preliminary engineering result and requires new starts, longer contact-driven walking, biological comparisons and the continuous flight and landing sequence.
