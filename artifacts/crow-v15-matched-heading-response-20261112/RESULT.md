# Same-start heading-target response

The experimental and retained same-lane controls matched all recorded state endpoints through trace submission 602; lanes 2–7 first differed at 603. Unmodified lanes 0–1 remained equal through submission 1051. All eight experimental episodes reached the 1050-transition horizon with no post-600 recorded joint-speed caps, nonfoot ground impulse or reported contact termination. Course obstacles were disabled and ground remained present.

Changing the existing bounded pronation-joint feedback from its zero-heading target to paired positive and negative heading targets did move heading and trajectory in opposite directions. At trace submission 850 (about 17 seconds), relative to each matched lane’s zero-heading control, the heading changes were +0.118/−0.116 rad for +/−0.15 rad targets, +0.228/−0.220 for +/−0.30, and +0.287/−0.278 for +/−0.45. These are differences from drifted controls, not the absolute target errors; actual candidate heading at that time is in `analysis.json`. After the target returns to zero, heading differences at submission 1050 were still approximately +0.044/−0.043, +0.096/−0.090 and +0.135/−0.135 rad. The recovery is incomplete within the three-second post-pulse observation.

This supports directional heading authority in this model, not precise target tracking, biologically realistic banking or a qualified flight controller. Lanes 2–7 retain an empirical heading-hold loop, lane-dependent height gains and 0.60 unsteady-force blending; controls 0–1 use a different wing-normal blend and no corresponding heading loop. Lanes are compared only with their exact same-lane controls. Perturbations, longer recovery, uniform configuration, substep convergence, genuine aerodynamics, obstacles, landing and unused seeds/layouts remain unqualified. Logs are split into byte-reconstructible parts with `stderr-parts.json`; the trace submission includes the separate pre-reset warmup.
