# Substep contact-motion observer: validated for this diagnostic

This passive observer reproduced the retained walking baseline exactly for 2,400 logged state samples and 38,400 contact substeps. All 12,800 walking-window q/v vectors were recorded; final-substep q/v matched the endpoint records exactly. All 800 final-substep loaded-contact records matched independently published endpoint contacts. In lane 5, articulated forward kinematics matched replay foot/toe positions within 1.45e-8 m, linear velocity within 1.48e-8 m/s and angular velocity within 3.91e-7 rad/s; the ground had zero sampled velocity.

In the used seed's eight 4–6 s walking starts, the normal-impulse-weighted tangential contact-point speed, sampled every 1.25 ms while a foot was loaded, had per-foot means .049–.054 m/s and p95 values .228–.258 m/s. After excluding the first and last 10 ms of each contiguous loaded interval, per-foot means were .041–.049 m/s and p95 values .201–.239 m/s. The integral of these per-substep weighted speed magnitudes over loaded intervals was .061–.070 m per foot. Results at frame boundaries are also retained and use a different weighting from earlier per-contact p95 reports.

This establishes significant sampled contact-point motion during modeled support, not a measured biological tolerance or a tracked material-point slip distance. Contact identities can change, and collision points and end-of-substep q/v have a time offset up to one substep. The current baseline therefore requires more work and is not a qualified realistic gait. The observer adds no control forces.
