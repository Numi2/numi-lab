# Rear-toe collision envelope attachment audit

A native inspection of the two-segment crow model underlying the current hip-roll body finds the left and right sole collision boxes centered at x=0 with half-length 0.010 m and identity local rotation. The rear proximal toe hinge parent anchor is x=-0.044 m and the neutral toe box begins at that hinge and extends rearward. Thus, at neutral toe angle, the sole and rear-toe collision envelopes have a 0.034 m gap on each foot. The joint remains a real articulated constraint: this is a missing collision-envelope connection, not a disconnected mechanical joint or proof of a numerical solver failure. Hip-roll insertion remaps the bodies but does not change those sole shapes or rear-toe anchors.

The gap appears because the foot proxy is shortened from the inherited 0.045 m half-length to 0.010 m while the rear-toe anchor remains at -0.044 m. That creates an invisible unsupported span between a 20 mm sole proxy and the rear toe, and a long lever arm around the foot. It is a clear geometric simplification, not a measured crow anatomical gap. It may influence stance and toe-off but this static inspection does not prove causality. Moving the hinge or filling the gap changes contact mechanics, mass distribution and world fingerprints; it must be tested on an isolated model and all policies and flight/landing requalified before promotion.

The native inspector builds the actual equal-toe model used by the current hip-roll model; inspect.cpp and inspection.log retain exact fields and assertions. The measured related hooded-crow digit ratios do not provide the American-crow rear-hinge location or prove any particular replacement length.
