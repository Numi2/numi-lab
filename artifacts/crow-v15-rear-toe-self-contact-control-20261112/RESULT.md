# Diagnostic shank-toe collision control

The switch-off arm reproduced the previous reattached-foot trial exactly across all 2,400 recorded endpoints and 38,400 contact substeps, including its eight physical failures at first-episode steps 156–230. The diagnostic arm excluded exactly two named collider pairs, each shank versus its own proximal rear toe; all ground contacts and other self-collisions remained enabled. Its altered world correctly rejected the source policy. All 32 compiled contact-envelope radii matched, remaining diagnostic action/observation hash inputs matched, and copied policy weights and transforms were checked identical after rebinding. The two-step smoke preceded the full run and reported no physics errors or failures.

All eight diagnostic lanes survived 300 steps, with final pitch approximately -0.033 to -0.034 rad, compared with -0.74 to -0.79 rad at the failed control endpoints. This matched intervention supports a major causal contribution from the modeled shank-toe pair to the observed falls; it does not validate removing those contacts in production or prove an anatomically correct foot. The control still produced distal rear-toe/shank contacts in 578 of 800 sampled lane endpoints during the first 100 intervals, starting by step 4 in every lane. The geometry remains internally problematic. No collision exclusion is promoted.

During the diagnostic-only final two-second walking window, each lane made 0.0426–0.0434 m forward displacement, with three left and four right supported-clearance bouts and no both-feet-zero-normal substeps or observed nonfoot ground contacts. These slow movements and thresholded endpoint measures are not biological gait validation. Rear-toe contact still ended after the front group in 40/40 complete runs. The candidate had 87 sole-clear/toe-low endpoints versus 49 in the no-extra-stroke held reference; 34 had accepted toe-ground contact, with sampled point speed median/p95 0.0950/0.1068 m/s. The body positions and geometry were evaluated using model-specific native FK checked against lane-5 replay, and the shifted sole center was accounted for. Contact-point and velocity timestamps may differ slightly; these are not integrated slip estimates. The first analysis attempt failed from a quoted-name error and a copied unequal-toe geometry assumption; no numerical gait scores were emitted until those were corrected, and the error is retained.

The repaired attachment still requires a collision geometry that avoids the demonstrated overlap with contacts enabled, plus independent standing, walking, takeoff, flight, grip and landing tests.
