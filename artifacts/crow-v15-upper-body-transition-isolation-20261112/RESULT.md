# Upper-body transition and toe interaction

The held-command no-toe run matched the original sham exactly in q/v/action and accepted contact substeps through step 300, with identical host and actor hashes and a different shader hash. Holding the previous filtered upper-body actions from 6 to 10 seconds reduced the number of both-feet-below-normal-impulse-threshold substeps during steps 301–400 from 846 to zero across eight lanes, with no non-foot intervals. The held run also recovered positive middle-section root travel in the baseline lane that moved backward. This supports sensitivity to this combined command change, not a unique force mechanism or a biological controller. Full six-second gait comparisons found zero unloaded substeps in every held sham lane.

The separately planned phase-led toe test with this upper-body hold failed. Every candidate lane maintained >=90% of sham forward travel (90.99–95.04%), >=11 supported clearance bouts per foot, zero unloaded substeps, no nonfoot contacts and no sampled toe-speed caps across all 4800 walking substeps. However the aggregate sole-clear/toe-low endpoint count was 125 versus 60 in sham, exceeding the prescribed <=80% threshold. Thus the short-screen improvement did not persist and must not be promoted. The compared runs used one previously used development seed and represent only six seconds; the gait remains very slow, and neither run qualifies walking biology, takeoff, flight, landing or navigation.
