# Contact-conditioned center-of-mass balance screen

In the matched stationary-perch zero-gain bench, the -0.04 rad initial-pitch lane begins translating forward long before falling, whereas the +0.04 rad lane does not, despite reaching a similar pitch around interval 40. A hypothesis is that contact-conditioned hip drive using the modeled whole-body center of mass and its velocity can manage this fore-aft departure more directly than the existing torso-origin feedback. This is privileged perfect-state feedback in a constructed bench, not a deployable sensor or biological muscle model.

Use the same v15-derived body, perch, contact, 32-substep solver and two initial states as the retained baseline. Compute center of mass and velocity as the mass-weighted native forward-kinematic body values at the prior accepted endpoint, then blend the existing hip position correction into gain times (-COM x - 0.2 COM velocity x), with gains +0.5 and -0.5 assigned within a single eight-lane batch (two deterministic duplicates per tilt/gain combination), starting at interval 10 and fully blended at 60. Clamp hip correction to +/-0.16 rad, require recorded pre-intervention q/v parity with the zero-gain baseline, and preserve all rollback and failure records. This tests signs and feasibility; those numerical gains are engineering choices, not crow data.

Both initial tilt signs must complete 550 intervals, with no below-height fall, missing substeps, solver rollback, contact overflow, nonfoot contact or toe speed cap, and maintain bilateral opposed-toe support and |pitch|<=0.25 rad over the final 100 intervals. Deterministic duplicate lanes are not independent initial conditions. Passing does not qualify the controller on landing, release, diverse starts, sensory observations or biological targets; any candidate requires separate integration and full-sequence testing.

Before any run, the planned separate eight-lane arms were replaced by this balanced single batch to avoid four redundant copies of each start and reduce compute. Each lane is still compared to its same-lane baseline through the intervention boundary.
