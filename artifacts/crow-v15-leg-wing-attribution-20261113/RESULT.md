# Matched leg contribution during the flight transition

On used seed 20261126, both modified held-leg lanes and their matching leg-extension controls completed the observed first 500 intervals without a physical failure. All unchanged lanes matched through 500 and candidate lanes matched through step 368. The recorded physical leg targets show the extension was suppressed in the held lanes, while all 120 compared flap/pronation targets per lane were identical over steps 371–400. Actual flap positions and velocities differed after the leg intervention, as expected for coupled dynamics. All contact, toe-speed and leg-effort observations over steps 335–500 were present with no logged observer error or contact overflow.

Over the same steps 371–390, the extending legs produced 0.09769 and 0.03782 N s more upward foot-normal impulse than their respective held-leg controls. Whole-body COM vertical velocity at step 390 was higher by 0.07285 and 0.05880 m/s; the FK executable passed a translation-velocity check. In the observed period both held-leg controls also achieved three consecutive unloaded intervals and did not recontact through step 500. Thus extension contributed to this modeled transition, but the comparison does not show that leg push was necessary for airborne motion or establish a qualified takeoff. The unchanged wing *targets* do not imply identical aerodynamic forces: the body and wing motion changed, and the observer did not record aerodynamic wrenches during steps 335–500. The separate wing-held comparison establishes only a short leg-driven hop. Neither result demonstrates real-crow biomechanics, robust flight, navigation or landing.
