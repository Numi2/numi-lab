# Landing failure timing on the retained development run

All eight lanes made at least transient foot/perch contact, but lanes 1–4 never entered the landing-hold phase and had no ten-interval half-weight support bout through the 3800-step horizon. First contact therefore did not imply a landing: these are acquisition/approach failures, with possible later recovery unobserved. Lane 0 entered hold and had long foot loading but no ten-interval opposing-toe grip bout before tipping. Lane 6 had 154 consecutive qualifying opposing-support intervals, lost the qualifying grip after step 3682, and subsequently failed at step 3798; at the last recorded endpoint its pitch was about 1.19 rad and pitch rate 0.73 rad/s. Lane 5 met the final 25-interval grip screen, while lane 7 had 14 qualifying intervals at the horizon. This is a short mechanical screen, not proof of a stable or biological landing.

Phase 2 remained latched through both falls. Foot loading alone did not ensure opposing grip or stability. The findings motivate separate tests of acquisition and supported balance rather than increasing general gains or relaxing the grip criteria. Every first-episode control interval had all 16 recorded physics substeps and no reported contact-capacity overflow or observer errors. Values are discrete samples; the packed phase state and accepted-state snapshot may come from different points within an interval. Contact correlations do not establish cause. The layout and seed were used for development and do not qualify unused courses.
