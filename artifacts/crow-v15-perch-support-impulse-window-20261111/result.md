# Window-averaged perch support in one teacher-guided development start

The previous per-interval full-nominal test was unnecessarily strict: dynamic support can fall slightly below nominal in one interval and rise above it in another. For lane 3 in the perch-root-feedback screen, the 1,315-interval sustained opposing-grip window (intervals 3686–5000; 26.3 seconds) averaged 0.9999497 times nominal gravitational impulse through the feet. Its minimum 100-interval mean was 0.9988025 times nominal, and maximum was 1.0008049. All positive-normal bird/perch contact records in that window involved feet or toes; no nonfoot perch impulse was recorded. Root endpoint speed stayed within the earlier 0.12 m/s criterion and the selected wing joints were almost still in the window-end samples. That run recorded no toe speed-cap hits. This is much stronger evidence for contact-supported weight than the previous single-interval threshold, and supersedes using its longest-five-interval result as the sole support summary.

The impulse reconstruction uses normal, tangent and cross(normal,tangent) with body-A/B signs matching src/core/Tactile.cpp. The estimate has not been independently closed against full-system momentum and external aerodynamic forces, and position/attitude criteria are sampled at control endpoints. The sample is one start, teacher-guided, with perch feedback derived from a privileged teacher command and no release. Other starts failed or timed out. It is not a qualified controller, autonomous landing, or both-layout result.
