# Lower symmetric pronation load with matched turn controls

The all-body-wrench-ablated leg-launch turn screen achieved opposite small heading changes but the physical pronation drives saturated around 65% of the full steering window. The corresponding straight-flight baseline saturated about 58% over the earlier common interval, so a large part of the load exists before differential steering. Test whether lowering only the existing symmetric pronation carrier amplitude from 0.08 to 0.06 rad in candidate lanes 6/7 reduces saturation without losing physical flight or heading. Previous amplitude screens used different onset and feedback conditions; they are not the matched control for this question.

First run a no-turn 0.06-rad carrier with the same leg push, 7.42/7.46 second onsets, height/lateral feedback, body, flight aero, model damping, teacher-off policies and complete pronation observer. Then, only if both first episodes satisfy the previously registered no-recontact, state/coverage, wing/height/forward bounds and full-window pronation effort/position/speed gates, run the identical 0.06-rad carrier with the original 0.04-rad phase-gated differential turn signal, opposite signs in lanes 6/7. For both, compare unchanged lanes 0-5 and candidate launch prefixes against the earlier phase-turn run up to tick 310; compare candidate with its same-seed 0.06 no-turn control through tick 600 and compare full command targets. Turn acceptance requires each direction to achieve at least 0.10 rad signed yaw relative to that matching control with no more than 2 m lateral deviation, all other flight/physical checks, and lower measured full-window pronation saturation than the original respective 0.08-rad turn (0.64668/0.64689) by at least 0.05 absolute. Record actual joint tracking and effort rather than inferring them from targets. Failed or incomplete baseline/candidate runs are archived and do not qualify flight. This used-seed diagnostic does not validate biology, recovery, general turn commands, landing, or unused layouts and seeds.
