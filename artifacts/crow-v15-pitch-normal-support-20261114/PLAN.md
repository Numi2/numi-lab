# Pitch feedback during vertical stance contact

In the previous stationary perch return, the right foretoe's opposing-contact signal vanished around interval 304 even while the right foot still carried positive normal-only vertical impulse. The existing controller immediately slews its pitch correction toward zero when opposing-toe contact is missing; this may leave the body pitching forward while the stance foot is still loaded. Test that specific dependence without changing gains or toe/leg targets elsewhere.

Run 450 intervals on two otherwise identical matched v15 lanes with the original 0.8/0.04 pitch feedback. Lane 0 retains the existing gate requiring positive vertical impulse and opposing toe contacts. Lane 1 allows that same right-hip pitch correction whenever the right foot's preceding endpoint normal-only upward impulse exceeds 0.001 N s, even if opposing contacts are absent. No correction is added when this vertical-contact threshold fails, and the grip qualification continues to require opposing toes on both feet. Do not interpret final-substep normal as full-interval load. Preserve same actuator, body, contact, friction and physics settings, with no body force or position override.

Record first command and state divergence, control parity against the retained control, sensor-gate changes, contact and substep completeness, release, grip streaks, body tilt, first fall, toe-speed threshold and nonfoot contact. Require completed release, 100 consecutive terminal bilateral supported opposing-toe intervals, no toe-speed cap or fall and no non-foot contact; an increased fall time alone does not pass. Archive all outcomes. This is a constructed stationary test, not a walk, landing or mission qualification.
