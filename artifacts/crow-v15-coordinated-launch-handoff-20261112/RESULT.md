# Coordinated gait handoff improves contact; push-off remains capped

All eight trajectories matched their corresponding uniform-walk reference for 485 endpoints and 7,760 contact substeps before the transition. The two original-cutoff lanes had 163 and 165 both-feet-unloaded substeps in steps 501–515. All six coordinated gait/toe/crouch arms had zero such unloaded substeps in that interval. Every lane completed 575 steps, with 6,000 toe-velocity and contact observations, no recorded nonfoot ground impulse, overflow or solver errors. This supports the coordinated handoff on these closely related used-seed starts.

No arm passed the full no-cap criterion: controls capped a toe at step 502 and all coordinated arms capped toes at step 552 during push-off. In late-fade lane 2, explicit coordinate mapping identifies the left and right rear proximal toe velocities, which reach the configured -12 rad/s. Their endpoint angles moved from about -0.09 rad at step 551 to -0.288 rad at 552, near the -0.3 rad limit. Contact and actuator loading at the exact capped substep have not been isolated, so the cause remains a hypothesis. The old 160 ms push window previously avoided toe caps in a different configuration; the result does not transfer automatically.

The total foot normal impulse in steps 549–556 is 0.238–0.263 N s and every lane is foot-unloaded in steps 571–575, with rising root height. These facts alone do not prove leg-only power, flight stability, real-crow mechanics or mission completion. A matched physical control and full flight observer remain necessary. A targeted rear-toe contact and target screen follows; the present trial is retained as a failure of the full gate.
