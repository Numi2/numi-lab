# Hip-plus-ankle velocity feedback does not pass

With hip gain fixed at +0.5 in candidate lanes, ankle gains +0.5, -0.5, +1, -1, +2, -2 all failed the predeclared criteria: reduce the 800 Hz normal-load-weighted contact-point speed integral by at least 25% on each foot, maintain collective foot support, avoid toe-speed caps and nonfoot contact, and progress at least 0.05 m. Ratios, support and limits appear in fine-evaluation.json. Five of six candidate settings lost support for 3–152 substeps; two hit toe-speed limits. The remaining +0.5 ankle setting maintained support but ratios were 0.994 left and 0.941 right. This is a changing-contact velocity proxy, not tracked material slip, and the used starts are not held out. No controller was promoted.

The two reference lanes reproduced the previous baseline during the intervention, and all 1,600 pre-intervention states and 25,600 contact substeps matched. The sensor varied; mean absolute differences from the independent point-velocity reconstruction were 0.00037–0.00250 m/s with isolated maxima up to 0.215 m/s, subject to the stated tiny-contact threshold and timestamp differences. Those isolated discrepancies are an unresolved measurement limitation. This is a short development experiment, not proof of natural walking.
