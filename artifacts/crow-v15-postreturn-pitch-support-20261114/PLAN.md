# Pitch feedback during post-return contact loss

The matched distal-coupling trial retained opposing bilateral contact for eight intervals but both birds fell. In both, the pitch target decayed toward zero when the right-foot opposing-contact gate failed at intervals 300/302, while foot normal support persisted; pitch and forward drift then rose. Test the hypothesis that retaining bounded existing pitch feedback while either foot has measured normal upward contact improves recovery. Both lanes use the same 0.85 stance-front distal coupling after return. Only lane 1 replaces the right-foot opposing-contact condition for pitch feedback with either foot's prior last-substep normal upward impulse above 0.001 N s, after the return transition. All other control, drive bounds, model, contacts and solver remain unchanged. Log the changed gate, commands and matched prefix; inspect grip, posture, all physical substeps and toe speeds. Require 100 consecutive terminal qualified bilateral grip intervals without falling. Archive all failures. This constructed test is neither walking nor landing qualification; last-substep contact/state epoch alignment remains unproven.
