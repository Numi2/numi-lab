# Matched supported-push toe dynamics audit

For the four front proximal joints that reached the diagnostic speed threshold before a completed release, inspect every substep from 351.0 through the first threshold in the used-seed, paired push and crouch-hold traces. Start and end velocities are continuous across successive logged substeps, and the sum of the logged pre-solve and subsequent velocity changes reproduces the end-minus-start velocity to floating-point print precision. Push and hold have identical initial velocity in each matched lane.

The four push joints reach -12 rad/s by tick 355. Their summed pre-contact predicted velocity changes are +22.46 to +23.90 rad/s, opposed by -34.70 to -36.14 rad/s after that logging point. In each push lane, 69–73 of 74–76 substeps have at least one positive ground contact impulse. The matched hold joints finish between -0.016 and +0.077 rad/s. These results show that the large negative joint acceleration is associated with processing after the pre-contact observation during the push, countering rather than being directly explained by its pre-contact velocity change.

Post-presolve processing can contain contact, joint constraints, speed limiting and other solver operations; generalized joint velocities reflect the whole coupled body. These traces do not isolate a particular collider, friction term or numerical defect, and neither the speed threshold nor toe preload is a measured crow reference. This is a posthoc audit of correlated lanes on one development seed. Further targeted ablation or instrumentation is needed; no flight, landing or biology qualification follows.
