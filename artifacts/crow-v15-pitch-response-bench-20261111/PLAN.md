# Contact-conditioned pitch response identification

The retained landing runs tip both forwards and backwards while their load center moves away from the body center of mass. Before changing the flight-to-landing controller, measure the direction and stability of a small bilateral hip correction on the existing v15-derived stationary perch bench. This is a constructed component experiment, not an arrival or biological measurement.

Use eight first episodes in the same 32-substep native bench, with the same foot/toe model, contact solver and grip closure. Disable the release sequence for this test. Pair initial root pitch perturbations of +0.04 and -0.04 rad for each of four contact-conditioned hip-position terms: zero, +0.15, -0.15 and +0.30 times (minus pitch minus 0.4 times pitch rate), added to the existing reference feedback and bounded by the existing 0.16 rad cap. Start the additional term only after control step 10 and recorded foot contact. Use identical other state within each pitch sign. Treat positive and negative gains as direction-identification probes, not calibrated bird parameters.

Record all native substeps, accepted q/v and contact evidence, finite values, solver errors and saturation. Compare each candidate with its paired zero-gain start, including prefix parity before the new term, maximum pitch and rate, final pitch/rate, half-weight supported/opposing contact and falls. Require both pitch signs and both feet to remain within a 0.25 rad pitch bound for the final 100 intervals with continuous 100-interval half-weight bilateral opposing-normal support, no nonfoot contact, no speed-limit hit, and complete substep records before considering integration. A pass would qualify only this stationary bench; no gait, release, whole-route or real-crow claim follows. Preserve every arm, including numerical rollback or physical failure.
