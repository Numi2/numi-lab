# Related-crow takeoff-force comparison

Meilak et al.'s 2021 preprint (https://doi.org/10.1101/2021.11.19.469279; author-posted full text https://www.researchgate.net/publication/356409582_On_the_hindlimb_biomechanics_of_the_avian_take-off_leap) reports force-plate takeoffs of one carrion crow, six trials, sampling at 10 kHz and zero-phase low-pass filtering at 40 Hz. It reports a mean peak of 2.20 ± 0.29 bodyweights per leg. The source says peak GRF, while its precise vector/scalar extraction and force-plate-to-leg decomposition require checking before any direct comparison. This is a related species, not American-crow population data, and a preprint rather than a verified peer-reviewed study.

Use existing 16-substep 500-step development traces of the paired v15 takeoff candidate and baseline. In intervals 340–360 report maximum per-foot upward ground impulse divided by 1.25 ms and model bodyweight; a centered 25-ms moving-average proxy and last supported substep; no new physical runs. Also report full combined upward impulse and substep sample counts. These are unfiltered or simple moving-average vertical forces inferred from solver impulses, not the paper's filtered force-plate resultant. Contact quantization, filtering, leap conditions, model shapes and mass constrain comparison. Do not infer that any controller is biologically validated or set pass thresholds from one related-crow study. Preserve negative observations.
