# Carrion-crow takeoff-force comparison

A 2021 preprint reports six force-plate takeoffs by one carrion crow (Corvus corone), with a 10 kHz recording filtered at 40 Hz and a reported mean peak ground reaction force of 2.20 ± 0.29 bodyweights per leg. That is related-crow evidence, not an American-crow population range; the paper's exact component/resultant and per-leg computation require closer verification. The preprint is not presented here as a peer-reviewed result.

In the retained eight-lane 16-substep v15 candidate and matched baseline, the raw upward ground impulse divided by substep duration gives individual-foot peak vertical forces of 7.14–7.93 bodyweights. Peaks of a simple centered 25 ms moving average are 2.09–2.26 bodyweights. These candidate and baseline values match because the changed wing gate has not yet altered push-off in this window. The raw spikes and strong filter dependence are reasons to inspect numerical convergence and force profiles, not a biological pass or proof of a defect. A box average is not the study's 40 Hz zero-phase filter; upward force is not necessarily the same reported vector metric. Ground contact includes all simulated toes and soles, not isolated muscle force.

The traces cover 340–360 at 1.25 ms resolution from a single development seed on the estimated 0.45006 kg model. They cannot establish the observed crow's 10 kHz peak, species calibration, full sequence, flight, or landing. Reproducing the study's exact filtering and force definition, and matched temporal-resolution comparisons, are required before quantitative calibration. Raw results, scripts and prior traces remain in Git.
