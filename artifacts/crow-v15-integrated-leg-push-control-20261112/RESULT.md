# Integrated leg-extension control: negative result

The two-seed, same-lane paired test compared the current toe-bypassed launch with a control that actively holds the crouched leg target, while retaining wings, contacts and all other physics. All 16 pairs matched accepted states and contact records through step 550; their first difference was step 551, after extension could act. Observations were complete through step 625; no observed toe or authored joint speed-limit hits, nonfoot ground impulse, overflow or solver error appeared in the inspected windows. All starts survived to the horizon; this is not a flight or landing qualification.

In every paired start, the extension condition produced less vertical foot contact impulse over steps 549–556: approximately .476–.484 N s versus .505 N s for crouch hold. Root vertical velocity at step 556 was approximately .310–.380 m/s with extension and .342–.411 m/s with hold. For example, seed 20261126 lane 0 was .476 versus .505 N s and .351 versus .394 m/s. Vertical foot contact impulse in those cases did not increase in the subsequent windows through step 600. These are simulated coupled outcomes including active wings, feedback, gravity and contact, not biological force measurements or muscle-work estimates.

This configuration fails to demonstrate a leg-powered benefit under this matched control. The extension command's early ground phase and concurrent wings require further investigation; a separate predeclared wing-delay experiment tests timing rather than presuming that timing is the cause. The result does not invalidate older comparisons under different launch configurations but prevents applying their conclusion to this one.

Correction: the vertical contact impulse includes both normal and tangential contributions; the original result and JSON key called it normal-only. The numerical data and comparison are unchanged.
