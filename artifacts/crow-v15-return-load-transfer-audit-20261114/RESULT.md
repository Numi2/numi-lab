# Abrupt returning-foot load transfer in failed trials

Summing all 32 recorded contact substeps in the earlier matched pitch-feedback trial, the control's left-foot share of total vertical foot impulse changed from 0.074 at observer interval 306 to 0.579 at 307; in the stronger-feedback lane it changed from 0.017 at 307 to 0.441 at 308. Values include both contact-normal and tangential vertical contributions. Every substep in the audited interval range was present without duplicates. The fractions are descriptive and may be negative or exceed one if a foot's net impulse points downward.

This identifies an abrupt change in modeled load sharing that merits a contact-driven transfer investigation. The controller's last-substep normal-only contact signal is not the same quantity as these summed impulses, and the observer epoch has not been independently aligned to accepted state. The result does not establish a cause of the fall or a qualified gait or landing.
