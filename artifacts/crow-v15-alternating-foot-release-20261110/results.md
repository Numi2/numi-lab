# Alternating foot release: development endpoint screen

On matched teacher-off original J and I full starts, the alternating-leg candidate exactly reproduced every checked pre-release q/v, actor observation and action frame through steps 4432 and 5564 respectively. Both reached all five waypoints, had no observed fatal, solver or nonfoot perch contact, and ended bilaterally supported. Contact-gated walking and leg push remained unchanged. Accepted-step minimum obstacle clearance was 0.07416 m on J and only 0.00614 m on I; these are used development layouts and the latter is too narrow for robust navigation qualification.

During the predeclared left and right lift windows, the longest runs with all four lifted toes more than 1 mm clear at accepted endpoints, toe/perch normal impulse at most 1e-8 N s, opposite-foot task load above 0.1 N and upward contact impulse above 0.00005 N s were J 39/44 and I 41/39 endpoints. With unchanged release, the same windows gave J 19/0 and I 25/0. Thus the intervention improves this matched endpoint measure, especially for the right foot. Both runs regained bilateral load in all 100 late-window samples and at the final endpoint. Contact samples were aligned to the accepted pose using the measured one-step offset.

Before release, opposing front/rear toe impulses on both feet occurred in only 86/100 J and 74/100 I sampled endpoints, so uninterrupted grip remains unproven. The endpoint results do not establish all-substep force-free release or broad robustness; matched substep observer replays are pending. The event remains programmed after contact-conditioned support. No unused-layout or biological validation is claimed.
