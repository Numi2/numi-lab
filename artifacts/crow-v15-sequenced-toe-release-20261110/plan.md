# Isolated sequenced toe release

The full-contact observer exactly reproduced the preceding square-perch baseline for all 1200 sampled states. Five of eight grip-and-release starts retained final support under the complete foot/toe and nonfoot checks; all eight open-toe controls stayed supported but did not grip. Two bounded hip/ankle feedback signs reduced the passing count to three each. This suggests the simultaneous opening maneuver, not merely standing, can disturb the simulated balance; it does not identify the cause.

Keep the same 6x6 cm isolated perch, revised body, solver, eight matched initial states and contact-driven slow closure. Compare four arms: original simultaneous opening at 0.02 rad per step; rear-then-front opening at 0.04 rad per step; front-then-rear at 0.04; and an open-toe control. Both sequenced arms have approximately the same total maximum opening duration as the original. Record every contact; run 200 accepted steps so released toes and terminal support have time to settle. Require the first 150 baseline samples to match the saved prior run exactly before using the comparison. Predeclare success as at least 6/8 starts for one sequenced arm with at least three consecutive bilateral opposing-impulse endpoints, absence of that grip after release, all final 25 steps supported by both feet at >=half body weight with no nonfoot impulse, tilt <=0.35 rad and speed <=0.12 m/s, and terminal toe angle below 0.05 rad throughout those 25 steps. Open-toe controls should not produce bilateral opposing grip. This tests already-supported initial states only, not autonomous flight, landing or real birds.
