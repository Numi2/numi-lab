# Posthoc wing phase relative to release

Using the native v15 body forward kinematics, this audit reconstructs left and right wing-body center-of-mass positions relative to the torso center for the retained continuous-target development trial on seed 20261126. A +0.1 rad perturbation of both flap coordinates at the reference pose raised those two modeled relative wing-body centers by approximately 9.10 mm, and native model metadata identifies q indices 9 and 10 as the left and right flap joints. This establishes a local direction check, not a global wingtip or feather-motion calibration.

In the sampled 20 ms endpoints, the local high point of the mean left/right relative wing-body height between steps 355 and 360 occurred at step 358 in lanes 2–5 and step 359 in lanes 6–7. The first three consecutive unloaded control intervals began at steps 357, 357, 358, 358, 362 and 362 respectively. Thus the sampled high point was one interval after the start of sustained unloading in lanes 2–3, coincident in lanes 4–5 and three intervals before in lanes 6–7. A high point is only a proxy for onset of a downward stroke; torso rotation, wing geometry and substep motion are not separately measured here. The release definition is also a three-interval observation and not exact toe-off.

The comparative small-bird studies suggest relating leg impulse and actual first downstroke around lift-off. The current lane-specific absolute ramp produces variable relative timing in these samples. These are exploratory observations from one previously used seed, not crow-specific criteria, evidence of an actual aerodynamic impulse, or a passed flight screen. Measure continuous wingtip/force timing and toe-off before prescribing a contact-triggered controller or claiming biological realism.
