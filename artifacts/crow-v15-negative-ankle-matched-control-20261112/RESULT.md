# Same-start ankle feedback comparison: not qualified

Changing only ankle gain from zero to -.5 in four same-lane, same-seed hip +.5 comparisons reduced the right-foot 50 Hz p95 contact-point speed ratios from 2.81 to 1.71 and 2.80 to 1.95 on seed 20261126, and from 2.57 to .96 and 2.15 to 1.94 on seed 20261127. Both-feet-unloaded substeps changed 1 to 0, 13 to 0, 1 to 1 and 1 to 0 respectively. Nonetheless none of these four new trials passed all short-window gates: three exceeded the speed gate or missed knee timing, and the remaining trial lost one support substep and missed knee timing. Thus feedback can materially alter contact but does not reliably resolve the coupled gait.

Changing ankle +.5 to -.5 in four other same-start comparisons helped some and worsened others: seed 20261126 lane 4 right p95 ratio 2.77 to 1.71 and 50 to zero unloaded substeps; lane 5 1.34 to 1.63 and zero to seven. Seed 20261127 lane 4 was 2.35 to 1.18 and 52 to one; lane 5 was 1.54 to 2.03 and zero to one. All failed one or more gates.

The unchanged lanes 0, 1, 6 and 7 matched their earlier recorded states, toe-contact records, q and v over 300 steps on both seeds. Consequently the earlier two passing lanes were reproduced, not two additional independent successes. All pre-intervention records matched the original baseline. Sensor values varied; the independent reconstruction uses the same cutoff. These are correlated development starts, slow diagnostic walking, a small number of recovery windows, and changing contact-point speed is not measured material slip. The aliased sensor state remains a blocker. No controller or biological fit is qualified, and takeoff, flight, navigation and landing are not demonstrated.
