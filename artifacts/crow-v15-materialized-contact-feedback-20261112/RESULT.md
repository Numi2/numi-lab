# Materialized physical contact feedback: sensor responds, gait still fails

The isolated research host materializes body velocities from accepted generalized state in a separate pass after the original task completion, then measures normal-impulse-weighted fore-aft foot contact-point velocity. This preserves the prior task's observation and reward inputs; it is not a production repair to those inputs. The signal has a control-step delay and contact-point/state timestamp mismatch and is not tracked material slip.

Before feedback starts, 1,600 endpoint states and 25,600 contact substeps match the baseline exactly. The two unchanged control lanes match in the intervention window. Against independent articulated kinematics over steps 201–299, candidate sensor values varied (45–59 distinct packed values per lane); mean absolute disagreement was 0.00037–0.00125 m/s, with isolated differences up to 0.121 m/s. The audit uses a different impulse threshold for tiny contacts and a staggered state; these discrepancies remain a limitation. This is materially different from the prior invalid zero-velocity sensor test, not evidence that the previous zero sensor worked.

None of six gains passed the predeclared screen on the used seed. Positive gains 0.5, 1 and 2 yielded right-foot contact-motion integral ratios 0.879, 0.885 and 0.878 and left-foot ratios 0.947, 1.000 and 1.054 relative to each lane's matched baseline. The 2 setting also lost collective foot support for 6 substeps. Negative gains increased the proxy; -1 and -2 additionally hit toe-speed caps. All gain results and forward movement are in fine-evaluation.json. The metric aggregates speed at changing normal-load-weighted contacts, and is not measured slip of tracked surface material. This short 2 s development test does not qualify walking or establish biological realism.

The inputs.json host hash is the actual experiment host; screen-hashes.json's host entry describes the earlier base host used during shader assembly and is not the run host. No controller was promoted.
