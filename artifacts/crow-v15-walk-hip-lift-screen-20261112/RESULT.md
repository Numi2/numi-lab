# Hip phase and lift screen: not admitted

All eight first episodes completed 300 intervals, without reported physical failures or logged overflow. Recorded pre-walk endpoint and contact samples match the previous hip screen, and lane 0 exactly reproduces the entire previous -0.9 rad, 0.15 lift lane. These tests cover recorded values, not every hidden state. The same hip phase with 0.15–0.30 lift on lanes 0–3 produced 145.7–162.4 mm root x displacement in two seconds, versus 25.3–29.2 mm for their respective held baselines, with zero both-feet-unloaded substeps in both arms. Different lift values use different starts, so this does not establish a paired lift effect. Higher lift settings had 4–50 unloaded substeps per lane; two hit recorded toe-velocity caps. None is a qualified controller.

The old low-toe proxy counted endpoint configurations with a lifted sole and low toe, without knowing whether that toe was supposed to be loaded. In this screen, 510 of 540 such candidate observations had a toe contact at some point during the same 20 ms interval, while 30 had no recorded normal foot load throughout the interval. The baseline had 35 entirely unloaded among its 49 low-toe observations. Because endpoint geometry and interval contact are not simultaneous continuous measurements, these numbers do not establish slip or absence of drag. They demonstrate that classifying all low-toe observations as failed clearance was wrong; toe-only push-off can be normal. The prior screen RESULT is corrected by a retained addendum. Per-interval and final-substep classifications are in low-toe-classification.json.

The contact-point speed screen still fails: pooled endpoint median/p95 were 0.00522/0.09358 m/s versus 0.00276/0.03484 held. These are instantaneous velocities at accepted contact points, not integrated slip or a crow reference. Across the eight lanes, 42 complete recorded stances with both toe groups released rear-toe first, while 35 complete runs had a missing group. Whether the latter are true missing contacts or threshold/gap-fragment artifacts requires diagnosis. A longer matched, phase-resolved measurement with integrated contact-relative motion, regular touchdown/release cycles, perturbations and supported landing is needed before promotion. Two seconds on a used seed is not independent biological or full-task validation.
