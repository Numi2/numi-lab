# Contact-gated rear-toe release: failed short screen

With the supporting-leg stroke enabled, the rear-toe release achieved three left and four right support-qualified full-foot clearance bouts in all eight lanes over the two-second walking window. Aggregate sole-clear/toe-low endpoint counts fell from 639 to 403, and accepted endpoint contact-point speed median/p95 decreased from 0.02241/0.09706 to 0.02201/0.08559 m/s. The matched prefix states and contacts agreed, substep and replay checks passed, and there were no reported solver errors, overflow, nonfoot contacts or sampled toe-speed caps.

It failed the declared gates: lane 0 traveled 81.4% of paired sham, and lanes 0, 1, 2 and 5 had extra both-feet-zero-normal-impulse substeps (8 vs 1, 3 vs 0, 14 vs 3, and 11 vs 7 respectively). Those thresholded substeps can be transient and are not by themselves proof of a fall. The underlying contact-point speeds are still high and the short benchmark uses one previously used development seed. Do not promote; this does not qualify gait, biological realism, takeoff, landing, flight or autonomous navigation. This test changed both release amplitude and the requirement that the releasing foot be unloaded, so it does not isolate their individual contributions.

Post-hoc half-second block analysis in cycle-diagnostics.json shows that lane 0 gained 5.0 mm relative to sham in the second block but lost 5.4 mm and 9.2 mm in the following two blocks; its extra no-support threshold samples appeared in those later blocks. Other lanes show different patterns. A single early-step comparison or aggregate average would not explain all outcomes, and this observation does not isolate the causal component.
