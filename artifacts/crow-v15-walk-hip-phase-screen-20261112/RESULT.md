# Hip phase screen: not admitted

All eight first episodes ran 300 intervals without reported physical failure or logged contact overflow. The pre-walk recorded endpoints and substeps match the preceding sweep, and the unchanged phase/lift lane 6 reproduces every recorded endpoint and contact substep exactly. This supports the intervention comparison for these recorded states; it does not establish identity of all hidden state.

The -0.9 rad lane traveled 155.3 mm in two seconds versus 25.3 mm for its held baseline, with zero both-feet-unloaded substeps versus zero held, but it had 74 low-toe endpoints across its feet, and six complete foot-contact runs lacked one toe group. Its five complete stances with both groups were rear-toe-first on release. This is a useful lead but fails clearance and does not establish robust grip, causality, biological realism or full sequence success. Each offset used a different start, so differences among phase lanes are not paired estimates. A follow-up must test the same phase across starts, retaining a zero-phase control, and vary swing clearance rather than trading support for speed.

The full eight-lane sweep was rejected: 866 both-feet-unloaded substeps versus zero in the held baseline; 438 low-toe endpoints versus 49 held; pooled contact-point speed median/p95 0.00522/0.08737 m/s versus 0.00276/0.03484 held, exceeding the preexisting 1.5x criterion. Positive offsets also produced backward travel or severe support losses. Toe-contact order, missing groups, and individual outcomes are in the accompanying machine-readable analysis. The gait is much slower than the related-magpie study walking range; that range is not an American-crow threshold. No change is promoted to the autonomous controller.
