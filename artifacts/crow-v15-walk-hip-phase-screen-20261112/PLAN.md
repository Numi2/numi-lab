# Matched hip timing screen

Hypothesis: timing hip motion relative to the knee/ankle lift may change the cancellation between rear-toe propulsive and front/sole braking impulses without sacrificing toe clearance and support. This is a diagnostic intervention, not a claim that either contact component is inherently wrong. Sweep hip phase offsets [-0.9,-0.6,-0.3,+0.3,+0.6,+0.9,0,+1.2] radians, retaining the same opposite-foot support gate, knee and ankle lift, toe controls, eight initial states and 16 substeps as the prior experiment. Fix lift amplitude at 0.15 for all lanes; lane 6 has offset zero and must reproduce that prior lane exactly. All lanes must match the previous run before walking. Two seconds of walking on a used development seed is a screen, not a biological validation or autonomous qualification.

Measure physical failures, overflow, continuous load, supported swing clearance, low-toe endpoints, contact-point velocity, travel and front/rear release order, retaining all failures. Compare against both the prior lane-6 zero-phase control and held walking baseline; no promotion unless all gates pass, improve travel and reproduce on independent starts with the full sequence. Never trade grip, support or clearance for displacement. Because lanes use different starts, lane comparisons are screening evidence rather than matched treatment effects; promising offsets require repeated paired starts.
