# Contact-gated supporting-leg stroke

The existing gait allows a leg command only while the opposite foot is loaded. When the other foot lifts, that suppresses the negative half of the carrier for the supporting leg: the supporting hip returns toward neutral. Native forward-kinematics inspection of a development pose indicates increasing hip angle moves the foot backward relative to the torso, which could push the body forward if the foot remains planted. This does not establish in-vivo biomechanics or predict whether the coupled simulation will improve.

Test one surgical change against the held-upper-body sham: keep the positive carrier (swing lift) gated on opposite-foot support; gate the negative carrier on that same leg being supported, using existing accepted contact bits. Keep amplitude, cadence, toe command off, body, policies, upper-body hold and all other behavior unchanged. Run 8 lanes of used seed 20261127, 500 steps, 16 substeps, no scheduled resets. Require exact q/v/action and contact parity through step 200, complete no-reset episodes and all substeps, no errors, overflow, nonfoot contact, or toe-speed caps. Require at least ten support-qualified clearance bouts per foot per lane, no more unloaded substeps than its paired sham in each lane, positive forward travel and at least 1.5 times matched sham in at least six of eight lanes without any lane losing >10% travel. Also report loaded contact-point speed, if available, and toe-clearance endpoints; this is an engineering screen, not evidence of biologically realistic gait. Record negative and inconclusive results; do not promote without additional starts, longer tests, realistic speeds, and independent biological comparisons.
