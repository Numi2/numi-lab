# Supporting-leg stroke: failed gait screen

Native forward kinematics at an actually loaded foot supported the hypothesized backward foot movement relative to torso for positive hip motion; see hip-geometry.json. With the tested contact-gated stroke, all eight lanes moved 3.40–3.73 times farther than their matched no-stroke controls over six seconds (0.328–0.352 m vs 0.094–0.099 m). Prefix state and contact parity, full no-reset episodes, replay cross-check and complete walking-window substeps passed. There were no logged non-foot contacts, constraint overflow, substep errors or sampled toe-speed caps.

This is not a walking success. Every lane failed the ten-bouts-per-foot criterion: only 1–2 qualified clearance bouts per foot remained, largely at the beginning. The total sole-clear/toe-low endpoint count rose from 60 to 3,054; 7 of 8 lanes also had extra both-foot-zero-normal-impulse substeps. Candidate foot-plus-toe positive-contact endpoint speed median/p95 increased from 0.00272/0.04969 to 0.01963/0.15118 m/s. Those point speeds are instantaneous 20 ms endpoint estimates, not integrated slip distance, but the result is consistent with substantial sliding/drag and cannot be promoted. Root speed is still far below the speed range covered by the related-corvid reference experiment. A one-seed six-second trial cannot establish biological function, takeoff, landing, flight or navigation.

The first post-hoc contact-speed script invocation attempted to read velocity from the position-only kinematic observer and failed with IndexError. The corrected script invokes the established velocity observer and checks its positions against the independent position observer before computing results. The simulation and gate comparison were unchanged.
