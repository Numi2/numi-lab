# Result: the modeled first transition lacks upward impulse in the recontact window

A read-only GPU observer copied the body-wrench buffer immediately before and after the existing wing/tail/fuselage aerodynamic kernel and logged the per-body force difference. The observed 400-step run reproduced the reference q/v/action/contact records exactly for all eight lanes, and all recorded contact substeps from 335–400 matched. The observer produced all 3,584 expected force records for steps 348–375, with one 41-body stride, finite values, no reported observer errors or overflow. The body indices correspond to the native v15 model; the root, left wing, right wing and tail contributions sum to the logged total to rounding precision in the reported windows. This measures the simulation kernel's output, not a real bird's force.

For development lanes 2–7, over steps 358–365 (0.16 s), the aerodynamic kernel added +0.3346 to +0.3370 N s vertical impulse and foot contacts added +0.2432 to +0.2590 N s. With assumed -9.81 m/s² runtime gravity and modeled mass 0.45006 kg, gravity contributes approximately -0.7064 N s. Native whole-body FK reports -0.1032 to -0.1162 N s vertical momentum change in that interval; the remaining accounting residual is +0.0096 to +0.0103 N s. The result indicates that the measured modeled support and aerodynamic impulse together are insufficient to hold vertical momentum in this interval, consistent with the observed descent and subsequent foot recontact. It does not by itself identify whether the aerodynamic closure, wing kinematics, leg release or control timing is the cause.

The vertical aerodynamic impulse over steps 348–365 was +0.4691 to +0.4718 N s; the two wing bodies accounted for most of it, with smaller modeled root and tail contributions. The force accounting assumes the runtime gravity matches the nominal model and uses independently reconstructed endpoint momentum; residuals are not proof of a particular omitted force. These are six correlated lanes of one used seed with empirical aero coefficients and do not establish real-crow realism, leg-powered takeoff, flight or landing qualification.
