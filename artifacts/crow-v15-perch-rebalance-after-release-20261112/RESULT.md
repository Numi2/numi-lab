# Rebalancing after toe release: stable support but no complete regrip

The two unchanged controls and the other six lanes up to return exactly reproduced 3,842 logged states and 122,944 contact substeps against the high-traction reference. All 140,800 expected substeps were present, with no observer errors; endpoint foot-contact impulse sums matched native named contacts within 1.01e-9 N s.

Two constructed starts (lanes 2 and 3) retained a qualified single-foot release for 46 and 42 consecutive 20-ms intervals, returned the other foot, remained upright and had no nonfoot contacts or falls. Gradually returning the shift target to neutral eliminated their previous brief loss of two-foot load. However, in each of the final 100 intervals the returned left toes lacked the required opposed grip, even while both feet carried load. The other six lanes failed release/regrip or fell. Zero of eight passed the complete original gate. The comparable high-traction reference also passed zero of eight.

This exposes a placement-versus-balance tradeoff to investigate, not a completed grip fix. Starts were constructed on a stationary perch with uncalibrated friction; no autonomous landing, full sequence or biological validation is established.
