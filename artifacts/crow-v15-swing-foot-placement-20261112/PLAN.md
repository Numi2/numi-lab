# Preregistered exploratory swing-phase placement

The recent same-lane development traces show that a gradual correction after rear-toe contact preserves support better than an abrupt one but does not reduce the front-toe loading delay. Hypothesis: beginning the same limited paired knee/ankle placement in late swing before rear contact can improve front-toe loading while avoiding a sudden loaded-leg correction. This is a deliberately falsifiable control hypothesis, not a biological claim. Observed rear-touchdown phases concentrate near 0.18 cycle left and 0.68 cycle right in these correlated development starts, with earlier outliers and transient cycles; a fixed clock can fail under perturbation and is not a general gait solution.

On the same two development seeds, eight lanes and 4–6 second walking interval, lanes 0–3 remain untouched matched controls. Candidate lanes 4 and 5 use offsets (-0.015,+0.025) radians for (knee, ankle); lanes 6 and 7 use (-0.030,+0.050). In leg-relative cycle phase, even candidate lanes use smooth onset .02–.12, hold through .28, smooth release .28–.38; odd candidate lanes use onset .07–.17, hold through .33, release .33–.43. Right leg is shifted half a cycle. The existing 4.02–4.22 second fade remains. The actual position drives and contact solver remain in control; no body force is injected.

Require recorded pre-intervention and control parity, plus existing support, toe-speed cap, nonfoot, p95 sampled contact-speed, progress, joint-recovery coverage, knee-timing and at least 15% reduction of rear-to-front loading delay on both feet. Report all lanes and failures. A short pass would not establish biological fidelity or perturbed-start, sustained walk, checkpoint, takeoff, grip, flight, navigation or landing qualification.
