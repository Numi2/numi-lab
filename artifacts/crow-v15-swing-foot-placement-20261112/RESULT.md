# Swing-phase placement: rejected

All eight changed candidate lanes failed at least one prespecified physical gate. The median rear-to-front toe-loading interval was reduced by at least 15% in only one of eight candidates: seed 20261127, lane 5, ratio 0.66 versus its same-lane no-placement control. That candidate also had eight both-feet-below-threshold substeps and failed recovery coverage and knee timing. The other seven ratios were 0.99–1.12. No candidate met the joint physical and toe-timing gates.

The both-feet-below-threshold substep counts for lanes 4–7 were 0, 22, 8, 1 on seed 20261126 and 0, 8, 0, 7 on seed 20261127; these tiny-impulse thresholds do not establish that the bird fell. The 95th percentile changing-contact-point speed exceeded the 1.5 baseline ratio in five of eight candidate lanes on at least one foot, and several lanes had incomplete recovery coverage. The corrected toe audit found 55 rear-first and one front-first modeled touchdown in each seed, both reversals in lane 6. This does not prove real crow foot order: modeled contact groups, event debounce, other corvid species, surface and much lower speed limit the comparison. No toe speed caps or nonfoot ground impulse were recorded in the candidate windows.

Lanes 0–3 matched the corresponding previously retained negative-ankle control in all recorded states, toe contacts and q/v samples; pre-intervention state/contact records also matched the uniform baseline. These are two short, correlated development batches, not untouched layouts or independent animals. Contact-order and p95 metrics concern modeled proxy contact groups and changing contact points, not measured real-bird joint kinematics or exact stance slip. A phase-clock controller also cannot be assumed to match actual recovery under perturbations. In particular, the target-difference audit shows that at the beginning of the faded intervention the left foot already had both modeled contact groups loaded in seed 20261126. It is therefore not evidence of an intervention confined to unloaded swing, even though its phase was chosen to lead the usual touchdown. Differences after trajectories diverge cannot isolate the direct command.

No candidate is promoted. A reliable contact-driven gait, leg-only takeoff, toe grip and release, qualified/frozen flight, and uninterrupted navigation and supported landing on unused layouts and seeds remain unproven. The baseline and candidate run logs are preserved in Git; gait, support and geometric measures are exploratory rather than validation of crow biology.
