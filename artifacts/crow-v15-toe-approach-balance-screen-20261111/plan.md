# Slower and bilateral toe approach on perturbed perch starts

The early balance-gain and centre-of-mass interventions did not produce robust grip: all modified modes lacked a successful release, and most starts fell. During pregrip, the current controller closes rear toes at 0.02 rad per control tick while front toes remain open until rear contact is detected; the resulting transient is one plausible source of instability. Compare three physical position-drive variants: rear closing rate 0.005 per tick, a mild concurrent front preload capped at 0.20 rad, and both together. Preserve later sensor-triggered grip/release logic, the original hip/ankle controller, all physical forces, body, perch and solver. Four matched starts (reference, +/-2 mm position, +0.01 rad roll), four modes, 16 substeps, 900 ticks, no external forces or resets. The original control must exactly match the retained acquisition trace; each modified start must match its original initial state. Measure bilateral support, opposed toe impulses, separation-axis overlap, release, falls, nonfoot contact and native failures. Brief acquisition followed by falling fails; a final layout or flight-arrival qualification is outside this exploratory screen.
