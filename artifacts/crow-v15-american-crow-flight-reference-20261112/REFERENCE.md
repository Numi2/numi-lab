# American-crow takeoff measurement source

Brandon Edward Jackson, [The allometry of bird flight performance](https://scholarworks.umt.edu/etd/960/), University of Montana dissertation, 2009, describes force-plate jump measurements and high-speed video of maximum burst takeoff across 32 passerine species, and pectoral muscle measurements including American crows. The public abstract supports existence of measurements and identifies the tested behavior; it does not expose the American-crow force-time trace or numeric jump impulse. The full-text download returned HTTP 403 during this audit. Do not invent or treat a cross-species relationship as an American-crow calibration target. An independently accessible full text or underlying data would be needed to verify specimen counts, methods, units and individual crow values.

This supports keeping leg impulse, wing onset, body motion and aerodynamic work as coupled, separately measured variables in the simulation. It does not validate the current v15 mass, limb drives, estimated aerodynamic closure or simulated takeoff. Jackson and Dial's crow wingbeat observations are separately documented in the existing references and remain specific to escape takeoff.
