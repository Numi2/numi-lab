# Staged toe extension: stationary release and regrip component

Eight constructed stationary trials completed 550 control intervals each with complete recorded substeps and no observer errors. At the two stronger open-toe targets (-0.20 and -0.28 radians), all four trials (two starts per target) satisfied the specified 10-consecutive-interval one-foot release gate and finished with 100 consecutive intervals of bilateral opposing contact and support, with no nonfoot perch impulse in that final window. Those four windows averaged 100.04–100.06% of nominal body-weight impulse, subject to force measurement limitations. At -0.10 radians, one of two trials returned and met that gate; neutral extension produced no return in either start. No lane crossed the 0.1 m root-height fall threshold. The observer last-substep normal impulses were compared independently with named endpoint contacts.

The release gate requires at least half nominal body-weight impulse, not full support in every interval. This is a stationary development screen with two closely related starts, estimated joint parameters, an experimental modified v15-derived body, and privileged geometry; it does not qualify autonomous flight, landing, biological realism, or both layouts. Toe speeds here are sampled only at control endpoints. A separate substep-velocity repeat is required for the original flight regression.
