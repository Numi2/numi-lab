# Lift-control screen

Three experimental copies of the flight law held pronation bias at -0.75 and added respectively 0.20, 0.40 or 0.60 to the bounded flap-amplitude command after six seconds. Each ran eight lanes for 1600 control steps on an estimated revised wing and shoulder-aligned aerodynamic prototype, fixed head, zero actor, no obstacles. No lane had a solver or physical failure. Across samples 1000–1500, the +0.20 arm met the preset heading (0.15 rad), lateral speed (0.20 m/s), forward speed (0.20 m/s), and height (0.15 m) tolerances in 8/8 lanes covering straight, both turns, climb, and descent. The +0.40 and +0.60 arms met them in 0/8 due to overshooting height, among other errors. The +0.20 arm still had roughly 0.11 m height bias; it is not qualified for changed starts, recovery, other speeds, real flight, or navigation. The V1 source remains unchanged.
