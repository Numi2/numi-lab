# Returning-foot placement ablation

During both previous return trials the stance foot moved forward, its front toes approached their joint limits and its opposing front contact disappeared as the returning leg's additional knee and ankle placement targets ramped. Earlier return accelerated stance grip loss without earlier opposing returning-foot contact. Test whether that additional placement ramp causes the support loss: the matched candidate disables only the return placement ramp, retaining preclose at 0.44 rad and the existing gradual reduction of lift, knee and ankle offsets, toe feedback, stance balance and all plant and solver limits. The control retains the original ramp. This may prevent the returning foot from reaching the perch, so check actual contacts and the full 100-interval bilateral grip requirement as well as stance-foot movement, speed limits and falls. Require exact matching logged prefix and archive failures. This is a stationary constructed diagnostic, not walking, landing or autonomous mission qualification.
