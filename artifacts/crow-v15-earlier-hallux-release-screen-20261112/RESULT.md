# Earlier hallux phase: rejected

The 0.50-rad earlier, -0.02-rad peak target completed matched 300-step trials in eight development lanes. Both arms had exact prewalk parity through step 200; the candidate had at least three supported full-foot-clearance bouts per foot per lane, no additional both-feet-zero-normal substeps versus the no-extra-stroke held reference, no reported physical failure, nonfoot contact, overflow, reset or toe velocity cap, and 1.04–1.25 times held forward displacement. These are short used-seed results, not biological gait qualification.

The candidate fails the specified toe gates. It had 198 sole-clear/toe-low endpoints versus 49 in the held reference; 134 had accepted toe contacts, with sampled point-speed median/p95 0.04552/0.06993 m/s. The matched original-phase sham had 183 such endpoints, 140 with accepted toe contacts. In all 40 complete candidate stance runs containing both toe groups, rear toe contact ended after the front group: zero met the desired rear-first sequence. Anatomical digit mapping and segmentation remain unverified; point speeds are not integrated slip or biological limits.

Direct motor-target joins found the same timing classification as the original phase: 24/40 stances had no target below -0.001 rad by last front contact, and 16/40 first had one in the same 20 ms interval. The earlier phase did change actual captured target values, so it was not an inactive switch. The previous accepted opposite-foot support flag was absent at the two immediately preceding control intervals in all 40 stances; at the last interval it was present for 16/40. This supports the hypothesis that the opposite-support condition restricts earlier release, although copied prior-step flags and interpolated targets do not prove the exact gate evaluation or why the toe remains in contact. Raw events and audit methods are retained.

The candidate is not promoted. The study cannot establish continuous walking, takeoff, flight, gripping, landing or autonomous navigation on either layout.
