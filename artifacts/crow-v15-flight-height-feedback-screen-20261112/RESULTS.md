# Common-mode height correction: promising, not qualified

Two unchanged controls and six experimental prefixes matched the retained run exactly: 5,100 state samples and 81,600 contact substeps. All eight first episodes remained airborne through 24 s with no post-4-s joint speed caps or nonfoot contacts. Obstacle collisions were disabled; direct body moments were disabled. Two early pre-4-s toe-cap events occurred in different lanes, as recorded in the analysis.

At a .55 m/s and .95 m target, the experimental gains .4, .8 and 1.2 produced 12–24 s mean height errors +.055, +.041 and +.033 m respectively, with mean body-forward speed .506–.508 m/s. The same-lane, same-command uncorrected comparison for lane 2 had +.0837 m mean height error versus +.0554 m with gain .4. Other lanes had different reference speed or height requests, so their corresponding prior values are not matched direct comparisons. Control lanes request .35 m/s and different heights; the comparison script's printed control speed/height errors use the experimental .55/.95 target and should not be interpreted as control error.

This supports further controlled tests of height correction but does not establish accuracy across the command envelope, transient recovery, biological realism or flight qualification. The tests use development seeds and an obstacle-free diagnostic scene.
