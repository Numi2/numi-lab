# Early wing motion during unassisted push: development screen

Six experimental lanes begin a smooth phase-inverted wingbeat after a bilateral-contact initiation of the leg push; paired onset durations are 0.04, 0.10 and 0.20 s. The two direct body-moment actions are disabled from 6.30 s, although those actuators were available earlier and other empirical aerodynamic closures remain. Two control lanes keep the previous no-assistance leg push without early wings. Eight used development starts, 16 substeps, teacher off, 10 s horizon. All lanes exactly match their no-assistance counterparts at the logged endpoints and contact substeps through interval 350; controls match throughout their common 500-interval horizon.

All six early-wing lanes become airborne and retain no recorded foot loads from 8–10 s, reach the first waypoint flag, and reach sampled root heights of about 1.02–1.19 m by 10 s. The two controls remain grounded. This is a horizon-censored diagnostic, not a completed flight or mission. Before first foot unloading, the two modeled wing centers move downward relative to the body by roughly 0.010–0.040 m. Some lanes unload, recontact and unload again; the explicit transition list must not be replaced by a last-contact-as-first-toe-off interpretation. There are no observed toe velocity-cap events in intervals 340–370. The synchronized q data match endpoint q and the replay-derived body poses within the reported tolerances.

Combined upward foot impulse in intervals 348–353 is 0.397–0.589 N s compared with 0.612–0.613 N s in grounded controls. These impulses include body weight and other contact and aerodynamic effects, and the intervals include different contact histories. They do not isolate leg work or prove leg-powered takeoff. The results do not establish real-bird timing, stability under perturbation, qualified flight, navigation, grip or landing. A matched no-extension-command control is required before interpreting the contribution of the legs.
