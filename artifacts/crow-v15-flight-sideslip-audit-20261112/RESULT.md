# Sideslip in the bank-and-pitch flight screen

Reanalysis of the retained, previously used-seed flight traces shows that the earlier sampled minimum-speed check did not establish coordinated forward flight. At the last recorded interval 1600, the two mild-bank lanes had body-relative horizontal velocity approximately (-0.105, -0.769) and (+0.235, +0.803) m/s, where the first component is along the body's projected forward x-axis and the second is lateral. Their velocity directions were about -98° and +74° from that axis. In other words, those particular sampled states are predominantly sideways; one is slightly backward. They had not completed the requested turns.

This is a diagnostic of the simulated trajectory, not a biological threshold or a complete flight history. Each lane has only ten or twelve snapshots between intervals 500 and termination, 100 control intervals apart. The body axes derive from quaternion orientation and v[0:3] is interpreted as world linear velocity, consistent with the simulation's aerodynamic kernel; the code and input hashes are preserved. The source data do not establish which aerodynamic or controller mechanism caused the sideslip. The stronger positive-bank lane physically failed at interval 1466; its later accepted samples are not counted.

Future flight screening must report body-relative forward and lateral velocities and continuous or sufficiently sampled course/heading, alongside altitude, attitude, collisions and actual waypoint progression. Do not accept the old minimum-speed check as proof of realistic flight. A practical sideslip limit must be declared for each maneuver and duration before its experiment and validated against appropriate bird data where available. This audit does not freeze or qualify the controller.
