# Gait joint-boundary motion, stratified by ipsilateral foot load

Reanalyzing the retained 2 s development walk at all 16 physics substeps, we grouped knee and ankle second-difference magnitudes by control-update boundary versus within-interval, and by whether that same foot carried normal impulse for all three samples used by the finite difference. Pooled across eight simulation lanes and both sides, the foot-unloaded boundary samples had mean knee/ankle proxies 973/682 rad/s² (547 samples each), versus 211/60 rad/s² for foot-unloaded within-interval samples (8,169 each). For foot-loaded boundary samples the corresponding means were 327/180, versus 66/21 within intervals. The detailed distribution and counts are in `analysis.json`.

This establishes that large boundary-associated changes are present even without measured load on that foot; it does not isolate the controller as the cause. Other joints and the other foot remain dynamically coupled, contact classification is based on an impulse threshold, and consecutive samples are correlated. The calculated quantity is a finite-difference motion proxy, not measured torque, material stiffness, or American-crow joint acceleration. A previous 50 ms action smoothing intervention failed and increased boundary peaks; simply adding lag is not supported as a fix. A successful change must preserve support and stride while measurably reducing boundary concentration, and must still be checked against biological gait evidence.
