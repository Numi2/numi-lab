# Stance pitch support: failed controlled trial

The baseline exactly reproduced all 450 states of the archived toe-closure lane, and the new and baseline lanes matched for 225 pre-intervention states. Both independently achieved 11 qualifying clearance intervals; neither obtained any interval satisfying all bilateral opposing-grip, support, tilt and speed gates after return. The baseline and modified lanes fell below 0.1 m at intervals 308 and 304 respectively. All 32 contact substeps were observed with no recorded observer errors or nonfoot impulse; both reached the 12 rad/s accepted-state toe-speed reporting threshold, while substep toe speed and effort remain unmeasured.

The pitch-support modification temporarily kept stance support available and let return placement advance to its full target, whereas the baseline stalled at 20%. The returning foretoe nevertheless remained separated: independently reconstructed nearest gap at zero-based tick 250 was about 9.67 mm versus 3.75 mm in the baseline; the modified lane had no endpoint samples with positive returning-foretoe normal versus 20 for baseline. The combined hip target had a maximum interval jump of approximately 0.13 rad after the fall when the common balance term switched off; that post-fall spike must not be inferred to have caused the initial failure. This constructed-state experiment is not walking or landing qualification.
