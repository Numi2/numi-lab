# Supported knee-ankle placement during return

In the anticipatory-close trial, modeled foretoe boxes remained several millimeters from the perch after return began, despite measured toe motion. A previous fixed-root geometric search at the reached clearance pose predicted that positive knee and negative ankle offsets can shift the foot toward the perch; those static predictions do not account for coupled motion. Prior attempts which waited to complete this motion in free clearance stalled. Test a gradual physical joint-target adjustment during the existing return, including contact, rather than requiring extended clearance to finish it. The starting clearance and toe-opening gates are unchanged. Lane 0 targets +0.075 rad knee/-0.15 rad ankle, lane 1 +0.15/-0.30, with 20-interval slew, advancing only with opposing support-foot normals and bounded posture. Continue the existing anticipatory toe command. No changes to model limits, friction, forces or material are permitted.

Check pre-intervention matched states, commanded and accepted joint bounds, support normals, substep contact coverage, toe speed, 10 initial qualifying clearance intervals, then 100 continuous final intervals of bilateral opposing-toe support and safe pose. Record contact and any fall without attributing failure to the solver. This constructed test is neither an airborne landing nor qualification; a static geometry prediction does not prove dynamic improvement.
