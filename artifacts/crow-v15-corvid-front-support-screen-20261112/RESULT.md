# Front-toe support sweep: rejected

All eight first episodes completed 300 steps without reported physical failure, solver error, overflow, nonfoot-ground contact or toe velocity caps. The zero-front-drive lane reproduced its retained own-support predecessor exactly across 300 endpoints and 4,800 substeps; all lanes matched the 200-step prewalk prefix. Measured target buffers confirmed nonzero front-toe position requests in lanes 1-7; the actual joint-angle comparison is in front-target-audit.json and is not a direct torque measurement.

Front-toe drive changed contact order, but not into a usable forward gait. Of 42 complete stances with both toe groups, 14 ended rear-first, 26 front-first and two in the same recorded 1.25 ms substep. Thirteen complete foot-contact runs had no detected front group, and 32 runs were partial. The 0.15 rad lane had four rear-first stances in five but traveled -8.33 mm in the walking window, backwards versus +26.08 mm held. The 0.20 rad lane had five rear-first in seven but traveled -10.58 mm versus +29.72 mm held and had 20 both-feet-zero-normal substeps versus zero held. The 0.08 and 0.10 lanes traveled forward but achieved two and three rear-first stances out of five. These per-lane settings use distinct initial conditions and are not replicated causal estimates.

The overall prespecified gates failed: 277 sole-clear/toe-low endpoints versus 49 held, and loaded contact-point speed median/p95 0.00426/0.05968 m/s versus 0.00276/0.03484 held. Both forward progress and no-extra-unloaded-substep gates failed; all lanes had at least three supported clearance bouts per foot. These thresholded samples are not integrated slip, measured biology or proof that every unsupported sample is a fall. No setting is promoted.

The magpie's related-corvid release order is a qualitative reference only; the tested front angles are engineering hypotheses rather than American-crow measurements. The two-second used-seed screen does not qualify gait, takeoff, flight, grip, landing or full autonomous execution.
