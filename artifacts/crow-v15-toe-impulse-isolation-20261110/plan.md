# Isolate the aligned toe-contact impulse

In a retained aligned-catch run, the immediate-closure case accepted a 97.1 N s summed toe impulse and a -136 m/s root velocity before body contact. First replay that case exactly and capture full q/v immediately before the suspect step, verifying the prefix and original suspect-step summaries against the retained trace. Then restart from that captured state with a fresh solver/contact cache: compare the same actuator target, holding each toe at its reached position, a 2x finer temporal resolution over the same 5 ms, and zero-friction contact with the original targets. Keep masses, geometry, and initial q/v identical; report target, all accepted q/v, contact impulses and status. These one-step counterfactuals cannot establish a fix, real-bird contact, a full landing or autonomy. A fresh-cache difference points toward history sensitivity but cannot uniquely identify its cause. Do not bypass large accepted impulses or classify ordinary contacts as numerical defects.

Follow-up diagnostic: the failed contact list contains manifold points with 35-43 mm reported positive separation. Compile a byte-sourced baseline collision shader and a research-only variant that suppresses tangential friction on points more than 4 mm apart while leaving their normal constraints intact. At the same captured state compare both under the original friction materials, on the same one-step fresh-cache replay. This deliberately probes causality; its threshold is not a calibrated contact model or a production change. Check baseline against the installed shader first. A bounded variant, if observed, does not alone establish a valid grip or justify changing general contact behavior.

A further one-step screen varies velocity sweeps because the observed impulse increased with iteration count. The Wave32 block-Jacobi damping is based on normal-row coupling and has a 1/32 lower bound, while the unstable case includes densely coupled friction rows. Test a research-only alternative that caps damping for non-rod constraints by the inverse island constraint count, with 4, 16, 64 and 128 velocity sweeps. Compare the unmodified source-compiled shader to the installed baseline and examine actual residual/status plus physical output. This is only a stability hypothesis: small updates may under-solve contact, and this conservative bound has not been proven for the full coupled friction operator. Do not promote it without convergence, independent reference and existing-scene regression tests.

The Wave32 damping probe left the one-step result unchanged, so it did not establish that path as active. The ordered contact-island solver also applies a full projected 3D block update. As a separate diagnostic, interpolate that ordered update toward the prior feasible impulse with factors 0.25 and 0.10, then test 4, 16, 64 and 128 sweeps at the same captured state. Measure the reported normal residual as well as velocity and impulse; merely suppressing divergence without convergence is not a solution. Keep all modifications to research shader copies.

The bound is a diagnostic, not a convergence proof or performance-qualified correction; projection and full friction coupling need independent verification.
