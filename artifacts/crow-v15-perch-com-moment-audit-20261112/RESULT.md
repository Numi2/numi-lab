# Perch angular impulse about the modeled center of mass

For the six tilt failures in the rejected earlier-wing sequence, we reconstructed the center of mass from native forward kinematics and the estimated v15 segment masses. In 25-interval windows centered on the first 0.8-rad pitch crossing, the net perch-contact angular impulse about that center of mass had the same sign as pitch in all six cases: -0.247 to -0.340 N m s for the four negative-pitch lanes and +0.295 to +0.297 N m s for the two positive-pitch lanes. Across the selected windows centered on 0.1, 0.2, 0.4 and 0.8 rad, each lane also had a same-sign net perch impulse. The windows overlap and are not independent trials.

The reconstruction matches all 35 bird-body positions in two retained replay frames of lane 5 to 2.4e-7 m and orientations to 3.5e-8 in quaternion distance; source q matches to 4.7e-9. This validates those checked frames against replay, not every state or the biological segment masses. The center of mass comes from the estimated model and is sampled at each control endpoint while impulses and contact points are substep measurements. This is the perch contribution only; aerodynamic impulses, direct moments, and temporal changes in the center of mass within an interval are omitted. The result shows an observed destabilizing contact contribution and motivates contact-mediated control, but does not establish total torque or a unique cause. The data use a previously used seed and the rejected early-wing takeoff.
