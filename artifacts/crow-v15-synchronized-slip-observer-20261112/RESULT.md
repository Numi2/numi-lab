# Synchronized walking contact observation

The host-only observer exactly reproduced all eight same-seed state endpoints and ground-contact records through trace submission 371, including the separate pre-reset warmup. Lane 5 supplied 496 complete 1.25 ms q/v samples and 7,131 positive-impulse foot/toe contacts across trace submissions 335–365. This is a 0.62 s interval during one development walk, not an independent animal or full mission.

Independent v15 articulated kinematics applied to each q/v sample yielded impulse-weighted tangential contact-point speed median 0.00794 m/s, 90th percentile 0.0647 m/s and 99th percentile 0.242 m/s. About 14.1% of sampled normal impulse was associated with estimated speed above 0.05 m/s; the threshold is a descriptive engineering cut, not a biological criterion. Results by side and individual samples are retained. The earlier video-rate estimate is not comparable as a continuous estimate and missed much of this variation.

At the 1.25 ms rate, the mean difference between the reconstructed center-of-mass velocity and the centered finite difference of consecutive positions was 0.0022 m/s for the body and 0.011–0.014 m/s for the checked feet/toes. This improves the temporal consistency check but leaves material error relative to the lower contact speeds. The solver contact point and the post-substep q/v are not guaranteed to represent the same instant; substep convergence and a direct solver-velocity check remain necessary. Ground is static in this trial. These estimates do not prove frictional failure, biological realism, full-stance slip distance or robustness across starts and surfaces. Do not tune friction from this one run alone.
