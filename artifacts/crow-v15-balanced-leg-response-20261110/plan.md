# Reached-grip coordinated hip and ankle response

The matched isolated hip-offset experiment did not produce a clear control choice: positive offsets reduced the short-term pitch increase but worsened opposing toe contact, and negative offsets moved the root back while increasing the initial pitch increase. Test whether opposing ankle offsets preserve foot orientation and contact while changing the response. Use the identical accepted sample 3820, exact perch pose, fresh-cache single-perch scene, five 0.4 s arms, body, solver and position-drive setup. For each symmetric hip delta (-0.06, -0.03, 0, +0.03 or +0.06 rad), command both ankles by the equal and opposite delta; all other joint targets are held at the reached q. The zero arm is an exact independent control of the prior experiment.

Record all states, toe contact normals and upward support, nonfoot impulses and solver status. Compare first 80 samples to the hip-only experiment. Require zero-arm trace parity before attributing a change to ankle coordination. Select no candidate unless it improves the relevant pitch/contact tradeoff, shows no nonfoot contact, and is subsequently demonstrated in the full uninterrupted rollout. This isolated measurement is not a landing, release, biological or autonomy qualification.
