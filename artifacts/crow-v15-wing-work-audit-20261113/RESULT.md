# Post-hoc flap effort–motion audit

For the push-first fast-stroke experiment, integrate the retained working-effort buffer times recorded joint angular displacement across both flap DOFs and steps 348–375 (0.56 s). Positive sums are approximately 18.6 at bound 4, 61.5 at bound 8 and 93.6 at bound 16; signed net sums are approximately 9.4, 21.8 and 16.3 respectively. A trapezoidal velocity approximation gives positive sums approximately 18.3, 59.9 and 89.0. The discrepancy between integration estimates is material and is retained. Under SI interpretation of a revolute generalized effort and angle the products have work units, but the host buffer timing and the contributions of other drive or constraint paths have not been independently reconciled; these values are not established total muscle work.

Jackson and Dial (2011, DOI 10.1242/jeb.046789) report a representative first complete crow wingbeat of 159 ms and pectoralis mass-specific power 179 W/kg, and mean mass 0.0276 kg per pectoral from three birds. Multiplying these different reported quantities gives an illustrative one-pectoral work scale around 0.79 J for that representative stroke, not an exact same-bird comparison, total two-wing work, or a physiological upper limit. The model values and measured work loop differ in window, body, anatomy and measurement method; their apparent scale gap warrants actuator-energy accounting and calibration, not a numerical pass/fail threshold or a claim of exact physiological excess.
