# Restart reconstruction and first continuation

**Correction, 2026-11-14:** The retained runs below had automatic resets on task termination despite `--no-scheduled-resets`. The apparent 112-interval hold occurred only after a reset, so it is not a continuous landing from the checkpoint. The original phase-5 fixture first terminated for tilt at replay step 149. See `CONTINUITY_AUDIT.md` and the retained audit JSON; the earlier interpretation below is preserved for transparency and is superseded by this correction.

The first host-only attempt returned exit code 0 but was rejected: the GPU task reset replaced the saved q/v with a new start. A separate shader then restored q/v and scene poses after task reset, and an independent GPU readback immediately after that task kernel measured maximum q discrepancy 3.55e-8 and v discrepancy 2.37e-8 in the printed precision. All six post-step scene records exactly matched the saved replay. All five course collision masks were enabled. The one-step smoke finished with finite state near the endpoint; this validates reconstruction of positions, velocities and scene poses, not missing controller histories or a full resident checkpoint.

The first 250-step exploratory continuation initialized task/controller history from scratch. Its first observed contact was a right rear toe against the ground at observer step 16; by step 25 the root height was 0.185 m. The final root was at [17.771, 0.463, 0.169] m, about 12.86 m from the original branch center. It did not reach a waypoint or the perch. The simulator reported no termination, but this does not make the branch-landing attempt successful. Raw state, contact records and the original failed smoke are retained.

The original saved run included the line `CROW_PHASE_SAMPLE 2400 5 117501952 0 0 0 0 0 -0.149997637 0.159960449 0.163015828`. Its status word is available for a separately labeled partial-clock experiment, but the oscillator phase and other internal history were not recorded. That candidate cannot be called a bit-exact restart.

The partial-clock 250-step comparison also contacted the ground: the first recorded nonzero ground impulse occurred at observer step 17; final root height was 0.1690 m. It did not approach the original perch. Its ending waypoint count of one was explicitly initialized from the old replay and is not a newly completed waypoint. This rejects the hypothesis that restoring just these clock and status fields is sufficient to sustain the old flight.

On a separately constructed fixture with the static perch moved close to the checkpoint, the 250-step phase-1 trial made front-toe contact but no rear-toe contact, reached 1.068 radians of tilt and touched the ground with its tail. The perch observer is gated on landing phase 5. In a controlled trial forcing that phase, both rear toes contacted the perch; the longest bilateral front/rear contact span in last-substep snapshots was 34 intervals, with peak tilt 1.215 radians during capture. This is an altered fixture and forced phase, not a seamless autonomous continuation.

A 500-step repetition with the same forced landing phase was audited over all 16 substeps for intervals 348-500. Intervals 348-459 (112 consecutive intervals) satisfied the preregistered bilateral opposing toe impulses, upward support, no sampled ground or nonfoot support, tilt and speed criteria. At interval 460 tilt exceeded 0.35 radians; by interval 490 opposing grip was lost, and at interval 500 tilt was 0.673 radians. The trial therefore did not establish a stable landing. Full-substep records start at interval 335 (the ground-contact trace at 348); earlier capture has only the last-substep observer. These tests retain original q/v but not unrecorded solver and controller history.
