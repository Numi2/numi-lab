# Balance after the first continuous opposing grip

The x=17.745 m, zero-yaw fixture produced an uninterrupted landing with all 16 substeps measured and no termination through interval 400. The bilateral opposed-toe/support/tilt/speed gate passed for intervals 74-380, a 307-interval streak, but tilt grew from 0.153 rad at 200 to 0.437 rad at 400 and the trial did not end in the predeclared stable hold. This is a reconstructed checkpoint, moved box perch and forced phase, not an original-course autonomous landing.

Question: can contact-gated hip feedback arrest that late pitch drift without losing the initial grip? Compare positive and negative signed bounded hip target feedback on the same x=17.745 fixture. After both toe-contact ramps reach 12, add `clamp(sign * (0.40*(pitch - 0.10) + 0.08*pitchRate), -0.12, 0.12)` rad to each hip target; otherwise preserve the x=17.745 baseline, physics, policy, seed and full-window contact observer. This is an exploratory, single-state paired directional test; the prior 400-interval baseline is the control. Pitch and rate are accepted-state task feedback. No direct wrench, external force, reset suppression or body pose override is used.

Run 600 intervals per sign, 50 Hz, auditing only the first episode. Require no termination, no root jump, no ground or nonfoot impulse, and >=250 consecutive terminal intervals with all 16 substeps recorded, bilateral upward and opposing toe impulses, tilt <=0.35 rad, and speed <=0.12 m/s. Any loss of these conditions or early termination refutes the candidate. Compare time to first 0.35-rad crossing and grip continuity against the baseline over its observed 400 intervals; no extrapolation past 400 is implied. The user-requested original-course branch and truly autonomous landing remain separate from this fixture.
