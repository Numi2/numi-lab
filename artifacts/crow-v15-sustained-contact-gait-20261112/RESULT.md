# Sustained gait screen: prediction contradicted

Both arms survived all 500 steps without reported physical termination, nonfoot ground impulse, contact overflow or solver errors; all eight matched their counterpart for the first 200 accepted endpoint states and 3,200 contact substeps. The six-second window had complete 4,800 contact and toe-velocity observations per lane. Matched no-hip-feedback arms advanced 0.220–0.231 m and had 66–76 both-feet-unloaded substeps; hip-feedback arms advanced 0.242–0.258 m and had 65–111 unloaded substeps. The candidate improved that count in five of eight lanes and worsened it in three; these closely related lanes are not independent birds.

The toe velocity observer recorded two capped substeps in every control lane and six in every candidate lane, so no lane met the predeclared engineering screen. In every lane the first cap is at step 302 on velocity index 8; the candidate also caps index 14 at step 307. All candidate lanes have 65 unloaded substeps in the 6–7-second interval; the control has 42. Toe indices and contact forces are simulation observables, not proof of biological joint velocities or material slip. Body and wing speed caps were not recorded.

Source inspection after the run found a confound: the original crouch contribution terminates abruptly at 6 s and the contact-feedback publisher stops at the same time. Although wing transition was deliberately deferred, these two transitions were not. Thus the result is a valid failure of this specific test configuration, but does not isolate whether sustained gait itself or those transitions caused it. The next preregistered factorial separately extends the crouch and contact feedback, preserving this failure and comparing each lane against its own retained prior trajectory. The experiment does not qualify launch, navigation, or real-crow gait.
