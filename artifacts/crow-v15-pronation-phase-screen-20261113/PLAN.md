# Coupled pronation timing screen: preregistered

In the previous experiment the flap first-stroke oscillator used its own 6.4 Hz counter, whereas the existing flight law commanded pronation from a separate pre-existing phase clock, and the recorded target remained approximately -0.049 rad through much of the first stroke. A paper measured nontrivial lift and drag contributions across wingbeat phases in parrotlets, not crows (https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-13347-3); it motivates a sensitivity test, not a crow-specific target or physical calibration.

Test phase-coordinated pronation joint targets about -0.049 rad with 0.06 rad amplitude and smooth entry/exit; offsets 0, +pi/2, -pi/2 use two lanes each, all with wing working-effort bound 2 and first flap counter at 7.04 s. Retain the existing joint drives, body, passive aero and contacts and disable the direct pitch/yaw body moments. This does not insert forces and does not claim realistic feather deformation or calibrated wing motion. Compare matched leg-extension and crouch controls in one used development seed 20261126, eight correlated lanes, 400 steps and 16 substeps. Require exact logged state/action/contact prefix parity between matched arms through tick 345; require complete first episodes and all samples with no overflow or trace error. Check recorded bounds, impulse differences in ticks 348–355 and 348–357, support through release, recontact, rise, toe threshold, and aerodynamic force/work proxies. A development screen requires both early impulse differences above 0.05 N s, continuous support from tick 340 until release by 375, no recontact through 400, root rise at least 0.05 m, no nonfoot impulse, and at most one toe speed threshold substep in both lanes of a phase setting. Never score after first-episode termination. Preserve every run and failed screen. This cannot qualify flight, landing or biological realism.
