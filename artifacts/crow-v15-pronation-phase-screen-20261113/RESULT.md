# Coupled pronation timing: failed paired launch screen

All eight first episodes ran 400 ticks in both arms, and recorded state, actions and contacts matched between arms through tick 345, including contact substeps in ticks 335–345. The applied wing-drive effort bound 2 was observed in all six comparable lanes. The three phase settings were represented by two correlated lanes each. Lanes 0–1 were the original high-drive controls, not phase test lanes.

The altered left pronation targets ranged approximately -0.111 to +0.011 rad in ticks 354–370, with recorded left joint angles approximately -0.108 to +0.006 rad. Thus the joint did respond to the commanded oscillation, though this does not establish biologically realistic feather motion or an aerodynamic benefit. Push-minus-hold upward foot impulses were 0.166–0.187 N s over ticks 348–355 and 0.116–0.123 N s over 348–357. Every test lane had an unsupported interval at tick 356 before its three-interval release at tick 358 or 359, followed by foot recontacts from tick 362; each had 8–16 toe speed threshold substeps. Maximum root rise was 0.0335–0.0351 m, less than the preregistered 0.05 m. No phase setting passed either lane, and the peak modeled upward aerodynamic force was about 4.22–4.25 N. The positive wing working-effort-displacement proxy was about 5.6–5.7 over ticks 348–375; it is not isolated muscle work.

No takeoff, flight, landing or navigation is qualified. This used-seed, correlated-lane sensitivity study does not establish a unique cause, validate aerodynamics against a crow, or exhaust the range of possible wing kinematics. The parrotlet flight study cited in the preregistration is motivation rather than direct crow calibration. Preserve source, inputs, matched controls, full traces, replays, binaries, and failures.
