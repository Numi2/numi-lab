# Avian landing-contact evidence and limits

[Roderick et al. 2019](https://elifesciences.org/articles/46415) recorded landings of three Pacific parrotlets on surfaces of varied size and texture, and characterized toe-pad and claw interactions. The birds prepared their feet before touchdown and adapted foot and claw contact after touchdown; the paper distinguishes more predictable toe-pad friction from surface-dependent, less predictable claw engagement. It reports that grip geometry and approach velocity influence stability, and that larger perches allow less circumferential wrapping. The supplement reports substantial timing variability; these are parrotlet observations, not numerical American-crow parameters.

The study's [open dataset](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi%3A10.5061/dryad.89rr53h) is approximately 211 MB in its 2020 revision; it has not been downloaded or fitted. The current v15 digit segments and Coulomb material do not model independent claw-tip asperity hooking, and their high-friction development setting is not a measured crow-surface coefficient. A broad flat top cannot be treated as automatically equivalent to a narrow branch with front/rear opposing normal forces. A future wide-surface gate needs independently measured toe pressure, tangential force, slip, release and support, alongside a separate branch-wrap test, without weakening the requested landing criterion.
