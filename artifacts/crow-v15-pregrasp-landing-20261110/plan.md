# Perch-approach rear-toe pre-shaping

The measured substep impact can happen before the contact-driven toe closure is applied on the next control step. The all-substep sensor alone did not create sustained support. Test a bounded anticipatory rear-toe posture during the last part of the privileged guide's descent, while front-toe closure remains contact-driven. Mark the approach-ready state only when the guide has reached its final perch-relative height target and the body is within 0.10 m of that target; clear it if that condition is lost. In this state, set the rear toe to a 0.45 rad partial curl, retaining the larger of this target and the existing contact-driven closure; all other flight, gait, takeoff, front-toe, collision and 25-step landing-support rules remain unchanged. Before waypoint five toes remain neutral in flight. This is a simulated approach cue from the privileged guide, not autonomous sensing and not a biological calibration. On the same 16 used development starts, predict at least 12 survival, at least four runs with 10 consecutive bilateral averaged-load steps, and at least one with three consecutive bilateral opposing toe-contact endpoints. Retain any failures and do not treat pre-shaping as demonstrated release.
