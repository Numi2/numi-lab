# Height-triggered leg retraction after a loaded push

The matched 16-substep push produced 0.432 m/s upward root speed and 19.2 mm rise in all eight development starts, but toe contacts remained loaded until the body came down. Test whether existing bounded leg position drives can retract the feet after the observed contact-powered rise, without modifying body state, adding force, changing the frozen flight controller, or relaxing contact thresholds. Starting from the exact push shader, after five seconds and only while the measured accepted root height exceeds 0.166, 0.170 or 0.174 m, command either the existing crouch (-0.85, 1.00, -0.50 normalized hip/knee/ankle) or a moderate retraction (-0.45, 0.55, -0.30). Six variants share the original push and eight perturbed development starts. The controls and original push are the retained matched reference. Active wing flapping remains suppressed in the evaluation window; passive aerodynamics remain.

Require all candidates to reproduce the matched push q/v exactly through step 250, zero native failed steps or physical terminations, no non-foot ground contacts in the leg-only interval, and, in all eight lanes, at least two consecutive 20 ms intervals with total foot normal impulse <=1e-7 N s across all 16 substeps and root vertical speed >0.05 m/s. Require at least 20 mm maximum root rise relative to step 247. Report the matched held-crouch control and unchanged push outcomes, every lane, full normal and upward impulses and first divergence. A survivor requires native replay toe and sole geometry checks, holdout perturbations, 32/64-step refinement, safe transition into the frozen flight controller, autonomous course and landing. This is a development experiment, not an unused-layout or real-bird result.
