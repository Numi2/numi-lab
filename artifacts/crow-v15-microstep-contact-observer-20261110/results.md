# Microstep toe-contact observation audit

The 16-substep single-submission control exactly matched the first 40 saved states of the force-normalized reference. Splitting each control interval into 16 resident submissions changed 38/40 logged states, beginning at step 2. In that different split-execution trajectory, three toe/interval pairs had a total normal impulse exceeding the chosen mean-force threshold while the final microstep alone was below its equivalent threshold. These were generic toe/perch contacts, not demonstrated opposing grip. This observation shows sampling can miss brief impacts in the split execution, but the trajectory mismatch prevents attributing those contacts to the original 16-substep run or claiming split execution is equivalent. A same-run internal substep observer, preserving physics and contact-cache semantics, is needed to evaluate that hypothesis. Neither arm establishes complete grip, landing, flight, navigation or biology.
