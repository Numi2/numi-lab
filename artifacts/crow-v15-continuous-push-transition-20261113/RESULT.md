# Result: continuous push targets improved contact continuity but failed the impulse screen

Both first episodes reached 400 steps; the full 335–400 contact, effort, toe and target records were present with no reported substep errors or overflow. The paired arms and previous push candidate matched in recorded q/v/action/contact through step 345.

On the used development seed, five of six comparable push lanes maintained foot support on every control interval from step 348 until their first three unloaded intervals; lane 7 had one fully unloaded interval at step 356 before that final release. All six subsequently remained unloaded through step 400 and rose by at least 0.565 m relative to step 350. No nonfoot impulse or >=11.99 rad/s toe-speed substep was recorded in the 348–400 window.

The matched push-minus-crouch early upward impulse was -0.02085, -0.00997, +0.15031, +0.20347, +0.07863 and +0.04895 N s for lanes 2–7. Lanes 2, 3 and 7 missed the preregistered +0.05 N s requirement; lane 7 also missed uninterrupted support. The screen therefore fails, and the control change is not promoted. The earlier candidate showed a support interruption in all six comparable lanes on this seed and toe-speed events in two, but this closed-loop comparison alone does not establish which mechanism caused each difference.

Wing flapping, passive aero and prior body-moment action remain in the physics; these paired foot impulses are not isolated muscle work. One used seed and correlated lanes cannot qualify realistic leg power, flight, navigation or supported landing. Preserve all trace data, source, input hashes and unsuccessful results.
