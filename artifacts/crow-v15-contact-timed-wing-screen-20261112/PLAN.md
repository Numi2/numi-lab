# Contact-timed wing initiation screen

American crow escape takeoffs in Jackson and Dial (2011), DOI 10.1242/jeb.046789, began the first downstroke before toe-off. The current candidate suppresses flapping until a contact-based release latch, so it cannot reproduce that ordering. A previous 6.90 s onset unloaded the feet before the leg push developed and was rejected. Test 7.00 and 7.02 s onset against the current 0.45-rad gait and 0.185 m release, with the same used development seed and first-episode starts; retain a re-observed unmodified control. Preserve matched pre-intervention substeps and compare loaded duration, cumulative impulse versus body weight, toe-speed caps, wing center motion relative to the torso and first unloaded time. The times are engineering hypotheses, not biological estimates. A positive short screen does not qualify a full takeoff, flight, landing or model.

The observer adds synchronized substep coordinates to the current stance-contact host; verify its baseline against the already retained current candidate before interpreting results. Abort any comparison with policy fingerprint mismatch or observer drift.

After the first two timings produced pre-release upward wing-center motion, add 180-degree phase-offset variants at 7.00 and 7.02 s. These change the subsequent oscillation as well as the first stroke and are exploratory, not a calibrated controller. Preserve and report both rejected initial phases.

After inverted 7.00 s produced an early downstroke but reduced push impulse, screen 8 onset strengths from zero to full across lanes for the first 40 ms, then return to the full inverted oscillator. This is a small used-seed engineering sensitivity study; preserve the abrupt transition caveat and do not promote on this screen alone.
