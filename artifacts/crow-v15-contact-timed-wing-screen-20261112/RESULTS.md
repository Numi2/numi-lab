# Contact-timed wing initiation: no promotion

All six eight-lane screens completed their 370-interval development horizon on used seed 20261127, with synchronized 16-substep foot contact and wing-coordinate observation. The new observer baseline exactly matched 2,960 q/v/action/contact endpoints and 47,360 contact substeps from the previously retained candidate. All 3,968 coordinate snapshots per arm were present, final substep q matched each endpoint, and sampled replay/forward-kinematics positions matched within 3.1e-8 m. These checks establish observer consistency, not biological accuracy.

The unmodified candidate first becomes continuously unloaded at interval 352 substep 8, after 0.725–0.745 N s ground upward impulse from interval 348, compared with 0.397 N s model weight impulse over the same window. Its wings were gated while loaded. Starting the original-phase wingbeat at 7.00 s produces upward, not downward, wing-center movement before unloading. Starting it at 7.02 s generates a recontact after the original toe-off; its last-loaded sample must not be interpreted as the first toe-off.

Inverting the wingbeat phase at 7.00 s moves the two modeled wing centers down about 8.7 mm before first unloading, consistent in order with the American-crow escape takeoff observation, but unloads five 1.25 ms substeps earlier than baseline. Ground upward impulse falls to about 0.470 N s from 0.725–0.745 N s; weight impulse over its shorter window is 0.370 N s. This intervention therefore substantially reduces the developed push and is rejected as a finished leg-powered takeoff. Inverting at 7.02 s starts only after baseline first unloading.

A subsequent eight-lane onset-strength screen (0 through 1 in increments of 1/7 for the first 40 ms, followed by full amplitude) found no acceptable combined result. The 1/7 lane remains continuously loaded until interval 354 substep 8 and shows downward wing-center movement, but ground impulse is about 0.602 N s versus 0.725 N s in its matched baseline and weight impulse over the longer window is about 0.580 N s. Several other strengths unload and recontact before the last loaded sample; contact-transitions.json records these transitions and prevents treating last contact as first toe-off. Higher strengths again truncate the push. No toe-speed caps were recorded in intervals 340–370. No longer flight, independently perturbed starts, touchdown or toe grip was tested here.

Ground impulse includes passive and aerodynamic forces and is not isolated muscle impulse. Wing-center displacement is not feather geometry, stroke force, or within-substep ordering. The phase switch and strength step are abrupt engineering probes, not a calibrated motor program. Early direct moment assistance and unvalidated aerodynamic closures remain; no controller is frozen. Source: Jackson and Dial (2011), DOI 10.1242/jeb.046789, three American crows, escape takeoff context.
