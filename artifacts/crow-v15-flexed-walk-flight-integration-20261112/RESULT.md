# Flexed walk into short ablated flight: partial

Uniform 0.55-rad experimental crouch was tested in eight continuous, teacher-off development lanes on the previously used seed 20261127. The candidate and the matched 0.40 reference, both using the new contact-gated release and suppressing the two identified direct body moments after 7.10 s, matched all 800 endpoints and 12,800 contact substeps through interval 100; their first logged differences occurred at interval 103. Both arms completed 12 s without reported physical termination, trace errors or contact overflow, and all lanes reached two waypoint criteria. These are criteria, not completion of the route or landing.

In the 4–6 s window neither arm recorded both feet unloaded, nonfoot ground impulse or toe-speed-cap substeps. All flexed lanes retained measurable combined upward foot impulse of 0.727–0.746 N s in intervals 348–353; this includes all contact and aerodynamic effects, not isolated muscle work. After 8 s no foot contact was recorded, and after 7.4 s no toe-speed caps were recorded. The candidate's root heights sampled between 8 and 12 s ranged 0.749–1.261 m. Its selected model knee minima during recovery were about 105 degrees versus about 114 degrees in the reference, with many biological and segmentation limitations.

The flexed arm failed the per-foot 1.5x sampled contact-point-speed comparison in two of eight lanes: their right-foot p95 ratios were 1.815 and 1.835 even though the overall pooled p95 decreased from 0.0430 to 0.0412 m/s. Point speed is not integrated slip. The 0.55 parameter therefore remains an unqualified candidate; the positive 12 s screen does not fix the contact regression or establish a biologically correct crouch. Other modeled aerodynamic closures and the initial assisted launch remain, and no unused layouts, full navigation or supported landing were tested.
