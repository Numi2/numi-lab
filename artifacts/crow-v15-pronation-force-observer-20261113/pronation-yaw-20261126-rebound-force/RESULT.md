# Matched pronation-drive and aerodynamic observation

The passive observer run exactly reproduced every candidate lane through tick 1800 and both control lanes through their complete first episodes. Metallib, policies and scene masks matched the prior pronation trial; current-model metadata matched the expected symmetric pronation joints. All requested 501 x 16 substeps in lanes 4–7 had the required samples, finite data and completed GPU status, with no observer errors or duplicate records. Kernel-input q/v exactly matched substep-start observations; the sum of recorded root, wing and tail force increments agreed with the total within 6e-11 N.

The physical pronation-drive output reached its modeled +/-0.15 effort clamp on 7402–7494 of 8016 observed left-wing substeps (92.3–93.5%) and 7874–7914 right-wing substeps (98.2–98.7%). No recorded effort exceeded that bound. The limited steering authority and measured position lag therefore coincide with sustained actuator saturation; this observation alone does not prove a causal fix or a biologically correct motor capacity. The two wings had notably different modeled force and torque traces. For the commanded lanes, the mean world-z torque at the left wing COM was -0.0265/-0.0212 and at the right wing COM +0.0121/+0.00304 in the modeled torque units. These are moments about individual wing COMs; without force lever arms they are not the whole-bird yaw moment and cannot alone diagnose the source of asymmetry.

The source and complete measured output are retained. This is a used-seed modeled-load diagnosis, not muscle-force measurement, aerodynamics validation, flight qualification, frozen flight, autonomous navigation or landing. Preserve it when testing a lower-amplitude pronation carrier or other physically motivated change; do not simply increase an estimated effort limit as if it were animal data.
