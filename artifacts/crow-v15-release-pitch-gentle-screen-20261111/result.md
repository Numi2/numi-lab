# Gentler release pitch feedback did not retain balance

All eight constructed-perch lanes completed 350 intervals without observer errors, missing or malformed substeps, or native solver rejection. Four zero-feedback control lanes reproduced the previous two-start release trajectories: at least ten qualified unilateral-clearance/support intervals, a regrip command, and a subsequent fall at interval 275 or 280. With -0.25 feedback, one lane achieved ten qualified release intervals and regrip, but both fell at interval 287; the other had only eight release intervals. With +0.25, neither lane achieved a qualified release and both fell at 224 or 225. None maintained the required terminal bilateral grip. There were no reported nonfoot contact impulse intervals before termination, but the root-height fall is decisive. This is an interrupted or failed release outcome, not successful regripping. Toe speed limits were not recorded in this screen.

The 32-substep analysis reconstructs clearance and contact, but its half-weight support threshold does not alone establish full weight bearing. The constructed stationary initial condition is not a complete landing or autonomous course. These results rule out this small pitch-feedback adjustment as a complete solution on the tested starts; the fallback control also changes fore-aft velocity damping and leg targets during release.
