# Support-aware stance command experiment

The earlier amplitude/cadence variants all missed at least one preregistered gait criterion. Inspection shows that the current controller gates the entire leg carrier on contralateral support; when the opposite foot lifts, this abruptly resets the loaded stance leg target to zero. Large observed stance contact-point speed spikes occur during those single-support intervals. Test a causal correction: continue the stance phase even when the contralateral foot is unloaded, while requiring contralateral load before starting a swing. Keep the original half-second phase, swing amplitude, physical body, launch, native observer and seed fixed. Compare three stance-hip gains (100%, 50%, 25% of baseline), recording exact patches. Use the preceding explicit gate: 8/8 full trials, >=0.02 m forward travel in the two-second window, no nonfoot contact or unsupported endpoint, each side >=3 consecutive unloaded swing endpoints with >=2 mm clearance and per-side stance slip 95th percentile <=0.05 m/s. A successful exploratory outcome must be tested on new starts and a sustained walk; no claims of biological validity or full-sequence landing follow. A failed test refutes sufficiency of this stance-gating correction, not necessarily the hypothesis of stance snapping.
