# Contact-driven stance foretoe anti-windup screen

In the archived stationary-release control, the stance foretoe target remains at 1.2 rad through regrip. Its actual six joints approach the 1.2 rad joint limit by intervals 305–310, while the stance foretoe opposed contact disappears; increasing friction and broadening pitch-support feedback did not rescue regrip. Test whether continually commanding closure contributes to this failure. In lane 1 only, after the other foot has completed its release-and-return state and while the stance foot still reports contact, bound each stance foretoe segment's existing position target to at most its last accepted joint angle plus 0.05 rad. This is a position-drive command bound, not a change to the joint range, effort limit, model, contact friction or solver. Once support disappears, retain the existing original target. Lane 0 remains unchanged. The 0.05 rad lead is an exploratory control parameter, not a biological measurement.

Before seeing results, require exact recorded lane state, command and contact parity before intervention; identify the first clipped target and actual joint limit and contact behavior. Apply the same complete 450-interval stationary release, unilateral-support and terminal 100-interval bilateral opposed-toe support requirements, no root fall, no nonfoot impulse and no nominal 12 rad/s toe-speed hit. Retain both lanes, every failure, source/binary and logs. A successful constructed perch screen would not qualify walking, an aerial landing, flight or the continuous mission.
