# Neural branch-balance research task

Objective: train a neural policy on the actual v15 contact dynamics to keep both feet supported and oppose slip on the round branch. The initial learning environment is a development curriculum reconstructed from the 48-second recorded state; its branch is deliberately relocated and landing phase initialized. It is not the original autonomous mission.

The baseline uses the in-range front-toe target and the existing frozen flight and terminal carrier. During terminal contact, four sampled neural outputs command bounded residual position targets to left/right hip and ankle drives. No direct body force, animation, reset suppression, privileged future state, or policy action outside the existing joint-target bounds is permitted. The policy must receive live proprioception and contact information; inspect the actual actor observation contract before calling the result autonomous.

Use PPO on randomized branch offsets and initial position/velocity, then evaluate deterministic actions on reserved random seeds and offsets never used for training. The reward should encourage bilateral physical support, upright posture, low speed and survival while penalizing failure; contact impulses, opposing-toe forces, ground and nonfoot contacts are evaluated independently from the learner's reward using all 16 substeps. A success requires a continuous first episode without reset, termination or nonfoot/ground support, and >=250 final consecutive intervals with bilateral vertical support, opposing toe forces, tilt <=0.35 rad and speed <=0.12 m/s. Save training checkpoints, exact source/host/policy/config hashes, seeds, failure episodes and held-out replays in Git. Start with a small smoke test that demonstrates actor actions actually alter the articulated trajectory and valid PPO updates; do not treat a training launch or the development fixture as completion.
