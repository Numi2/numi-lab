# Contact-free toe impulse response

Development landing trials show front-toe angles swinging to -0.27 rad with approximately -3.66 rad/s joint speed after 25 recorded candidate-free steps, despite a nominal neutral toe target. Tripling joint damping in full-route trials did not meet the reopening threshold, but changed the entire trajectory. Isolate the native body in free fall at 100 m, apply no flapping or scene contact, and initialize the eight toes at +/-0.27 rad with +/-3.6 rad/s in four sign combinations. Keep all non-toe joints at their initial target and command all toes to zero. Compare joint damping 0.003, 0.009, 0.015 and 0.030 with stiffness, mass, inertia, effort bounds, timestep and solver unchanged, four substeps per 0.02 s. Record every accepted toe angle and velocity and every contact count for 100 control steps. A useful candidate requires zero contacts, all cases below 0.05 rad absolute angle continuously after step 25, no solver failures, and lower worst-case angle than baseline. No biological claim or automatic promotion follows: re-test the coupled route and impact behavior.
