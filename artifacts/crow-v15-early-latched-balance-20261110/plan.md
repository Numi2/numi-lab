# Early latched ankle control

In the preceding post-release-only latched experiment, the native failure flag stayed false although contact records showed the crow fall from the perch and rest on its beak. Its trunk had already reached about 9.5 degrees of pitch before the intervention began. Prediction: beginning bounded ankle feedback as soon as the existing sensor-qualified landing phase latches, instead of waiting until release, reduces the growth in trunk pitch and prevents ground contact through step 2750. Compare gain factors one and four to the 45-second release-only baseline and prior post-release-only gains on the same exposed full-start layout J. The flight controller, body, target and no-injected-force solver stay fixed. Early feedback may interfere with toe closure, so a pass still requires a sustained pre-release opposing grip on both feet and subsequent observed loss on release, plus five waypoints and 25 final low-speed foot-only perch-supported endpoints. Record any off-perch ground contact as a physical failure even if the native flag remains clear. Treat both arms as exploratory, not held-out evidence.
