# Contact order against a related corvid

Verstappen, Aerts and Van Damme filmed black-billed magpies walking, running and hopping on a thin cork-covered track. They report the hallux touching down before the front toes and leaving before the third digit during toe-off; the toes flex after leaving the ground and extend later in recovery. The recorded gait is a related-species reference, not an American-crow measurement; body size, speed, surface and anatomy differ. Source: https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.203.14.2159 . The paper used side-view markers and acknowledges larger hip and knee localization errors. Do not transfer its angles to the simulated crow without an anatomical mapping.

At 1.25 ms contact substeps in the used-seed 4–6 s development window, complete stance runs with both rear and any front toe groups were identified after merging up to two silent substeps. The held reference had rear-first touchdown in 40/40 and rear-first toe-off in 14/40 such runs. The reduced-lift and swing-toe-release trials each had rear-first touchdown in 40/40 but front-first toe-off in 40/40. Restricting the front group to model front_toe_2, presumed but not validated as digit III, produced 40/40 front-first toe-offs in each candidate; 21/40 held runs lacked one of the two groups. Partial edge runs were excluded.

This is a contact-order comparison, not a full gait validation. The precise anatomical mapping of model toe segments, the impulse threshold and run-bridging rule matter, and same-substep events cannot be ordered. The candidate release command starts only after its entire foot is already marked unloaded, so it cannot directly initiate hallux unloading while the front toes are still in contact. That control-flow observation motivates a bounded loaded-release experiment, not a claim of cause or a guaranteed improvement.
