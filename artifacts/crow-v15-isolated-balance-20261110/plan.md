# Isolated post-release balance and complete contact observation

The revised-body isolated probe produced bilateral opposing toe grip in 8/8 closure starts per shape and none in the open-toe controls, but toe-only terminal support met the 25-step threshold in 5/8 square-shape starts. Sole and nonfoot contacts were not recorded, so actual support and forbidden-contact status remained unknown.

Use the same 6x6 cm square isolated perch and eight matched initial states. Four arms per start: (0) same closing/releasing controller, (1) plus signed positive bounded hip/ankle pitch feedback, (2) the opposite sign, and (3) toes held open without feedback. The feedback uses last accepted pitch and pitch rate, applies only after established grip while a toe has upward contact, clips the joint target offset to 0.2 rad, and applies equal and opposite hip/ankle corrections; no force or pose injection. Log all nonzero robot-perch contact impulses, not just toe contacts. The baseline arm must exactly reproduce the previously recorded square closure-arm trace before observer data are used. No retries; retain all failures.

Exploratory prediction: at least one feedback sign has 6/8 starts with three consecutive bilateral opposing toe-contact samples, loss of that grip after release, and 25 consecutive final samples with each foot/toe supporting positive upward impulse, total upward impulse >= half simulated weight times 0.005 s, no nonfoot impulse, tilt <=0.35 rad and root speed <=0.12 m/s. The original toe-open controls should not establish opposed grip. This is an already-supported isolated test and cannot qualify autonomous walking, approach, takeoff or landing.
