# Centered, slow-foot approach experiment

The touchdown-hold development run reached five waypoints without a nonfoot collision, but only brief loads and no opposed grip. Its first contact at step 3658 was on front toes while the feet were about 0.11 m behind their commanded x target; later attempts alternated between front-only and rear-only contacts. The toe reflex did move following contact, so the immediate problem is not simply failure to actuate the toes. Test whether commanding zero forward speed at zero foot-position error and waiting for tighter position and velocity alignment before descending can place both front and rear toes around the perch at low speed. Keep the v15 body, frozen flight law, touchdown response, toe reflex and SAT hybrid 16-event physics. On the already used development J seed, require 25 consecutive bilateral loaded samples, bilateral opposed toe contact, no nonfoot/perch collision, and a stable supported pose without reset. Run up to 7000 steps; failure or inability to enter the gate is informative. This guide-on experiment is exploratory, not an autonomous or held-out evaluation.
