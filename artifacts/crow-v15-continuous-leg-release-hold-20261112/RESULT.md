# Continuous leg-release handoff: passed short development screen

Across two used seeds and 16 closely related starts, the extension and powered-crouch controls matched accepted states and ground-contact records through step 550 and first differed at step 551. Every one of the 16 paired comparisons met the preregistered engineering criteria: extension increased vertical total foot-contact impulse over steps 549–556 by .0831–.0845 N s and increased root vertical speed at step 556 by .1439–.1473 m/s. These are coupled simulator results with active wings and feedback, not direct muscle-work or real-bird measurements.

All 32 first episodes reached step 625. Foot/toe coverage was complete over steps 201–625 and all 58,240 driven-joint observations per lane were present over steps 535–625. Neither arm had a recorded joint or toe speed-limit hit, nonfoot ground impulse, contact overflow or solver error in those windows. Feet had some recorded impulse in every transition substep 501–515 and were unloaded in every final substep 621–625. The small impulse threshold does not itself establish meaningful support or low slip. A subsequent observational scan found no logged self or scene impact in steps 501–625.

All four blend durations passed the short screen; .40 s is selected for further diagnostics as a representative setting, not an optimum. On the two same-lane .04 s wing-delay starts, this trial reproduced the earlier release state through step 556 and first differed at step 557, where the old target reversal caused knee-limit hits; the complete ground-contact history matched through 625. Continuing the extension after release is an engineering control choice, not validated crow kinematics.

One initial attempt to launch the second seed failed before creating a simulation because an analysis helper named select.py shadowed Python's standard library. It was renamed selection.py and the run was repeated successfully; no physical trial was discarded. Starts are correlated, speeds are slow, aerodynamics are uncalibrated, and longer autonomous flight, actual gait slip, grip/release, supported landing and genuinely unused layouts remain open. The result is not a flight-controller freeze or mission completion.
