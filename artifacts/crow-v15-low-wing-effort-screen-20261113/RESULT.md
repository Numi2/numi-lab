# Lower wing-effort matched launch screen

All first episodes ran for 400 ticks without reported termination. Push and hold state, policy action and contacts matched through tick 345 in all eight lanes, including substep foot-contact traces from ticks 335–345. Applied bounds were observed for every tested wing. The six relevant paired lanes used bounds 0.5, 1 and 2; lanes 0–1 retained the old high-drive control and are not evidence for those bounds.

Early push-minus-hold vertical foot impulses were 0.114–0.173 N s for ticks 348–355 and 0.0501–0.0859 N s for ticks 348–357. This is a matched model contrast, not a measurement of crow muscle force. Peak model upward aerodynamic force in the push arms was about 0.98, 2.00 and 4.44–4.55 N for those bounds, and peak flap speeds 6.02, 8.53 and 12.96–13.04 rad/s. Maximum rise was only 0.0238–0.0315 m. No bound passed the earlier narrow takeoff screen: every lane had at least three toe-speed threshold substeps, and the apparent releases either included support gaps or recontacts, or failed to occur. At bound 0.5 one lane did not release; at bound 2 the same was true for one lane. A no-release lane's empty pre-release-gap list is undefined, not proof of continuous support.

Working-effort times accepted displacement in ticks 348–375, both flap DOFs, yielded positive-work proxies 1.18–1.37, 2.18 and 5.05–5.07 in increasing bound order; buffer timing and coupled effects prevent equating these with biological muscle work. Their magnitude falls sharply compared with higher effort, but does not establish physiological validity. No takeoff, flight, landing, held-out layout or full mission is qualified.
