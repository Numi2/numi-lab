# Differential wingbeat steering screen: not qualified

All six obstacle-free development lanes completed 800 intervals without reported physical failure, missing contact substeps or contact overflow. Before 9 s, 48 sparse samples of q, v and policy actions exactly matched the prior no-body-moment baseline. Two known direct body moment actuators were disabled only after 9 s; the earlier launch and flight still used the old controller. Toe speed caps occurred before step 400 in two lanes. No ground contact or toe speed cap was recorded from step 400 onward.

Over 9–16 s, final sampled heading errors for the requested alternating +/-90-degree headings were 1.463, 1.515, 1.464, 1.505, 1.698 and 1.682 rad at increasing differential amplitudes 0.12, 0.24 and 0.36. The 0.12 lanes retained the same heading demands as their paired baseline; final yaw was +0.1075/-0.0560 rad versus +0.1050/-0.0447 rad. The 0.36 lanes had final yaw of -0.1270/+0.1110 rad, opposite their respective targets, and sampled altitude reached 1.535/1.369 m; four widely spaced endpoints do not establish continuous extrema. Flap joint sample deltas were nonzero, but sparse samples cannot measure stroke amplitude, applied actuator torque or aerodynamic force.

This experiment does not provide useful turn qualification or enough evidence to choose the steering sign. Its short horizon, exposed seed, altered demands in lanes 2–5, prior direct moments, and the unvalidated aerodynamic closure preclude statements about natural crow flight or the full sequence. The next step is instrumenting actual per-wing loads, flap motions and control saturation through complete wingbeats before retuning. No controller is promoted or frozen.
