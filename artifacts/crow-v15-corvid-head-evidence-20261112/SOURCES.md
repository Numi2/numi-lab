# Head-motion evidence and scope

Itahara and Kano's [large-billed crow motion-capture study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11007591/) observes 3D head rotations while tracking objects, including smooth pursuit and head-roll saccades, in *Corvus macrorhynchos*. The authors link a sample dataset and MATLAB code at https://osf.io/3dmg7/. The OSF API returned HTTP 401 without credentials when checked on 2026-11-12; no dataset was obtained or fitted. The experiment concerns visual attention, not a walking-gait trajectory, and the species is not the American crow.

Their [motion-capture methods paper](https://doi.org/10.2502/janip.72.1.1) reports reconstruction of eyes and beak relative to head markers. Other [avian work](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17987297/) describes visually related head thrust and hold in some species; this is not a universal gait cycle or an American crow numerical template. The current v15 model lacks a direct neck-roll degree of freedom (see crow-v15-head-stabilization-audit-20261112), so reproducing physical roll stabilization requires a topology change and requalification. No data here justify a scripted head bob or quantitative gait target.
