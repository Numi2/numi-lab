# Contact-observed staged release and lift

The last paired experiment moved the knee abruptly while the releasing toes were still curled and contacting the perch; neither lane cleared all independent release criteria and both fell. Test a state-driven sequence on the same 35-body starting conditions and weight-transfer gains, leaving the existing bilateral readiness and strict release/regrip gates unchanged. On entering release, keep the leg target at its stance value while driving the existing toe joints toward open. Begin a gradual knee/ankle target change only after five consecutive accepted intervals with every measured left toe angle within 0.16 rad of open, every left toe velocity below 2 rad/s, right foot upward and opposing-toe contact present, right-foot-relative COM within 0.015 m fore-aft and 0.030 m laterally and both relative speeds below 0.05 m/s. During lift, pause increases whenever these support conditions are absent. Increment flexion by at most 0.01 rad per control interval to the existing 0.15 rad knee offset, with an ankle offset of -0.5 times the flexion; after ten qualifying clearance intervals, lower at the same bounded rate for contact-driven regrip. All targets remain within the separately measured joint limits. Record stage, targets, actual q/v, all contact observer substeps, failures, and exact pre-release q/v parity with the immediately preceding reversed-ankle trial.

Success remains ten consecutive toe-and-foot-clear intervals with independently measured other-foot support and opposing grip, followed by 100 terminal consecutive bilateral supported opposing-grip intervals over a complete 350-step run, with no fall, nonfoot impulse, reset or solver rollback. A never-opened toe, failure to lift, incomplete trace or lost grip is failure; stop interpreting at first fall. This is a constructed-start model test, not biological validation or a flight landing, and does not use reserved layouts or seeds.
