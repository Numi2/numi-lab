# Same-binary gait smoothing: preliminary matched pass

In eight lanes on one used development seed, the research-only 16-substep target interpolation passed its prespecified two-second walking screen against the same compiled host and Metal shader, with identical 5 ms gait phase lead in both arms. The only intended runtime difference was whether previous targets were retained during walking. The recorded q/v/action/contact states and all contact substeps matched through the pre-walk interval, while 792 of 800 walking endpoint state samples subsequently differed. Each lane produced at least three opposite-foot-supported endpoint-clearance bouts per foot, no nonfoot impulse or toe-speed cap, and 94.4–122.1% of its matched control forward travel. The candidate had zero both-feet-unloaded substeps versus one in the control.

The candidate knee/ankle interval-boundary q second-difference means were 4.9–13.2% of the matched controls, and whole-substep p95 values were 8.1–22.8%. These are simulated motion proxies, not measured actuator torque or crow gait targets. The source switch changes an otherwise-unused task-header field, changing the task fingerprint; the first candidate attempt was correctly rejected by policy contract validation and is retained. Copied policies were rebound to the new task signature with exactly identical weights and observation/action transforms; matched pre-walk dynamics were then verified. The first posthoc script saved its identical result under the final joint name due to variable shadowing; the corrected output and both logs are preserved.

This is not sustained walking, a general reset/rollback-safe implementation, biological validation, a landing, or full-sequence success. The target history prototype assumes chunk-one execution. A second development seed and longer continuous walking, contact-point motion, speed robustness and transitions remain required before considering integration.
