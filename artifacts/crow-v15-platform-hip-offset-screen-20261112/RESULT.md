# Result: terminal hip geometry offsets

The same teacher-off, continuous development-layout run used an older unqualified flight controller. Two unchanged control lanes matched earlier first-episode state and contact traces through their original endpoints. Six signed hip target offsets were tested once each; offsets act through bounded physical hip position drives only after terminal support, not a body wrench. Five of six offset lanes either remained airborne at the horizon or physically failed. The +0.12 lane 7 reached five waypoint flags, survived all 4800 intervals, and had 1001 consecutive intervals from steps 3800–4800 in which upward foot impulse exceeded half body weight and no platform nonfoot normal impulse was observed. It had four 11-interval open/unilateral-support spans followed by renewed closed bilateral foot contact. Its last 25 intervals all had bilateral closed contact, maximum endpoint tilt 0.00613 rad and speed 0.0211 m/s.

Across the entire 3800–4800 span, maximum endpoint tilt was 0.0459 rad and the 95th-percentile endpoint root speed was 0.02493 m/s, though maximum speed was 0.2313 m/s and 26 samples exceeded 0.12 m/s. All 16016 toe-velocity and contact substeps in this span were present, with no toe speed caps, contact overflow or logged substep errors and no positive platform nonfoot normal impulse. Body and wing joint-speed substeps were not recorded. Flexion and contact alone do not establish geometric toe wrap, foot clearance, true material-point slip, or biological grip. One successful start and one used layout cannot qualify landing, an autonomous two-layout mission or the newer flight/gait controller. A uniform same-seed replication has been prepared; preserve all failures.
