# Second development seed: walking filter repeated, limitations remain

On the previously used development seed 20261126, zero- and 0.05-rad toe-preload conditions completed all eight 300-step first episodes without reported physical failures or trace errors. The arms matched exactly through 4 s at all 1,600 sampled endpoints and 25,600 contact substeps. They used the same standing feedback and the exact retained shader binaries from the first matched seed.

During 4–6 s, the low-preload arm had zero toe-speed-cap substeps in all lanes versus 2–3 per lane at zero, zero both-feet-unloaded substeps in both arms, and 3 left/4 right sampled support-qualified clearing bouts per lane. Travel was 130.1–140.2 mm versus 130.9–132.4 mm at zero. Pooled accepted contact-point median/p95 was 0.00334/0.04475 m/s versus 0.00365/0.03951; the previously specified 1.5x speed filter passed. These are instantaneous point speeds rather than integrated slip, and do not prove a naturally walking crow or stable grip.

All 48 complete low-arm contacts containing both toe groups showed rear-first touchdown and release at recorded substeps, while five other runs lacked a group versus two at zero. The 20-ms recovery observer retained 27 low-arm windows: 16 ankle-proxy-first and 11 at the same endpoint, none knee-first; endpoint ties, excluded fragments, proxy anatomy, species and surface differences prevent a firm comparison to the related-magpie finding. During the common preparation, two lanes still hit toe-speed caps in the first 2 s, on 3 and 6 substeps. All lanes had zero both-feet-unloaded substeps during 2–4 s; no claim is made that all transient loading is realistic.

This is a second exposed development seed, not independent biological data or held-out qualification. The test ends before takeoff and leaves the initial toe impacts, head/body realism, phase timing, full wing-driven flight and landing unresolved. The narrow improvement is retained for follow-up and is not promoted to a qualified controller.
