# Current-body contact-driven weight-transfer test

The actual 35-body release screen lost support and fell after a brief left-foot release. The fixed-state kinematic study shows that right hip roll can move the COM laterally relative to the supporting foot, but later forward COM drift remains a distinct failure mode. Test a bounded, state- and contact-driven bilateral hip-roll weight shift before the existing left-foot release, without changing the body, contact model, solver, toe targets, sagittal controller, initialization or external forces. Use the same two constructed-start lanes and corrected-axis body as the archived hip-roll grip-release screen, with 32 substeps. After 50 consecutive bilateral supported opposing-grip intervals, latch weight transfer. Compute whole-bird COM and velocity and right-foot COM and velocity from native kinematics on each accepted state. Command both physical hip rolls toward the right support based on the measured lateral COM-relative-foot error and relative velocity, at gains .5 and 1.0 in the two lanes, clamped to +/- .25 rad and with a .005 rad/interval target slew bound. Maintain the prior sagittal balance and toe controller. Release is permitted only after 10 consecutive intervals of continued bilateral opposing-grip support, COM within [-.005,.020] m laterally and +/- .012 m fore-aft relative to the right foot, and both corresponding relative speeds below .04 m/s. Continue the controller through release and regrip. Stop interpreting a lane at fall, nonconvergence or trace end. A candidate passes only if it achieves at least 10 consecutive real-contact-clear release intervals while the opposite toes support with opposing normals, then at least 100 consecutive bilateral supported opposing-grip intervals at the end of the complete 350-step run, with no fall, invalid state, reset or external body wrench. Compare the exact pre-intervention prefix against its same-lane retained baseline within 1e-6 per q/v component; record all actions, states, contact normals, 32-substep observer output, solver status, and complete hashes. The readiness rule is a safety gate, not a success criterion; failure to release is a failure. Model-scale numbers are experimental screening choices, not crow calibration. This constructed-start test does not qualify landing or touch the reserved layouts and seeds.
