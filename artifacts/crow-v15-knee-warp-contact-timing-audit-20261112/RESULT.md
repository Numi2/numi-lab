# Substep follow-up confirms greater contact motion in one development lane

The repaired observer reproduced the corresponding baseline and 0.16 knee-warp runs exactly in the recorded 2,400 endpoint states, 38,400 toe records and 38,400 contact records each. In lane 4 of seed 20261127, it captured 1,600 consecutive 1.25 ms q/v samples per arm and every positive ground-contact impulse over the two-second walk.

For right-foot contacts, the impulse-weighted tangential point-speed p90 rose from 0.0572 to 0.0921 m/s; the fraction of normal impulse associated with speed above the descriptive 0.05 m/s threshold rose from 12.4% to 19.2%. For the left foot, the corresponding p90 rose from 0.0447 to 0.0761 m/s and impulse fraction from 8.8% to 15.3%. Overall, 34,669 baseline and 38,872 candidate contact samples were measured. These post-substep measurements support the earlier warning that the knee-only change raises contact motion, though their impulse weighting differs from the earlier 50 Hz p95 statistic.

Centered finite-difference validation of center-of-mass velocity differed by about 0.0020–0.0021 m/s for the body and 0.010–0.013 m/s for the feet across the two arms. Solver contact positions and accepted q/v may still refer to different instants; there is no established continuous material-point tracking, so these are not accumulated slip distances or proof of inadequate friction. This is one used-seed, correlated development lane, not biological qualification. The initial incompatible observer attempt failed before execution and is retained alongside its amendment and repaired runs.
