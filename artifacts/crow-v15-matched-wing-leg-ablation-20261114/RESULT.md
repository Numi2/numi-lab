# Matched-wing leg-extension comparison: useful difference, no takeoff qualification

Both arms received identical recorded commands for the six wing coordinates (indices 6–11) throughout intervals 335–500 in all eight lanes. Logged q/v, policy actions and contacts matched through interval 368. The controls used the same native observer and nonshader inputs, with teacher off and no scheduled resets. The model source mapping is retained; the recorded observer independently identifies indices 8–9 as flap and 10–11 as pronation.

Both arms first achieved three consecutive unloaded intervals at 377 in every lane. In intervals 371–376, the extension arm's summed upward foot impulse exceeded the held-leg arm by 0.101–0.107 N s per lane. The modeled vertical aerodynamic impulse before the detected unload was about +0.0393–0.0397 N s in the extension arm and +0.0397–0.0398 N s in the held-leg arm. Later ground contact or missing contact observation first occurred at 447 or 453 in the extension arm and 406 in the held-leg arm; the registered coverage check found no missing substeps. One held-leg lane reached the 11.99 rad/s toe-speed threshold. All arms had full finite substep coverage and no recorded observer errors.

The held-leg arm also unloaded with wings active, so this does not prove a reliable leg-powered takeoff or leg necessity. Tail target index 12 first differed at interval 376 in every lane, so the counterfactual does not hold every nonleg command fixed through the transition; the later flight difference cannot be attributed solely to leg extension. Contact/state epoch alignment remains unverified, the aerodynamic forces are model outputs, and this fixed schedule does not establish autonomous flight, landing or biological realism.

The original analysis mistakenly compared indices 8–13 as wings, thereby including the first leg coordinate and omitting the two sweep coordinates. Its source, output and flagged mismatches remain archived; `analyze-corrected.py` and `analysis-corrected.json` compare indices 6–11. Complete push and hold native traces and their failures are retained.
