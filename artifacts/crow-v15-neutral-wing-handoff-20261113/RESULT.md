# Result: strict takeoff screen failed

Both arms completed 400 steps; all eight lanes matched one another and the smooth-release reference in recorded state, action and contact through step 345, with no detected trace, solver or overflow error. The six prespecified comparable push lanes all showed a fully unsupported interval at step 353 before the first three-interval release at step 360, followed by renewed foot support at steps 363–369. Two lanes also had 3 and 4 toe-speed-threshold substeps, above the screen limit. Although early added upward foot impulse was 0.1055–0.1530 N s in steps 348–355 and 0.0667–0.1149 N s in steps 348–357, this is not a successful sustained takeoff.

The instrumented substep comparison did not improve the transient: peak flap speeds increased from 37.68–38.00 to 38.07–38.89 rad/s, and their common timing shifted from step 369/substep 3 to step 372/substep 3. Peak vertical model aerodynamic forces were 33.46–35.30 N in this finite observation window. These are simulated quantities, not biologically calibrated limits; peak reduction or increase does not itself prove physical or biological realism. The target intervention did not remove the problem. The values come from the simulated model and observer, using a previously used seed and correlated lanes. Source, compiled metallibs, exact hashes, raw traces, analyses, and failed results are retained together. This does not qualify flight, takeoff, landing, either layout, or a full-mission video.

The pre-release first downstroke in the representative American crow in Jackson and Dial (2011, DOI 10.1242/jeb.046789) also shows that this release-triggered diagnostic is not a faithful timing model. Their reported 6.4 ± 0.1 Hz mean is for pooled wingbeats excluding the first; neither it nor this study supplies an instantaneous v15 joint-speed limit.
