# Matched neutral-wing handoff diagnostic, preregistered

The release-triggered smooth crossfade produced recontact in all six comparable lanes, and the substep observer exposed a 37.68–38.00 rad/s flap-speed transient. Test whether fading the triggered oscillator to the existing neutral position over 7.20–7.30 s, then fading the original carrier in over 7.30–7.40 s, reduces that transient without modifying the solver, changing the body, clamping state or injecting a wrench. This is a controller diagnostic, not a measured bird trajectory; it may reduce useful lift and worsen recontact. Keep all other launch and direct-moment controls identical to the smooth-release experiment.

Use the instrumented aerodynamic-wrench host, matched push and active-crouch arms, previously used development seed 20261126, eight correlated lanes, 400 control intervals, 16 substeps, no intermediate resets. The comparable lanes are 2–7. Require complete records and exact q/v/action/contact parity through step 345 between arms and against the smooth-release push reference. Require no observer overflow or simulation error. Report early extra foot impulse in steps 348–355 and 348–357, support gaps, final release and later recontact, rise, nonfoot impulse and toe threshold events. Apply the prior strict screen: each of six lanes must gain at least 0.05 N s in both early windows, have no unsupported gaps before final release by step 375 or foot recontact through step 400, rise at least 0.05 m, have zero nonfoot impulse and at most one toe-threshold substep.

Separately report maximum flap speed at every physics substep and model vertical aerodynamic peak in 348–375 versus the retained smooth-release observer, with their timing; improvement on a numerical diagnostic is not a biological qualification. Retain both complete logs, sources, build inputs, results and failures. This single used seed cannot qualify flight, landing, layouts, or the mission.

Biological context: Jackson and Dial (2011), DOI 10.1242/jeb.046789, studied three American crows during burst escape. Their Table 3 reports 6.4 ± 0.1 Hz for wingbeats excluding the first; their representative first crow downstroke begins before toe-off. That frequency is not an instantaneous joint-speed limit and does not calibrate this artificial neutral handoff.
