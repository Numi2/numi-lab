# Toe collision isolation during flight

Question: Are toe collision impulses responsible for the articulated-body flight regression, or does it persist without toe collision? Compare the same baseline toe body, high toe armature, frozen flight controller, fixed head, obstacle-collision-off environment, unmodified ground and flight screen under two arms: original toe collision and all toe collision masks disabled. Disabling toe collision is diagnostic only and cannot establish walking, takeoff or grip. Instrument solver contact evidence at every 100th accepted 20 ms control step for all eight lanes, preserving every reported contact pair, separation and normal impulse, and distinguish self, ground and other contacts. Run 8 lanes, seed 20261120, 1600 steps, four substeps; compare position, velocity, original flight gates, physical and solver failures, and toe speed. Original versus modified sensor observation is irrelevant because the diagnostic policy is independently verified zero-output. If collision-off returns all eight lanes to the speed gate while collision-on fails, toe interactions are implicated (including effects at the start, not necessarily flight); if both fail similarly, collisions alone do not explain the regression. If the unmodified instrument differs from the archived baseline, regard the instrument as invalid. This exploratory ablation is not a flight, walking, landing, or biological qualification; all outcomes retained. Subsequent confirmation requires untouched seeds and all physical criteria.


## Follow-up after collision-off failure

The unmodified instrument reproduced all 128 baseline sampled states exactly. Toe-collision-off failed during ground preparation in all eight lanes because the narrowed sole lost its supporting toe boxes. This does not isolate airborne flight. Add a third diagnostic arm: disable collisions on the eight articulated toe shapes, but attach identical, rigid collision boxes at the toes neutral offsets to their parent feet. Thus the full toe dynamics remain present while the ground support envelope approximates the on-arm; toe motion and contact distribution are not identical, so no claim of exact equivalence is made. Repeat the same seed, commands, gates and recorded contacts. Require survival through the full 1600 steps before comparing tracking. Record actual positive contacts, including any after step 400, and never deploy these non-gripping surrogate shapes.
