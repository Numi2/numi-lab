# Alignment-gated descent: brief support, then beak collision

The 16-substep recorder was complete and reported no overflow or errors; the first 1305 q/v samples exactly matched the matched-launch teacher and the minimum accepted-step obstacle clearance before failure was 0.139 m. After waypoint five the bird reached the perch. It had 51 consecutive 20 ms intervals meeting the stated bilateral half-weight support criterion without another-body perch impulse, but no interval met the opposing toe-normal impulse criterion. The first other-body impulse occurred at step 2516 and the first episode terminated at step 2518 with lower-beak/perch contact. There were no failed native environment steps. Post-reset movement is excluded.

Inspection of the accepted task states and joint angles identified two likely contributors: the toe ramp depends on rear-toe force, so front-toe-only support cannot start rear-toe closure, and the existing landing phase can cut off the flight action on brief bilateral contact before grip. These are hypotheses for a separate test, not proof of the causes. This privileged trial does not demonstrate sustained safe landing, toe grip or release, autonomous navigation or a held-out layout.
