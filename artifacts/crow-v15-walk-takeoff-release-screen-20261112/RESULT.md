# Later release threshold: promising short development screen

Lowering the release height from 0.195 to 0.185 m, while retaining the three-consecutive-unloaded-interval and upward-motion requirements, changed the seven development lanes that had risen and settled. In all 16 lanes across two previously used seeds, the last recorded above-threshold foot load was interval 352 substep 7; no foot load was recorded in intervals 371–400. At 8 s root heights were 0.750–0.820 m. There were no toe-speed-cap substeps or nonfoot ground impulse during 6–8 s, no reported contact overflow, trace error or physical termination. This is a short horizon, not evidence of lasting flight, control, realistic flight forces, a landing, or performance on unused layouts.

All 16 lanes exactly matched their respective 0.195 m control through interval 354, including all measured substeps; in the seven lanes affected by the threshold, the first sampled endpoint divergence was 355 and the first contact-record divergence was interval 357. The other nine remained exact through interval 400. The bilateral upward impulse during intervals 348–353 remained unchanged, 0.718–0.741 N s combined versus 0.530 N s of model bodyweight impulse. This is combined contact impulse, not isolated leg muscle work. The accepted evidence supports the threshold/timing hypothesis for these starts and rules out preempting their recorded push as the earlier-wing test did. It does not establish wing downstroke before toe-off, as observed in American-crow escape flight. The subsequent flight still uses an artificial body-moment controller and is not physically qualified.
