# Front-toe friction ablation does not fix launch

The predeclared diagnostic was valid for its stated scope. All eight first episodes ran 400 intervals; sampled states, policy actions and contacts were identical to the retained baseline through tick 350, and all recorded support and toe-substep observations were complete. In all six candidate lanes, logged front-toe/ground static and dynamic friction and tangential impulse were zero after the intervention, while other logged ground contacts retained nonzero coefficients.

Removing front-toe tangential friction did not yield stable launch: every lane recontacted after its first three unloaded intervals. Toe-speed threshold substeps for baseline versus ablation were 36 vs 33, 27 vs 29, 17 vs 27, 15 vs 22, 10 vs 11 and 16 vs 19. Thus five of six increased and one decreased slightly. Whole-body COM peak rise through tick 400 was 0.032–0.088 m in the ablation; greater rise in one lane did not prevent recontact. These are used-seed correlated development trials. The intervention alters subsequent states and contact loads, so it does not isolate every force component or exclude friction effects under other conditions.

Do not promote the deliberately frictionless intervention. This result argues against front-toe tangential friction alone as a sufficient explanation or fix for the observed toe-speed events. Normal contact coupling, geometry, joint constraints, launch timing, toe actuation and recontact remain competing mechanisms; a solver defect is not established. Takeoff, flight, toe grip, landing and the full mission remain unqualified.
