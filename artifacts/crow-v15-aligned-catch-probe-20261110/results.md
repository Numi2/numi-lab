# Result: aligned neutral contacts support; active closures fail

All four matched neutral-toe continuations remained supported through 0.8 s, with no positive-impulse nonfoot contact before the nominal release and final toe angles under 0.007 rad. No active closure achieved a qualifying opposed bilateral grip or supported release. Several active runs physically lost support; four of eight later reported a numerical solver failure. In the case-0 immediate-closure run, the accepted root vertical speed changed from -0.209 to -136.284 m/s in 5 ms while the perching toe constraints reported a summed 97.1 N s impulse. This implausible jump preceded its first body contact, so this case is a numerical instability during toe contact, not an ordinary fall onto the body. It warrants controlled isolation; it does not identify a specific solver defect by itself.

The compared state came from a guided development route but the continuation holds non-toe joint targets and restarts a fresh cache. Four perturbations were counterfactual. The open controls do not demonstrate articulated grip and the result does not establish autonomous landing or biological realism. The next investigation must distinguish cache/history, control, collision settings and step-size effects before changing production behavior.
