# Result: uniform contact feedback through walking and launch

In eight closely related starts on used development seed 20261127, all matched the same-lane no-feedback integrated control for 200 state endpoints and 3200 contact substeps before four seconds. The same -1 pitch / 0.05 pitch-rate loaded-hip feedback eliminated the controls' 26–27 consecutive both-feet-unloaded substeps during the measured 4–6 s gait window in all eight lanes. All progressed approximately 0.081 m in that window, with no recorded toe-speed caps or nonfoot ground impulse. Relative to their matched no-feedback counterparts, left/right loaded contact-speed integrals were 1.031–1.043 / 0.996–1.003; this is a changing-contact-point proxy, not measured material slip. No lane met the previous 25-percent reduction gate against the different older configuration; both comparisons are retained.

All first episodes survived the 12-second horizon and were foot-unloaded after eight seconds, with no observed toe-speed caps after 7.4 seconds or sampled nonfoot contacts. In the six-interval launch window, total upward foot normal impulse was 0.430–0.441 N s; this is not leg-only work and does not alone prove a leg-powered takeoff. Earlier matched leg-extension controls separately show a contribution, but do not qualify takeoff for this configuration. Wing/body speed caps were not recorded by this host.

The related-corvid hip and knee phase signs were reproduced, but the median late-ankle change was only about +0.0037–0.0039 degrees, effectively flat rather than a meaningful biological match. These starts are nearly identical and use one development seed; sustained walking, a second seed, actual slip, realistic whole-body motion, toe grip/release, flight qualification, route and landing remain unproven. The next experiment uses matched control and candidate on a second development seed. Do not promote or claim completion from this screen.

## Related-corvid toe-contact sequence

A later substep analysis grouped physical ground-contact points into individual stance bouts. In all 40 assessable bouts, rear-toe normal contact began before any front toe; in those same 40, the third front toe remained loaded last or tied for last, but was never uniquely last. Eight additional bouts contained only a loaded rear toe and could not be assessed against front-toe ordering. The 40 assessable bouts came from eight very similar starts and do not represent 40 independent birds. In the related [magpie study](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.203.14.2159), the authors described rear-toe touchdown first and third-toe lift-off last; the present v15 results reproduce only the coarse order, not distinct third-toe release. A few observed 0.14–0.20 s rear-before-front delays and isolated rear-toe bouts warrant further scrutiny; the paper does not provide a comparable numerical delay here. Thresholds, model contact geometry, speed, surface and species differ, so these results do not establish crow-realistic contact timing.

## Refinement: toe-defined stance and left-right asymmetry

The earlier near-zero pooled late-ankle median mixed 40 bouts with all relevant toe contacts and eight additional rear-toe-only bouts. Re-analyzing the 40 assessable bouts from hallux touchdown through third-front-toe final contact gives a pooled median +0.460 degrees, but pooling hides a consistent asymmetry across these closely related starts: per-lane left-foot medians are about +0.980 degrees and right-foot medians about -0.420 degrees. Thus the earlier description of uniformly flat late-stance motion was too broad: the left shows a small extension proxy while the right shows flexion. Sixteen of 40 bouts have a negative late-minus-mid ankle angle, and the eight hallux-only bouts cannot be aligned to this toe-defined stance and were excluded. The kinematic angle is an approximate v15 segment-geometry proxy and remains subject to species, speed, surface, digit grouping and anatomical-model differences. The result does not establish a realistic crow gait; subsequent analysis must keep the two feet separate.

## Head and trunk motion within measured stance

The accepted v15 states were reconstructed into segment positions and checked against replay positions (maximum discrepancy below 10 micrometres). Across the 40 toe-defined stances from the eight closely related seed-20261127 starts, the median within-stance ranges of head position relative to the root were 0.166 mm forward and 0.535 mm vertical; root pitch range was 0.0209 rad, neck yaw range 0.00830 rad and neck pitch range 0.00686 rad. These describe this simulation, not a measured crow target or the whole-stride trajectory. The positions are root-relative, not independent head stabilization measurements. Related-corvid data support examining coordinated body motion, but do not justify imposing a guessed neck stretch or claiming a validated amplitude.
