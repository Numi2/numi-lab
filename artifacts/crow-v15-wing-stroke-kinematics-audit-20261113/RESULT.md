# Posthoc flap lag and implicit-gain discrepancy

In the used-seed 6.4 Hz passive-closure amplitude screen, fit the applied flap target and actual angle over ticks 376-400 to a sinusoid at 6.4 Hz. On the left wing, target amplitudes about 0.243, 0.437 and 0.631 rad correspond to actual amplitudes about 0.191, 0.185 and 0.172 rad, with actual-target phase about -2.40, -2.50 and -2.58 radians. Lanes are correlated; this is a descriptive fit, not a crow measurement. Right-wing values and force by coordinate-velocity sign are in analysis.json. Sign is not labeled as biological upstroke or downstroke.

The research wing feedback uses kP=10 and kD=0.1 with an effort bound of 3, but the v15 model DOF metadata still carries 800 and 8. The native implicit articulated dynamics and implicit contact inverse-mass operator both derive a numerical armature from the metadata gains. For timestep 0.00125 s and unit controller scales, the old term is 0.01125 kg m^2 versus 0.000140625 kg m^2 for the research feedback gains. A representative FP64 free articulated flap effective inertia is about 0.0002955 kg m^2 (fixed-other coordinate mass term 0.0003653); contacts and other forces are excluded. The discrepancy is thus a testable explanation for the slow flap response. Observed substep velocity change alone cannot isolate motor force from aerodynamics and other coupled forces. Controller scales, actual implicit flags, derivative handling and full coupled behavior require explicit verification.

The research-specific mismatch arose from overriding the drive law without changing its model gain metadata. It does not establish a generic solver defect. Next test a v15-only model-input correction retaining original mass, physical armature and torque limits; ensure forward dynamics and contact response both receive the same corrected gains, record controller scales, and compare matched controls. Do not promote or qualify without separate physics and flight tests.
