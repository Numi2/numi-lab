# Matched leg-first wing-delay screen: rejected at joint limits

Across two previously used seeds, all 32 first episodes in the extension and powered-crouch arms survived to step 625. Each matched pair had complete recorded prefixes and first diverged at step 551, after the push could act. Four tested wing delays were 0, .04, .08 and .12 s; each setting has only four closely related development pairs, not independent biological trials.

At .04 s, leg extension produced .6113–.6115 N s of vertical total foot-contact impulse over steps 549–556 versus .5271 N s with crouch hold; root vertical speed at step 556 was .3721–.3725 versus .2254 m/s. These impulses include normal and tangential ground-contact components; gravity and active wing and feedback forces also affect the trajectories. The difference is an intervention effect in the coupled simulation, not measured muscle work or proof of legs-only liftoff. With no delay, the hold instead produced more early impulse and upward speed.

The .04 s extension lanes hit the authored 22 rad/s limit 14 times at each knee at step 558 on both seeds. The .08 and .12 s extension lanes hit each knee limit 22 times; their hold controls also had toe and joint limit events. All positive-delay settings therefore fail the preregistered safety gate despite the impulse gain; zero delay fails the leg-contribution criterion. None is promoted. A subsequent separate experiment tests whether the release-time target reversal causes the knee spike. No biological accuracy, sustained flight, landing, navigation or held-out qualification follows from this result.
