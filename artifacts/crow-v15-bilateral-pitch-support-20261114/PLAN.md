# Contact-supported pitch feedback during regrip

The archived baseline loses the right foretoe contact around interval 305, causing its pitch-feedback gate to turn off even while upward contact on the other foot remains; root pitch and forward velocity subsequently grow until the bird falls. The high-friction-only test did not repair the regrip. Test a bounded feedback hypothesis without changing materials, actuator limits or forces: in lane 1 only, after the left foot has completed the release-and-return state, allow the existing pitch feedback whenever either the original right-foot opposed-toe support gate is true or the measured combined upward foot impulse in the last accepted contact sample exceeds 0.001 N s. Lane 0 retains the exact previous gate. The existing pitch error, gain, damping, target bounds and slew limits remain unchanged, and no artificial body wrench or contact is added. This is a development controller hypothesis, not an assertion that contact alone can always arrest a fall.

Before observing the result, require the two lanes to match in all logged states, commands and contacts until the support-gate intervention; document its first differing interval, active gates, support and toe-speed substeps. Evaluate the same full-coverage stationary release and terminal 100-interval bilateral opposed-toe supported regrip gates, no root fall, no nonfoot contact, and no nominal toe-speed cap. Retain both lanes, failures and source/binary identity. A pass here would still not qualify walking, realistic limb morphology, flight, landing, or the continuous mission.
