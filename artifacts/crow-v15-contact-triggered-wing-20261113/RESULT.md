# Result: contact-triggered first stroke failed, with a discontinuous target

The two 400-step episodes passed the predeclared recorded push/hold and prior no-moment/no-commanded-flapping prefix-parity checks through step 345. Full 335–400 contact, toe, effort, phase and target observations were available with no reported overflow or substep error. Direct yaw and pitch body-moment requests were set to zero at the application point from the beginning. Passive aerodynamic effects and other modeled forces remain.

Every comparable push lane had an unloaded control interval at step 353 before first three-interval release at 355, then recontacted during steps 364–373. The strict no-gap/no-recontact requirement failed for all six. Lane 7 also failed the +0.05 N s push-minus-crouch impulse criterion in both windows, at +0.0043 N s; the other five were +0.0826 to +0.1283 N s. Lanes 2, 5, 6 and 7 had three, seven, three and five toe-speed threshold substeps respectively. No nonfoot ground impulse was recorded during steps 348–400. No controller is promoted.

The recorded task counter began at step 353, and the flap target for the reference lanes rose from 0.675 rad at step 353 to 1.000 rad at 354 because the first-stroke target assumed the wing was fully raised when the trigger fired. This is a design discontinuity. The sampled physical flap velocity reached approximately 31.3–31.5 rad/s at step 373 near the return to the old carrier; the model's authored speed limit is 90 rad/s, but neither value is a crow-validated physiological limit. The two-consecutive-last-substep support trigger also need not match a full-substep contact transition.

The controller is an exploratory position-drive experiment with unvalidated phase, force and transition dynamics. The recontacts and high sampled speed cannot be attributed to a single cause from these records. These used-seed, correlated lanes do not qualify realistic takeoff, flight, navigation or landing; preserve both full runs, failed results and the target discontinuity.
