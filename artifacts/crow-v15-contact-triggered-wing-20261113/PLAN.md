# Contact-triggered bounded first-wing-stroke development test

Rationale: the retained continuous-push simulation on a development seed shows the wing-body high point ranging 25 ms before to 47.5 ms after the last measured foot contact, depending on lane. Comparative small-bird measurements indicate that the first downstroke begins near release, but those finch/dove measurements are not quantitative crow calibration. The prior zero-commanded-flapping, no-body-moment control produced a leg-associated impulse but no clean launch.

Test a development-only contact-triggered first-stroke controller on top of that exact 160 ms continuous-leg push and matched crouch. Suppress the two identified direct body moments at the physical wrench application point from episode start. Suppress flapping until 6.96 s as in the prior paired experiment. During 6.96–7.4 s, position-drive the flap toward a raised target from 0.35 to 1.00 rad over 120 ms; after the existing initiated-push latch and two consecutive last-substep low-support observations, count control intervals in the task's otherwise-zero launch toe-flight bits and command a bounded cosine first stroke centered at 0.35 rad with 0.65 rad amplitude and 0.36 s period. Clear the temporary counter at 7.4 s and return to the existing wing/toe logic. Disable the front-toe walking override in this launch interval because the same bits temporarily carry the counter; those bits were zero in the reference during that interval. This is an engineering timing experiment with bounded position drives, not an empirically calibrated crow controller or direct body force. The support observation is last-substep and must be compared against full-substep contact; transient false triggers are possible.

Use fixed v15 body, contact parameters, policies, extended observer, 16 substeps, 400 steps, no resets, previously used seed 20261126, eight correlated lanes with 2–7 comparisons. Passive aerodynamics remains. Compare push and active-crouch arms, verify exact recorded q/v/action/contact prefix parity through step 345 against each other and the prior no-moment/no-flapping push; require complete first episodes and full 335–400 contact, toe, leg effort and target records without reported error/overflow. Observe actual leg and wing targets, packed counter and wing coordinates; report onset relative to last positive foot impulse, early push-minus-hold upward foot impulses over 348–355 and 348–357, support gaps before first three-interval release and later recontact, root rise, nonfoot impulse, modeled toe threshold. Strict screen: every comparison lane has >=0.05 N s extra foot impulse in both windows, continuous support through a final release by step 375 without subsequent support through 400, >=0.05 m root rise, no nonfoot impulse and at most one toe threshold substep. Keep failures and all raw records. Even a screen pass does not establish biological realism, independently validated forces, sustained flight, turn/recovery qualification, navigation or landing.
