# Result: same-state unsteady-term ablation

The observer repeated the earlier used-seed launch with no logged state, action or contact mismatches across 400 ticks in all eight lanes, and exact contact-substep parity for ticks 335-400. The actual aerodynamic input q/v exactly matches the substep-start trace. The copied descriptor differed byte-for-byte only at the specified x coefficient in each wing; both original coefficients were 8. The actual dispatch recorded still air and density 1.225 kg/m^3. Scratch evaluations never fed into the simulation.

For the six bounded-wing failure lanes, the signed actual aerodynamic work over ticks 376-400 was -0.614, -0.594, -0.704, -0.668, +0.395, +0.797 J. On identical states with only the fixed-direction unsteady coefficient zeroed, it was -0.019, -0.019, -0.035, -0.035, -0.081, -0.085 J. The difference isolates this term for the sampled states; it is not the outcome of an ablated trajectory. The analytic observer accounts for total forces within 1.2e-10 N and uses ABI world-frame COM wrenches and FP64 body twists. Positive aerodynamic work in still air, from a term without a modeled wake energy state, exposes an unaccounted energy transfer in this quasi-steady model. It does not establish that all real unsteady aerodynamics must be dissipative or diagnose the contact solver. Wrench/twist conventions and observer parity do not validate the crow model against live birds.

This is a research failure and a qualification blocker, not a completed launch or flight. No final layouts or seeds were used. Examine an energy-consistent research variant with matched trajectory controls; do not promote it without separate validation and complete physical qualification.
