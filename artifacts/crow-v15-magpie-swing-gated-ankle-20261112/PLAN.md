# Contact-gated ankle phase hypothesis from magpie data

Prior unconditional ankle delays improved one knee/ankle ordering proxy but worsened foot-point speed and introduced unloading. Compare the existing 0.40-rad crouch, 0.05 toe preload baseline with 0, .10, .20 and .30 rad ankle-carrier delays in paired lanes. Blend the delay only during an unloaded swing foot while the opposite foot is supported; preserve the baseline target during stance. Two previously used development seeds, eight starts each, first 6 s, 16 substeps, teacher off, same host and policy. Verify no-delay controls against the retained same-lane baseline at all endpoints and contact substeps; measure toe speed limits, both-feet-unloaded time, nonfoot impulses, contact-point velocity and the prior anatomical-order proxy. The phase convention, 50 Hz samples, different corvid species, slower speed and surface do not constitute real-crow gait qualification. Reject any candidate with worse support or point-speed gates rather than optimizing proxy order alone.
