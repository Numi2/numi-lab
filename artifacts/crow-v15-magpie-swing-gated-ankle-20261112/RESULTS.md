# Contact-gated ankle delay: rejected

The related-magpie knee/ankle sequence motivated a delay applied only when the moving foot was unloaded and the other foot supported. Both 0-rad controls matched their same-seed, same-lane baseline at all 300 endpoint states and 4,800 contact substeps per lane on both used development seeds.

No tested lane hit the toe velocity limit in 4–6 s. However, .10- and .20-rad delays produced 2–3 both-feet-unloaded substeps per affected lane, versus zero in the paired baselines. Per-foot 50 Hz contact-point speed p95 increased from approximately .025–.033 to .059–.082 m/s on the right at those settings. The .30-rad lanes retained continuous foot loading, but left contact-point speed p95 increased from approximately .047–.049 to .102–.116 m/s. These violate the prior 1.5x per-foot contact-speed gate; instantaneous contact-point speed is not integrated slip distance. Some ankle/knee proxy windows shifted to knee-first, but incomplete or missing-toe contact groups increased considerably, and the ankle landmark, side-view versus 3D mapping, speed, surface and species remain unvalidated.

All candidates are rejected; this evidence does not establish whole-gait biological realism. It supports further work on synchronized anatomical measurement and contact-coupled control instead of transplanting magpie timing as crow ground truth.
