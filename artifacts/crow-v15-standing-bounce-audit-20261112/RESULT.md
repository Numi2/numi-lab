# Initial standing bounce audit

In the retained zero-preload crouch control during the first three seconds, four starts had 1,354–1,689 substeps with neither foot carrying normal load, while the other four had 22–79; no nonfoot-ground normal impulse was recorded in that interval. Peak absolute reconstructed center-of-mass vertical speed in the four bouncing starts was 0.318–0.398 m/s, with peak-to-peak COM height 10–12 mm. The body does not settle before the 2 s crouch begins in those starts; the same phenomenon was present in the earlier no-crouch control. Wing coordinates barely changed in the earlier retained trace, but this observation alone cannot rule out all aerodynamics or controller forces.

The model-mass-weighted COM position and vertical velocity were checked against 149 lane-5 replay frames, with maximum errors 2.5e-9 m and 2.2e-9 m/s. Comparing 1,184 successive endpoint velocity changes after the duplicated initial endpoint to same-interval summed vertical foot impulse divided by modeled 0.45006 kg plus gravity gave an RMS residual 0.00144 m/s and maximum 0.00681 m/s. Different one-step alignments had much larger residuals. These measurements support ground impulse as the immediate source of the observed vertical momentum changes, not a diagnosis of why the contact response repeatedly launches the body. Impulse and state timing, actuator work, other wrenches and contact-point preimpact velocities require controlled follow-up.

The reconstructed robot material has static/dynamic friction 0.8/0.6, restitution 0.1, restitution velocity threshold 0.5 m/s, zero configured compliance and dissipation; the authored flat ground uses material index 0. These are model values, not measured crow-foot material properties. Effective runtime values and contact-point speed must be verified before concluding whether restitution contributes. Compare controlled restitution, damping and actuator conditions with unchanged starts and track injected/removed energy and successful settling; no contact or solver defect is asserted from this trace alone.
