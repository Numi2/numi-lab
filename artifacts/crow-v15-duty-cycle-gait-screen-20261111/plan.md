# Contact-gated gait with longer stance and larger knee swing

In matched v15 development walking, simply reducing the knee drive increased support loss. Increasing only the knee command from 0.40 to 0.60 gave about 52% larger knee excursions but increased no-foot-upward-support substeps from 33 to 2,112 out of 25,600. A likely issue is that the periodic controller initiates the next swing before the returning foot has settled, despite gating each swing on observed opposite-foot support. This is a hypothesis, not proof of the cause.

Test an isolated gait-controller candidate against the same original baseline on both previously used development seeds and all eight lanes each. Keep body, material, initial conditions, frozen flight controller, support sensors, simulator and 300-step run unchanged. In the two-second walking window use the larger 0.60 knee multiplier, a longer 0.80-second gait period (previously 0.50), and a smoothly narrowed knee lift pulse smoothstep(0.5,1,swing) rather than max(swing,0). This leaves more time with knees near stance between leg lifts, while the existing opposite-foot contact gate remains active. All other commands remain unchanged. Do not modify the qualified flight carrier.

Predict at least 15% larger mean bilateral knee span in at least 6/16 lanes, no more than the baseline 33 total no-foot-upward-support substeps in the walking window, no nonfoot contacts, no added simulator failures or terminations, and forward displacement in every lane. Require exact logged prefix states/contact summaries before walking and retain all traces and failed runs. Report step events, net displacement, body motion and contact coverage. Reject promotion if any criterion fails. This development screen does not establish whole-journey walking/takeoff/landing, unused-layout generalization or biological validation.
