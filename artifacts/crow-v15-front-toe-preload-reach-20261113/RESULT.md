# Larger front-toe preload offsets: failed paired launch screen

Both arms completed 400 first-episode ticks in all eight lanes. Recorded state, actions and contacts matched through tick 345, including substep contact samples over ticks 335–345, and the 2-unit wing effort bound was observed. Lanes 2–7 tested +0.25, +0.30 and +0.35 normalized crouch-ankle offsets in pairs; lanes 0–1 are the unchanged high-drive controls. Those offsets are control settings, not measurements of crow ankles.

None of the six comparable lanes passed the preregistered screen. Lanes 2–3 lacked continuous support in ticks 340–347, the +0.30 pair never reached a three-interval release, and all observed releases were followed by recontact. Maximum root rise was 0.0242–0.0377 m, below 0.05 m; five lanes had 6–10 toe threshold substeps while lane 3 had none but still failed other criteria. The two early push-minus-hold vertical foot-impulse differences ranged 0.0441–0.1860 and 0.0472–0.1159 N s, with some lanes below 0.05 N s.

Despite the larger offsets, all logged positive ground normal impulse in the matched crouch-hold controls remained on rear-toe bodies during ticks 348–365. In the push arm, during ticks 356–365 several lanes shifted almost entirely toward non-rear-toe bodies, with front-toe and foot contact, but recontact persisted. The response illustrates why endpoint extrapolation cannot substitute for a dynamic matched trial. This used-seed correlated-lane result does not establish a unique cause, validate crow anatomy, or qualify takeoff, flight, landing or navigation. Preserve all sources, binaries, controls, raw traces, replays and failures.
