# Related-corvid gait coordination for model comparison

Verstappen, Aerts and Van Damme, 2000, [Terrestrial locomotion in the black-billed magpie: kinematic analysis of walking, running and out-of-phase hopping](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.203.14.2159), studied three hand-raised birds on a thin-cork outdoor track. Their close-up high-speed recordings covered fragments of strides, supplemented by wider 50 Hz recordings. Hip and knee landmarks had greater expected digitization uncertainty than distal points. This is a related species and different surface, not direct American-crow ground truth.

The authors define stance from rear-toe touchdown to third-digit lift-off. They describe hip extension for most of stance (sometimes brief early flexion), knee flexion through stance with an occasional small late extension, and ankle flexion followed by extension. In recovery, knee extension precedes ankle extension, with hip extension beginning close to the next stance. After toe-off the toes flex, then extend later in recovery; rear-toe touchdown preceded the front toes in their recordings. These are qualitative, experiment-specific comparison hypotheses, not fixed timing parameters to insert into a crow controller.

To test the modeled whole gait, reconstruct anatomically meaningful hip, knee and ankle angles using the model joint centers and the paper's defined geometric convention, and align cycles to the stated rear-toe touchdown and third-digit lift-off, not an assumed sole or phase oscillator. Log pelvis, trunk and head independently; joint drive target angles alone are not measured anatomical angles. Quantify stance and recovery duration, double support, toe-contact sequence, foot-relative displacement, and force timing, including incomplete cycles and confidence in contact grouping. Compare like surfaces and speed where possible; current simulated walking speed is well below the studied magpie walking range, so differences cannot directly establish an anatomical error. No neck stretch or passive tendon function is established by this study for this model.
