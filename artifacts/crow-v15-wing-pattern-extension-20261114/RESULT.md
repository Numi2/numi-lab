# Extended launch wing pattern: failed

The preregistered matched diagnostic did not sustain flight through interval 800. Every first episode reported physical failure: lanes 0–7 ended at intervals 579, 761, 765, 573, 587, 705, 765 and 573. The unchanged normal-handoff comparator ended at 607, 691, 708, 777, 623, 669, 708 and 771 respectively. Lane trajectories are correlated developmental comparisons, not independent estimates. Both runs used seed 20261126 and the same environment and model; none of the reserved layout/seed trials was used.

The candidate exactly matched the comparator's recorded states through interval 500 and logged targets over intervals 335–500 in all lanes. Logged state, target and contact/toe substep coverage through the metadata endpoint was complete, state and targets finite, and no observer errors were recorded. The two .70-rad comparison lanes recontacted at 396 and 389. None of the six .75–.85-rad candidates showed foot recontact before its reported endpoint; lanes 1 and 5 did record nonfoot impulses at interval 703, while the other four had no positive nonfoot impulse in the observed substeps. Four candidate episodes terminated at root altitudes around 1.08 m or 2.49 m, with no observed foot recontact before termination. Contact observations and termination metadata epochs have not been independently aligned, so these data do not establish a fatal collider pair or exclude a noncontact state-limit failure. All lanes fail the continuity screen regardless.

The intervention changes wing targets after the original 10.02-second cutoff and consequently changes the trajectory. It refutes only the simple hypothesis that continuing this open-loop wing pattern through 800 intervals is sufficient; it does not prove why the original handoff failed, nor does it establish stable flight, toe grip, landing or navigation.
