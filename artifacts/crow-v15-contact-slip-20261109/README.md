# Contact-point timing and task-visible body velocities

This is an experimental research record, not a completed real-crow model or an enabled production controller. The working v15 flight source and production host code remain unchanged. See the adjoining ground-contact investigation for the preceding preregistered baselines and experiments.

## Trace alignment and walking

The contact observer labels its physical state with step N while the replay labels the same state N-1. We established this by matching task-time generalized root velocity and then checking every used q and v sample. Older contact-point and foot-center speed summaries that pair equal step labels are invalid and are retained only as provenance. `aligned-analysis.json` recomputes every initial gait and correction candidate from the matching physical states.

For the original contact-gated candidate, during the three-second gait window, the selected lane advanced 0.0534 m, with both left-only and right-only positive-load samples. Contact-point tangential speed at positive upward endpoint impulses exceeding 0.0001 N s had 95th percentiles 0.0181 and 0.0217 m/s for the two feet. Including all positive impulses, including very lightly loaded contacts, gives 0.0669 and 0.1149 m/s. The distinction matters: the feet can still slide during light touchdown or release. Values are sampled estimates, not continuous-time slip or integrated work. The three alternate period/amplitude candidates fail the preregistered stronger-load screen. None of these results qualifies realistic walking in varied terrain.

## Missing moving-link velocities

A fixed-action probe and a fixed-signal probe demonstrated that the stance branch and task-state feedback lane can affect the body. Nevertheless, three velocity feedback gains produced bit-identical physical trajectories. The on-device diagnostic, validated against known constants and aligned replay, showed the compiled left-foot reference was body 10 as expected, but its task-completion buffer velocity was zero while the same accepted replay foot moved; the generalized root velocity matched. The contact projection intentionally initializes articulated body entries with position and orientation and zero velocity. This is a task-visible kinematics limitation; it is not evidence that the contact solver itself ignored articulated velocity.

`materialized-velocity-host.patch` tests an isolated host library that runs the existing articulated-velocity kernel into the task-time body buffer immediately before task completion. Its reported foot x velocities agreed with aligned replay within 4.5e-8 m/s in 150 sampled endpoints. The no-velocity-feedback matched control retained identical selected-lane q, v, body states and actor observations over 350 frames, but rewards differed in 266 frames, so replacing production behavior requires explicit versioning and broader learning/regression checks. The experimental velocity-feedback gait was physically different and passed only the narrow stronger-load screen; it is not frozen.

## Continuous route and landing

Using the isolated materializing library and corrected walking-window handoff, two lanes on each of the two already development-exposed layouts completed five waypoints and had positive upward, foot-only low-speed support on each of the final 25 correctly aligned frames. No first-episode physical failure or failed simulator step was reported. A second run with the late-contact observer reproduced the selected-lane physical states exactly. The minimum obstacle clearance sampled at accepted endpoints was 0.1456 m on layout I and 0.0740 m on layout J across these lanes; this is not continuous clearance and excludes the perch. The first materialized-route recording lacked late contact telemetry, so it cannot independently prove landing. The body has rigid feet and the perch is a flat box: support is not toe grip.

## Push-off and unresolved work

Three leg-only extensions and the deeper crouch experiment are retained with matching wings-suppressed and wings-normal controls. The deeper crouch generated a peak upward body speed of 0.221 m/s with wings suppressed, but both feet never simultaneously cleared for the two sampled endpoints required by the preregistered gate. With wings active it altered the takeoff profile, but its contribution is not a qualified sustained push-off. Genuine contact-driven grip needs separately modeled, physically articulated toe contacts and controlled tests of grasp, release and resistance to perturbations. The user-facing task remains incomplete until those and a reliable leg-powered launch are demonstrated, and the walking control is validated across physically varied starts and surfaces.

The plans, generated diffs, matched controls, native outputs, read-only telemetry, failures, build bindings and compressed replays are retained here. `SHA256SUMS` verifies the archive. Scripts identify the original macmini paths and require the matching native build and previously archived actor packs. A readout experiment initially failed to compile because its isolated source lacked an include path; its original log and successful retry are both retained.
