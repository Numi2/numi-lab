# Matched second-development-seed replication

At used seed 20261126, eight same-start control and candidate pairs matched exactly for 200 pre-gait state endpoints and 3200 contact substeps each. The control had 26 both-feet-unloaded substeps in each 4–6-second window; the contact-conditioned -1 pitch / 0.05 pitch-rate candidate had zero in every lane. Its loaded contact-speed integrals were 1.033–1.043 times the matched control for the left foot and 0.997–1.003 for the right; these changing-contact, impulse-weighted integrals are proxies, not tracked material-point slip. No observed toe-speed cap or sampled nonfoot contact occurred in the walking window. All candidate first episodes survived 600 intervals, had no toe-speed caps after 7.4 seconds and were foot-unloaded throughout 8–12 seconds. Upward normal foot impulse in the six-interval launch window was 0.432–0.441 N s; it includes passive and aerodynamic effects and is not proof of leg-only work. Body and wing substep speed limits were not observed by this host.

The initial state was not identical to the first used seed: at the first recorded endpoint, matched-index root positions differed by 0.0077–0.0745 m and the largest joint-coordinate difference by 0.0072–0.2000 rad. By step 200 the largest joint-coordinate difference was 0.00033–0.00548 rad, showing substantial posture convergence despite root position differences. These are not independent birds or unused biological trials. The candidate's late-stance ankle angle median changed only +0.00347–0.00386 degrees, effectively flat; the result therefore cannot support a realistic ankle-motion claim. The current two-second walking window, contact proxy and used flight/route remain insufficient for sustainable realistic gait, flight qualification, supported landing or the requested two-layout sequence.

## Toe-defined reanalysis

The earlier near-zero pooled ankle median included eight hallux-only contact bouts. When bounded instead by hallux touchdown and front-third-toe final contact, the 40 assessable bouts show the same side asymmetry as seed 20261127: pooled median late-stance change +0.460 degrees, with 24 positive and 16 negative bouts; per-foot inspection is necessary. The rear toe contacted before any front toe in all 40 assessable bouts, but front digit 3 was never uniquely last to release, only tied for last. Eight hallux-only bouts could not be assessed for those toe-defined comparisons. These starts converge to closely similar postures and are not independent biological data. As before, angle estimates and toe-contact ordering use a model, impulse thresholds and test conditions different from the related-corvid source.
