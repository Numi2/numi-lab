# Improved-stance return placement ablation: failed

Both lanes requested return after the existing 40-clear-interval counter. The original placement lane and the lane with the additional placement progression disabled fell at one-based intervals 360 and 362 respectively. Neither produced any interval satisfying the simultaneous bilateral upward support, opposing toe forces, posture and speed checks; neither met the 100-interval grip or release gate. Their longest strict release streaks were 41 and 42 intervals. The no-placement lane reached the accepted-state toe-speed threshold on both feet before falling; accepted samples do not bound substep speeds or motor effort.

The geometry audit found no positive front-toe normal impulse in its logged post-return observations for either lane. At zero-based tick 310 the nearest front-toe box gap remained about 7.7–8.0 mm while a rear toe was in contact. This suggests an approach and toe-placement problem; the static box gaps alone do not prove why the controller fails or establish a correction. The geometry script labels lane 1 as `placement`, but this experiment's lane 1 actually disables the extra placement increment.

Both lanes exactly matched the archived improved release q/v and logged controls before return, and matched each other until the first logged target and state difference at tick 282. All 32 contact substeps were present; there were no recorded observer errors or nonfoot impulses, and the runtime binary hash matches the prebuilt hash. Contact/state epochs are not independently synchronized here. The constructed stationary-perch test is not contact-driven walking, flight, or an airborne landing. Full inputs, traces and failure are retained.
