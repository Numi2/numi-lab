# Modeled stance trends differ from the magpie reference

Across 48 complete loaded-stance windows from eight closely related v15 development starts, the modeled hip forward angle decreased in all windows (median -11.65 degrees), consistent with a rearward thigh motion in this chosen torso-relative proxy. Knee interior angle increased in all windows (median +3.26 degrees), while ankle interior angle increased from early to middle stance (median +1.54 degrees) then decreased from middle to late stance (median -1.18 degrees). Those trends contrast with the black-billed magpie report of knee flexion and ankle flexion then extension during stance. See REFERENCE.md in the corvid-body-motion reference artifact for the paper and its scope.

This is a qualitative diagnostic, not a direct biological validation or refutation: the simulated American crow moves much slower, the stance detector and angle definitions differ, the articulated ankle/foot morphology is estimated, and the starts and successive windows are not independent animal subjects. The foot-axis geometry, event selection, phase averages, joint coordinates and rejected windows are retained. The result motivates a bounded physically driven phase experiment; it does not justify forcing magpie numerical trajectories onto the crow.
