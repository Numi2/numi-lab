# Small support stroke: failed development screen

A matched eight-lane experiment on previously used development seed 20261127 compared the existing held-upper-body walking controller with a quarter-strength extra supporting-leg stroke. Both arms ran 300 steps with 16 substeps, no scheduled reset, and the extra toe command disabled. The arms matched at the end of the pre-walk prefix and completed their first episodes.

The candidate traveled 2.20–2.84 times as far as the matched control, but this did not constitute acceptable walking. Sole-clear/toe-low endpoint counts rose from 49 to 686; each lane had only one qualifying left-foot clearance bout and zero right-foot bouts, below the prespecified three per foot. Zero-normal-support substep counts rose in six lanes, and one candidate lane hit a toe velocity cap. Contact-point speed median increased from 0.00276 to 0.00369 m/s and the 95th percentile from 0.03484 to 0.05251 m/s, slightly over the prespecified 1.5x limit.

The candidate fails the physical gates and is not promoted. Contact-point velocities and toe clearances are sampled at endpoints; they are not integrated slip or a direct measure of biological realism. A zero-normal-support threshold is not by itself proof of a fall. These are development results on previously used lanes, not an independent qualification of walking, takeoff, flight, or landing.
