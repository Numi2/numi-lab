# Twelve-second post-takeoff body-moment ablation

On the previously used training layout and seed 20261127, eight no-scheduled-reset, teacher-off first episodes using the new release threshold completed 12 s both with the existing control and with the two identified direct body yaw/pitch moment actions zeroed at their final application point after 7.10 s. All 2,840 sampled endpoints and 45,440 contact substeps through interval 355 matched exactly; the first endpoint difference was 357 in every lane. Thus the already observed push and unloading were preserved in this matched ablation.

Neither arm reported termination, contact overflow or trace error. From 7.4–12 s no toe-speed-cap substeps were recorded, and from 8–12 s no foot-loaded substeps were recorded. On this short trajectory, the existing control's sampled post-8-s root heights ranged 0.750–1.218 m; with the two moments removed they ranged 0.752–1.269 m. At 12 s the control had reached waypoint criterion one in seven lanes and two in one lane, while the ablated arm had reached criterion two in all eight. These are waypoint flags, not route completion or supported landing. No endpoint-sampled nonfoot contacts were observed; this is not a continuous clearance or fatal-collider audit.

The ablation does not remove every modeled aerodynamic closure, validate the wind or feathers, or prove the maneuver is driven solely by biologically plausible wing loads. The launch period before 7.10 s still has the original actuation and the test ends before the more demanding route and landing. No independent layouts, unseen seeds, controlled turns, climb/descent, recovery, or full autonomous mission were tested here. This is encouraging evidence for a more physical controller, not qualification or a frozen controller.
