# Equal-target interpolation bypass rejected

Neither variant is an isolated, acceptable comparison. All eight lanes differed in pre-walk q/v/action samples and contact substeps against the retained sham, beginning as early as the first interval, despite skipping interpolation when old and new targets were equal. The equal-value mixing hypothesis therefore does not explain the observed mismatch. The zero-lead candidate also traveled only 54–69% of its lane-matched reference. It reduced the boundary joint second-difference proxies to 5–14% of reference and had 26 versus 30 both-feet-unloaded substeps, but those differences are not causally attributable with the mismatched prefix.

The 5 ms lead candidate traveled 92.6–103.3% of reference and had zero versus 30 unloaded substeps; its whole-substep knee/ankle second-difference p95 was 8.3–25.1% of reference. These numbers do not overcome failed pre-walk parity. A same-binary matched switch experiment is prepared to isolate smoothing. All results are one used seed and two seconds of walking; finite differences are neither torque nor biological gait measurements. This research-only target history is not production-safe for reset, rollback or arbitrary chunking.
