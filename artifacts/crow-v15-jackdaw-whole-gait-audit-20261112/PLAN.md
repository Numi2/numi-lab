# Jackdaw-informed whole-gait audit

Stößel and Fischer, *Comparative intralimb coordination in avian bipedal locomotion*, J Exp Biol 215, 4055–4069 (2012), DOI 10.1242/jeb.070458, studied jackdaws and two other birds using biplanar high-speed X-rays. Their abstract reports shared fore-aft limb coordination but distinctive mediolateral motions and pelvic rotation, including greater hip mobility in jackdaws. This related-corvid observation motivates measurement of v15 hip-roll and trunk orientation during accepted physical gait; it does not provide numerical American crow calibration or justify inventing an angle target. The separately cited magpie paper concerns stance timing in a different corvid.

Analyze retained sustained-walk first episodes, two development seeds and eight closely related lanes each, 4–10 s. Join the endpoint root/hip states to the preceding 16 contact substeps. Classify foot loading using 5% of the model body-weight impulse per 20 ms, and compare left-only, right-only and double support. Report actual hip roll and trunk roll/pitch/yaw distributions and temporal variation, with sample counts. These are descriptive, correlated exploratory data; endpoint trunk angles do not isolate pelvic joint motion or center-of-mass displacement, and the paper abstract supplies no numeric target. Do not call the gait biologically validated or force an arbitrary amplitude.
