# Teacher-free explicit-goal approach: development trial

On one used development layout and seed, all six first episodes that cleared five waypoints made toe-to-perch contact. Two had temporary bilateral opposed toe support for 66 and 169 consecutive control intervals, then failed; two others failed after contact without meeting that opposition criterion, and the two remaining birds survived to the horizon without sustained terminal support. The two other first episodes collided with slalom A before reaching the perch. None met the required last-25-interval bilateral support criterion, and no toe release occurred. Pre-fifth-waypoint accepted endpoint q, velocity, policy outputs and contacts matched the previous student in all eight lanes, with complete logged substeps and no capacity overflow in the landing audit. This is a useful alignment improvement, not a qualified landing, biological grip or unused-layout result. Final guidance is bounded feedback from the explicitly supplied goal and body motion, rather than a learned terminal policy.
