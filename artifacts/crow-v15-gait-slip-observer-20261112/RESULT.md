# Loaded contact-point motion in the development walk

The added contact-point observer reproduced all 2,400 first-episode q/v/action/contact endpoint samples and all 38,400 contact-substep summaries exactly after removing only the added point field. Independent native forward kinematics matched 100 replay frames for all 18 foot/toe bodies: maximum position error 4.11e-9 m, linear velocity error 7.15e-9 m/s and angular velocity error 2.19e-7 rad/s. The ground was stationary in the checked replay.

Across 7,720 accepted loaded, near-vertical ground-contact endpoint samples over the two-second walk and eight used-seed lanes, side-specific median tangential contact-point speeds were 0.0018–0.0036 m/s and side-specific p95 values 0.0297–0.0415 m/s. Depending on lane and side, 18.1–32.3% of samples exceeded the descriptive 0.01 m/s threshold. These are correlated endpoint samples, including contact transitions, rather than integrated slip distances or independent animal observations. They show nonzero contact-point motion in the simulation but do not establish whether friction, control, impact dynamics or numerical effects caused it, nor whether any value is biologically wrong. No friction change follows without a matched experiment.

The initial analysis failed because the log also includes interval 301 beyond the 300-interval first episode; its failed traceback is retained in analysis.log. The corrected analysis filters both reference and observer to intervals 1–300 before requiring exact coverage and parity. This is a development gait diagnostic, not qualification of sustained contact-driven walking.
