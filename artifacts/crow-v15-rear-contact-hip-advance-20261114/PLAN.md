# Rear-contact-triggered returning hip advance

The improved-stance return trial produced rear-toe contact without opposing foretoe contact. Its fixed-state geometry predicts that a small positive hip change reduces the front gap, but also risks foot collision and lifting the rear toe. Test this tradeoff with two physically simulated v15 lanes from the same initial conditions. Both use the preceding original-placement controller; only lane 1, once its returning rear toe reports nonzero contact after return, increments the returning hip target by at most 0.002 rad per control interval while that contact is present, up to 0.05 rad. The increment remains held when contact is absent. No force, contact, joint limit or motor limit is changed.

Require the control to reproduce the entire previous original-placement lane exactly, candidate parity up to its first hip intervention, 450 intervals of complete 32-substep contacts, no observer/native errors, no nonfoot support, bounded joint positions, and no accepted toe-speed threshold before fall. Independently require 100 consecutive strict released intervals and 100 bilateral opposing toe-grip, posture, support and speed intervals. The existing early return counter deliberately makes this a regrip screen and cannot qualify sustained release. Report front and rear contacts, geometry, timing and all failures. Contact/state epochs are not independently synchronized. This is a stationary constructed-perch test, not walking or airborne landing.
