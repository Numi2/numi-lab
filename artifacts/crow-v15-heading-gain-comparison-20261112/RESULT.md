# Paired heading-feedback gain comparison

The 2.80-gain trial matched the prior 1.40-gain trial in every recorded state and ground-contact sample through trace submission 602; lanes 2–7 first differed at 603, while controls 0–1 remained identical through submission 1051. All eight experimental episodes reached 1050 transitions without reported contact termination, recorded nonfoot ground impulse or recorded joint-speed cap after trace submission 600. The comparison uses identical per-lane starts, targets, commands, body and research aerodynamics; obstacles were disabled.

At submission 850 during the heading pulse, actual candidate headings for the +/−0.15, +/−0.30, +/−0.45 rad targets were approximately +0.126/−0.160, +0.241/−0.278 and +0.261/−0.318 rad. For the preceding gain they were +0.090/−0.146, +0.199/−0.249 and +0.258/−0.308. The larger gain reduced the magnitude of target error in five of six lanes at that selected sample but slightly worsened the negative 0.15-rad lane. At submission 1050, three seconds after the target returned to zero, candidate absolute headings were approximately 0.001, 0.039, 0.050, 0.113, 0.082 and 0.185 rad; the previous gain yielded 0.009, 0.079, 0.060, 0.125, 0.099 and 0.171 rad. Thus five recoveries improved at this sample and the negative 0.45-rad case worsened. Neither gain establishes robust heading tracking or timely recovery.

The same fixed +/-0.30 command clamp limits the possible effect of the increased gain. One development seed and correlated lanes cannot support a freeze, and the model retains different per-lane altitude feedback and empirical unsteady force settings. No flight disturbance, biological validation, clearance, autonomous navigation or supported landing was established. `analysis.json` preserves the control and candidate trajectories at all sampled times. Logs are retained as verified byte-concatenable gzip parts; restore before running the analyzer. The trace index includes the separate warmup submission before reset.
