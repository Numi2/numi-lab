# Isolated reached-grip hip response

All five matched 0.4 s simulations completed without a recorded nonfoot impulse or solver failure. Relative to each arm's first accepted sample, hip target deltas of -0.06, -0.03, 0, +0.03 and +0.06 rad produced pitch increases of 0.0271, 0.0216, 0.0160, 0.0084 and 0.0038 rad, respectively. Root x changed by -5.16, -1.91, +2.82, +5.70 and +9.69 mm. The opposing-toe-plus-upward-support criterion was met at 70, 75, 73, 51 and 19 of 80 five-millisecond samples. These outcomes show a tradeoff rather than a single chosen hip target: positive offsets reduced the initial pitch increase but reduced qualified toe-contact samples, while negative offsets moved the body back but increased the initial pitch increase.

The runs begin from a physically reached first-episode grip state and exact perch pose, but use a fresh contact cache and a single-perch scene; all position drives are held at the reached q except for the tested hip offsets. This does not reproduce the full route controller, establish longer-term stability or prescribe a safe gain. A separate coordinated hip/ankle test is warranted to see whether foot orientation can be preserved while shifting the body.
