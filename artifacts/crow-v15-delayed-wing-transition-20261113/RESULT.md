# Result: delayed flapping exposed a push, but four lanes recontacted

Both 400-step runs passed the predeclared integrity checks, including exact recorded paired state/action/contact parity and parity with the prior observer through step 345, and complete 335–400 contact, work, toe and drive-target records. No substep errors or overflow were reported. In comparable lanes 2–7, the push arm increased upward ground impulse during steps 348–357 by +0.1351 to +0.3011 N s relative to the active-crouch-with-flapping control. Model-FK center-of-mass vertical momentum increased +0.1283 to +0.2924 N s in the push arm during this interval, versus approximately zero in the hold arm.

All six lanes had three consecutive unloaded intervals by step 367 and then rose at least 0.542 m relative to the root at step 350, with no recorded nonfoot ground impulse in steps 348–400. However, lanes 2, 4, 5 and 6 recontacted the ground after their first three unloaded intervals. Their first recontact steps were 361, 366, 365 and 361 respectively; lanes 3 and 7 had no such recontact through 400. Lane 6 also had five substeps with toe speed >=11.99 rad/s. Thus the predeclared no-recontact condition failed in four of six lanes; this is not a qualified transition and no controller is promoted.

The early difference supports a leg-command-associated ground impulse in this model, not isolated muscle force: flapping suppression is not wing immobilization, passive aerodynamic and prior direct-moment effects remain, and the active crouch control has powered joints. The onset delay is an engineering setting, not a measured biological timing. Six correlated lanes on one used seed are not independent subjects, and the experiment does not qualify realistic takeoff, disturbance recovery, obstacle navigation, grip or landing. Keep the two full traces and all inputs for follow-up.
