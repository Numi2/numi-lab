# Open-loop longitudinal actuator screen

All 48 pre-intervention state, velocity and policy-output samples exactly matched their corresponding retained control. Every first-episode contact substep was recorded without reported observer errors or overflow.

At the first shared post-intervention sample, equal-sign ±0.20 normalized residuals to the two wing-sweep channels changed body pitch by +0.047 and -0.050 rad versus the respective same-lane controls, with corresponding actual sweep-coordinate changes. Both sustained sweep trials ultimately failed for excessive tilt, at intervals 1346 and 1130. The former had seven recorded nonfoot ground-contact substeps after intervention; the latter had none. A ground collision is not inferred for the second failure.

The tail and pronation residual lanes survived this screen, and their first-sample pitch differences were around 0.002–0.003 rad. Their actual joint response and every later paired sample are retained; these results do not prove zero authority across other conditions. The long-run comparisons mix multiple evolving aerodynamic and dynamic effects. The sweep result motivates a smaller bounded closed-loop pitch test with proprioceptive feedback, not adoption of a constant sweep offset.

This is a single used-seed development screen on the experimental wing model, with direct body yaw and pitch moments still present before 9 seconds. It does not establish real-bird aerodynamics, successful turns, stable flight or autonomous navigation.
