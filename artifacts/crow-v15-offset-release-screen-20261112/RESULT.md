# Release and regrip from two supported initial states

Each of the eight lanes exactly matched its corresponding no-release baseline up to the shift trigger, for 87 or 95 complete state intervals and all their contact substeps. All 550 intervals and 32 contact and toe-velocity substeps per interval were recorded, with no reported observer error or overflow. The geometry calculation agreed with the controller's clearance sensor within the predeclared tolerance on all recorded intervals.

The -0.28 rad toe-extension pair passed the predeclared engineering screen for both +0.04 and -0.04 rad initial pitch. The left foot achieved at least 1 mm modeled sole-and-toe clearance without contact for at least ten consecutive intervals while the right foot met the specified upward-support and opposed-toe criteria; the controller then requested return, and the final 100 intervals met bilateral opposed-toe support, with no recorded toe-speed cap, nonfoot impact, or fall. The two starts achieved minimum-box-clearance peaks of 3.21 mm and 8.08 mm. The peak is not the same as continuous clearance throughout the episode.

For the positive-pitch start, all four extension settings passed but share the same outcome before the extension setting takes effect, so they are not four independent successes. For the negative-pitch start, no added extension failed to complete clearance, and -0.10/-0.20 rad reached return and final regrip but hit the toe-speed cap 11/9 substeps; these are rejected under the declared rule. Only -0.28 passed both starts. Contact and speed are substep observations; geometry and posture are endpoint samples, so continuous geometric clearance between samples is not established.

These are two constructed, near-rest starts at a development perch and no flight approach. This does not establish performance across other starts, autonomous landing, actual bird forces, or success on either held-out layout. A further perturbation screen is required before considering the controller for integration.
