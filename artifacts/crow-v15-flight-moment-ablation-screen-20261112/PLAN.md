# Obstacle-free direct-moment command ablation

The journey controller uses direct body-wrench yaw and pitch channels as well as aerodynamic wing and tail forces. Determine how much the retained flight behavior depends on those control channels, without assuming that the model or wing forces are biologically calibrated.

Repeat the retained toe-body obstacle-free development turn screen on the same seed, 12 lanes, body, environment, route commands, physics and existing flight law. For lanes 0–5, request zero on the direct yaw-moment channel after 9 seconds; for lanes 6–11, request zero on the direct pitch-moment channel after 9 seconds. Each target mode occurs once in each group, and the corresponding original lane is the matched reference. Keep the other direct moment and the aerodynamic actuators active. Run 1600 intervals, with no resets and the same 16-substep observer. Existing actuator response filtering may leave a decaying prior output after the command change; this is a command ablation, not an instantaneous proof of zero applied moment. No claim about the applied physical wrench is made without separately measuring it.

Compare all available logged q/v/policy samples through the first 450 control steps, contact coverage and capacity, first failures, post-400 ground impulse and toe speed caps, and sampled yaw, roll, pitch, altitude and velocity against each matched prior lane. Check the intervention boundaries and preserve all failures. One lane per mode and intervention gives exploratory identification, not reliable qualification, a disentangled causal model, trained flight or bird-species calibration. A surviving lane cannot qualify a physically self-sufficient controller; a failure does not by itself distinguish numerical instability from genuine loss of control.
