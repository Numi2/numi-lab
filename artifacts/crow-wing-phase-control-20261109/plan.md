# Development study: phase-dependent wing feathering and yaw authority

Question: Can bounded phase-dependent differential feathering increase the current v15 body's right-turn authority without destroying speed, height or lateral stability? Earlier equal-sign constant pronation offsets produced limited yaw improvement or speed problems; the existing controller changes pronation sinusoidally but has not tested differential phase-dependent input. Mechanism: each wing's resolved pronation changes blade-element forces during the wingbeat. The physical wing geometry, aerodynamics, actuator limits, solver and v15 damping remain unchanged.

Use the previous roll-1.5 accepted-state stabilizer, +0.55/-0.55 opposing pronation speed trim, 0.70 m/s and 0.85 m straight reference, with -1 yaw action from 6 s. On top of the baseline pronation commands, add the same normalized offset to actions 4 and 5, clamped to [-1,1] before the existing actuator response. Nine groups: zero; +/-0.30 and +/-0.60 times sin(wing phase); +/-0.30 and +/-0.60 times cos(wing phase). 72 environments, group=lane mod 9 (8 native starts per group), development seed 20261111, 1600 control steps, four physics substeps, obstacle collisions disabled, sampled accepted state every 2 seconds. Lane is the unit. Group starts are not paired, and this is exploratory.

Prespecified candidate screen: all 8 first episodes in a group survive to 32 s; median across lanes of mean wrapped yaw rate over 20–30 s <= -0.03 rad/s; every lane's sampled 20–30 s maximum absolute yaw-frame lateral speed <=0.20 m/s, speed error relative to 0.70 m/s <=0.25 m/s, and root height error relative to 0.85 m <=0.20 m. All required samples and zero failed simulator steps are necessary. Record every group's failures, individual metrics, raw samples, exact shader and policy identities. Do not relabel failed groups as passing. The screen does not qualify flight: a candidate must subsequently pass symmetric turns, climb/descent, recovery with deliberately perturbed starts, and independent conditions. Stop after this fixed 72-lane study and preserve all results. No final navigation layouts or seeds are used.
