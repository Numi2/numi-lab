# Related-corvid stance evidence and v15 observation

Verstappen, Aerts and Van Damme, [Terrestrial locomotion in the black-billed magpie: kinematic analysis of walking, running and out-of-phase hopping](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.203.14.2159), J Exp Biol 203, 2159-2170 (2000), used high-speed video in trained magpies, not American crows. Its discussion describes toe-ground contact with the tarsometatarsus above the surface during stance; the article reports results at substantially higher walking speeds than the present v15 short tests. It provides a comparative constraint, not a measured American-crow target, and surface-marker angle definitions and speed range must be respected when comparing joint motion. A related paper, [Meilak et al.](https://doi.org/10.3389/fbioe.2021.676894), uses a 3D model of a magpie hindlimb to examine hip moment-generating capacity during a takeoff range; those model estimates are not direct measurements of crow muscle forces.

Post-hoc analysis of 16-substep accepted contact records from both paired development screens gives the fraction of total foot-plus-toe ground-contact normal impulse carried by bodies named as toes; the remainder is assigned to bodies named foot. This is a simulated impulse proxy, not anatomy or measured biological pressure. We have not established whether the v15 foot collider represents the tarsometatarsus, a toe pad, or a composite structure; therefore the non-toe fraction cannot yet be labeled anatomically invalid. Check the actual source geometry and physical joints before redesigning contact. The walking runs were only two seconds, on already-used seeds, and are not qualified gait. Source scripts, complete per-lane numbers and links to retained traces are provided here.

Backus et al., [Mechanical analysis of avian feet](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.140350), included Corvidae among grouped taxa and uses simplified comparative models and available force proxies; its supplementary table was not accessible from the current host. Do not turn its mixed-taxa aggregate into a crow-specific actuator value.

Source inspection: `src/core/RunProgram.cpp` constructs the original estimated dove hybrid with a `foot/sole` box attached by an ankle pitch joint; the crow hybrid inherits and renames that topology. The generated v15 host in `build/crow-v15-hip-roll-journey-integration-20261111/c_api.cpp` narrows the sole box and attaches the two-segment toe chains to that foot body. This establishes the modeled connectivity and inherited proxy, but neither source assigns an unambiguous biological bone or pad identity to that box. The foot collider accounts for roughly 52–56% of summed normal impulse in these short runs, making anatomical and ground-contact calibration a substantive open issue, not a validated correction.
