# Experimental v15 articulated-toe checkpoint

This is a research prototype and is not enabled in the production crow body or flight controller. The user-requested walking, leg-powered takeoff, landing grip and full-sequence video remain incomplete. The existing flight-controller source is unchanged.

The first body admission failed because the older dove indices identified a wing as a foot. The revised implementation resolves v15 feet and soles by name. Eight independently hinged toe links add collision shapes and partition mass and neutral inertia from the feet. Geometry 2 removed initial rear-toe/shank interpenetration; geometry 3 aligned the toe soles to the v15 sole. These are estimated engineering parameters, not measured crow anatomy.

The initial toe body passed free-space mass/invariant and 12-lane GPU/FP64 single-step tests. It nevertheless became unstable on a longer fixed-position support test with toe collisions disabled: the 50-step test rolled back at control step 5, while the original body completed the same control. Reducing the toe drive gains tenfold did not fix that. Independently run prefixes showed the original candidate reached the 12 rad/s toe speed limit by 40 ms. A different candidate increased the toe drive armature to 5e-5 kg m^2. Both collision-on and collision-off fixed-pose runs then completed 50 x 20 ms periods; the final endpoint of the collision-on run had 32 positive toe/ground rows. This larger generalized inertia is an unmeasured modeling change, not a biological calibration. The revised candidate passed 36 neutral pose/twist invariant comparisons and 12 native Metal free-space lanes against FP64 with maximum scaled acceleration error 3.61e-5; this is not multi-step or contact oracle validation.

The ground flexion probe held all toes fixed versus commanded 0.35 rad during steps 10-29, then zero. At step 29 the front toes reached approximately 0.186-0.188 rad; at step 49 they were approximately 0.011-0.012 rad. The rear toes moved oppositely under ground constraints and remained around -0.051 rad at step 49. Both one-second simulations completed; this is neither verified toe release from a perch nor contact-driven landing control. The first recording interpretation used the wrong frame stride and is invalid: the native ABI includes 13 scene-body values per frame. The corrected trace uses the dispatch stride and each arm's final q exactly matches finalQ. Original bad output remains in flex2.out.

The bounded static-box perch experiments start with the feet already at perch height; no approach or landing occurred. Fixed toes and 0.35 rad flexion both completed one second, but the flexed endpoint did not establish opposing loaded horizontal contact normals. At a 0.85 rad flexion target the endpoint had no positive toe-perch impulses. Neither run demonstrates grasp. Labels inherited from the flex/release probe say `flex_release` even though these perch variants hold the commanded flexion through the final sample. The inherited scene body's name `locomotion_ground` denotes the deliberately substituted box in these tests, not an infinite ground plane.

All source probes and logs, including compile/solver failures and invalid trace interpretation, are retained. Preregistered steps and limits appear in plan.md, contact-plan.md, flex-plan.md and perch-plan.md. The probes are isolated executables and do not implement an end-to-end autonomous task. Sustained gait on varied surfaces, contact-driven push-off, physically reached landing, active grasp/release with disturbance controls, qualified frozen-flight handoff, independent layouts, and a video of the full successful sequence all remain open.
