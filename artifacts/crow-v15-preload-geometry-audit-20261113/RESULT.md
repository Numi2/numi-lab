# Posthoc crouch clearance geometry

Reconstructed collision-box poses from accepted endpoint q/v were checked against the native lane-5 replay at five frames in each matched arm. Maximum position error was 7.3e-9 m and quaternion-component distance 3.0e-8. At tick 347, just before the recorded push, the left middle-front-toe group clearance was about 6.3, 5.0 and 3.6 mm for the +0.05, +0.10 and +0.15 normalized crouch-ankle offsets respectively; corresponding rear-toe clearances were near zero, and sole clearances approximately 4.6, 3.7 and 2.7 mm. Both matched arms had identical values before the push. The right-side and other sample data are retained in analysis.json.

The static direction prediction was qualitatively consistent with this sample, but the previous offsets did not close the gap, explaining why they did not establish front-toe ground contact in the hold arm. This endpoint reconstruction uses the prior source model's box dimensions and a ground plane at z=0; timing can be staggered relative to substep contact samples. It is not an exact solver separation or a direct observation of real crow feet. Extrapolating to a larger offset is an engineering hypothesis requiring a new matched dynamics test, not a predicted biological angle or proof of success.
