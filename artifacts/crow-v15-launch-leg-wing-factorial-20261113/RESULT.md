# Matched leg-command and flapping comparison

The proposed large leg-extension effect was not observed in this specific development screen. In the six comparable experimental lanes, with commanded flapping suppressed and the two identified direct body-moment actions disabled from 6.30 s, the extension-command arm increased combined upward ground impulse during control steps 348–365 by only 0.00842–0.00856 N s relative to the crouch-target control. The corresponding change in vertical root velocity at step 365 was -0.0050 to -0.0054 m/s. At step 400 the roots of both arms were at approximately 0.175–0.176 m. None of the six push-without-flapping lanes had two consecutive unloaded intervals in the observed launch window; all six crouch-without-flapping lanes had two unloaded intervals followed by recontact. This does not demonstrate a leg-powered takeoff.

With the prescribed early flapping enabled, all six experimental lanes in both the extension and crouch-target arms reached two consecutive unloaded control intervals and remained unloaded through the 8 s observation horizon; step-400 sampled root heights were 0.741–0.785 m with extension and 0.846–0.884 m with the crouch target. This is a closed-loop intervention, not a measurement of wing force or sustained flight. The crouch-target/no-extension arm still has active leg drives and passive contacts: it is not a leg-disabled animal. Two no-extension/wing-on lanes showed six toe-speed-cap substeps each during steps 348–400. No nonfoot ground impulse was recorded in that window. Lanes 0–1 did not use the prescribed early-flapping intervention and are retained as controls, not included in the six-lane interpretation.

The four native runs each retained 400 endpoint samples and 6,400 contact substeps per lane, with no reported observer errors or contact overflow. For every lane, recorded state/action/contact samples matched through step 345 for the matched extension comparison and through step 315 for the matched flapping comparison. The first recorded endpoint divergence in both extension contrasts was step 351. These checks do not prove identity of every hidden internal state. The prewritten velocity prediction did not define a single later endpoint; step 365 and the other prespecified sampled endpoints are therefore descriptive, not a confirmatory time-specific test. The 0.05 N s impulse comparison also fails the proposed effect threshold, but six highly correlated lanes on a used seed are not independent birds.

Per-foot impulses include gravity support, contact coupling and passive aerodynamic effects. Root vertical velocity is not center-of-mass velocity, and command suppression is not a measurement of joint work. Wings-off means flapping-position amplitude zero, not all wing joints immobilized or passive aerodynamics disabled. Empirical aerodynamic closures, earlier body-moment control, feedback and timing can affect both arms. The outcome contradicts a large extension effect in this specific setup; it does not contradict the separately retained earlier assisted wing-suppressed ballistic rise, whose controller and body-moment authority differ. The next physical question is whether the extension target actually produces positive joint work and an effective contact impulse when whole-body attitude and the launch latch are controlled; it requires actuator torque/work and force accounting before revising the launch controller.

No controller is promoted. This experiment uses a previously used layout and seed and does not qualify perturbation recovery, continuous flight, navigation, toe grip/release or supported landing.
