# Walking-to-takeoff integration: incomplete

The walking-only source had inconsistent gait and takeoff timing. Restoring the 6.3–7.4 s launch guard and ending the experimental gait at 6 s preserves the walking run exactly through all 2,400 sampled endpoints and 38,400 substeps on each of two previously used seeds. Both eight-lane runs completed 8 s with no reported physical failures, contact overflow, nonfoot ground impulse during 6–8 s or toe-speed-cap substeps during 6–8 s. These results do not establish a stable or realistic full sequence.

Both feet contributed upward ground impulse during control intervals 348–353: the combined impulse was 0.718–0.741 N s over 0.12 s, compared with 0.530 N s for the model's 0.45006 kg bodyweight over that time. This includes passive/contact and aerodynamic effects and cannot isolate leg-muscle work. Nine of 16 lanes were airborne without recorded foot load during 7.4–8 s; the other seven briefly rose and then loaded the ground again for 443–456 of 480 substeps, with root height returning to approximately 0.176 m. They are failed launches for the sustained-flight objective even though the episode monitor did not terminate them. The flight-enable rule includes a height threshold, upward velocity and sustained unloading; its relationship to this result is a hypothesis to test, not an established cause. Wing initiation relative to final toe-off has not been measured here, and the flight controller still contains artificial body moments.

The initial analysis incorrectly assumed the observer records ended at interval 400. The diagnostic includes one additional sampled interval; the failed assertion is preserved in analysis-first-coverage.log, and the reported analysis explicitly restricts intervals to the intended first 400. The two seeds/layouts have been used during development. Neither this result nor the preceding gait screen is a held-out autonomous-navigation or landing demonstration.
