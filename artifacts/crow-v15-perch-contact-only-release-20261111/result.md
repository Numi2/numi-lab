# Contact-and-phase return trigger, two difficult starts

The two constructed starts completed 550 control intervals without a recorded fall or non-foot perch impulse. Both had 34 consecutive qualifying 20 ms intervals with the left foot and toes at least 1 mm clear at endpoints, zero left-foot normal impulse across all 32 substeps, and opposing, supporting right-foot contact. Their maximum minimum clearances were 6.98 and 6.81 mm. Both regained 100 terminal consecutive bilateral opposing contact intervals, with mean upward impulse corresponding to 100.05% and 100.06% of nominal weight. Sampled toe speeds remained below 4.57 rad/s and the 12 rad/s limit. The observer matched named endpoint contact impulses.

Correction: the original analysis counted qualifying samples after return began and reported 38 intervals. The corrected window ends at the return command; the retained trace is unchanged.

The return decision uses time, left contact loss and right contact, while shape clearance is evaluated offline. Balance still uses exact root position in the known perch frame and root velocity; this is privileged developmental state, not a validated autonomous sensor. The body, added hip properties and stationary starting states are experimental and do not demonstrate a flight arrival, route, biological realism or held-out layout. Velocities are sampled at substep ends, and geometry at control endpoints.
