# Shank and reattached-toe collision geometry

Native-FK endpoint box separating-axis checks quantified the collision shapes in the changed body. In the original reattachment run, the same-side shank and rear-proximal toe boxes geometrically overlapped in 1,595 of 1,600 side/endpoints during the first 100 intervals, up to approximately 0.99 mm; solver response changes the realized overlap. With the two proximal-pair exclusions in the diagnostic run, those same boxes overlapped in 1,600/1,600 endpoint samples, up to 2.14 mm during the first 100 intervals. Contact margin can also produce accepted contacts at positive box separation. These are simulated collision envelopes, not measurements of crow tissue or direct proof of the anatomical overlap.

On the complete 300-step diagnostic trajectory, a counterfactual separating-axis screen compared each of eight toe segment boxes per side with the shank box at 4,800 side/endpoints for each segment. The original shank proxy overlapped the rear proximal toe at all 4,800 samples and the distal rear toe at 3,381; the minimum proximal separation was -3.77 mm. Without rerunning dynamics, 4 mm removed from the lower end of the box removed geometric overlap at the recorded endpoints but left 472 proximal samples within 1 mm. A 6 mm lower-end reduction left at least 1.91 mm proximal separation and 5.72 mm distal separation on those fixed states. All front-toe segment pairs were at least 16.8 mm separated in this particular recording. The top of the box is held fixed in this counterfactual.

These are candidate-shape screens based on a trajectory that itself used two diagnostic collision exclusions. They cannot establish what the modified physical dynamics will do, exclude unrecorded substep collisions, or validate leg, foot, flight, perch or other poses. The shank dimensions and the proposed 6 mm trim are engineering proxies, not American-crow measurements. Avian foot anatomy literature describes a hallux attached through the first metatarsal near the distal tarsometatarsus, but location and shape vary by species; it does not justify fabricating a precise crow dimension.
