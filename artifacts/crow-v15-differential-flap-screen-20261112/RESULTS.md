# Differential flap steering identification

The two unchanged controls and six experimental prefixes reproduced 5,100 state samples and 81,600 contact substeps. All eight first episodes remained airborne 24 s without nonfoot contacts or post-4-s joint speed-cap events; early toe caps in two lanes are recorded. Five obstacles had collision masks disabled, and direct body moments were disabled.

During 12–24 s, flap offsets +.10/-.10 gave world trajectory angles -.463/+.179 rad, with maximum sampled absolute roll .036/.034 rad. Offsets +.20/-.20 gave -.746/+.431 rad and .070/.068 rad roll. In contrast, zero-offset controls drifted -.121/-.130 rad while requesting a different speed, and opposite-sign lanes had different starting conditions and target heights. The +/- .30 inputs generated large sideways displacements of -25.87/+30.28 m and roll up to .491/.676 rad; they are not acceptable controlled flight.

Small differential flap offsets produce a directional response in the model and motivate a bounded closed-loop test. They do not establish left/right target tracking, recovery, realistic crow aerodynamics, frozen flight control, navigation or supported landing.
