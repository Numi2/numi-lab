# Transition factorial: crouch cutoff implicated

All eight runs exactly matched their own retained baseline for 300 accepted endpoints and 4,800 contact substeps before the six-second transition. All survived 500 steps with complete 4,800 measured-window contact and toe-velocity substeps, no nonfoot impulses, overflow or reported solver errors. Control lanes 0–1 exactly reproduced six toe-capped substeps and 65/79 both-feet-unloaded substeps after six seconds. Extending only the contact-feedback publisher in lanes 4–5 left those respective outcomes unchanged (six caps; 82/65 unloaded substeps). Extending the crouch command alone in lanes 2–3 removed all toe caps and both-unloaded substeps after six seconds, versus six caps and 65/111 unloaded substeps in the matching baselines. Extending both changes in lanes 6–7 likewise removed the caps and unloaded substeps (baseline six caps and 65 each). All four crouch-continuation lanes advanced about 0.261 m over the six-second interval.

This supports the hypothesis that the abrupt crouch removal caused this particular transition failure, rather than proving general gait stability. Two lanes per setting share one used seed and are closely related starts. The result does not measure true slip or unobserved body/wing speed caps and does not validate crow motion, takeoff, navigation or landing. The next controlled trial applies the crouch-only change uniformly and repeats on both development seeds.
