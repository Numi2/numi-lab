# Unloaded-gait interpolation bypass: rejected

The same-binary paired seed 20261127 experiment failed its declared gates. Pre-walk q/v/actions and contact summaries matched in all eight lanes. During walking, the candidate had 133 both-feet-unloaded substeps versus zero for the sham; lane 2 accounted for 118. Candidate travel in lanes 2 and 3 was 7.42 mm and 17.54 mm versus 25.70 mm and 29.17 mm in the sham. There were no reported candidate nonfoot impulses or toe-speed caps. Boundary knee and ankle second-difference means were 5.3–13.5% of sham, but that does not offset loss of support and travel. See candidate-comparison.json and candidate-posthoc-motion.json for all gates and limitations.

The candidate trace and replay gzip files are byte-identical (SHA-256 respectively 224f223fc28deea39ceecfa6fd2b9998508c0bad789f56b6b18daa45945c59c0 and e84905252a0d766e4700e2484b90c041cd12b45a3ee209e22ebc06dee7c7c658) to the prior smoothing candidate for the same seed. Thus this bypass made no observable difference in the retained trajectory; these data do not establish whether the condition fired when history and current targets already matched, or never fired. The support bit is accepted control-interval feedback, while unloaded events are measured at solver substeps, so this cannot guard the entire transition. No improvement, biological validity, or full-sequence qualification is established.

The first build invocation failed because required compiler output/input arguments were omitted; the retry used the correct inputs and succeeded. The first sham attempt consequently did not simulate. Both failed logs are retained. No production controller was changed.
