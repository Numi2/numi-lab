# Extended obstacle-free turns with the toe-equipped body

All twelve first episodes survived 3000 control intervals without recorded ground impulse or toe speed-cap samples after step 400; every expected contact substep was logged, with no overflow, observer error or failed native environment step. For each of the four signed 90-degree lanes, the heading was within 0.15 rad at three or more consecutive 100-step-spaced samples. The positive lanes first met that sampled bound at step 2300 and the negative lanes at step 2600; all four ended about 0.021 rad from their targets. These are slow turns: commands began after 9 simulated seconds, and the sampled first entry occurred roughly 37 or 43 seconds thereafter. Sampled height after command onset was 0.921–0.948 m for a nominal 0.85 m height command. Sampled absolute pitch or roll stayed below 0.065 rad in those lanes.

The existing flight law was unchanged. All 240 recorded q, v and policy-output samples through step 1600 exactly matched the earlier run; this does not assert substep-by-substep parity. The two nominal signs of the 180-degree command both selected the same positive direction, and still had about 1.12 rad error at the final sample, so neither half-turn qualification nor both turn directions at 180 degrees is established. Four lanes retained initial-settling toe speed-cap hits. Heading and attitude were sparsely sampled, not continuously bounded. This used development seed is not a perturbed-start, landing, obstacle-route, unused-layout, aerodynamic or biological validation. The wider flight controller remains unqualified and cannot yet be promoted as frozen for navigation.
