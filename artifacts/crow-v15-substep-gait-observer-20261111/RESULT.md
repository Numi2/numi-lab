# Substep-resolved physical gait measurement

The isolated native observer snapshots generalized coordinates after each physical substep alongside existing contact records. On the unchanged development seed 20261126, all 2,400 accepted control-step q/v/action/contact endpoints and all 38,400 aggregate contact substeps matched the retained baseline exactly. All 12,800 requested coordinate snapshots were present in steps 201–300; each final substep matched its logged control endpoint exactly at printed precision. There were no reported native errors or contact overflow. The result applies to this observer and this development start, not every simulation or unseen seed.

Physical joint anchors were reconstructed using native forward kinematics at 1.25 ms intervals. In the 48 previously identified interior sampled-clearance bouts, projected knee and ankle internal-angle minima occurred in the same physics substep in 47 bouts; in one, the ankle minimum preceded the knee minimum by 20 ms. No measured minimum lay at the edge of these selected intervals. The selection is the interval between two endpoint samples that met the earlier clearance test: continuous clearance and a complete toe-off-to-touchdown swing were not established, and a lower minimum could exist outside the selected interval. Therefore this is strong evidence of synchronized motion within the observed portions, not a complete gait-cycle comparison or proof of how a real American crow should move. The related-magpie study and its limits are documented separately.

Hallux and middle front-toe normal-impulse substep counts are retained per side for future contact-event work. Counts alone do not establish touchdown, toe-off, grasp or slip. No walk controller was promoted by this observer; sustained contact-driven walking, a reliable leg-powered takeoff and a supported autonomous landing remain open.
