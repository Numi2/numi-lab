# Matched altitude-command comparison

The zero-step control and pulse trial both survived all 1200 post-reset transitions in all eight correlated development starts. The four zero-command lanes have identical logged state, action and contact records for the entire comparison. The +/−0.20 m lanes first differ at trace submission 603, after the commanded change begins; all earlier logged records match. Trace submission includes one pre-reset warmup. The full navigation summaries differ, so only the specifically reported survival and state comparisons should be treated as matched.

Relative to each same-lane no-step control, mean pulse-window height differences at 16–17 s were +0.199997–0.199998 m in lanes 4/5 and −0.200004 m in lanes 6/7. During 22–23 s after return to the nominal target, residual differences were approximately +0.000030–0.000031 m. The very close target-relative values require scrutiny, and do not by themselves establish realistic aerodynamics. At 50 Hz the differential height derivatives were consistent with the average recorded differential vertical velocities to a mean absolute discrepancy of 0.00295–0.00313 m/s over the command onset comparison; this is a different-lane approximation and is not a force audit or proof against all hidden assistance. See `height-differences.json` and `kinematic-check.json`.

The wing-force model retains empirical unsteady-force directions and a stroke-dependent root yaw moment. The trial also uses a research-only world-interpolation variant and no recorded full joint-speed substeps, so this is not production, biological, disturbance, obstacle-clearance or full flight qualification. The user-requested perturbed starts, flight-law freeze, reliable navigation, physical grip and release, landing on unused layouts and complete video remain outstanding.
