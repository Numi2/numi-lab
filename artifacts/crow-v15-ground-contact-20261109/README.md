# v15 physical ground-contact investigation

Status: experimental. Walking acts on the simulated v15 leg joints and is gated by solver-reported foot loads; leg-powered takeoff and articulated landing grip are not complete. None of the experimental shaders has been deployed or frozen. The original `src/metal/CrowFlightControllerV1.metal` is unchanged, SHA-256 `aa2fe10901cb0494d80ae445902303a17ce0e73d030072bab09461d8f3593ebe`.

The matched original controller produced bilateral loaded feet and approximately 0.00068 m net prelaunch motion in the selected lane. The walking-band carrier retained bilateral support throughout and produced 0.00325 m over the selected seven-second recording. Both were observed with the physical solver, not rendered animation. `parity.json` verifies that early contact tracing left all 350 recorded replay frames identical to the observer-off run.

The candidate uses the previous accepted step's solver contact force to gate bounded alternating hip, knee and ankle position targets. The sensor-zero arm moved 0.00068 m in the observed lane; the live sensor arm moved 0.0570 m and showed both left-only and right-only loaded samples. Three additional exposed-course runs showed 0.0573-0.0576 m with similar nominal starts. A separate eight-lane body-perturbation screen used initial roll, leg-joint and velocity variations; each lane had both alternating loaded-foot patterns, no non-foot ground contact, and 0.0573-0.0585 m horizontal net movement over its recorded supported endpoints. The estimated rigid feet lift only a few millimetres, and the 95th-percentile loaded foot-center horizontal speeds reached approximately 0.066 and 0.116 m/s in those nominal replications. Foot-center speed is not actual contact-point slip and the results do not establish biological realism, variable terrain performance, or long-term balance.

The first band-10 handoff kept overriding flight leg targets after the walking window, contrary to the original plan's wording. All four full-route first episodes then ended in obstacle contacts. A backward-spawn-offset hypothesis also failed in all four first episodes. See `full-handoff-*-first-failures.json` and raw recordings; do not discard those failures. `handoff-boundary-correction-plan.md` records the discovery and corrects the walking override so flight actions regain control after 5 s. The corrected experimental shader completed all five waypoints in each of two lanes on each development-exposed layout, followed by 25 consecutive terminal solver-reported foot-only upward perch-support samples at low root speed. There were no reported physical failures or failed environment steps in these four runs. These are previously exposed layouts and seeds, not a new held-out test. The policy uses simulator-issued waypoint coordinates. The late observer's corrected on/off parity was checked against all 350 replay frames; see `late-observer-parity.json`. The first comparison was invalid because both arms had trace variables present, and is retained under `invalid-presence-check-*` with explanation in `invalid-observer-parity.md`.

An isolated wings-suppressed push-off candidate produced peak upward root speeds of 0.0386 m/s, versus approximately 0.00005 m/s with no leg push. A faster ramp reached 0.0770 m/s. Neither cleared both feet from the ground in the sampled wing-suppressed window, so both failed the preregistered push-off gate. With wings active, takeoff occurred but the candidate was not established as improving it. The recorded impulses are final-substep endpoint values, not integrated force or work.

The v15 body has one rigid sole collider per foot and no articulated toes. Existing foot-only support on a flat box is not a grasp. A physically meaningful grip requires a separately identified and tested foot embodiment, contact geometry, actuated or passive flexion and release, and appropriate load/perturbation controls; it must not be represented by an animation or an asserted support flag. Full qualifying work also remains for causal leg push-off, multi-step gait and flight handoff across perturbed starts, contact-point slip and force integration, and the final grasp-to-landing sequence.

Each plan was written before its corresponding run. Experimental patches are relative to the base source or the explicitly named preceding shader; `shader-source-reconstruction.json` records byte-for-byte reconstruction checks. The observer source patches reconstruct to the recorded source hashes (`observer-restore-verification.log`). Input and binary hashes are recorded in the binding JSON files. The actor packs are archived under `artifacts/crow-conditional-landing-native-20261109/`. Paths in run bindings identify the original macmini workspace. Rebuilding requires the matching native build, Metal tools, and the archived policy packs.

The runner can reset after a terminal failure even with scheduled resets disabled. Full-route reports use `first_episode_navigation` for the first-episode outcome; frames or contact samples after an early physical failure belong to later episodes and must not be interpreted as a successful continuous first run. The recordings' inherited `controller_authority` metadata predates these experimental low-level overrides and is not a description of this experimental controller.
