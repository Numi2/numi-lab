# v15 contact-gated push-off: first causal screen

Pre-registered question: can leg position targets create a measurable launch impulse on the v15 body through solved ground contacts? Use the same exposed course, seed, navigation actor, model, two lanes, 350 x 20 ms steps and four physics substeps as the exact baseline. Test a 2x2 comparison, push command off/on and wings normal/suppressed in the 4.4-5.4 s interval. The suppressed-wing experiment is a mechanical isolation control, not a flight-capable controller. Each source variant retains the frozen flight law and does not inject body forces. All arms publish previous accepted foot load in a diagnostic task-state lane; no teacher is used. The candidate ramps from the original leg command toward leg extension at 4.6-4.88 s, individually gated by each foot having >0.05 N solved load, and releases the command at 5.2 s. It is intentionally a first attempt and may fail.

Prediction: compared with the wing-suppressed no-push control, wing-suppressed push will raise the observed root peak upward speed by at least 0.03 m/s during 4.6-5.4 s, while at least one foot has positive solver-reported loading; both feet will subsequently be off the ground in at least two sampled steps. A pass also requires no reported physical failure, no nonfoot ground contact and 350 recorded steps. Compare the normal-wing arms separately for takeoff and report failure or censored observations, never exclude them. Final-substep contact reports are not integrated impulse; the trial does not by itself prove a sustained launch, biological force profile, robust flight handoff, or grip. All four arms and source diffs are retained.
