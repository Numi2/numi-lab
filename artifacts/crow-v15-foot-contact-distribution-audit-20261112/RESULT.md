# Where the measured contact motion occurred

In the earlier 800 Hz, two-second, same-seed lane-4 comparison, the simulated right rear proximal toe carried 2.30 N·s of the baseline right-foot normal impulse and 2.44 N·s under the knee-warp test; its share with estimated tangential speed above 0.05 m/s was 0.298 and 0.446 N·s respectively. The modeled right foot body carried 1.04 and 0.94 N·s. Contributions from the front toe distal bodies also increased in the high-speed subset. This points to multiple modeled foot segments, not a single visibly stiff joint, as relevant to the observed change.

These are post-hoc, impulse-weighted summaries of one correlated development trajectory. Segment names are simulation labels rather than validated anatomical correspondence, the contact point and accepted state may differ in time, and point speed is not accumulated slip. They do not establish inadequate friction, realistic force distribution or a safe controller.
