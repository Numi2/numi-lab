# Flight-force evidence and calibration gap

The current research-v15 aerodynamic shader uses a specialized shoulder/wing kinematic correction, blade-element quadrature and a simplified estimated unsteady term aligned with a combination of body up and forward. Its geometry correction passed a numerical kinematics comparison, but that result does not validate the model's aerodynamic loads, changing wing area, feather motion or energetic requirements. The current whole-body trajectory and wing-drive evidence show insufficient modeled support and large tracking errors; changing a controller or multiplying aerodynamic forces without independent validation cannot establish realistic flight.

A comparative dataset in Deetjen et al. reports an American crow row, with a 387.6 g body mass, a 6.4 Hz wingbeat reference, and a 30.8 g single-wing mass; the table combines different historical sources and notes that some entries are scaled or from related species. It is not one measured individual, and these values do not independently establish a complete crow wing morphology, takeoff force, muscle power or acceptable joint torque. The existing research model's body mass and literature-scaled wing inertia are estimates, not specimen-calibrated. Source: https://cdn.elifesciences.org/articles/89968/elife-89968-supp3-v1.pdf .

Jackson's corvid research reports maximal burst takeoffs, ground forces and 3D kinematic measurements including American crows, and pectoralis strain/force measurements in the larger corvids. Its collection and published summaries should inform an independent crow-specific evidence search and calibration rather than a cosmetic target. Source: https://scholarworks.umt.edu/etd/960/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21228204/ .

Deetjen et al. report time-resolved wing surface, force and muscle measurements for four ringneck doves, not crows. Their second takeoff wingbeat includes stroke-dependent area and angle-of-attack changes and differs by individual. The data are a useful cross-species sanity check only, not a crow calibration or evidence that the simulated crow satisfies it. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10942624/ . The public Dryad dataset is approximately 19.29 GB and includes multiple unrelated-to-the-immediate-test assets; do not download it wholesale to the space-constrained Mac mini. Source and README: https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.wwpzgmsqs .

Next physical work must establish an auditable mapping among the actual wing joint coordinates, phase, changing planform, signed aerodynamic forces and joint/air power under controlled motions; validate against independent measurements at matched species or explicitly identified cross-species conditions. The observed positive `workingEffort * accepted delta-q` is only a buffer-timing-dependent proxy and must not be labeled pectoralis work or compared as though it were a calibrated muscle measurement. Require free-flight, turn, height and disturbed-start tests, then freeze the successful controller before untouched-layout mission and landing qualification.
