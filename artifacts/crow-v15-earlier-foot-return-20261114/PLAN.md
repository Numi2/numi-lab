# Earlier contact-qualified returning foot

In the constructed stationary release/return test, the stance foot lost opposing grip soon after the returning foot first regripped. Earlier lateral weight transfer at the original return transition did not fix it. Test a distinct timing hypothesis: both lanes use the unchanged controller and model, but lane 1 begins the physical return after 15 consecutive already-qualified foot-clearance/support intervals instead of 30. The gate uses the existing measured clearance, absence of released-foot normal impulse, and stance upward and opposed-toe contact; no forced pose, altered friction, motor limit or body. Fifteen 20 ms control intervals is an exploratory timing parameter, not a claim of a measured crow gait. Lane 0 remains the unchanged 30-interval control.

Before viewing results, require exact same-run logged state and contact prefix until the return-state intervention, complete finite 450 accepted states and all 32 contact, toe-velocity and effort substeps, no observer error, nonfoot support or toe-speed cap, at least 10 consecutive qualified single-foot clearance intervals, followed by 100 terminal intervals of bilateral opposed toe grip with existing support, posture and speed gates and no fall. Record first intervention, all contacts, actual toe positions/efforts and failures, and compare control to archived same-model run. Passing this constructed stationary release/regrip screen alone would not prove a walking stride, takeoff, landing, biological realism or a full mission. Contact/state epochs remain independently unverified.
