# Exploratory wingbeat steering authority with speed feedback

Question: can bounded phase-dependent common pronation produce enough left and right steering authority for a tighter turn, while preserving speed, height and lateral stability? The previously selected +0.60 sin phase raised maximum right yaw but closed-loop +/-0.06 rad/s commands both failed. Test eight fixed waveform controls in each yaw direction, not a navigation or qualification run.

On the unchanged v15 body, retain the candidate speed-feedback trim clamp(0.55 + 1.40*(forwardSpeed-0.70),0.25,0.95), roll stabilizer and filtered actuators. Hold yaw action at -1 or +1 after 6 s. Use 128 lanes, seed 20261118, 1600 steps, four substeps, obstacle collisions disabled, 8 lanes per direction/waveform group. group = lane modulo 16; groups 0–7 are negative yaw, 8–15 positive yaw. Within each direction, add identically to actions 4 and 5 one of: zero; +0.80 sin(phase); -0.80 sin(phase); +0.80 cos(phase); -0.80 cos(phase); 0.55*(sin(phase)+cos(phase)); +0.80 sin(2*phase); +0.80 cos(2*phase). Clamp the total requested action before the normal actuator filter.

A direction/waveform passes its exploratory screen only if all eight first episodes survive 1600 steps, failed simulator steps are zero, all required samples exist at 20–30 s every 2 s, median mean wrapped yaw rate is at most -0.055 rad/s for negative yaw or at least +0.055 for positive yaw, and every lane's maximum sampled absolute lateral speed <=0.20 m/s, speed error <=0.20 m/s, and height error <=0.15 m. Record all groups even if no candidate passes; groups are unpaired starts. Constant maximum yaw is a controllability probe, not the deployable feedback controller. No final navigation layouts or seeds are used.
