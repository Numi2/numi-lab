# Perch-relative fixed-pose translation screen

The zero-offset repeat matched all 512 prior minimum gaps exactly and had no missing toe bodies. On the coarse common-angle grid, the counts of foretoe settings with at least one segment within 0.5 mm of the perch and no segment penetrating it by more than 1 mm were 2, 4, 1, 0 and 0 out of 256 at model x translations of -4, -2, 0, +2 and +4 mm respectively. Rear-toe counts were 13, 10, 9, 8 and 6. These counts describe only a tolerance-based geometric surface screen and include configurations with no actual contact; a positive/negative x translation moves the whole bird in this diagnostic.

The coarse grid, fixed body/foot posture, common foretoe angles, lack of self-collision, forces, dynamics or control mean this is not evidence of a stable grip or a reachable reposition. It supports testing small rearward placement changes physically, with full contact and fall evidence, rather than changing joint limits to force closure.
