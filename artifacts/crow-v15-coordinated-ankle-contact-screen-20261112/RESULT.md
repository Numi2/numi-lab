# Coordinated ankle feedback: partial screen, not qualified

Two of four correlated development trials with knee warp .16, hip gain +.5 and ankle gain -.5 passed all the preregistered short-window criteria: seed 20261126 lane 7 and seed 20261127 lane 6. The other two failed: lane 6 on the first seed briefly lost both-foot support and exceeded the loaded-point speed ratio; lane 7 on the second lost support and advanced the knee minimum only 4.0 percentage points. Across all 12 candidate trials, only these two passed. The two passing trials advanced the knee minimum 17.4 and 14.3 percentage points respectively, but each compares small numbers of recovery windows and none of these trials is an independent held-out layout. The unwarped reference lanes reproduce their same-lane baseline records, and all recorded pre-intervention state and contact records match.

For the +.5 ankle gain, same-lane comparisons against the prior hip-only +.5 screen gave mixed outcomes: seed 20261126 lane 5 right-foot p95 speed ratio fell 2.01 to 1.34 and both-feet-unloaded substeps 12 to zero, but lane 4 rose 2.56 to 2.77 and unloaded substeps 1 to 50. On seed 20261127, lane 5 fell 2.01 to 1.54 while lane 4 rose 1.88 to 2.35 and developed 52 unloaded substeps. None passed the full gate. The -.5 ankle lanes changed hip gain relative to the earlier same-lane experiment, so those are not isolated ankle comparisons.

The sampled contact sensor varied in each candidate lane; its reconstructed comparison uses the same cutoff and has small residuals in this experiment. The sampled p95 speed is not integrated material slip. The sensor still temporarily aliases a mechanical-power state field and this diagnostic shader cannot be promoted. The model uses related-corvid measurements as exploratory targets, not proven crow anatomy or species-specific ground truth. This does not qualify normal-speed gait, takeoff, flight, navigation, grip or landing.
