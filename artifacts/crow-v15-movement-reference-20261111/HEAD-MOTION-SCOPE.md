# Head and body motion: species limits

Hancock (2010), *Terrestrial locomotion and head-bobbing in birds*, table 6-1, lists an American crow row with `HBG/BBG`: the table legend explicitly defines those suffixes as head- and body-bobbing reported within the **same genus**. They are not direct measurements of American-crow head-bob amplitude, phase, or joint motion. The comparative experiments in chapters 3 and 4 measured tinamous and charadriiform birds, respectively, not American crows. The dissertation is available at https://etd.ohiolink.edu/acprod/odb_etd/ws/send_file/send?accession=ohiou1282333191&disposition=inline .

The finding supports retaining the capacity for context-dependent head and whole-body motion, not imposing a pigeon-like head cycle or a fixed phase relation on the crow. Real head-bobbing is related to visual behavior and can vary with context; the cross-species results do not provide quantitative crow targets. The separately archived Paul Danese American-crow branch excerpt is qualitative and one individual on a sloped surface; it also cannot calibrate a level-ground cycle. Do not substitute genus-level or unrelated-species numerical values for American-crow observations.
