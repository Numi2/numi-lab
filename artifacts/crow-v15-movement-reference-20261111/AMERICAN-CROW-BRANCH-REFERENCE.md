# American crow branch reference: qualitative only

Source: Paul Danese, *20240204 american crow 646 hop.webm*, recorded 4 February 2024, Pittsfield, Massachusetts, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:20240204_american_crow_646_hop.webm. The source page identifies the animal as an American crow and licenses the video under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). The page describes vocalization, not calibrated locomotion. Species identification is that of the uploader, not independently verified. The page reports a 21.212 s, 3840x2160 original and SHA-1 e3b823dcb7c86b9ae54e51a7faf56f3e29ea54c2; we did not independently hash the complete source stream.

Two contact sheets are modified derivatives of that recording: `crow-reference-contact-sheet.png` samples at nominal 0.5 frames/s and `crow-reference-branch-sequence.png` samples a nominal 5 s segment beginning at 5 s at 4 frames/s. They were scaled and tiled from a streamed original using ffmpeg, and are retained here under the source CC BY-SA 4.0 license with credit to Paul Danese. Source-frame timing, frame rate and possible dropped/duplicated resampled frames have not been checked. An initial attempt to seek in the network stream ended prematurely; decoding linearly succeeded.

Inspection shows one crow changing whole-body posture, head orientation and wing/tail presentation while on a broad irregular branch; several frames show the bird shifting along it. Parts of the legs and toes are occluded against the branch and plumage, the bird changes orientation, and the ground and object scales are uncalibrated. The 4 fps montage is too sparse to establish footfall timing or toe-joint trajectories. It is useful as qualitative species-specific context for perching and posture, but cannot establish American-crow ground-walking kinematics, joint stiffness, forces, step frequency, slip, or a numerical target. Its poses are not evidence of the simulated bird succeeding.
