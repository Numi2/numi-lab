# Related-corvid whole-leg reference and limits

Verstappen, Aerts and Van Damme, Terrestrial locomotion in the black-billed magpie: kinematic analysis of walking, running and out-of-phase hopping, Journal of Experimental Biology 203, 2159–2170 (2000), https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.203.14.2159 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10862728/ . The studied animal is Pica pica, not Corvus brachyrhynchos: its values are comparative evidence, not American-crow calibration.

Three hand-raised birds were encouraged along a cork-covered outdoor track after treadmill attempts produced irregular, wing-flapping bouts. The authors selected 28 walking high-speed sequences, mostly partial strides, and seven conventional-video walking sequences containing at least one complete stride. Close-up recordings were made at 500 frames/s and digitized at 250 frames/s; full-stride recordings were made and digitized at 50 frames/s. Hip, knee and ankle locations carried greater expected digitization error than other landmarks, even with an X-ray-based overlay. Fragments were combined to describe a gait cycle; sequences and observations are not independent birds.

The paper defines stance onset by hallux touchdown and recovery onset by third-digit lift-off, and walking by two double-support phases per stride. It describes a phased pattern rather than simultaneous joint motion: early stance knee and ankle flexion, knee flexion continuing through most of stance with a possible brief late extension, hip generally extending through stance, ankle extending after its mid-stance minimum and flexing again near liftoff. During recovery, knee extension starts before ankle extension, followed by hip extension near touchdown. The authors note speed and gait effects, and digit visibility and anatomical angle definitions matter. The trunk and femur move relatively little in their studied birds, but this does not establish that an American crow has the same amplitude, has no neck movement, or should be forced to a fixed pattern.

Use this as a qualitative, falsifiable comparison with v15: instrument actual toe contact, joint angles using matching anatomical definitions, full stride timing, foot clearance, trunk and head world/torso movement. Compare within the same gait phase and comparable speeds; retain missing or ambiguous contacts. Do not interpret model joint coordinates directly as anatomical internal joint angles without verifying axes and sign, nor infer foot contact from clearance alone. The existing 20 ms v15 endpoint audit establishes neither digit-specific liftoff and touchdown timing nor complete stride correspondence, and its reported joint spans are not a phase-aligned comparison. The sole available real-American-crow branch clip is sloped and occluded; it cannot provide those measurements. Do not replace this missing evidence with magpie numerical thresholds or synthetic animation.
