# Corvid head-tracking data: scope and limitations

[Itahara and Kano, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.246514) tracked the 3D head orientation of freely moving **large-billed crows**, *Corvus macrorhynchos*, during object-viewing tasks. They report processed data, code and sample data at [OSF](https://osf.io/3dmg7/). This is a different species and an attention experiment, not American-crow whole-body walking or synchronized footfall data. The experiment did not track eye movements in the behavior study; the authors discuss uncertainty in inferring visual direction from head orientation. The source may inform later object-directed visual tracking, but it does not establish numerical stride, trunk, neck-translation, toe-force or flight targets. Access and contents of the OSF data have not yet been verified.

The [1983 four-species crow gait paper](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7998.1983.tb05784.x) reports slow walking and faster hopping in its abstract, but that abstract does not identify its studied species or provide American-crow joint trajectories. Preserve the distinction between qualitative cross-species cues and numerical American-crow gait validation.
