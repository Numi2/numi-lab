# Carrion-crow biologging used as a cross-species reference

Hoffman and colleagues, *A benchmark for computational analysis of animal behavior, using animal-borne tags*, https://doi.org/10.1186/s40462-024-00511-8, describe tail-feather accelerometers on eleven free-living carrion crows (*Corvus corone*), not American crows. The benchmark stores normalized, calibrated three-axis samples at 50 Hz. Five-second labels include Flying, In Nest and Unknown; flying can include gliding and is not a measurement of joint kinematics or ground walking. A feather-mounted sensor differs from the simulated rigid-tail center of mass.

The source dataset is https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7947104; its embedded license requires citation of https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7807281 and permits reuse under CC BY 4.0. Source metadata, license, a documented and bounded selection of original samples, extraction code, training comparison and reserved-individual evaluation are archived under `../crow-v15-carrion-biofit-20261112/`. The 277.5 MB full source archive was not downloaded; HTTP ranges retrieved selected data and exact sample hashes were recorded. The published ZIP MD5 and partially read entry CRCs were not independently checked.

The derived tail-acceleration comparison is a cross-species fit signal, not a claimed American-crow wingbeat measurement. See the experiment for selection, held-out limitations, simulator sensor approximation and physical screening.
