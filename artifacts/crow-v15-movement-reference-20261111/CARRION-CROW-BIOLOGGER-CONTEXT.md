# Carrion-crow biologging: potential flight context, not walking ground truth

Hoffman and colleagues, *A benchmark for computational analysis of animal behavior, using animal-borne tags*, https://doi.org/10.1186/s40462-024-00511-8, describe tail-feather-mounted accelerometer recordings from 11 free-living **carrion crows** (*Corvus corone*), not American crows. Accelerations were collected at 1,000 Hz, stored at 200 Hz, and further processed to 50 Hz and normalized for the benchmark. Labels distinguish Flying, In Nest and Unknown, based on audio and five-second clips. They do not label ground walking or provide stride-resolved joints, poses, contact forces or direct wing kinematics; Flying can include gliding as well as flapping. A tail-feather sensor does not directly give center-of-mass acceleration without a measurement model and orientation information. Therefore neither benchmark walking targets nor an American-crow wingbeat or acceleration target can be inferred from the label alone.

The published dataset is linked at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7947104 and formatting code at https://github.com/earthspecies/BEBE-datasets. The cited version lists a 277.5 MB formatted crow archive and notes that licenses are specified inside dataset folders; the web preview lists 20 clip CSVs, metadata and a license file, but their contents and the license were not read (https://zenodo.org/records/7947104/preview/formatted_baglione_crows.zip?include_deleted=0). No data were downloaded or introduced as a calibration target. This is a lead for a later cross-species, sensor-aware flight comparison, conditional on checking its license and samples and independently separating flapping from gliding.
