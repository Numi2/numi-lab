# Related-bird flight turning evidence

Ling et al., Collective turns in jackdaw flocks: kinematics and information transfer, Journal of the Royal Society Interface (2019), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31640502/ . Three-dimensional field observations describe wild jackdaw flock turns, including speed changes, group-level turn time, turn radius and acceleration. This is a related corvid, but the reported group quantities and group-coordination time are not single American-crow low-speed maneuver measurements. They cannot be installed as controller targets or used directly to qualify this simulation.

Hedrick and Biewener, Low speed maneuvering flight of the rose-breasted cockatoo, Journal of Experimental Biology 210:1897–1911 (2007), https://biewenerlab.oeb.harvard.edu/publications/low-speed-maneuvering-flight-rose-breasted-cockatoo-eolophus-roseicapillus-i . Three-view high-speed recordings and muscle measurements from six cockatoos performing 90-degree turns showed flapping, banked turns and a correlation of heading change with roll angle. Cockatoos are not corvids; flock and laboratory conditions differ. This supports measuring coupled wing, body, trajectory, and aerodynamic forces, not merely yaw or a prescribed crow bank angle.

The v15 toe-body extended-turn screen required about 37–43 seconds after the command to reach a sampled 90-degree heading and sampled absolute roll below 0.065 rad. Its initial settling showed toe speed caps, and its 180-degree and perturbation recovery tests remain unqualified. The available studies are scientifically useful context, but speed, turn geometry, measurement definitions, species and environmental conditions are not matched. No numerical bird-versus-simulation equivalence is asserted. A valid flight-qualification test must measure continuous course, turning dynamics and physical wing forces, protect held-out starts, and distinguish aerodynamic and actuator contributions.
