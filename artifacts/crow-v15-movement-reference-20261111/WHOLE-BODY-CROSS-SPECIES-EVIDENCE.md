# Whole-body walking evidence: cross-species context

These studies inform observable quantities and model limitations; they do not supply American crow numerical targets.

- [Bird terrestrial locomotion as revealed by 3D kinematics](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0944200611000778) used high-speed X-ray views of quail and reconstructed skeletal movement. The abstract describes coordinated three-dimensional trunk and thigh movement as well as distal-limb motion. This motivates measuring trunk orientation and center-of-mass path together with hip, knee, ankle, foot and toe motion, rather than judging isolated knee flexion. It does not show that the trunk itself must deform; the study describes a largely rigid trunk with a mobile head and neck.
- [Andrada et al. 2014, On vision in birds](https://doi.org/10.1186/1742-9994-11-27) analyzed head, neck, trunk and limb landmarks in eight quails, with substantially different numbers of usable trials across birds. The timing of head thrust relative to foot support differed between mechanically defined walking and running. The paper reports that behavioral context and vision affect head bobbing, and notes timing variability and conflicting findings in another species. Do not impose these quail timings, amplitudes, or head-bobbing as a universal American crow gait. The current v15 neck degrees of freedom cannot reproduce arbitrary neck translation; visually moving a mesh would not establish head/neck dynamics.
- [Bishop et al. 2018, The influence of speed and size on avian terrestrial locomotor biomechanics](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5821450/) studied 12 species of ground-dwelling birds over a broad size and speed range and includes ground reaction forces. The authors discuss the continuum of avian gait mechanics and posture. It supplies a reason to examine support timing, force profiles and speed together, not to equate a single duty factor with a crow gait or transfer a regression to crow without validation.

For the simulator, record simultaneous physical joint states, foot support impulses, trunk and head poses, whole-body center of mass and forward speed across complete strides. Compare original animal observations only where species, speed, definition, measurement method and coordinate conventions are known. Report absent American crow stride-resolved data explicitly. This reference note establishes no new performance or biological validation.
