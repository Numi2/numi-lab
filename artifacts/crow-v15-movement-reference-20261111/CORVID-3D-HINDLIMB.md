# Additional corvid whole-limb and pelvic evidence

- Stoessel and Fischer, 2012, [Comparative intralimb coordination in avian bipedal locomotion](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.070458), used biplanar high-speed X-ray videography of jackdaws (*Corvus monedula*) and two ground-dwelling species on a treadmill. They measured joint and limb angles and pelvic rotations. The abstract reports similar fore-aft joint coordination among species, but differences in lateral limb movements and pelvic rotations, including increased jackdaw hip mobility. This is related-species evidence, not a measurement of American crow stride angles, and it argues against assuming sagittal-plane leg motion captures the complete gait. We have not extracted a usable time series or studied how treadmill and overground behavior differ here.
- Meilak et al., 2021, [On the 3D Nature of the Magpie Functional Hindlimb Anatomy During the Take-Off Jump](https://doi.org/10.3389/fbioe.2021.676894), combines one magpie CT skeleton, literature muscle anatomy including carrion crow histology, literature muscle cross-sections, and takeoff ranges from dove and finch XROMM. It estimates three-dimensional pelvic muscle moment-generating capacity and model uncertainty; it is not a direct measurement of American crow takeoff muscle forces. The source suggests examining all three hip axes, not copying modeled magpie values into the crow.
- Hayes and Alexander, 1983, [The hopping gaits of crows and other bipeds](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7998.1983.tb05784.x), analyzed film of four crow species. Its abstract reports low-speed walking and faster out-of-phase hopping. Species-specific ranges and transition speeds have not been extracted; do not infer them from the abstract.

The current v15 model and accepted trajectories have not been quantitatively fitted to these studies. We cannot infer neck displacement, trunk deformation, joint stiffness or species-specific forces from these abstracts. These sources motivate measurements and model improvements rather than decorative whole-body oscillations or unverified parameter changes.
