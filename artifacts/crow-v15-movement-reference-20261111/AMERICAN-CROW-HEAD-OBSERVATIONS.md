# American crow head-movement observations

Fernández-Juricic, O’Rourke and Pitlik (2010), “Visual coverage and scanning behavior in two corvid species: American crow and Western scrub jay,” *Journal of Comparative Physiology A* 196:879–888, [doi:10.1007/s00359-010-0570-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00359-010-0570-0); [author-hosted paper](https://estebanfj.bio.purdue.edu/papers/JCompPhysA%20196.pdf).

This is direct *Corvus brachyrhynchos* evidence, unlike jackdaw or magpie proxies. The authors describe analysis of 10 ground and 18 perching American-crow videos, selected for quality and corresponding to different individuals. They scored head movement about pitch, yaw, and roll axes in stationary versus walking/hopping conditions. While walking/hopping, they report American crow mean head movement rate 23.72 events/min (SE 4.22, SD 17.38) and the mean interval of stationary head orientation between events 943.68 ms (SE 156.09, SD 690.94). These values are study-specific aggregates; the combined walking/hopping category is not an isolated stride cycle. The paper's statistical reporting and sample selection should be consulted before inference.

The observations support non-rigid head-orientation behavior and motivate a separate, context-aware head/vision evaluation. They do not provide neck extension, head translation, stride frequency, limb joint angles, body deformation, ground reaction forces, or event alignment to individual footsteps. Head scanning events per minute must not be used as a walking cadence or as a per-step head-bob target. The rate and average stationary interval are different aggregate measures and should not be treated as reciprocals. The paper does not by itself justify a scripted head oscillation or biological qualification of this simulation.
