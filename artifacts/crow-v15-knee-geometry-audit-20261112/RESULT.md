# Knee geometry and related-corvid data

Using the native forward-kinematic thigh and shank joint anchors, the geometric angle at the knee over the 4–6 s window was 136.8–164.3 degrees for the matched experimental gait, versus 150.6–164.1 degrees for held. Parent/child knee anchors agreed within 2.2e-9 m; the reconstructed angle agreed with 180 degrees minus the absolute knee coordinate within 7e-7 degrees in these samples. These are full-window extrema at 20 ms endpoints in 3D, not phase-specific extrema or continuous limits.

For context only, Verstappen et al. (2000), https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.203.14.2159, table 1 reports black-billed magpie walking knee-angle minima during stance of 72 ± 12 degrees (26 observations), recovery minima 50 ± 12 degrees (20), and pre-stance maxima 143 ± 9 degrees (34); the published values are mean ± SD. Their landmark angles are side-view measurements, with different species, speed and conditions. A full-window simulated range cannot be compared directly with phase-specific group means, and no American-crow knee range is established by this paper. Nonetheless the marked difference motivates testing a more flexed posture with explicit support and phase controls.

The inherited v15 configured knee coordinate limits are -0.1 to 1.55 radians; the latter corresponds geometrically to about 91.2 degrees with this model's conventions. Thus the configured bound is above the related-magpie mean minima, although it is not evidence of a correct American-crow limit, and actual constraint tolerances have not been measured here. Any change to physical limits needs independent geometry, numerical stability, support, flight and landing tests and compatible controller/policy provenance. No limit or production controller was modified by this audit.
