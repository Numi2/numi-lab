# Contact-held returning toe targets

The v15 return control keeps increasing the returning toe targets after the fore and rear toes have acquired opposing endpoint contact; a brief opposed grip is then lost and the model falls. Test the specific causal hypothesis that continued target closure destabilizes the returned foot. Two control lanes retain the original behavior. In candidate pairs, when the returned foot has sensed upward contact and opposed fore/rear normals after return, hold its existing fore and rear toe targets; resume normal bounded closure after 1, 3 or 8 consecutive control intervals without that opposed-contact gate. No pose editing, direct support force, model, friction, solver, joint limit or drive-limit change. The gate uses existing endpoint contacts and the existing 0.000006 N s support threshold; it is not a measurement of sustained load.

Require the paired candidates to show a real target intervention and exact logged state, target and contact parity beforehand, complete finite 450 state and 32 contact/toe/effort substeps, at least 30 release intervals, no observer error, no fall, no nonfoot impulse or toe-speed cap, and a terminal 100-interval consecutive bilateral opposed supported grip streak. Report every lane and gate activation; duplicate same-start lanes are correlated. A pass is only a stationary control experiment and must be challenged by perturbed starts, walking and landing. Contact/state epochs are not independently verified.
