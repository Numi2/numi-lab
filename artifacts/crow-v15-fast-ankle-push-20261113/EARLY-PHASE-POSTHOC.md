# Posthoc early push phase: short and long windows measure different things

A posthoc comparison of the identical 348–357 substeps finds the 80 ms candidate produces +0.1255 to +0.2475 N s upward ground impulse relative to the active crouch in each comparable lane. The previous 160 ms candidate produces +0.1351 to +0.3011 N s. The later 348–375 window gives negative differences because it includes more of the subsequent airborne or recontact motion; its failure does not mean there was no early leg-associated impulse. Whole-model forward kinematics find +0.1161 to +0.2396 N s center-of-mass vertical momentum change for 80 ms and +0.1283 to +0.2924 N s for 160 ms during 348–357. FK was calibrated with an imposed uniform root translation; this does not validate every part of the coupled solver.

The timing window and contrasts were selected after seeing the traces. These are correlated development lanes, with passive aerodynamics and earlier body moments, and ground force also reflects gravity support and contact coupling. They establish a useful paired early contact impulse in this simulation, not isolated muscle power, a reliable unsupported launch, or a qualified full mission. The next experiment must preregister the early impulse and center-of-mass phase, independently measure flapping onset and test the coupled transition with a matched crouch control; the earlier long window should remain reported.
