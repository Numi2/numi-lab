# Earlier first stroke with low wing effort: preregistered paired screen

The lower-effort push-first trial produced a measurable initial increase in COM upward momentum that was lost over the next ten ticks. Test a single coordination change: start the first-wing-stroke counter at 7.04 s instead of 7.08 s, after the 6.96 s push latch, while preserving the 0.5/1/2 wing working-effort bounds by lane. The bounds and the stroke timing are engineering controls, not biological measurements. This experimental intervention tests coordination, not a unique cause or a validated bird.

Use the same development seed 20261126 and eight correlated lanes; lanes 2–7 provide two lanes per bound. Compare matched extension and active-crouch arms, 400 steps, 16 substeps, current body and passive aerodynamics, no direct applied flight moments, and no scheduled resets. Require complete first episodes, no trace errors, exact state/action/contact parity between arms through tick 345, intact substep samples, and recorded effort within bounds. Evaluate early push-minus-hold foot impulse in ticks 348–355 and 348–357; report three-interval release, all pre-release support gaps, later foot recontacts, nonfoot impulse, maximum rise and toe-speed events through tick 400. The development screen requires both impulse differences above 0.05 N s, continuous support from tick 340 until release by 375, no recontact through 400, root rise at least 0.05 m, no nonfoot impulse and at most one toe speed threshold event. Both assigned lanes must pass for a bound. An incomplete first episode invalidates scoring after termination; no early or survivor-only promotion. Preserve all inputs, binaries, runs and failures.
