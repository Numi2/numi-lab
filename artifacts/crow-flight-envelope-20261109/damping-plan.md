# Diagnostic root angular damping ablation

Hypothesis: the v15 estimated hybrid's isotropic 0.50 root angular damping closure limits yaw response as well as stabilizing pitch. Competing explanation: the yaw limit is dominated by articulated dynamics or other controls. In a diagnostic-only runtime, change the root body's isotropic angular damping to 0.15 or 0.05, keeping all mass, inertia, geometry, actuators, fuselage aero, other body damping and solver settings unchanged. This changes the body model/fingerprint and is **not** current-v15 qualification or a production change. The original policy pack must be rejected and may only be explicitly rebound with identical actor tensors for the diagnostic world.

For each damping setting run matched -1 and 0 normalized yaw-action arms, with same 32 native starts, seed 20261111, +0.55/-0.55 speed trim, roll-1.5 feedback, straight reference and 1600 steps as the existing 0.50 damping controls. Measure first-episode outcomes and per-lane mean wrapped yaw rate at t=20–30 s. Prediction: at least one reduced damping setting improves the median mean negative-action yaw rate by >=0.015 rad/s relative to the 0.50 control, with at least 90% first episodes surviving and zero simulator failures. All failed episodes count; missing required samples invalidate the corresponding rate comparison. Report zero-action drift, speed, altitude, tilt and every outcome, because reducing isotropic damping can destabilize other axes. Stop after four arms. Neither a positive result nor visual survival establishes that the modified body is valid; all qualification remains on the unchanged current v15 model.
