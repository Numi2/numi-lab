# Exploratory v15 flight envelope: course-relevant turns

Question: does increasing yaw feedback improve bidirectional tracking when the lateral feedback that eliminated contacts in the earlier small-turn bench is present? Alternative: control authority, coupling or speed/height instability prevents useful turns even with stronger feedback. Prior comparisons: `crow-flight-source-diagnostics-20261108/slow-turn-yaw-gain-16.00-results.json` and `lateral-gain-0.80-results.json`. This is exploratory and is not controller qualification.

Arms: identical v15 obstacle-free diagnostic environment and teacher, lateral feedback 0.80, pitch-speed feedback 1.20, target height 0.85 m, target forward speed 0.70 m/s; compare yaw heading gain 3.0 (control) and 16.0 (candidate). Five lane-assigned commands: straight, +0.03, -0.03, +0.06, -0.06 rad/s, commanded from 6 s. Each arm uses 32 lanes, 1600 control steps, four physics substeps, development seed 20261111, native reset variation, full-flight teacher, no scheduled resets; native first-episode results and 2-second accepted-state samples. Lanes with matching index are matched across arms. All episodes, failures and timeouts count.

Prospective screen, not qualification: no physical terminations; after 12 s, every sampled surviving lane must have absolute wrapped heading error <= 0.15 rad, forward speed error <= 0.20 m/s and root height within 0.20 m of 0.85 m. Report all lane-level maxima and physical failures even if the screen fails. Samples are spaced 2 s and cannot prove continuous safety. A simulated fall/termination or missing sample fails the screen. Zero failed simulation steps and 32 complete first episodes are validity requirements. A passing screen permits further independent development, not freezing. No recovery perturbations are injected here. Limits are engineering targets, not biological validation. Reserve new seeds/layouts for later candidate validation and final navigation.

Record source, shader, library and policy hashes, exact commands, first-episode results and compressed sample streams. Stop after these two arms; revise only after inspecting both. Do not run on final navigation layouts.
