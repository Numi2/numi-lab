# Exploratory accepted-state roll feedback

The bank-gain and lateral tests show a tradeoff: stronger heading coupling improves heading but drives lateral runaway, while removing lateral feedback causes failures. Test a distinct controller structure that explicitly observes actual root roll and body roll rate on the next accepted-state transaction. Target roll is clamp(0.25 * yaw-frame lateral speed, -0.12, +0.12) rad; bank command is clamp(k*(target roll - actual roll) - 0.25*body roll rate, -0.45, +0.45), for k=1.5 and 3.0. The sign hypothesis is based on the prior trace in which negative roll coincided with growing positive yaw-frame lateral velocity. Both arms retain gain-16 bounded body-yaw control, fixed +0.55/-0.55 speed trim, v15 body, actuator/solver limits, 0.70 m/s target, same 32 matched lane starts, development seed 20261111 and five turn conditions.

Prediction: at least one arm lowers median maximum sampled lateral speed in -0.03 rad/s lanes by >=0.30 m/s relative to bank-1.40, while maximum heading error does not increase and no physical terminations occur in those lanes. Report all lanes, errors, physical failures and preexisting strict screen. Count only first-episode samples; zero simulator failures and complete samples are validity conditions. Stop after two variants. This instrument and controller are diagnostic only and do not represent qualified flight.
