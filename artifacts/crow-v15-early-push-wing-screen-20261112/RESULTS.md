# Earlier leg extension and wing initiation: no promotion

Eight matched development lanes per arm, used seed 20261127, teacher off, 370 control intervals and 16 substeps, using the previously validated synchronized observer. The first recorded candidate endpoint difference from the baseline is interval 349. The loaded contact transitions show one clean unloading transition per lane in the examined interval-340–365 window, with no recontact in that window. Modeled wing-center positions were reconstructed from native coordinates and validated against a replay frame within the recorded analysis tolerances. No toe speed cap was observed in intervals 340–370.

The original-phase earlier push loses foot load at interval 350 substep 7 or 8 while both modeled wing centers have moved upward about 3.6–4.4 mm relative to the torso. The phase-inverted earlier push loses load at interval 351 substep 3, after wing centers have moved downward about 13.0–14.7 mm relative to their interval-339 positions. Thus its stroke ordering is consistent with the American-crow escape observation, but the interval-340-to-first-unload upward ground impulse is 1.107–1.125 N s versus approximately 0.988 N s model weight over that same window. The matched baseline accumulates 1.431–1.451 N s versus 1.104 N s weight before its own later unloading at interval 352 substep 8. The difference between ground and weight impulse is only a force-balance diagnostic; passive and aerodynamic loads are present, and comparing windows of different lengths does not isolate leg muscle contribution.

The downstroke candidate develops much less excess ground impulse over its window, so it is not accepted as an adequate leg-powered takeoff. A 40 ms earlier commanded extension and abrupt oscillator phase switch are not evidence of natural crow timing. No perturbed starts, longer flight, independent traction, navigation, grip or landing were qualified; early body-moment assistance and unvalidated aerodynamic closures remain. Do not freeze or promote either candidate.
