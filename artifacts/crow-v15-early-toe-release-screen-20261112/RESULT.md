# Earlier rear-toe release: preliminary development pass

On one previously used development seed, all eight lanes passed the predeclared two-second matched screen. The candidate uses a narrower smooth phase envelope for the -0.02 rad contact-switched rear-toe target, releasing it before the end of the swing. Both arms use the same compiled host and shader; the pre-walk q/v/actions, endpoint contacts and all recorded contact substeps matched. All 38,400 contact substeps and 2,400 accepted endpoint samples per arm are present; neither arm logged errors or overflow. The sham stderr is byte-identical to the prior tiny-toe no-command sham.

Each lane retained three support-qualified clearance bouts per foot, no nonfoot contacts, zero unloaded substeps and no toe-speed caps. Forward travel was 25.19–29.83 mm versus 25.07–29.71 mm in the paired no-toe controls, with per-lane ratios 90.2–119.0%; the smallest margin is narrow. Aggregate endpoint samples with sole clear but toes low decreased from 49 to 17. Median loaded-contact point speed was 0.00252 versus 0.00276 m/s and p95 0.03490 versus 0.03484 m/s. These are dependent endpoint samples, not integrated slip or crow force measurements.

This is a promising research candidate, not an integrated controller or a completed physical sequence. The waveform is an engineering hypothesis, not a measured crow joint trajectory. A second previously used development seed is specified in REPLICATION-PLAN.md before testing; longer walks, varied starts, transition safety, and biological comparison remain necessary. Takeoff, flight, supported landing, unused-layout navigation and final videos are not established by this screen.

## Second development seed: rejected

The unchanged candidate failed the same matched screen on seed 20261126: six of eight lanes met the 90% forward-travel threshold, while lanes 1 and 3 achieved 89.0% and 83.3%, respectively. All lanes had three qualified clearance bouts per foot, no nonfoot contacts or toe-speed caps, and the candidate had zero unloaded substeps versus one for its paired control. Aggregate sole-clear/toe-low endpoint samples fell from 41 to 17; loaded point-speed median and p95 decreased from 0.00299 and 0.03813 m/s to 0.00247 and 0.03294 m/s. Exact pre-walk state and contact parity and complete trace checks passed. The candidate is not promoted.

A posthoc timing audit found the negative right rear-toe target coincided with some foot normal impulse in seven control intervals per lane on both seeds, but no active interval exceeded the audit half-weight impulse threshold; left-side active intervals did not exceed its numerical contact threshold. A target and a summed contact interval can overlap without proving sustained loaded toe motion or identifying the cause of the travel failures. The actual accepted support bit and actuator torque were not directly recorded in this audit.
