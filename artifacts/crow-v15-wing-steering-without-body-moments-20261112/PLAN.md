# Aerodynamic wing-sweep steering with known direct moments removed

The existing v15 flight law uses direct body yaw and pitch moments. The retained command-ablation screen showed that removing either command materially changed the trajectory, and the current law is not physically qualified. In this exploratory obstacle-free screen, apply exactly zero from the two identified direct yaw and pitch moment actuators after 9 s in the final actuator application path, after response filtering and safety overrides. This does not establish that all possible artificial body forces elsewhere are absent. Takeoff and initial flight before 9 s remain under the old law, so even a favorable result cannot qualify free flight or takeoff.

Use two separate six-lane arms with the same seed, lane identities and six target modes. The baseline retains the existing wing controller; the enhanced arm uses a stronger, bounded differential sweep response based on the same heading error and yaw-rate feedback. The prior 12-lane approach would have compared different lane starts and is not sufficiently matched. All other settings remain identical. Require matching sampled q/v/actions in each lane pair before step 450 and inspect both the direct-actuator source path and resulting states. Record all first-episode failures, angle tracking, orientation, altitude, speeds, ground contacts, caps and coverage. For the +/-90-degree modes, require final heading error below 0.6 rad in both and at least 15% better than their paired no-extra-sweep counterparts, without physical termination, missing contacts or ground contact; inspect straight-mode motion and retain every failure. These are provisional engineering gates, not biological targets. One lane per mode cannot qualify robustness; only follow on with perturbed starts and climb/descent if responses justify it. No controller is promoted from this screen.
