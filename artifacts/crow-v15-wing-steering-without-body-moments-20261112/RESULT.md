# Differential sweep without two direct body moments: rejected

Two six-lane obstacle-free development runs used identical seed, lane identities, starts and heading targets. From 9 s onward both removed the two identified direct yaw and pitch moment actuators at their final application point. The enhanced run added a bounded differential wing sweep command; the baseline retained the original wing commands. Forty-eight sampled pre-intervention states and policy outputs matched exactly. No first episode terminated, all 1,600 intervals had complete observed contact substeps, and no ground contact or toe-speed caps were observed after interval 400. Caps during earlier takeoff were observed in two lanes in both arms.

The requested +90/-90 degree turn final heading errors were 1.229/1.368 rad in the baseline and 1.226/1.365 rad in the enhanced arm. The enhanced arm did not meet the preset 0.6 rad or 15% improvement gates. Straight-mode final errors were about 0.043 rad. The intervention reached the modeled sweep joints: sampled mean joint position changes in the two turn lanes were approximately +/-0.030 rad per wing, in opposite directions. These samples do not measure applied actuator torques or aerodynamic forces, and cannot establish why the response is weak. The +/-180 degree commands also fell far short; their equivalent endpoints do not establish commanded turn direction.

This trial suppresses only the two identified direct-moment actuators after 9 s, while earlier flight used the old controller. It does not establish that other artificial forces are absent, does not validate aerodynamics or biological motion, and uses a previously used seed. No controller was promoted or frozen. Full command, inputs, sampled states, replays and contact evidence for both arms are retained. The next investigation must examine wing loads and actuator authority, then test physically realized turn, climb, descent and recovery before any navigation qualification.
