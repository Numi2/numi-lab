# Result: pre-release timing failed the strict screen

Both arms finished all 400 steps and the recorded state, action and contacts matched each other and the prior smooth-release reference through step 345 in all eight lanes. No trace, solver, or overflow errors were reported. In all six prespecified push lanes, the first-stroke counter was nonzero by step 355, but there were unsupported intervals before three consecutive unsupported intervals at step 360 or 361. All six recontacted at steps 366–369 and recorded 6–7 toe-speed threshold substeps. Each lane gained 0.1055–0.1530 N s in the 348–355 foot impulse contrast and 0.0596–0.1143 N s in 348–357, but the overall takeoff screen failed. Root rise alone does not establish sustained controlled flight.

The maximum sampled substep flap speed in 348–375 rose from 37.68–38.00 rad/s in the retained release-triggered reference to 38.52–39.74 rad/s in the pre-release candidate. Simulated peak vertical aero force ranged 35.92–39.36 N. These numbers are not calibrated physiological limits. This one used development seed does not qualify takeoff, the controller, flight, landing, either unused layout, or a full mission. Retain both full logs and failed source/binary and analysis.

The study of Jackson and Dial (2011, DOI 10.1242/jeb.046789) describes first downstroke starting before toe-off in its representative American crow. This experimental clock is only directionally inspired by that observation and is not a reconstruction of its actual measured timing or forces. Counter onset is a target phase indicator, not a measurement of physical wingtip downstroke.
