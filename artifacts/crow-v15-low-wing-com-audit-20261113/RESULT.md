# Posthoc low-wing-effort momentum accounting

The native forward-kinematics observer used the research wing-mass/hip-roll v15 model and passed its synthetic uniform-translation velocity sanity check to 5.6e-17 m/s. The source trial's push and hold arms matched through tick 345 and had complete first episodes. Gravity is assumed -9.81 m/s², the control interval is 0.02 s, and the fixed 16 substeps give an assumed 0.00125 s per substep; runtime dispatch and recorded aerodynamic force application have not been independently established by this audit.

For the six comparable push lanes, reconstructed vertical center-of-mass momentum increased by 0.094–0.126 N s in ticks 348–355, then decreased by 0.113–0.232 N s in ticks 356–365. Thus the early push did accelerate the model upward, but that momentum did not persist through the next ten control intervals. This identifies a timing/force-balance problem without claiming its unique cause. Over ticks 348–365, the difference between endpoint momentum and observed foot impulse plus assumed gravity was 0.118–0.299 N s. Integrating the recorded aerodynamic vertical force produces 0.125–0.297 N s in the push lanes; the remaining residual is between -0.0089 and 0.0041 N s. Similar approximate agreement appears in the hold arm. This is a consistency check, not proof that all forces or numerical effects are accounted for.

Body mass and wing mass in this observer derive from the later research model: the earlier very-light-wing audit describes an earlier body, not this later mass-adjusted hip-roll model. The literature-scaled mass and simplified geometry, inertia and aerodynamics remain approximate, not validated for an individual bird. One used development seed and correlated lanes do not qualify a flight controller, landing or autonomous mission.
