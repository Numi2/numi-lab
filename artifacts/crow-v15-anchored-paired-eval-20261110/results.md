# Anchored corrective policy: development full-start evaluation

With the teacher off and no intermediate resets, three of the four preregistered development cases reached all five waypoints, entered timed toe opening, and remained bilaterally loaded through step 7000: difficult J (waypoint five step 2649; release 4010; all 2991 later samples loaded), original J (2710; 5265; all 1736 later samples loaded), and original I (2782; 4445; all 2556 later samples loaded). The sampled minimum obstacle clearances were respectively 0.07870 m, 0.09427 m, and 0.000459 m. The last margin is unsafe for a robust qualification.

Difficult I reached waypoint five at 2733 but failed during the landing attempt: its tail struck slalom B at step 4032, with no release. The reported minimum accepted-step clearance was 0.00182 m; solver failures were zero. A waypoint completion flag alone is not a landing. Contact and clearance values are control-endpoint measurements except where another audit states otherwise. All starts used previously exercised layouts; I-hard supplied observations for a training preservation anchor. No untouched final-layout test was run.

The flight controller and original toe body were unchanged. Toe grip before release remained intermittent; release is timed after contact-conditioned support and does not by itself establish force-free clearance. The candidate is not qualified for final evaluation. Both J successes and the original I success are useful development evidence, not biological realism or broad reliability claims.
