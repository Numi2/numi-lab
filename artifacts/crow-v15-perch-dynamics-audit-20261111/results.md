# Passive perch dynamics audit

The instrumented replay matched all 1,000 stored reference trace samples exactly. It is one development start, not a qualifying landing. It remained supported for only part of the 20-second run and fell as in the reference. Recorded body mass was 0.45004 kg. All 16 substeps' solved bird/perch normal and friction impulses were observed; this diagnostic uses expensive synchronous readback and is not a production controller.

From ticks 150–449, the mean observed upward contact impulse was 0.08846 N s per 20 ms, near the 0.08830 N s weight impulse. The mean absolute linear momentum residual was 0.000032 N s horizontally and 0.000165 N s vertically. This supports the impulse sign during supported stance, but is not a complete force balance. Over all 1,000 samples, the much larger vertical residual includes the subsequent fall and other modeled forces, and must not be interpreted as a contact-force error.

The actual hip and ankle positions tracked their targets closely (e.g. at tick 400, hip -0.2049 vs -0.2048 rad, ankle -0.0950 vs -0.0949 rad). The center of mass moved from x=0.0180 m at tick 150 to 0.0083 m at tick 350, then toward x=0.0179 m by tick 500 and 0.0459 m by tick 550; the perch's top width extends from x=-0.025 to +0.025 m. The ankle correction reached its +0.08 rad bound by tick 500, with further body tilt; the hip command later reached its own combined bounds. These observations rule against poor joint target tracking as the principal early cause in this example and suggest that balance reference/authority and contact geometry need joint treatment. They do not identify a unique cause or establish biological realism.

The contact-pitch moment is summed about the world origin; translating it to the end-of-step center of mass is approximate because contacts and COM move within the interval. No sensor or controller output was changed. Full raw measurements, analysis, build and exact runtime are archived alongside this report.
