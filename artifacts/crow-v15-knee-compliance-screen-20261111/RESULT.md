# Knee compliance: hypothesis not supported by this screen

The softer-knee body is rejected as a gait improvement. Across the 16 paired used-development lanes (two seeds, eight lanes each), none increased mean bilateral endpoint knee-angle span by the preregistered 15%. Total physics substeps with neither foot reporting normal contact rose from 33 to 438 out of 25,600 scored substeps; nonfoot contact intervals stayed at zero, and all 16 lanes had positive net walking displacement. All candidate runs reported zero native terminations over 300 intervals and zero failed simulator steps. Each candidate lane exhibited three measured left and right swing bouts. Individual paired measurements and limits are in comparison.json; reconstructed body kinematics were checked against the recorded replay for lane 5 in each run.

The candidate reduced both knee position-drive stiffnesses from 65 to 26 N m/rad and damping from 0.50 to approximately 0.316 N m s/rad, leaving effort limits, toes, contact, position-command gait and the frozen flight shader unchanged. Initial dynamics were already affected by the intervention, and changed mechanics required diagnostic policy fingerprint rebinding with identical weights and transforms; this is not a causal isolation of the gait phase, a validated policy transfer or a general biological stiffness finding. The compact first preflight rejected the old fingerprint as expected, the rebound preflight passed, and a runner setup failed before its first simulator launch and is retained as invalid. These results do not validate walking, takeoff, landing, flight, materials or body flexibility, and no default controller or mechanics were changed.
