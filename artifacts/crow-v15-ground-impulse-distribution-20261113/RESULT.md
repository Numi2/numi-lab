# Posthoc launch foot-impulse distribution

In the coordinated-pronation trial, the modeled rear-toe proximal and distal segments (bodies 25, 26, 33, 34) carried about 66–67% of logged positive ground-contact normal impulse in each of the six comparable push lanes during ticks 348–355, and about 74–79% during 356–365. The remaining impulse came from front-toe and, in the early window, foot bodies. In the matched crouch-hold lanes over those windows, all logged positive ground-contact normal impulse was on the rear-toe segments. Contact groups, coordinate mappings and full per-body sums are in push-hold-comparison.json.

This is a descriptive solver-contact signal, not a measurement of a bird or proof that rear-toe loading caused the launch failure. Logged impulse may include solver/warm-start effects. The separate near-0.59 tangent-to-normal ratio calculation in analysis.json does not establish slip or a friction-law threshold. Prior retained geometry from a different walking setup found front toes above the ground during early rear-toe support; prior frozen-pose tests found increasing ankle angle could lower a front toe relative to the rear. Those observations motivate, but do not prove the outcome of, a matched foot-placement intervention.
