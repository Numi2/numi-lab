# Corvid whole-body motion references and model implications

These are related-species references for exploratory hypotheses and comparison, not American crow measurements or substitutes for validation on real birds.

* Stoessel and Fischer, 2012, [Comparative intralimb coordination in avian bipedal locomotion](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.070458), used high-speed biplanar X-ray video to measure locomotion of a jackdaw (Corvus monedula) and two ground-dwelling birds. They found similarities in fore-aft limb coordination but distinct transverse/frontal motion and greater hip mobility in the jackdaw. The paper's findings support looking at the coordinated three-dimensional leg and pelvis, but do not provide American-crow force or kinematic ground truth. The abstract alone is insufficient to extract quantitative jackdaw ranges or exact phase trajectories.
* Meilak et al., 2021, [3D magpie hindlimb function during takeoff](https://doi.org/10.3389/fbioe.2021.676894), constructed a 3D musculoskeletal model informed by one magpie's bone scan, existing magpie muscle data and registered carrion-crow histology. Its reported modeled muscle action is three-dimensional. The joint range used for the modeled leap came from zebra finch and diamond dove XROMM studies, not measured magpie or American-crow takeoff; do not transplant those ranges as crow measurements.
* Verstappen et al., 2000, [black-billed magpie walking, running and hopping](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.203.14.2159), provides a related corvid gait and toe-contact ordering comparison; its surface, species, anatomy and digit assignment must be considered before mapping to the simplified v15 contact groups.
* [Meilak et al. preprint](https://doi.org/10.1101/2021.11.19.469279) includes six force-plate trials from one carrion crow, and is separately discussed in the takeoff-force comparison. Its takeoff force traces and exact filtering are not available in this repository; bodyweight-normalized summary values alone cannot qualify simulation dynamics.

The current v15 experimental host adds a physical hip-roll link to each existing hip joint; it is not a full anatomically validated crow 3D hip or muscle-tendon model. The simulation's trunk, feet, toes, neck and dynamics must be assessed as a coupled system: no data here authorize a neck-stretch cycle, particular joint angles, or a torque profile. The existing finite-sized contact shapes, joint geometry and effective drives remain substantial modeling assumptions. Any future parameter change must retain the successful and failed physical traces, be tested on independent starts, and preserve a separate distinction between 3D numerical motion and biological validation.
