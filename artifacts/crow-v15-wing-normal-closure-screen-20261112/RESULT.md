# Wing-normal closure sensitivity produces opposite yaw in a development screen

This research-only variant blends the direction of the existing empirical stroke-force term toward the instantaneous mirrored wing surface normal. Its magnitude and other uncalibrated coefficients remain unchanged, and the altered law applies throughout each candidate trajectory. Two baseline lanes exactly matched 2,400 endpoint states and 38,432 logged contact substeps in the prior run. All eight lanes reached the 24 s screen horizon on one used seed in the obstacle-free model; no nonfoot ground contact was logged. Toe limits were monitored, but the host did not emit the requested body/wing joint speed stream, so those joint limits cannot be inferred from this run.

With fixed +0.20/-0.20 equal-sign pronation commands after 12 s, the 0.35 normal-blend lanes changed body yaw by -0.420/+0.168 rad, and the 0.60 lanes by -0.603/+0.398 rad from 12–24 s. The prior unblended paired command screen changed yaw in the same negative direction for both signs. These are different lanes and modified aerodynamics from the start, not matched-start command counterfactuals. The measured maximum absolute torso rolls in the 0.35 and 0.60 lanes were only 0.0010–0.0016 rad, a potential realism limitation, and trajectory headings do not perfectly follow yaw. The experiment shows model sensitivity and motivates same-lane reversed-command controls; it does not establish realistic crow aerodynamics, flight-control qualification, a frozen controller, waypoint navigation or landing.
