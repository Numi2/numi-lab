# Strong differential wing pronation: rejected

The stronger sensitivity screen used normalized residuals 0, 0.60 and 1.00 for alternating +90/-90 degree demands on the previously used obstacle-free development seed. It exactly matched the earlier bank-feedback run's 48 logged q/v/action samples before 9 s. All six first episodes reached 16 s without reported physical termination, contact overflow, missing contact substeps, or toe-speed caps after interval 400; two had caps earlier in the assisted launch.

Final heading errors were 1.466–1.581 rad against 1.571-rad targets. The larger input did not establish a turn; one strong-input lane also fell to a sampled 0.506 m root height. The sampled wing-pronation positions differed more strongly from the prior bank-feedback experiment, but that experiment had a different intervention and cannot isolate the causal effect of this input. These results justify rejecting the tested large residuals, not claiming that real birds lack pronation steering or that every possible wing controller fails.

This short run removes only the two identified direct body-moment actuators after 9 s and retains earlier launch assistance and other simplified aerodynamic closures. The development seed and sparse samples cannot qualify flight, continuous clearance, or biological realism.
