# Fixed-state aerodynamic sideslip sensitivity

The registered bank and sweep tests produced significant sideslip but insufficient yaw. Posthoc modeled damping estimates oppose most observed aerodynamic yaw moment. Lovebird and flapping-ornithopter experiments report both slip-reducing torque and yaw-rate damping; they do not establish crow parameters. Test the current research model's own aerodynamic primitive at retained physically reached in-flight states, without changing the live bird or its parameters.

Extract both original bank-test lanes, all 16 logged substeps at every fourth control tick from 650 through 1150. Re-evaluate the original compiled flapping-wing kernel at those exact q/v states under zero wind and under matched world-y winds of +0.10 and -0.10 m/s. Separately perturb the input yaw angular rate by +0.10 and -0.10 rad/s with zero wind. Keep all model parameters, q and all other v components unchanged. Compute moments about the whole-body COM from native FK. Require the zero-wind force and torque for all four observed aero bodies to match retained kernel observations before interpreting any differences; record inputs, executable, metallib and model hashes. Retain any mismatch or tool failure. This is an instantaneous fixed-state sensitivity, not a gust-recovery flight, physical validation, calibration, or evidence for adding forces. Do not use unused layouts or seeds.

Before interpreting perturbations, require all 4,032 retained states and all four body wrench vectors to match the zero-wind observer with maximum absolute component error at most 1e-5 in their recorded units (N or N m), with complete 96,768 component comparisons and no duplicate output. This is a numerical instrumentation threshold, not a physical-accuracy claim.
