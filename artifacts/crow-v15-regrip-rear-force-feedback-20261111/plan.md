# Isolating rear-toe force feedback

In the 15-candidate force-feedback screen, changing both front and rear regulators together weakened regrip. In supported examples the released rear toe also approached its 0.85-rad target cap slowly and carried much less opposing force than the reference, while front load and bilateral load balance changed. This observation does not by itself identify causation. Test only the hypothesized detector issue: leave front-toe regulation exactly at the baseline normal-cone law and switch the two rear toes to the measured signed horizontal impulse after the released foot returns. Keep the same 0.001/0.005 N s low/high thresholds and 15 bounded rear relief/reclosure rate pairs as the previous screen, plus an unchanged reference. All other physical control and the 16-substep body are unchanged.

Require exact reference reproduction for 1,500 states, candidate parity before the feedback switch, no non-foot ground contact on any substep, at least three consecutive physically clear and unloaded left-foot release poses with the right foot carrying at least half body weight, then 100 final consecutive samples of actual bilateral opposing horizontal force and support. Enforce the existing 0.5 mm maximum independent accepted-pose overlap during all opposing-force samples, bounded tilt and speed, and no native failed steps. Report original cone detection separately. Any survivor is an exploratory one-start result requiring independent frozen replay, other foot, perturbations, approach, time refinement and whole-route trials. Do not use reserved layouts.
