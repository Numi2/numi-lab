# Remove walking rear-toe swing during launch: failed screens

The candidate removed the rear-toe walking override at 6.96 s while retaining the preceding walk and the 160 ms ankle-inclusive leg push. The logging-only rerun used the previously field-parity-tested drive-target observer. All eight first episodes reached 400 steps; recorded state, action and contact fields matched the ankle baseline and active crouch through step 345, including contact substeps 335–345. Required contact/work records and 368 target rows were present, with no logged errors or overflow.

Rear proximal toe target samples for both sides were zero throughout steps 348–372 in all six comparable lanes. The intervention therefore removed that observed walking request in this window. Nonetheless every comparable lane had 5–7 toe-speed-threshold substeps; 74 individual toe-speed events >=11.99 rad/s were recorded, all on rear proximal toes. This does not directly prove solver clipping, and contradicts the hypothesis that removing this walking request alone would reduce threshold events to the preregistered screen.

All six lanes failed the impulse and lift screens. Their signed foot impulse over steps 348–375 was 0.0207–0.1466 Ns below the active crouch, and peak root rise was 0.0252–0.0414 m. Lanes 2, 4, 5 and 6 had three consecutively unloaded control intervals; lanes 3 and 7 did not. Exploratory native FK reports COM vertical velocity at first release of 0.196–0.353 m/s for those four, and peak COM rise of 0.0254–0.0404 m. Some control-lane measurements differ and are retained but not included as comparable successes.

These are correlated lanes on one exposed development seed. The long impulse comparison crosses a crouch transition; passive aerodynamics, earlier body moments and solver effects remain. The candidate is not promoted, and the target observation does not establish biological realism or reliable isolated leg-powered launch, flight, landing or navigation.
