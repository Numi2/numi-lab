# Matched-approach toe limit switch: sensitivity observed, no launch improvement

This diagnostic host deliberately overrides all 16 toe velocity limits after the model fingerprint is compiled. It is an instrument for a causal sensitivity test and is not a valid production body, a valid frozen-policy qualification, or biological evidence. The original 12 rad/s body and policy are unchanged outside this process.

GPU readback confirmed all 16 limits were 12 before and 24 after the step-348 switch, and remained 24 at sampled steps 349, 360 and 375. All eight first episodes completed 400 steps; the modified and original runs had exact equality of the recorded endpoint state/action/contact fields through step 345 and contact substeps 335–345, with 368 target samples and no logged errors or overflow. This does not establish equality of unrecorded hidden state.

In every comparable lane, the post-substep maximum toe speed exceeded 12.1 rad/s after the switch (15.0–24.0 rad/s versus 12.0 rad/s in the original run), meeting the prespecified sensitivity prediction. Nevertheless, maximum root rises were unchanged to recorded precision in all six lanes (0.0252–0.0414 m), and the first three-unloaded-interval result was unchanged in each lane: lanes 2, 4, 5 and 6 unloaded, 3 and 7 did not. Signed upward foot-impulse changes over steps 348–375 ranged from about -0.0016 to +0.0373 Ns versus the original run. Thus this one used-seed matched test does not support the 12 rad/s cap as the explanation for the failed launch screens, despite proving that the post-step toe speeds are sensitive to it.

The six correlated lanes are not independent experiments, the current model and friction are not biologically calibrated, and passive aerodynamics and earlier direct moments remain. These results do not establish isolated leg-powered takeoff or qualify flight, navigation or landing. See analysis.json, all raw records, binary/source hashes, predeclared plan and the failed always-24 history for reproducibility.
