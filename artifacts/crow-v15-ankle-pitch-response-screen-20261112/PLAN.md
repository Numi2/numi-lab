# Contact-conditioned ankle pitch response: controlled stationary bench

The retained landing trajectories diverge in both pitch directions despite grip; prior hip pitch feedback did not pass both signs in a two-start bench. A testable alternative is that bounded ankle drive could change the balance response through foot/perch contact, without an external body torque. Use the same v15-derived stationary-perch bench, geometry, 32 substeps, solver, and +0.04/-0.04 rad initial pitch states as the archived zero-gain reference. The eight lanes are four deterministic duplicates per sign, not eight independent initial conditions. Keep existing hip balance and toe contact controls unchanged. Add only a contact-gated ankle position correction of gain +0.08 or -0.08 times (-pitch - 0.4 pitch rate), linearly ramped from intervals 10 through 60 and bounded to +/-0.12 rad. Compare both arms to the zero-gain reference at every state through interval 10 and preserve errors and attempted rollbacks.

Require all intervals present, no solver rollback, overflow, nonfoot contacts or toe speed cap, no root-height fall, bilateral opposing-normal support and absolute pitch at most 0.25 rad throughout the last 100 of 550 intervals for both start signs. An improvement in this constructed bench does not establish a successful landing, release, a controller for arbitrary starts, or real-bird muscle actuation. No gain is integrated into the journey unless the gate passes and a new full-sequence test supports it.
