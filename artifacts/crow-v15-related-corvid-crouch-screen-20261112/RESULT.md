# Whole-leg crouch screen: candidate, not qualified

Increasing the experimental whole-leg crouch parameter was tested on two previously used seeds with 0.40, 0.55, 0.70 and 0.85 rad settings, two lanes per setting. The 0.40 control lanes exactly reproduced their corresponding baseline for all 300 sampled endpoints and 4,800 contact substeps. Every first episode completed six seconds without reported termination or trace error. No toe-speed-cap substeps or nonfoot ground impulse were recorded in the 4–6 s walking window.

The qualifying recovery windows in the 0.55 lanes had modeled geometric knee minima of approximately 105.3–105.6 degrees, versus approximately 113.8–113.9 degrees in 0.40 lanes. There were no both-feet-unloaded substeps in the 0.40 and 0.55 lanes. Each 0.70 lane had 25–26 unloaded substeps, and each 0.85 lane had 11–12. At 0.55 the per-foot sampled p95 contact-point-speed ratio against its own lane baseline ranged 0.75–1.57 across both seeds; one foot on seed 20261126 exceeded the 1.5 comparison threshold. The p95 pooled over the mixed settings decreased, but that pooled value must not conceal the individual failure. Point speed is not integrated slip, and an unloaded substep by itself does not prove a fall.

This small screen suggests a more flexible crouch might be useful, but it has not passed the contact comparison, full gait, takeoff or flight tests and is not promoted. The knee minima are from a small, contact-selected set using model joint anchors and cannot be directly equated with the related-magpie video statistics: species, speed, surface, perspective, segmentation and sample size differ. Larger crouches excluded many cycles and destabilized support; do not infer biological accuracy from numeric proximity. A separate full walking-to-flight integration test is retained as an exploratory follow-up.
