# Contact-gated flight leg tuck: limited development screen

The flight-toe candidate was extended to actuate the hip, knee and ankle through the same gradual, physical liftoff ramp. In five obstacle-free bounded-command first episodes, all ran 1600 intervals without physical termination or a ground impulse after interval 400. Four met the strict screen; lane four retained the same nine initial-settling toe speed-limit samples as the comparison. There were no toe speed-limit samples after flight began. Replay lane one matched the reference q/v, actor observations and recorded actions through the first 370 frames, before the ramp, and the physical toes curled at frame 450.

At frame 450 the left foot was about 8 mm higher relative to the root in world vertical coordinates than in the reference replay. That single-frame measurement does not establish a natural tuck or camera visibility; visual review and wider motion qualification remain outstanding. Raw height/speed tracking errors in flight-analysis.json use uncorrected command coordinates and are not valid exact tracking metrics. Commands replace the actor, obstacles are disabled, and final approach, release, landing and biological calibration are untested. The frozen low-level flight source is unchanged.
