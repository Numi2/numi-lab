# Matched leg-extension contact impulse during launch

For the previously archived exact-prefix eight-lane comparison, sum the vertical component of total foot contact impulse over control steps 348–355 (0.16 s) and compare the root vertical velocity at step 355. Extension produced 0.702–0.717 N s versus 0.590–0.658 N s with powered crouch holding; root vertical velocity was 0.445–0.481 m/s versus 0.238–0.350 m/s. The extension arm had higher impulse and upward speed in each matched lane. Other windows and lane-level values are retained in analysis.json. These starts and layout were used for development.

The vertical foot impulse includes both normal and tangential components, and the resultant contains gravity, changing contacts, active wings, posture control and other dynamics. The control actively holds the legs rather than disabling them. This is evidence of a leg-command contribution to simulated launch, not an estimate of muscle work, proof of legs alone achieving liftoff, real-crow force calibration, or full takeoff qualification. It does not qualify flight, navigation or landing.

Correction: the observer includes normal and tangential components in its vertical impulse. Earlier versions incorrectly called this normal-only; the numerical values and paired comparison are unchanged.
