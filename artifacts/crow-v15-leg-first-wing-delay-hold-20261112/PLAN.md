# Matched leg-first wing-delay screen

The matched integrated takeoff comparison found that the powered-crouch hold generated more early vertical foot contact impulse and upward speed than the leg extension command, with wings already active. Test the hypothesis that wing onset is too early for this modeled leg push: keep the same .16 s extension ramp, no toe floor, same aero and controller, and delay the .05 s wing command ramp by 0, .04, .08 or .12 s after push onset, two closely related lanes per setting. A second otherwise identical binary replaces the extension target with the powered-crouch target, so each lane and seed is matched against its own control. The delay is an experimental control parameter, not claimed biological timing, and no extra forces are injected. Both seed runs retain all outcomes.

Demand complete state/contact prefix parity before the push can act, full observer coverage for driven joints during steps 535–625 and ground/toe contacts through 625, no joint/toe speed-limit hits, solver errors, overflow or nonfoot ground impulses, and no both-foot-unloaded substep during the transition 501–515. Compare per-lane vertical foot contact impulse and root vertical velocity over 549–556, 549–560, 549–575 and 549–600; report missing/censored observations and post-contact flight separately. Matched positive differences would show a leg-target contribution in the coupled simulator, not independently validated muscle work, legs-only launch, biological accuracy, flight or mission qualification.

For development selection, require each of the four matched starts for a delay setting (two lanes on each seed) to show at least +0.020 N s vertical foot contact impulse in steps 549–556 and +0.030 m/s root vertical velocity at step 556 versus its own crouch-hold control, with the observer and safety screens passing. These are pragmatic detection thresholds, not biological targets. Failed or censored starts reject the setting; no pooled averaging can mask one.
