# Contact-triggered grip from reached touchdown

The full guided trajectories fail to sustain the first impulse and often strike the perch with the shank. This independent probe asks whether the modeled feet can catch the narrow actual perch from the retained state just before its first bilateral toe impact, without a flight controller or forces injected. Resume the same four matched initial state perturbations and static perched scene as the compliance screen; use the unchanged baseline contact material. Hold non-toe target positions at the physically reached pose; command open toes until the first measured positive normal impulse on each respective foot. Compare open control, immediate front-and-rear closure, and rear-first closure followed 50 ms later by the front toes. The toe target ramps no faster than 0.045 rad per 5 ms and stays within 0.85 rad; release after 0.5 seconds. Sample all contacts every 5 ms for 0.8 seconds. Do not carry a route teacher, alter gravity, teleport, or change friction.

Screen for at least three of four perturbations with at least three consecutive 5 ms samples of opposing front and rear toe contact normals on both sides before release, no nonfoot contact, and vertical support during grip; after release require toes to return within 0.05 rad while the bird remains supported. Track body height, velocity and all contact impulses. Probe success would only justify further continuous-sequence investigation, not establish real-bird mechanics, reliable full landings, or autonomous navigation. A failed prediction is retained.
