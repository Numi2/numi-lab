# Perch support and body balance in development traces

The two reference falls and all five falls in the altered descent trial show support moving farther from the articulated center of mass as the body tips. In reference lane 6, the sampled pitch rose from 0.30 to 1.19 rad in the last 100 control intervals while the vertical-load-weighted contact center shifted from roughly 32 mm to 168 mm behind the body center of mass. Reference lane 0 and the candidate lane 3 show the same forward-tipping pattern. Four candidate falls tipped in the opposite direction: their support centers moved ahead of the center of mass as pitch became negative. The corresponding estimated contact pitch impulse increasingly had the same sign as the tipping motion. Lane 5 in the reference had not yet failed by the horizon; lane 7 had only just entered support, so neither is a long-duration stable control.

This supports investigating control of both tipping directions and the changing contact support point. It does not prove that contact forces caused the falls: the torque estimate combines full-substep contact impulses with the center of mass at the control endpoint and excludes aerodynamic, gravity and other dynamics. Positive upward components weight the support point. All included first-episode intervals had complete recorded substeps, no reported contact-capacity overflow, and no observer errors. Independent reference-replay positions for head and both feet at three steps agreed with forward kinematics within 0.00000024 m; this checks body positioning, not a direct measured biological center of mass or every substep. Both runs used a previously exposed course and seed.
