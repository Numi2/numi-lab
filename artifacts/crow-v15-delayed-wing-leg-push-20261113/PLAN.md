# Delayed-wing matched push and held-leg development experiment

The prior wing-held push produced an approximately three-interval hop before recontact, and the concurrently flapping held-leg control also became airborne. On used seed 20261126, delay the existing physical wing target onset in lanes 6 and 7 until 7.56 s, after the previously observed short hop. Compare the otherwise identical push and held-leg variants, using the same current body-wrench ablation, toe controls, wing targets, contact and aerodynamic observers, and policy inputs. Record 500 intervals for each, without intermediate reset. The wing delay is a control target, not a force or claim about bird kinematics.

Prediction: the push may establish unloaded flight before the delayed wing stroke, then transition into sustained flight; the held-leg condition should distinguish a leg effect. Require precise matching of all other lanes and pre-intervention state, full substep observations, low actual wing movement and small modeled aerodynamic impulse prior to the first three consecutive unloaded intervals, substantial positive leg impulse versus held, and continued airborne motion without recontact after the wing transition. Evaluate both lanes and retain any failures, actual motion, timing, force and contact records.
