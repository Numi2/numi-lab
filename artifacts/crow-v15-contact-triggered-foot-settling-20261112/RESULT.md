# Contact-triggered paired settling: rejected

The paired knee/ankle drive shortened the same-lane median rear-to-middle-front loading delay by at least 15% in six of eight candidate trials. Nonetheless all eight failed the physical screen: every candidate had both-feet-unloaded substeps and excessive right-foot sampled contact-point speed; one also hit a toe speed cap, and others lost recovery coverage or progress. These measurements invalidate the proposed control as a stable gait despite the toe-lag improvement. No setting is promoted.

Unchanged lanes 0–3 exactly reproduced recorded state, toe-contact, q and v samples on both development seeds, and every lane matched the original recorded pre-intervention prefix. Rear-only contact bits occurred 29–40 times per side in the sampled candidate windows. The active-contact sensor and independently logged final-substep contact records differed on zero to two samples per side, so they are not assumed perfectly synchronous. At the first changed right-leg drive target in each candidate lane, the prior recorded signal was valid, rear loaded and middle-front unloaded; the target offsets had the expected ramp-scaled signs and magnitudes. After physical trajectories diverge, target differences no longer isolate the added command.

The trigger used a one-step-old contact signal and changed the requested offsets immediately when the boolean condition changed; this may contribute to the instability, but the experiment does not establish cause. Sampled point velocity is not integrated slip. The sensor and host remain isolated research versions with unqualified checkpoint and rollback behavior. Results are correlated slow development starts and do not establish American-crow gait, genuine grip, takeoff, flight, landing or navigation.
