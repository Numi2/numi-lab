# Test rear-toe release while own foot is supported

The prior actual target/contact audit showed opposite-foot accepted-support gating was absent in the two control intervals before the last front-toe contact in all 40 complete development stances. In the preceding retained contact data, at three and two control intervals before that last front contact, own-foot normal and front-toe normal were positive in all 40 runs; the interval-summed fraction of own normal outside the rear-toe segments was at least 0.681 and 0.183 respectively. These are logged forces in this simulator, not biological data and not a guarantee of future support. See support-distribution.py; digit identity remains unverified.

Test permitting the already bounded -0.02-rad rear-toe release when either the acting foot or opposite foot has accepted support, with the same earlier phase and all other state, body, contact and gait drives fixed. This is deliberately an isolated diagnostic: the own-foot flag aggregates rear toe as well as sole and front, so it does not prove that the remaining support can carry the bird. Test that with recorded substep contacts rather than assuming it. The matched sham uses opposite-foot gating only. No force, teleport, kinematic foot motion or collision bypass is added.

Pre-register eight paired lanes, used seed 20261127, no resets, 300 steps, 16 physics substeps. Require prewalk parity through step 200, complete first episodes, no physics errors, overflow, nonfoot contact or toe velocity caps, at least three supported full-foot-clearance bouts per foot per lane, no increase in both-feet-zero-normal substeps compared with the no-extra-stroke held reference in any lane, at least 90% of its forward displacement per lane, no more than 120% of its sole-clear/toe-low endpoints, contact-point median and p95 speed no more than 1.5 times held, and at least 75% rear-first toe-off in complete contact runs with at least two complete runs per lane and foot. Report command timing and support. A pass is only a development screen, not whole-gait or full-sequence qualification.
