# Own-foot-supported rear-toe release: rejected

The matched eight-lane development comparison completed 300 steps per lane without a reported physical failure or reset. Prewalk state and contact traces matched through step 200. The candidate had at least three supported clearance bouts per foot per lane, no extra both-feet-zero-normal substeps against the held reference, and no recorded solver errors, overflow, nonfoot contact or toe velocity caps. Forward displacement was 1.06–1.27 times the no-extra-stroke held control. These observations describe a short used-seed run, not qualified biological gait or a full autonomous sequence.

Permitting the small rear-toe release while the acting foot was marked supported changed the captured command from at-or-after final front contact in most stances to 32.5–42.5 ms before it in all 40 complete stances. At the candidate last-front-contact substep, candidate rear-proximal angle differed from the same-lane, same-time sham by a median -0.00636 rad; this is a joined trajectory comparison, not an isolated actuator tracking error. Candidate rear toe still left after the front group in all 40 runs. Its 196 sole-clear/toe-low endpoints exceeded the held control 49 and the predeclared limit; 132 candidate low-toe endpoints had accepted toe contacts, with sampled contact-point speed median/p95 0.04601/0.06902 m/s. Contact point and body velocity timestamps can differ slightly, and sampled point speed is not integrated slip.

Both rear-first toe-off and toe-clearance gates fail. The coarse own-foot support flag includes the rear toe itself, so this experiment does not prove that the rest of the foot could sustain the load if the rear toe were removed. Body and digit identity, contact grouping and inter-species reference transfer remain uncertain. The candidate is not promoted, and the result does not qualify longer walking, leg-powered takeoff, flight, toe grip, landing or navigation.
