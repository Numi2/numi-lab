# Preload ankle offsets: failed paired launch screen

Both matched arms completed all 400 first-episode ticks in all eight lanes. Logged state, actions and contacts matched between push and hold through tick 345, including recorded contact substeps for ticks 335–345. Applied wing effort respected the bound. Lanes 2–7 tested normalized crouch ankle offsets +0.05, +0.10, +0.15 in pairs; lanes 0–1 are unchanged high-drive controls.

Every test lane met the early impulse contrast and had support in ticks 340–347, but all six failed the preregistered full screen: they had support interruptions before their first three-interval release, followed by renewed ground contact. Release first occurred at ticks 358–376. Peak root rise was 0.0325–0.0366 m, below the 0.05 m threshold. Five lanes recorded 10–20 toe-speed-threshold substeps; lane 7 recorded zero but still had support interruptions and recontacts. Nonfoot contact impulse was zero in the analyzed window.

All logged positive ground normal impulse in the matched crouch arm remained on rear-toe bodies during ticks 348–365 for all three offsets. In the push arm the rear-toe fraction in ticks 348–355 was 0.687–0.756; later distribution varied. Thus the prior frozen-pose geometric prediction did not produce the hypothesized front-toe loading in the dynamic hold controls. This result does not identify the unique cause; whole-body motion, filtering and contact dynamics differ from the frozen-pose comparison. It is a used-seed correlated-lane model result, not biological calibration or qualification of takeoff, flight, landing or navigation.
