# Corvid flight measurements: calibration boundaries

- KleinHeerenbrink, Warfvinge and Hedenström, `Wake analysis of aerodynamic components for the glide envelope of a jackdaw (Corvus monedula)`, Journal of Experimental Biology 219, 1572–1581 (2016), [doi:10.1242/jeb.132480](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.132480). The wind-tunnel study analyzed 414 wake measurements, 389 within 20% of weight support; posture was analyzed for 118 wakes. It reports speed-dependent wing span, area, tail span, body angle and drag. Its tables S1–S6 include regressions and a complete dataset according to the paper. Measured glides in the figures are roughly 7–12 m/s, far above the ~0.35–0.55 m/s commanded development flight; do not extrapolate or directly fit these coefficients to powered low-speed turns. One jackdaw is not an American crow population.
- `Wake analysis of drag components in gliding flight of a jackdaw during moult`, [doi:10.1098/rsfs.2016.0081](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsfs.2016.0081). The paper links a [supplementary collection](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.c.3577826) with force and posture data. Feather moult is a separate experimental condition and must not silently become the normal crow phenotype. Accessibility and machine-readable data provenance require independent confirmation.
- `Collective turns in jackdaw flocks: kinematics and information transfer`, [doi:10.1098/rsif.2019.0450](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2019.0450). Group-level flight paths and aggregate turn timing are not direct measurements of individual wing force, pose or American crow maneuver limits. The study reports flock turns; they should guide a later separate ecological validation, not fix low-speed actuator gains.

Future fitting must separate individual and group data, species differences, glide versus powered flight, speed range, surface/feather state and the uncertainty in reconstruction. Validate lift, drag, moments and pose on withheld within-regime trials before transferring any parameter. The current lane-specific unsteady-force approximation and synthetic yaw term are not calibrated by these sources. No biological qualification follows from citation alone.
