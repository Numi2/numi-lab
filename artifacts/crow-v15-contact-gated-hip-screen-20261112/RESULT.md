# Contact-gated hip recovery: rejected

All eight lanes completed 550 simulated intervals with complete 32-substep contact and toe-velocity coverage, and the initial ten samples of each matched-start arm were identical. The logged gate input and output reconstructed without mismatch across all 4,400 intervals.

With positive initial pitch, both -0.08 and -0.16 rad gated settings passed the stationary support/posture screen, unlike the earlier constant offsets. With negative initial pitch, the baseline crossed below 0.1 m at interval 136; the two candidates crossed at intervals 155 and 194 and both reached the toe velocity cap. The gate remained active through almost all of the failing negative-start runs because the required front-toe opposition was absent. Thus both candidates fail the required two-sided screen and are rejected.

The two zero-offset lane pairs are deterministic duplicates, not independent samples. The experiment uses simulated contact feedback, does not prove why opposing contact is missing, and disables the explicit release stage. It establishes neither biological grip nor autonomous landing.
