# Perch impulse around late pitch divergence

For each of six tilt failures in the earlier-wing development run, we reconstructed logged perch impulses at the physics substeps in a 25-control-interval window centered on the first 0.8 rad absolute-pitch crossing. Total vertical perch impulses in these windows were 2.12–2.19 N s, comparable to the model weight impulse of approximately 2.21 N s over 0.5 s. Thus substantial support persisted as those birds approached the tilt threshold. In that window, the y component of perch-contact angular impulse about the sampled root position had the same sign as the growing pitch for all six lanes (approximately -0.27 to -0.37 N m s for four negative-pitch cases, +0.27 N m s for two positive-pitch cases).

This is not the total angular impulse or the gravitationally balanced moment: the root position is not the center of mass, it is sampled once per control interval, and gravity, body moments, aerodynamic forces and changing angular momentum are not included. It cannot by itself prove that the perch caused the divergence or tell which physical joint correction will stabilize the bird. Contact components, timing assumptions and all 25 individual samples per window are preserved in analysis.json. These are previously used seed and layout, with a rejected takeoff timing.
