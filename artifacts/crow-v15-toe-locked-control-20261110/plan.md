# Constrained toe-joint diagnostic

Contact-sensor isolation and passive toe-drive controls failed to explain the articulated-body flight-tracking regression. Keep the complete baseline toe body, contact groups, mass distribution, 5e-5 armature and flight law, but constrain each toe joint to +/-0.0001 rad as a diagnostic approximation to a locked joint. Run eight obstacle-free flight lanes with the same original seed and criteria. Require recorded toe angles to stay within 0.002 rad at every sampled point before interpreting the test as nearly locked. If flight tracking returns, relative toe motion or limit reactions are implicated; if it does not, articulated model structure, inertia or solver coupling remain plausible. A joint-limit constraint may itself exert nonphysical forces, so the control cannot determine mechanism alone, cannot grip and cannot be promoted.
