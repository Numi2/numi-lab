# Perch support during two initial pitches

Retained original, no-added-pitch-feedback bench trajectories and complete 32-substep contact impulse records were paired with reconstructed modeled center of mass. On the negative-pitch start, both feet carried near body-weight impulse by step 20, but neither front toe recorded the opposing fore-aft normal needed by the existing bilateral grip test during steps 20–100. Meanwhile the modeled center of mass moved forward from x=0.012 m at step 20 to 0.093 m at step 100 and the body pitched from -0.012 to +0.358 rad. It fell after interval 136. On the positive-pitch start, opposing toe normals were present at step 20 and persisted through these sample points; that start held.

The foot placement also differed: at step 10, the left foot center was x=0.039 m in the negative start versus 0.022 m in the positive start, while the perch edge is x=0.025 m. At step 40 the front distal toe center was x=0.057 m versus 0.039 m, respectively. These are simulated model positions and the nearest geometry, not proof of individual separation or cause. Weight support alone does not prove grip. This bench compares two specific, used development starts and does not establish a biological force model, an autonomous landing, or generalization. Contact impulse samples span a control interval, whereas the pose is an endpoint.
