# Bounded bank feedback: attitude less disturbed, turn still fails

All six development lanes completed 800 intervals without a reported physical failure, missing contact records or contact overflow. The 48 sparse pre-intervention q/v/policy samples exactly matched the retained baseline. Toe speed caps occurred in two lanes before step 400; none and no ground contact were recorded from step 400 onward. The wing-load observer produced 9,600 records in its two one-second windows. For one source state, reconstructed root/wing COM positions matched native replay within 7.42e-8 m, with source q within 1.16e-9. This does not validate the entire model or other unobserved forces.

Seven seconds after requesting +/-90-degree headings, final sampled heading errors were 1.466/1.516, 1.466/1.505 and 1.475/1.499 rad for the three paired bank targets. The largest target ended at yaw +0.096/-0.071 rad, far from +/-1.571; the result does not demonstrate useful turning. At four sampled endpoints after the intervention, its altitude range was 0.961–1.164 m and 0.962–1.104 m, less extreme than the prior strongest fixed-differential test, but these sparse values do not bound continuous altitude. Initial one-second mean flapping-kernel yaw moments about the root were opposite the requested heading sign, while roll moments were in opposite directions for the two requested turns. These moments omit other forces and joint effects and do not alone explain heading.

Earlier launch and flight still used the old direct body moments; the two known moment actuators were removed only after 9 s. The screen therefore cannot qualify takeoff, wing-only straight flight, sustained turning, recovery, navigation, landing or biological realism. Keep the feedback as an unpromoted negative result. Investigate force closure, forward flight speed and coupled wing/torso actuation with matched controls before retuning; no controller is frozen.

## Scope correction: tail contribution omitted

The reported `combined_kernel_about_root` sums the left wing, right wing and root-body wrench only. The simulated crow tail is a separate body (index 7); its flapping-kernel force was not recorded in this observer and is omitted from that sum. It must not be interpreted as the total aerodynamic or whole-kernel moment. The per-wing contributions and root-body contribution themselves remain as recorded. A tail-inclusive observer and replay-frame checks are needed to determine the full kernel moment.
