# Foot geometry during modeled toe loading

At the detected onset of rear-toe-group loading, the median signed geometric clearance of the middle front-toe collision-box group was 4.42 mm and 3.57 mm on the two prior negative-ankle development batches. Halfway through the loading interval it was about 1.81–1.82 mm; when both groups were detected loaded its median was about -0.01 mm. In the rejected front-toe target trial, the corresponding first-contact medians were 4.12 and 4.25 mm and midpoint medians about 1.99–2.00 mm. Each value summarizes 56 repeated events per batch from related lane/side trajectories. The filtered contact/q samples may be staggered; oriented box clearances use the ground plane and dimensions in the associated model source. They are not exact solver separation or proof of the cause.

The positive front-toe clearance at rear loading shows that the front group has not physically reached the plane in these median sampled states, consistent with a gradual foot/leg placement issue. It does not by itself rule out additional material, contact, control, or model-shape problems. Native reconstructed body positions and rotations were compared with six retained lane-5 replay endpoints per batch: maximum position error was below 8e-9 m and maximum quaternion-component distance 2.98e-08. The rotation check was added before interpreting results and is retained in the current analysis source. Other event times, body mappings and biological validity remain unverified.

The toe target trial was rejected under the separately recorded gates. This descriptive post-hoc analysis does not qualify walking, grip, takeoff, flight, landing or navigation, and does not infer an American-crow angle or friction coefficient.
