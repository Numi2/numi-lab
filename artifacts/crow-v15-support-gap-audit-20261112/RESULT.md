# Support-gap impulse and threshold audit

The latest full-foot-feedback experiment had ten 1.25 ms samples below the prespecified 1e-7 Ns summed-normal-impulse threshold across its eight changed development lanes, grouped into eight episodes of one or two consecutive samples. In all eight episodes, the next sampled substep carried a modeled foot-ground normal impulse between 0.00186 and 0.00430 Ns, and root vertical velocity at the first flagged sample was downward, between -0.099 and -0.037 m/s. No nonfoot ground impulse appeared in the flagged samples. These observations describe short contact transitions immediately before a larger subsequent impulse; they do not prove a fall, stable support, or realistic landing.

This particular count is threshold sensitive. In the same recorded traces, the number of candidate-lane samples below both foot thresholds falls from 10 at 1e-7 Ns to 4 at 1e-9 Ns and 2 at 1e-12 Ns. At 1e-14 Ns it is zero, but such tiny numerical impulses are not evidence that a foot bore meaningful weight. Do not silently relax the prespecified gate or claim physical support from a positive numerical value. The published trial still fails its original criteria, including other foot-speed and recovery checks. The eight lanes and two seeds are correlated development starts, and neither the forces nor kinematics have been validated against a real crow.

Inputs are the two independently verified run logs in crow-v15-full-foot-feedback-20261112; the script records their SHA-256 hashes. Per-substep root height, vertical speed, both foot impulses and immediately neighboring impulses are retained in the JSON. The logger reports accepted substeps against the ground surface; unobserved surfaces and within-substep forces are not covered.
