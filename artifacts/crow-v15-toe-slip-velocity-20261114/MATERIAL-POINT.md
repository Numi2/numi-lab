# Adjacent-pose material-point cross-check

The native hybrid-CCD path can advance and solve multiple event segments inside one physics substep; an endpoint velocity need not equal the finite-difference average over that substep. This observation does not by itself establish that CCD explains the discrepancy in this run. To provide an additional geometric check, propagate the first sampled solver contact point as a fixed point on its toe using consecutive native-FK COM poses and orientations, and project the resulting endpoint displacement onto the sampled tangent plane.

For lane 0 in intervals [290,300), all 87 qualifying sampled foretoe points moved tangentially at a substep-average speed above 0.01 m/s; the median was 0.0162 m/s. All 30 qualifying rear-toe points also exceeded 0.01 m/s; their median was 0.0242 m/s. Their associated sampled tangent impulses were at least 95% of the dynamic-cone bound. These are repeated, correlated samples. The contact geometry is from the earlier endpoint; the same point is not shown to remain in contact for the whole next substep, and the impulse's event epoch is not independently aligned. This strengthens the case for examining contact slip, but does not settle exact slip timing or establish biologically correct friction. The prior endpoint-velocity cross-check remains inconclusive as documented in RESULT.md.
