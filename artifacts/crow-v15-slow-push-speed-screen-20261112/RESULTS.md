# Slow push with early wings: development screen

Eight teacher-off lanes on used seed 20261127, 16 physics substeps, 10 s, with the two direct body moments disabled for the entire episode. Paired lanes use 40, 80, 160 or 320 ms smooth leg-target transitions, and the same 100 ms wing onset. These durations are engineering settings, not crow measurements. The first logged divergence from the previous matched full-start run is interval 351.

All eight lanes are airborne without recorded foot loading from 8–10 s and have no logged non-foot ground impulse during intervals 340–380. In that window both 40 ms lanes hit knee velocity limits 13 times per knee; both 80 ms lanes hit knee limits 5 times total; both 320 ms lanes hit a rear-toe speed cap twice. The two 160 ms lanes have no recorded limit hits on any velocity-limited joint in that specific window, no toe caps in 4–6 s or 6.8–7.6 s, and no both-feet-unloaded substeps in 4–6 s. They remain airborne through 10 s but reach zero waypoint flags. During the synchronized interval-340–365 sample, each 160 ms lane has one transition to unloaded with no subsequent recontact in that window, and the modeled wings move down before that first unloading. All measurements and transitions are retained in JSON and raw logs. The model's joint limits are from the matching v15 research model, not biological limits.

This is an encouraging screening result, not a qualified takeoff. Only two used starts and a narrow time window are covered; the rest of the episode may still hit a joint limit. Contact impulse includes weight and aerodynamic effects, and leg work has not been validated. Later flight, independent perturbations, crow motion, all-waypoint navigation, toe grip and supported landing remain unqualified. The 160 ms hypothesis requires uniform eight-lane tests, a second development seed, full-flight substep joint monitoring, and matched extension-withheld controls before any promotion.
