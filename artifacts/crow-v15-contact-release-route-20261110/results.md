With the same previously used J seed 3193706597 and the extended 7000-step host, a toe-opening diagnostic command was scheduled at 120 seconds (step 6000). Accepted q and v matched the no-release control exactly through all 5999 pre-command frames. The privileged crow reached all five waypoints at step 2784 and ended at step 7000 without recorded physical or solver failure or sampled nonfoot-perch impact. Both feet met the bilateral-load threshold for 2182 consecutive endpoints.

In the 50 samples immediately before the command, mean absolute toe angle was 0.9795 rad and all four opposing toe groups carried positive impulse simultaneously in 44 samples. In the later 50-sample window after opening, mean absolute angle was 0.01262 rad, maximum 0.03226 rad, simultaneous opposing impulse was absent and mean rear opposing impulse was zero; both feet carried upward impulse in all 50 samples. In the final 100 samples both feet still carried upward impulse and rear opposing impulse remained zero; mean toe angle was 0.02948 rad. This demonstrates commanded opening in a continuous guided run, with foot contact retained; it does not show the toes or feet lifting clear of the perch.

The command was time-scheduled and not selected by the bird. The run is on a used development layout under privileged guidance, not autonomous execution or unseen-layout validation. Opposing toe-force continuity before opening was intermittent and remains below the prior strict criterion. No biological calibration is established.
