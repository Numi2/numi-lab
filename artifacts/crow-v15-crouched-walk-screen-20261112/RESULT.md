# Crouched walking screen: not admitted

The zero-bias lane reproduced every recorded prior endpoint and contact substep; all lanes matched before the crouch began. All eight first episodes completed 300 intervals with no reported physical failure or logged contact overflow. Physical knee geometry became more flexed as the exploratory knee bias increased: over 4–6 s, lane minima ranged from 136.8 degrees at zero bias to 96.7 degrees at 0.7 rad bias, while median angles ranged from 162.7 to 122.9 degrees. These are window samples, not matched stance or recovery extrema; the related-magpie results remain a different species, surface and speed.

No nonzero setting passes all checks. Two representative lanes (0.4 and 0.6 rad bias) retained foot normal loading in both preparation and walking, but hit the 12 rad/s toe-coordinate speed cap on 2 and 6 substeps respectively. Other lanes had both-feet-unloaded intervals during preparation or walking. Across the mixed screen the accepted endpoint contact-point speed median/p95 was 0.00367/0.05789 m/s versus 0.00276/0.03484 held; this pooled statistic combines different interventions and misses the existing 1.5x p95 threshold. Toe groups had rear-first release in 46 recorded complete both-group runs, with 23 other runs missing at least one group under the two-substep gap rule. Contact-fragment sensitivity and the distinction between loaded push-off and low swing clearance are recorded separately.

The more flexed posture is physically achieved in an isolated 0–6 s screen, not a validated crow gait. Differences among bias lanes are not paired because starts differ; a selected offset would need matched starts and independent perturbations. The speed-cap events are primarily rear-toe proximal coordinates and cannot be fixed by hiding the cap. The diagnostic changes neither the inherited joint limit nor the production controller, and no takeoff, flight or landing qualification follows from it.
