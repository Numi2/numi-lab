# In-range knee target: valid command, failed launch

A native inspection built from the replay-checked v15 model builder and linked against this trial's observer library reports both knee limits as -0.10 to 1.55 radians. The prior 160 ms controller requested -0.70 radians; its actual World shader clamps position targets to joint limits during implicit-drive evaluation. This experiment changed only the requested knee endpoint to -0.10 radians while keeping the model limits and other controls unchanged. The change corrects an out-of-range command, but does not establish that the model's joint limits are biologically accurate.

All eight lanes matched the crouch control in recorded endpoint state, action and contact through step 345 and in shared contact substeps through that point. All first episodes reached 400 steps with complete state, effort and contact samples and no recorded substep error or contact overflow. The six prespecified comparable lanes failed both predeclared launch screens: the signed upward foot impulse from steps 348–375 was 0.1198–0.1417 N s below the active crouch control, the sampled root rose at most 13.94–14.59 mm in steps 351–380, and there were no three consecutive unloaded intervals. No nonfoot ground impulse or toe-speed-cap substeps were recorded in the measured window. Generalized leg-work proxy net values over steps 348–375 were 0.597–0.618 J, not isolated muscle work.

The crouch arm's surrounding controller changes after 7.4 s, so the total impulse difference is not an isolated constant-force measurement. A smaller rise or clean in-range target does not qualify takeoff. The test leaves passive aerodynamics, other wing motion and earlier direct body moments, uses one development seed, and does not qualify flight, autonomous waypoints, toe grip or landing. In particular, a toe-speed cap count of zero does not prove no slip.
