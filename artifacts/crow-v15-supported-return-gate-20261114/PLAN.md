# Matched support-gated returning-leg test

The prior constructed perch release/return lost the supporting right foot's opposing-toe contact immediately before a large left/right load redistribution. Test whether pausing returning-leg lowering on loss of right-foot support improves the transfer. This is a causal control experiment, not a claim that contact loss caused the fall.

Use two matched v15 lanes for 450 intervals, same initial state, physics, toe and body commands, 0.8/0.04 pitch feedback and all other parameters. Lane 0 is the existing control. Lane 1 pauses the three returning-leg lowering ramps whenever the previous endpoint's right foot fails the existing upward impulse and opposing-toe contact predicate, and resumes only after three successive supported endpoints. It does not stop toe closure or geometry adjustments and introduces no force or position override. The initial recovery counter is already three; the first actual support loss triggers the difference. The final-substep predicate is imperfect; three consecutive intervals are required for resumption, not a claim that it measures full-interval support.

Before seeing results, require exact prefix states and commands through first gated ramp, completed release and return, at least 100 consecutive final bilateral opposing-toe support intervals, no toe-speed-limit contact, no fall, and no non-foot contact. Compare per-lane support streak, first support loss, return-gate activations, maximum toe speed, fall and full-interval load transfer against control. Preserve raw traces and failed outcomes. This is a stationary constructed perch test, not walking, flight, navigation or landing qualification.
