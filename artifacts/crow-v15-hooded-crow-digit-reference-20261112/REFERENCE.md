# Related-crow digit proportions and v15 comparison

A radiographic study of adult hooded crows measured digit-bone ratios from 68 individuals (35 male, 33 female). Its mixed-model estimates of digit II / III were 0.709 in males and 0.699 in females; digit III / IV were 1.360 and 1.356. These are population estimates for a related Corvus taxon, not American-crow measurements or measurements of a particular crow. Whole external digits were measured separately and excluded the nail; the article discusses differences between whole-digit and skeletal measurements. Reference: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2644774/ .

The current research v15 body derives all three front toes from identical two-link rectangular collision segments. In the source, each link has a 9 mm longitudinal half-extent, so each straight model digit has 36 mm of longitudinal collision extent and all three are equal. There are no distinct terminal claws in this physical toe prototype. Model toe labels do not yet establish digit II versus IV side mapping; either way the center toe is not longer than the outers in the prototype. This comparison is a dimensional audit: collision envelopes are not bone measurements, and the source does not supply crow toe widths, contact pad shape, mass distribution, joint limits or tendon parameters. Do not directly claim that fitting the skeletal ratio gives anatomically faithful collision bodies.

The discrepancy motivates an isolated morphology prototype and independent contact, flight, grasp and landing retests. The existing body and prior results remain preserved; no geometry, learned policy or flight qualification follows merely from the reference. A proportion change must preserve positive masses and inertias, be physically rebuilt and tested, distinguish scale choices from measured ratios, and leave genuinely unused layouts and seeds untouched.
