# Physically reached isolated touchdown and toe compliance

The full-route observer recorded a bilateral front-toe impact in an intermediate substep, followed by bounce and no sustained grip. To distinguish impact mechanics from route control, extract the already-reached J lane-0 state immediately before that impact, including q, v and the actual perch pose. Resume an isolated native articulated simulation from that state with the original 5 x 11 cm perch, the revised v15 body, ordinary gravity and four 5 ms substeps per 20 ms; hold non-toe joint targets at the reached pose and command toes neutral. This deliberate simplified controller and fresh contact cache do not reproduce the full flight continuation, so compare arms only within this probe. Do not teleport during a run or inject a force.

Compare the original toe collision material against exploratory toe-only normal compliances 1e-6, 1e-5 and 1e-4 m/N, with friction, restitution, geometry, mass and actuator limits unchanged. These are uncalibrated computational hypotheses, not measured crow tissue. Use four matched variants of the reached state (x +/-3 mm and vertical speed 0.75x/1.25x), retain each first toe/foot, thigh and other contact, impulses and penetration at every 5 ms endpoint, and simulate 0.20 s. Require no solver failure, toe-first contact, no body/shank/thigh contact in the first 0.10 s, maximum penetration below 5 mm, and at least 25% reduction in the largest one-substep bilateral toe normal impulse for at least three of four matched cases versus the rigid control. These thresholds screen impact response only; they do not establish supported landing, realistic material properties, autonomy or a production change.
