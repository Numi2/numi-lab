# Eight-start teacher-guided repeat of perch hip feedback

All eight development starts reached five waypoints and physical perch contact, with no missing first-episode substeps or recorded observer errors. The retained q/v/action flight prefix through interval 500 matched the prior reference. Four starts tipped; four reached the interval-5000 timeout. Of the timeouts, three had sustained upright bilateral opposing toe grip at the end: 1,315, 1,324 and 1,066 consecutive intervals. The fourth had no interval satisfying the partial-support grip criterion. For these three grip windows, integrated vertical foot impulse averaged 99.9950%, 99.9960% and 99.9889% of nominal body weight, respectively, with 100-interval means no lower than 99.88%, 99.89%, and 99.90%; no nonfoot-perch impulse was recorded. This is evidence of sustained weight support, subject to external-force/momentum and endpoint-posture limitations recorded in support-windows-analysis.json. Two of the three had no toe speed-cap hits after the walking handoff; the third had two. All 5,001 lane-3 state/action endpoint samples exactly matched the same lane in the original mixed-arm screen; it is a replication of a start, not independent evidence.

Six of eight starts incurred post-handoff toe-speed cap hits, so the toe regression is not fully resolved. The teacher was on; this hip feedback derives perch-relative position from a privileged teacher command. There was no explicit release and no held-out-layout trial. The nominal-weight-per-every-interval criterion is overly strict for oscillatory contact; the window-averaged analysis is the more appropriate support summary, without establishing biological realism or the final autonomous sequence.
