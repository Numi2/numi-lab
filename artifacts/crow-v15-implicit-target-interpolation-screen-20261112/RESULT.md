# Full-interval target interpolation: smoother but slower

The active implicit-drive intervention reduced mean boundary joint-position second-difference proxies to 12.6% and 13.6% of control for the left/right knees and 5.5% and 5.2% for the ankles. Mean within-interval proxies also fell. The matched sham had previously reproduced all 26,400 joint, contact and target samples exactly. This intervention changes native motor targets, not rendering.

However, forward root travel over the two-second evaluated window was only 65.3%–80.7% of the matching baseline across the eight lanes, below the prespecified 90% threshold in every lane. All lanes had at least three support-qualified clearance bouts per foot, no observed nonfoot contact or toe-speed caps in the window, and zero substeps with both feet unloaded, compared with 30 in the control. The intervention is rejected as a walking controller despite the motion-proxy improvement.

These are one used development seed, dependent simulated lanes, and a limited window. Position second differences are not measured biological stiffness, acceleration forces, or proof of realistic crow gait. The experimental buffer assumes one-step chunks and is not production-ready or rollback-qualified.
