# Carrion-crow flight acceleration reference: bounded cross-species fit

Use the explicitly licensed 50 Hz formatted carrion-crow tag data in BEBE v1.0.0 (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7947104; cite https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7807281 and https://doi.org/10.1186/s40462-024-00511-8). License is CC BY 4.0 in the source archive. Select the smallest uncompressed clip for each of its eleven recorded individual IDs; reserve IDs 8, 9, 10 as held-out animals before examining their signals. After the initial pilot yielded no flying windows for some individuals, inspect consecutive nonoverlapping 5 s windows throughout each selected clip; retain at most the first 12 whose entire 250 rows have the flying label, with accepted window starts at least 20 s apart. This expansion was decided after pilot per-individual counts, before examining peak frequencies or held-out outcomes; the pilot is retained separately. Selection is independent of spectral features. Do not equate the flying label to flapping: it can include gliding.

Record the source CSV IDs and row offsets, exact retained data and SHA-256 hashes, metadata, licensing, and independently verify individual IDs and full-window labels. Use the demeaned, Hann-windowed three-axis power spectrum of each five-second segment, summed across axes, and report dominant frequency within 1–12 Hz and concentration in a +/-0.4 Hz band around the maximum; report counts and distribution per individual. This is a descriptive tail-tag acceleration spectrum, not an American-crow wingbeat measurement; normalisation, tail movement, unknown tag orientation and filtering limit interpretation. Do not tune to held-out IDs.

For comparison, construct an explicit approximate v15 tail sensor by differencing the recorded tail-body COM velocity at 50 Hz, adding gravitational specific acceleration, and rotating into each recorded tail orientation. It neglects the real tag's location on a moving feather, calibration and flexion, and uses existing already-developed simulation runs. Use identical spectral processing; report per-run candidate-to-training and candidate-to-held-out discrepancies, without presenting a match as physical or biological validation. A flight candidate must still pass the independently predeclared continuous physical maneuver and contact tests before promotion. If the real recordings lack adequate quality or usable flying segments, preserve the negative result and do not impose an invented target.

Predeclare the simulation comparison to the four existing `sweep-pitch`, `bank-pitch`, `flight-sideslip` and `forward-pitch` feedback screens, on the one replayed environment per screen. Use consecutive five-second windows beginning at 9, 14, 19 and 24 seconds where all required endpoint samples exist and no reset occurs. These are developed candidates from a single seed and are not independent physical replicates. No candidate can be selected as qualified from this comparison.

For the exploratory fit, reduce each bird to its own median window peak and band concentration, and each simulation run to its four-window medians. On IDs 0–7 only, choose the run minimizing the median across available individuals of `abs(peak difference)/1 Hz + abs(concentration difference)/0.25`; ties follow the four-run order above. The scales are engineering weights, not confidence intervals or biological limits. Freeze that choice before evaluating IDs 8–10, and report missing individuals. Because the runs are already developed and differ in several control settings, this ranks their approximate acceleration signatures only; it is not a controlled parameter fit, an identified wingbeat frequency, or flight qualification.
