# Cross-species tail-acceleration fit and diagnostic

From the licensed BEBE data, the expanded deterministic selection retained 12 flight-labeled, nonoverlapping 5 s windows for each of eight fitting carrion crows. The pilot and expansion rules, exact source rows and hashes, attribution and license are retained. An earlier sparse pilot found insufficient samples and motivated the expansion before spectral values or held-out outcomes were inspected. The data are 50 Hz, measured on tail feathers, and the flying label can include gliding. They are a related-species reference, not American-crow ground gait or direct wingbeat measurements.

Each fitting bird had a median dominant 1–12 Hz acceleration peak of 3.8–4.6 Hz. A predeclared objective ranked four already-developed, one-lane v15 flight replays using their approximate rigid-tail COM accelerometer signals. All four showed median dominant peaks at 9.2 Hz; the best-ranked run still did not resemble the reference spectrum and had previously failed the turn screen. Its selection and rules were committed before the reserved-individual evaluation. Reserved bird 8 had a 4.3 Hz median peak and bird 10 had 6.2 Hz; selected bird 9 had no eligible windows in its selected clip. These are two usable held-out individuals, not a population validation. Those two birds are now development context for subsequent waveform experiments.

In a post-hoc diagnostic of the selected simulation, the wing joint velocity peaked at 4.6 Hz, while squared wing velocity and vertical tail acceleration peaked at 9.2 Hz in each of four dependent five-second windows. The largest absolute correlations over the searched +/-0.2 s lag range were 0.89–0.90. The existing unsteady closure includes squared stroke speed, consistent with a possible double-rate contribution, but frequency and lagged association do not identify the physical cause. Gravity handling, feather flexion, tag location and orientation are not fully matched, and the simulated spectrum is derived from a single lane. A bounded physical stroke-shape experiment is being tested separately; no change has passed flight qualification or been integrated as a result of this comparison.
