# Preposition the stance foot before releasing the other foot

Recent recorded geometry shows the stance foretoe separating within a few intervals of return as the stance foot moves forward, while neither foot establishes opposing support. Instead of moving the returning foot after support has already degraded, test a slow stance-leg knee/ankle preposition while both feet retain measured opposing support, and delay release until this preload completes and bilateral support is again stable for at least ten intervals. On the same constructed state, compare right-knee/right-ankle target offsets of +0.075/-0.15 and +0.15/-0.30 rad, advanced at no more than 0.025 fraction per 20 ms interval only after at least 50 consecutive supported intervals. Existing slew, toe, balance, material and force limits remain unchanged. The static left-foot geometric search motivates the direction but does not predict the actual coupled right-foot response.

Require exact pre-intervention state parity against the prior matched baseline, valid joint positions and target bounds, complete 32-substep observations, initial clearance, no fall, and 100 consecutive terminal intervals of bilateral upward support with opposing toe normals, bounded tilt and speed. Report if preposition loses support or prevents release; retain all failures. This is a constructed perch experiment, not proof of autonomous landing or real-bird behavior.
