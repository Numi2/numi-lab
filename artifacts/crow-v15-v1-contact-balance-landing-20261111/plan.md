# Contact-triggered landing leg balance

The preceding +15 mm rear-first trial did not obtain stable contact. A separately retained perch bench explored hip position and pitch/ankle feedback, with a fragile reference acquisition but failed perturbed starts; it is a developmental control idea, not proof of flight landing. Test a conservative contact-only adaptation of its pre-acquisition leg law on the same full-start development J rear-first trial. At final approach only, expose root x error to a point 12 mm behind the perch center in a private task-state lane overwritten before the fixed flight law; on each loaded foot (>0.05 sensor units) add to its hip position target clamp(x-error - 0.2*forward-speed, +-0.08 rad) plus clamp(-0.25*(pitch + 0.15*pitch-rate), +-0.08 rad), and add the negated pitch correction at its ankle. No body force or teleportation is added; free-flight actuation is unchanged except the private unused carrier field. Keep the sustained-rear contact gate and rear-first toe control unchanged.

Run one teacher J episode with 16-substep contact traces. Compare q/v through waypoint five and first-contact intervention, support and signed opposed toe forces, endpoint overlap, and first failure. This is not the verified bench controller and does not establish robustness, biological realism, release, or autonomous navigation. Do not touch final held-out layouts.
