# Phase-adjusted takeoff: full development sequence failed

In eight teacher-off first episodes on the previously used training course and development seed, all five waypoint criteria were reached. Five episodes terminated for excessive tilt at intervals 3442–4503. Three continued to the 5000-interval timeout, but none of the eight ended with 25 consecutive intervals satisfying the engineering half-body-weight bilateral opposed-toe support screen. Of the three timeout lanes, one had a 157-interval grip bout ending before the final check; two never achieved even ten consecutive intervals of that grip criterion. First foot contact, phase transitions, bouts, first failures, and all other lanes are in the analyses. An opposed-contact screen is not by itself proof of biological grip or an autonomous supported landing.

The candidate matches the retained localized-route baseline for all 2,752 logged pre-intervention q/v/action/contact endpoints and 44,032 contact substeps through interval 344. Every first-episode contact substep was present; no substep errors or contact-capacity overflows were recorded. No nonfoot perch impulse intervals were observed by this observer, and no ground contact was recorded after interval 400. Toe-speed caps occurred in five lanes, some during the period after step 500, and deserve further investigation; the sampled cap does not establish its physical cause. This is not evidence that flight mechanics or the route are qualified: the current controller still uses direct body moments, and collision clearance and biological flight realism are not established by the waypoint flags. It is not an unused-layout evaluation.

The separate short test documented downward modeled wing-center displacement before release and positive above-weight ground impulse; this longer result rejects the integrated candidate for the requested supported finish.
