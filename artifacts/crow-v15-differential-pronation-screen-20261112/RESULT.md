# Differential pronation experiment

The 5,100 matched endpoint states and 81,600 substep contact records agreed exactly before intervention and in unchanged controls. All eight development lanes remained airborne to 24 s, with no recorded nonfoot ground contacts or post-4 s toe-speed caps. This is obstacle-free diagnostic flight, not navigation qualification.

Differential pronation did not produce useful opposite body yaw for the two signs. The 12–24 s yaw changes at +0.1/−0.1 were −0.162/−0.155 rad, at +0.2/−0.2 −0.116/−0.120 rad, and at +0.3/−0.3 −0.101/−0.079 rad; controls were −0.172 rad. Changes also altered forward displacement, reaching 13.25 m for +0.3 versus 3.53 m in its unchanged control. This uncalibrated wing model therefore needs further investigation; this experiment does not qualify a turn or demonstrate realistic aerodynamics. Waypoint flags in the collision-disabled scene are not navigation successes.

Interpretation correction: the tested opposite-sign action offsets follow the same command-sign pattern as the existing speed feedback. The commands therefore cannot be assumed to be a physically differential steering input. Their strong effect on forward travel is consistent with that ambiguity. A separate equal-sign command experiment is needed, together with a check of joint axes, executed positions and saturation.
