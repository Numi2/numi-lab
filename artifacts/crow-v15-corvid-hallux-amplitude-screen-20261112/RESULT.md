# Rear-toe release amplitude on the original v15 body: rejected

All eight used-seed lanes completed 300 steps without a reported physical failure, solver error, overflow, nonfoot contact or toe-speed cap. The -0.02 rad control lane exactly reproduced the retained own-support run across 300 endpoint and 4,800 contact-substep records. All eight matched that run through the 200-step prewalk prefix across 1,600 endpoint and 25,600 contact-substep records.

Every one of 40 complete stances containing front and rear toe groups ended with the front group releasing first; none met the provisional rear-first target. There were another 12 complete foot-contact runs with no detected front group, so their relative order is not observable and they are not successes. Missing-front counts by lane were 0, 0, 1, 2, 2, 2, 3, 2. All 40 qualifying stances did show a negative rear-toe target by the last front contact, with a median 41.25 ms lead. At that last contact the actual proximal joint angle differed from the matched -0.02 run by per-lane medians 0, -0.0069, -0.0143, -0.0200, -0.0266, -0.0325, -0.0466 and -0.0479 rad. Target and joint-angle sampling are not direct motor torque and trajectories diverged.

The gait gates failed. The two strongest lanes had 5 and 2 both-feet-zero-normal substeps versus zero in their corresponding held controls. There were 186 sampled sole-clear/toe-low endpoints versus 49 held, exceeding the 120% threshold; 127 of the candidate endpoints had accepted toe contact. These sampled quantities do not establish integrated slip or biological thresholds. All lanes met the three-bout and 90%-travel requirements, and aggregate loaded contact-point median/p95 speeds were 0.00383/0.03690 m/s versus 0.00276/0.03484 m/s held. Contact and kinematics timestamps can differ. The gait comparison covers only the 4-6 s window.

This mixed-lane sensitivity screen does not establish an independently replicated amplitude effect. The predeclared candidate is not promoted. The provisional contact-order reference is black-billed magpie walking on thin cork (https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.203.14.2159); it is qualitative evidence from a related corvid, not a measured American-crow toe angle, speed, or precise joint model. Foot and toe-group identification, proxy geometry, contact threshold, run bridging and surface differences remain limitations. Full walking, takeoff, flight, grip, landing and unused-layout autonomy remain unqualified.
