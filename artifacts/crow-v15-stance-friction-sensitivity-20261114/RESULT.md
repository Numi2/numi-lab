# Stance contact friction sensitivity: rejected

The high-friction constructed perch trial failed the preregistered grip/release screen in both identical lanes. The logged effective static/dynamic coefficients were 1.20/0.90, compared with 0.80/0.60 in the archived observation-only baseline; the selected material inputs were 1.8/1.35, so reporting the combined contact coefficients matters. The model starts and controller were otherwise unchanged, but changing the material from the beginning causes physical trajectories to diverge and this is not a same-state intervention at release.

Using the same analysis on both recorded runs, the high-friction arm obtained 36 qualifying unilateral-release intervals versus 43 in the baseline; after return it obtained zero qualifying bilateral supported-grip intervals versus an 11-interval baseline streak. Its root first fell below 0.1 m at one-based interval 378 versus 370 in the baseline. Both trials failed terminal bilateral support; the high-friction toes hit the nominal 12 rad/s speed threshold before falling. Both lanes within each run were identical, not independent repetitions. Recorded 32-substep contact and velocity coverage was complete and no nonfoot impulse was found in the evaluated standing period.

These are experimental sensitivity coefficients, not calibrated crow friction. Increasing them alone worsened the observed regrip outcome and did not fix the fall. The baseline itself is the observation-only trial documented in ../crow-v15-toe-friction-observer-20261114/RESULT.md. Event timing and actual persistent contact slip remain unverified; this does not qualify landing or the full mission. The source difference, native build, raw traces and analyses are retained.
