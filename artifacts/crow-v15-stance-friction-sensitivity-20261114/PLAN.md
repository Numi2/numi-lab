# Stance toe friction sensitivity: development counterfactual

The observation-only stance trial showed dynamic-cone saturation and material-point motion, with unresolved contact/event timing. Test whether the existing stationary-release failure is sensitive to contact friction. Repeat its exact initial state, controller, geometry, solver, timestep and observer with only the 16 toe and two foot materials changed from the existing material to static/dynamic coefficients 1.8/1.35. Retain the same two identical lanes and 450 intervals; compare to the archived original 0.8/0.6 effective contact control. Log the actual effective coefficients, not just material inputs. This is an intentionally high experimental sensitivity condition, not an empirical crow coefficient or a production material change. Because friction differs from initial contact, trajectory parity after contact is not expected and no within-run intervention timing is claimed.

Before inspecting the result, compare longest bilateral opposed-toe supported streak, support at release, longest unilateral stance opposed-toe support and maximum toe speed; record first root fall, release event and any missing records or errors, and repeat the original exact control comparison when possible. A candidate needs sustained supported release and no nominal 12 rad/s toe-speed limit hit to be considered for follow-up; any improvement does not prove biological realism, landing or continuous mission qualification. Retain the original failure and all new outcomes in Git.
