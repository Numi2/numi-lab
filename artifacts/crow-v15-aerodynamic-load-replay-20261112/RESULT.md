# Replayed aerodynamic wing loads

For the matched flight screen, 144 saved endpoint q/v states (12 snapshots per lane per arm, every 100 control intervals from 500 to 1600) were passed through the same corrected experimental Metal wing kernel with a reconstructed v15 program. The reconstructed robot fingerprint exactly matched the flight output (`15100829319006411524`). The wing program's conditional differential-stroke root-yaw coefficients were zero for both wings. GPU evaluation completed and a second replay produced identical output bytes. The first attempt is retained in `first-attempt-failure.log`; it correctly failed when a forward-kinematics buffer included the scene body rather than only the articulated bird, and the buffer was fixed.

For the +90 and -90 degree lanes, the absolute change in summed left/right wing yaw moment about the modeled root at the 12 matched endpoint snapshots was at most 0.000274 N m; the arithmetic mean differences were about -0.0000109 and +0.0000102 N m. These are sparse instantaneous evaluations, not wingbeat averages or net integrated impulse. They are conditional on the simulated model and states, not direct measurements of an American crow. They explain neither the entire turning response nor the full set of forces; the separately calculated root torque includes fuselage and any conditional root-yaw closure, and the report does not include tail contribution to the root moment. The original comparison remains a failed turn screen, not qualified flight.

Runtime and source hashes, all inputs and outputs, and the reproducible evaluator are retained. Identical robot fingerprints corroborate model identity; runtime configuration parity and the forces actually applied at every original microstep are not independently observed here.
