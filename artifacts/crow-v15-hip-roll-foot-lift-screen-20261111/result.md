# Knee flexion raised the foot but lost the supporting grip

The independent fixed-root prediction showed a +0.30 rad knee and -0.15 rad ankle adjustment raised the left foot center about 3.56 mm, versus 0.34 mm for the old -0.15/+0.075 adjustment. With the stronger +0.22 rad hip shift, sampled center-of-mass lateral offset before release fell from 36.46 mm to 8.35 mm in one start, and to 14.84 mm in the other. These are foot-center and endpoint measurements, not a center-of-pressure or dynamic stability proof.

All eight constructed-perch runs finished 550 intervals with no native errors and complete 32-substep contact coverage. The observer matched named native last-substep contacts within 6.9e-10 N s across 4,400 lane-intervals, and reconstructed sampled feet matched within 8e-8 m. Neither positive knee offset (+0.15, +0.30, +0.50) achieved a qualified release. The previous -0.15 control achieved at most five consecutive intervals, below the ten required; none returned to bilateral grip. The +0.30 and +0.50 lanes fell by intervals 252–291; the +0.15 and control lanes had not crossed the 0.1 m root threshold within the horizon but did not satisfy the release criterion. The +0.50 traces show the released foot clear the perch while the supporting foot's opposing toe contacts become intermittent. Four of eight lanes hit the toe speed limit in endpoint samples; other substeps were not observed for velocity.

The follow-up varies the timing of toe opening and knee lift. This constructed, estimated-parameter study is not a complete landing, new-layout result or biological qualification.
