# Coordinated front curl, rear closure and hip approach

The front-curl trial reduced the nearest foretoe gap to 2.57 mm but produced no front contact; the proximal front joints were already at their configured limit. At that reached pose, local FK predicts that a small positive hip change can close the remaining front gap while bringing the foot toward the perch and moving the already contacting rear toe away. Test this explicitly in physically evolved v15 lanes, starting both from the prior front-curl controller: lane 1 also ramps the returning rear distal/proximal ratio from 0.75 toward 1.0 by 0.01 per interval and permits its rear target to rise from 1.15 to the existing 1.2-rad joint limit after rear contact. Once front coupling has reached at least 0.99, and only during measured rear contact, lane 1 additionally increments the returning hip target by 0.001 rad per interval to at most 0.02 rad, holding the attained offset if contact drops. Lane 0 reproduces the previous front-curl candidate. No geometry, friction, injected force, gains, actuator or joint limits are changed. This combines two coupled control changes and cannot attribute any outcome to either in isolation.

Require exact control state, target and event parity to the previous front-curl candidate through all 450 intervals and candidate parity before the first coordinated intervention; full unique 32-substep contacts; no observer/native errors, nonfoot support or accepted toe-speed threshold before fall; and bounded joints. Independently require 100 consecutive strict release intervals and 100 consecutive bilateral opposing-toe grip, support, posture and speed intervals. The early return trigger means release cannot be qualified by this screen. Record all outcomes, including contact loss, gaps and failure; contact/state epochs are unverified. This stationary constructed-perch probe is not walking, landing or biological qualification.
