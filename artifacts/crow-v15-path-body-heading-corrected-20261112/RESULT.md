# Corrected body-heading feedback: insufficient alignment

The two unchanged controls matched the reference through 24 s and six experimental lanes matched through 12 s, totaling 6,000 state samples and 96,000 contact substeps. All first episodes stayed airborne through 24 s with no nonfoot contacts or post-4-s joint speed caps; two early toe-cap events are recorded. Course obstacle collisions and direct body moments were disabled.

For +/-.35 rad desired body/path heading, positive-target lanes had 20–24 s mean body-heading errors .352–.355 rad and velocity-heading errors .121–.128 rad; negative-target lanes had body errors .148–.151 rad and velocity errors .049–.052 rad. The positive-target body yaw changed about +.07 rad over the 12-second intervention, while negative-target body yaw changed about -.16 rad. The modeled body remains poorly aligned, so none of these gains qualifies as a reliable bidirectional turn. Different start lanes and target heights limit inference.

An initial comparison incorrectly used a reference with higher speed commands in four lanes and reported 1,772 differences after the 9 s speed-command ramp. The final parity comparison uses the separately retained differential-flap reference, which has the same per-lane speed and height commands through 12 s. Both comparator histories are disclosed; no trajectory was changed.

This is used-seed, obstacle-free development evidence, not biological validation, perturbed recovery, autonomous navigation or landing.
