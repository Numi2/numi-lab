# Numerical related-corvid comparison of knee recovery

In the retained 20261127 v15 development baseline, 25 usable contact-defined recovery windows across eight correlated lanes had a median 3D knee-angle minimum at 57.5% of recovery (range 51.7–61.8%) using unsmoothed 800 Hz samples. After the existing 21.25 ms boxcar, the median was 53.4% (51.0–58.2%). The 25 windows are repeated movements, not independent birds. The related-magpie study reports a mean of 27.3% ± 7.9% SD over 16 observations from its walking recordings. This is a measurable reason to investigate knee coordination; the difference is not an estimate of American-crow error.

The modeled distal ankle proxy has median minima at 51.7% raw and 50.0% filtered; it precedes the knee in 16 of 25 raw windows (the other nine are tied), and in all 25 filtered windows. The paper reports its anatomically defined ankle minimum at 52.3% ± 9.3% SD (16 observations). The simulation proxy uses a modeled front-toe base and is not validated as the authors' ankle landmark, so the close numbers are not an anatomical match or evidence of realistic ankle motion.

These observations are exploratory and use earlier retained data. Contact segmentation excluded 24 partial contacts, seven missing toe groups and 38 incomplete pairs. The locomotion speed, surface, species and 3D-versus-2D measurement differ, and filtered and unfiltered event times differ. No joint target, controller fit, gait qualification or independent validation is established by this comparison. Full records, prior instrument validation, and code are retained.

Reference: Verstappen, Aerts and Van Damme, 2000, Table 1, [DOI 10.1242/jeb.203.14.2159](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.203.14.2159).
