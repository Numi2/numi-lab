# Sustained contact-driven grip screen

The existing drop experiment forcibly opens the toes 10 ticks after grip, even though the task is to land and remain supported. Test the same development start, 16 substeps, physical toe and foot contacts, and existing force-regulated toes with no timed post-touchdown opening, holding for 1,000 ticks (20 s). The controller is unchanged until tick 85; require exact parity there. Record bilateral opposed impulses across every substep, supported duration, terminal 100-sample support and grip, speed/tilt, other-body contacts, and maximum toe–perch SAT overlap whenever both feet grip. A stringent success requires 100 consecutive terminal samples with bilateral grip and supported stance, no overlap worse than 0.5 mm, and no solver failure; this is only a one-start component screen. Departure release and leg-powered push-off must be separately measured; this does not relax full-sequence or untouched-layout gates.

Avian perching studies observe initial toe anchoring followed by grip relaxation and continued supported contact, not an unconditional timed opening; individual species differ. See https://elifesciences.org/articles/46415 and https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22539208/. These do not establish crow-model validity.
