# Isolated two-pitch neck prototype: preregistered mechanical screen

Motivation: the existing v15 head pitch and yaw share a point; the head cannot independently change reach and maintain its orientation. American-crow sources do not yet supply dimensions, mass distribution, range, or phase of cervical motion. This is an engineering feasibility experiment, not an anatomical reconstruction, and must not be presented as one.

Add an isolated proximal neck pitch about the existing head hinge and place the head pitch hinge 25 mm forward in the neutral pose. Partition 3 g from the existing 35 g head into the added body at its original center and partition its inertia proportionally, preserving the locked neutral configuration’s mass, center of mass and inertia. This is intentionally a mass-equivalent mathematical placeholder; its mass center is not an anatomical claim. Keep the collision shape on the head and preserve all other bodies, joint positions, and collision geometry. Append one degree of freedom; retain the original body and motion ordering by name for comparison. Both pitch axes use bounded limits and drives.

Before any dynamic or contact trial: verify valid topology; compare old and new body poses, total potential and kinetic energy, linear and angular momentum in at least 16 perturbed configurations with both pitch angles and velocities locked to zero (the relocated distal hinge intentionally changes nonzero head-pitch kinematics), with tolerances 2e-7 times 1+absolute reference; verify opposite nonzero pitch values produce a head center displacement of at least 1 mm while head orientation remains unchanged, and verify all values finite. Check 12 free-motion Metal lanes against CPU forward dynamics, scaled acceleration error below 5e-5, with deterministic repetition. Record exact source and runtime hashes and all failures. Static reach and free-space dynamics alone cannot establish realistic head movement, contact stability, correct mass anatomy, or an operational controller. Do not alter or promote the working body or policy.

A subsequent model would need measured anatomical proportions, control coupled to real support and vision, collision-aware head/neck geometry, contact trials, and renewed flight and complete-sequence qualification before use.
