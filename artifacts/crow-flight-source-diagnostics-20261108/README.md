# v15 crow flight source diagnostics — 2026-11-08

This is development evidence, not a qualified flight controller or navigation result. All courses and seeds used here are development data. Despite their names, `held-out-a` and `held-out-b` were previously used for development and are not independent test layouts.

## Failure attribution

`first-failure-analysis.json` records, for all 16 first episodes across the two development layouts, the candidate fatal-step collider pairs, previous accepted root position, roll/pitch/yaw, generalized linear and angular velocities, and 18 actual delayed, filtered normalized actuator inputs. These actions are not forces or torques. The diagnostic contact capture reproduced the selected uninstrumented 1,600-step trace byte for byte, and all 16 first-episode result arrays matched. Four episodes had a course-obstacle candidate contact (wing/gate left, wing/gate right, tail/slalom A, body/slalom B); twelve had ground contacts, mostly tail. The task aggregates termination across four physics substeps, whereas the published contact list is from the final substep. These records therefore do **not** establish the unique first microstep contact or its causal impulse. See `all-contacts-results.json`, `contact-capture-diagnostic.patch`, and `filtered-action-diagnostic.patch`.

The first two attempted obstacle suppression filters did not affect the active collision path. They reproduced the original collisions and are invalid controls (`obstacle-free-diagnostic.patch`, `obstacle-free-v2-diagnostic.patch`). The effective v3 control disables simulation collision on the five course shapes in the runtime while retaining the ground, visible geometry, route and v15 body. Its changed task/world contract required explicit rebinding of the same actor tensors; the policy was used for this diagnostic only. Captured contacts verified absence of course-shape contacts, and selected-lane traces agree before the first physical effect of removing those obstacles. Of 16 matched route-guided teacher first episodes, 14 physically failed (mostly tail-ground), two timed out, and none completed all waypoints; no simulator steps failed. The teacher still followed route commands, so this comparison identifies flight instability in these trials but is not an isolated straight-flight test. Results: `obstacle-free-v3-results.json` and `obstacle_free_v3.py`.

## Isolated flight and correction

The diagnostic flight bench uses the current v15 body, 32 lanes, 1,600 control steps, four physics substeps, obstacle collision disabled, existing native reset variation, a full-flight teacher, and lane-specific straight/left/right/climb/descent commands. It did not test dedicated perturbed starts or recovery. The baseline physically failed in 31 of 32 first episodes, with zero failed simulation steps. In one straight lane the 0.35 m/s speed command corresponded to 2.39 m/s root forward velocity at step 1,300. See `flight-bench-results.json`, `flight-bench.stderr.gz`, and `flight_bench.py`.

Two showcase-specific transitions were still active at 27 seconds: timed teacher handoff to the student and timed shutdown of the wing flight phase. The production change disables those transitions only for the fingerprinted active-head navigation task; it does not implement route-triggered landing. Legacy navigation remains unchanged. Old policy packs must pass the new task contract and are not implicitly migrated. The independently compiled versioned implementation matched the earlier diagnostic correction's selected 1,600-step trajectory byte for byte and first-episode results in all 32 lanes. This is a bounded implementation regression, not flight qualification: `versioned-flight-phase-regression.json`.

Development-only feedback experiments yielded no qualified controller. A small-turn and lateral-feedback candidate had no physical terminations in 32 nominal trials, but commanded right turns tracked poorly and forward speed was too high. With a very small ±0.006 rad/s command, at 30 s the median headings were approximately +0.167 and -0.091 rad versus targets ±0.144 rad, while median forward speed was about 0.72 m/s for a 0.35 m/s command. This turn envelope is too small for the course. Increasing wing speed feedback barely reduced speed and reduced altitude; the higher setting approached ground in descent. Equal and opposite maximum yaw action probes also showed asymmetric heading responses in this specific model and state, not proof of a solver defect. See `slow-turn-yaw-gain-16.00-results.json`, `wing-speed-gain-0.25-results.json`, `wing-speed-gain-0.40-results.json`, and `yaw-authority-probe-results.json`. Candidate shader modifications are experimental patches, not the production controller.

## Required remaining gates

1. Establish and test a useful bounded speed/turn/height envelope, including straight, both turns, climb, descent and recovery from deliberate perturbed starts. Define quantitative tracking, clearance, stability, and recovery tolerances before selecting and freezing a controller.
2. Train the high-level navigator with the low-level carrier frozen. A frozen flight controller for visual navigation has precedent in a different animal/model: [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09029-4).
3. Obtain reliable teacher recovery, then collect only successful demonstrations beyond waypoint one using physically reached arrival states and full-start trials. Add corrective labels on student-visited states as in [DAgger](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/ross11a.html).
4. Implement and verify continuous teacher-off evaluation with all waypoints, physically supported landing, first-failure location, and minimum actual body-to-obstacle clearance. The existing `safe_completion` evidence measures waypoint completion without subsequent physical failure; it explicitly does not establish supported landing. Reserve genuinely unused layouts and seeds until final testing.

No listed experiment claims an autonomous continuous route with supported landing.
