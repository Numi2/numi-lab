# Knee-only recovery advance: rejected

The bounded, support-gated knee timing intervention was tested in a native 16-substep v15 gait on two previously used seeds. Two lanes per seed were zero-warp controls; their 300 reported endpoint states and 4,800 contact substeps each matched the separately run baseline exactly. All eight lanes on each seed matched their baseline in all reported states and contacts through the first 200 transitions. The experimental arms, two lanes per setting per seed, all failed the preregistered gate.

For warp 0.16, knee-minimum timing moved earlier by approximately 7–11 percentage points in the observed, contact-defined recovery windows, and 2 s forward root travel rose to about 0.122 m. However, right-foot 50 Hz contact-point-speed p95 rose 2.11–2.81 times same-lane baseline, above the 1.5x gate; one lane also lost recovery-window coverage. At warp 0.08, the ratio rose 2.15–2.56 and some substeps had neither foot loaded. At warp 0.24 the ratio rose 1.86–2.76 and no complete qualifying recoveries were found, preventing timing comparison. No tested lane recorded a toe-speed cap or a nonfoot ground impulse in the 4–6 s window. Brief unloaded substeps alone do not establish a fall.

The timing comparison is exploratory, with correlated lanes, few complete windows and an anatomically unvalidated ankle proxy; the point-speed proxy is not integrated slip and has endpoint/contact timing limitations. The study used development seeds and an experimental world variant; neither biological realism, safe full-journey integration nor controller qualification follows. A separate 800 Hz contact follow-up is being evaluated. The leg-powered takeoff, physical toe gripping, all-waypoint mission and supported landings remain unqualified.
