# Larger proximal toe preload: continuous support but failed takeoff

Both matched arms completed 400 ticks in all eight first episodes. Recorded state, action and contact fields matched through tick 345, including contact substeps 335–345; the wing working-effort bound was observed. Lanes 2–7 tested +0.17, +0.20, +0.23 rad proximal front-toe target floors in pairs; lanes 0–1 were unchanged high-drive controls. The values and time-driven release are numerical interventions, not calibrated crow anatomy or autonomous grip.

All six push lanes maintained foot support through their first three-interval release at tick 375, and both early push-minus-hold foot impulse contrasts exceeded 0.05 N s. All six then recontacted the ground starting at tick 379 or 380 and rose only 0.0267–0.0286 m, below the 0.05 m criterion. Toe-speed threshold events ranged from 1–6 substeps: the +0.23 pair had two and one respectively, but both still failed the rise and recontact criteria. Nonfoot impulse was zero through tick 400.

Unlike the smaller preload trials, the hold controls had a small positive front-toe/other non-rear ground normal impulse in ticks 348–355, with rear-toe fractions 0.976–0.994; in ticks 356–365 they again loaded only rear-toe bodies. In the push arms, contact later shifted away from rear-toe bodies in the logged window. Those model signals do not demonstrate biological realism or prove a unique force cause. The support-continuity improvement is a developmental finding only. No setting, flight controller, supported landing or autonomous route is qualified.
