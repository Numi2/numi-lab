# Matched gait-to-launch trial: walking support regressed

On the same previously used seed and development layout, a single binary compared the original gait with the uniform corvid-informed candidate, retaining the same 0.40 crouch and physical launch law. Each arm used compatible policy packs with previously verified identical weights and transforms. Before the gait intervention, 1600 state endpoints and 25600 contact substeps matched exactly. All eight lanes in both arms completed 12 s without a reported physical termination and were foot-unloaded throughout 8–12 s. There were no recorded toe-speed caps after 7.4 s; wing/body joint speeds were not logged by this host. The 120 ms takeoff-window upward foot normal impulse was 0.431–0.443 N s across the candidate lanes. That impulse includes passive and aerodynamic effects; it is not a leg-only takeoff measurement.

The candidate is rejected for this integrated build: each lane lost both feet for 26–27 consecutive 1.25 ms substeps (32.5–33.75 ms) near 4.54 s. The baseline also lost both feet for six or seven substeps in that window. This contradicts the isolated-gait screen, which reported continuous support. The flight integration also changes the aerodynamic research model and removes direct body actions 13 and 14 throughout, so the cause has not yet been isolated. The full-substep observations establish unloaded contact under the declared threshold, not a diagnosis of why the feet unloaded.

The experiment is horizon-censored before navigation and landing. It is not a qualified gait, frozen flight controller, real-bird validation or full mission. Aerodynamics include an uncalibrated force-direction blend and residual stroke-dependent root yaw moment. Three earlier starts failed before simulation due to a missing observer kernel or mismatched policy signatures; their stdout/stderr and commands are retained. No simulated result is claimed from those preflight failures.
