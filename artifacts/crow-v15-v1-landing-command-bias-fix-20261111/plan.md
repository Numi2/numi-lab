# Correct landing-phase forward command bias

Audit of the frozen V1 flight law and its command adapter found a deterministic landing bias. During navigation, V1 forms its forward-speed error as `commandExtension.z - max(commandAndPhase.x, 0.15)`. The adapter sets `commandAndPhase.x` to 0.15 and adds `0.25 - commandedForward` to the measured forward velocity. Thus a landing command of zero is interpreted as a +0.10 m/s speed error, tending toward a -0.10 m/s physical equilibrium; it does not ask the controller to hover. The failed alignment-gated trace showed sustained backward drift during touchdown.

Isolate the correction to the adapter after all five waypoints: use 0.15 instead of 0.25 in that translation, yielding measured forward speed minus commanded forward speed. Retain the 0.25 translation before waypoint five, and preserve V1, the leg-powered launch, toe contact memory, alignment guard, wing relief, body, solver, teacher and all other task logic exactly as in the prior toe-memory trial. Run one full-start exposed development-J privileged trial, 16 substeps, 3000 ticks, with the validated full-perch observer. Require identical q/v through waypoint five, report actual root-to-perch alignment and speed, all foot/toe and nonfoot impulses, continuous support, opposing grip, endpoint overlap, release/regrip, failures and sampled obstacle clearance. The 50-consecutive-supported-opposition, <=0.5 mm endpoint overlap, no nonfoot impact and measured release/regrip gates remain mandatory. Missing data, hovering or failure does not qualify. This one teacher trial is not autonomous or an unused layout; a successful landing also requires revalidation of the combined controller and genuinely reserved layouts.
