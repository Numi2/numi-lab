# Contact-aware pitch during foot return

In the stationary foot-release/return control the returning foot achieves opposed contact briefly; the original stance foot then loses opposed support, the pitch-feedback gate drops out, and forward pitch grows before the toe-speed cap and fall. Test whether coordinating the existing pitch-feedback signal with a physically supported returning foot preserves the grip. Both same-run lanes use the original 30-clearance-interval return trigger. Lane 1 alone continues the existing pitch feedback when either the original stance foot is opposed-supported or the returned foot is opposed-supported, and applies that existing bounded correction to the returned-foot hip while its contact is supported; lane 0 is unchanged. Actuation remains through existing joint position drives, with no pose, friction, torque-limit or model change. This is a causal controller intervention, not a biological gait claim.

Before viewing results, require exact state, target and contact equality before candidate intervention, 450 complete accepted states and 32 contact/toe/effort substeps with no observer errors, at least 30 consecutive qualified single-support release intervals, no nonfoot support, no toe speed cap, no fall, and terminal 100 consecutive bilateral opposed grip intervals. Compare release and regrip streaks, pitch and failure timing against the same-run control. A pass is a stationary release/regrip screen, not walking, takeoff, landing or a full mission; contact/state epochs are not independently synchronized.
