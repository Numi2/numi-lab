# Coupling of toe motion and whole-body walking

The short toe command produced three support-qualified endpoint clearance bouts per foot in all eight lanes, as did its paired sham, yet reduced two-second forward progress to 56.0–72.5% of sham. Native forward kinematics shows the candidate whole-body center of mass first differs by more than 0.1 mm at endpoint intervals 226–227, and is 7.0–13.9 mm behind by interval 300. The comparison is on development seed 20261126. Endpoint clearance-bout times are broadly similar and often differ by one or a few 20 ms samples; these bouts are not validated touchdown times. The change in overall travel therefore cannot be explained simply as a failure to produce the defined cleared-foot bouts.

The wider earlier-release command is also retained in the comparison: its forward outcome varies by lane, and two lanes fail the 90% travel gate. Full time series include normal foot-impulse differences summed over all 16 substeps, root-velocity differences, and mass-weighted center-of-mass positions. These correlated observations do not identify a unique mechanism or establish frictional impulses or actuator torque. All results use the simulation model and the same already-used development seed, not real-crow gait measurements or a qualified continuous walk.
