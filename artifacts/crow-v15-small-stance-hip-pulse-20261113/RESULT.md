# Small stance-hip pulse identification

Both lanes shared exactly the same recorded q/v through 211 pre-intervention intervals, then applied one 20 ms interval of opposite right-hip target pulses, with a measured 0.005999997 rad target difference. All 235 intervals had the expected 32 observed substeps and no recorded solver or contact-capacity errors. Opposing stance-foot normals remained present at the accepted endpoints over the eleven-interval pulse and response window.

At the first following pre-step balance observation (zero-based interval 212), the positive-pulse lane's center-of-mass position relative to its stance foot was 0.424 mm farther forward, and its relative forward velocity was 0.0123 m/s larger, than the negative-pulse lane. The subsequent differences changed sign and remained small. Because the remaining closed-loop hip commands diverged after the pulse and the contact geometry also responds, this is a local paired closed-loop response rather than an isolated open-loop transfer function; it does not by itself justify a stable feedback gain or demonstrate reliable regrip. Endpoint support is not a guarantee of uninterrupted support at all substeps.

The same constructed stance, one-interval pulse, and predeclared window are development diagnostics, not a walking or landing qualification. Full physical states, contact substeps, executable, sources, command and input hashes are retained. The strengthened analysis also requires exactly one pulse and the measured command difference before classifying the paired response as usable within this limited window.
