# Preregistered leg-retraction timing screen

The matched wing-held test produced an early upward ground-impulse and COM-velocity increase, followed by sub-weight contact impulse and loss of upward momentum while feet remained supported. Test whether bounded active leg retraction timed around that transient permits the feet to physically leave the ground while retaining upward COM velocity. This is a research controller experiment, not a specimen-calibrated crow gait or a change to body forces or contact parameters.

Use the same corrected research v15 model, passive projected aero closure, active neutral-wing targets, used training layout and seed 20261126, 16 substeps, 400 ticks, with matched push and crouched-hold runs, no intentional reset and first-episode accounting. Preserve lanes 0-1 as existing controls; in correlated push lane pairs 2-3, 4-5 and 6-7, smoothly blend the current bounded leg position targets back toward the same crouched positions starting at 7.06, 7.10 and 7.14 seconds respectively over 0.10 seconds. The position controller, authored joint limits, maximum effort and toe controller remain unchanged; no direct body wrench or contact bypass is allowed. Hold stays crouched. First check the code, target histories, model and library hashes, gain metadata, initial conditions and push/hold parity through tick 345.

Record support, foot normal and tangential impulse, COM velocity and height, contact and toe speed threshold events, applied joint effort and motion, aerodynamic impulse, and full first-episode termination. For a promising exploratory setting require both lanes of a pair to reach at least three consecutive ticks of true foot unload before tick 380, with positive COM vertical velocity at unload, no nonfoot normal impulse, no toe speed threshold violation, peak flap speed <=1 rad/s during the push and absolute modeled vertical aerodynamic impulse <=0.02 N s. Report the same measurements for hold and compare the effect of push relative to hold. If this fails, retain and analyze all runs; avoid presenting a failed takeoff as success. Passing would still require controlled wing transition, full flight and landing qualification, untouched layouts and seeds, and biological calibration.
