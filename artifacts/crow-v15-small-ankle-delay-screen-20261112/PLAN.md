# Small ankle timing offset with substep observation

The retained baseline shows nearly coincident knee and ankle minima in 47 of 48 sampled cleared-foot intervals at 1.25 ms resolution. A 0.25 rad ankle carrier delay separated the minima in 31 of 32 side-lane endpoint comparisons but six lanes failed the walking gate. In their later right-foot windows, the opposite foot repeatedly fell below the predeclared support impulse threshold while the moving foot was otherwise sampled clear and unloaded. Test whether a smaller offset preserves support while producing measurable physical phase separation.

Use the exact baseline physical controller and body, two exposed development seeds with eight lanes each, and the parity-validated substep coordinate observer. Change only the ankle carrier during the 4–6 second gait interval: delay by 0.10 rad of its 0.5 second period (7.96 ms), preserving amplitude, hip and knee commands, contralateral support condition, flight and landing. This is a small engineering probe motivated by related-corvid joint sequencing, not a biological target.

Require matched endpoint state/action/contact and substep aggregate contact through step 199; at least two original support-qualified cleared-foot bouts per foot in each lane; positive forward travel at least 90 percent of the matched baseline in each lane; no interval with nonfoot normal impulse above 1e-7 N s; no new toe-speed-limit substeps; full contact and coordinate coverage; no new solver errors or overflow. For at least 24 of 32 side-lanes, a strict majority of interior sampled-clearance bouts must have the projected ankle minimum at least 2.5 ms after the knee minimum at 1.25 ms substep resolution. Equal minima, missing coordinates and boundary-truncated bouts do not pass. This phase gate is a measurement-resolution criterion, not a species calibration. Reject if any gate fails, and preserve all outcomes. Even a pass does not qualify sustained walking, real-crow gait, takeoff, flight or autonomous landing.
