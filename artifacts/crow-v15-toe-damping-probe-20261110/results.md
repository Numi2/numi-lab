# Matched toe damping comparison: rejected

The simulator initially rejected the unchanged policies because the body contract changed. We generated explicit diagnostic packs for the candidate contract and verified identical controller tensors, normalization, output scaling, activation functions and clipping; the ordinary contract checks then accepted a two-step smoke test. The source patch changes only toe drive damping, and no controller was retrained. This does not make the candidate qualified.

In a teacher-off, full-start development J run, all five waypoints were reached and bilateral support persisted for all 1678 endpoints after the timed release. Opposed contacts on both feet appeared in 92 of the 100 sampled endpoints before opening, versus 86 in the matched original-body J run. Opposing contact stopped after opening, but front toes still contacted the perch, so actual toe clearance was not demonstrated. Minimum accepted-step obstacle clearance was 0.10878 m; free-flight sampled toe angles after 25 contact-free samples stayed below 0.000324 rad.

On matched development I the candidate reached all five waypoints but had no release event and did not remain supported at the 7000-step horizon; its longest bilateral support streak was only 63 endpoints. The original-body matched run had released at 5565 and remained supported through 7000. Minimum accepted-step obstacle clearance was 0.01096 m. Both candidate starts walked about 0.110 m over steps 100–600 with ten clear swings per side; a push latch alone does not establish causal takeoff. No solver failure, nonfinite task state or nonfoot perch contact was observed. Both are previously used development layouts. A waypoint completion flag is not proof of landing.

The candidate fails its prespecified two-layout physical qualification and is rejected. Endpoint counts do not establish uninterrupted grip or toe separation, and the result does not establish biological realism. The original body remains the baseline.
