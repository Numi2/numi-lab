# Higher foot traction worsened the matched development walk

The new contact-observer host with the traction switch off exactly reproduced all 2,400 retained q/v/action/contact endpoint samples and 38,400 contact substeps. Both material arms completed their first 300-interval episodes; all toe-velocity substeps were present, with no reported solver errors, nonfoot ground-contact substeps or toe-speed caps. Independent native kinematics were validated against each arm using the lane-5 replay. The high-material trial changed only the 18 foot/toe material assignments to a material with authored static/dynamic values 1.8/1.35; the effective contact coefficients and biological values are not inferred here.

Across all loaded endpoint contact samples, median tangential contact-point speed changed from 0.00257 to 0.00278 m/s, p95 from 0.0356 to 0.0436 m/s, and the fraction above the descriptive 0.01 m/s threshold from 20.8% to 24.3%. Samples are correlated and include transitions; these are not integrated slip distances. Total both-feet-unloaded substeps increased from 30 to 210. Seven high-material lanes moved forward 29.0–33.9 mm during the two-second window; lane 3 moved backward 3.63 mm, while its matched control moved forward 31.14 mm. Every high-material lane still had at least two support-qualified endpoint-clearance bouts per foot. The material is therefore rejected for this gait rather than promoted as a solution to slipping.

The first high-material preflight correctly failed policy contract validation because the world change propagates into all four normal fingerprints. A separate diagnostic-only build omitted only the world fingerprint from observation/action layout hashing and found those layout hashes equal; the trial used the normal world-bound hashes with copied policies whose actor weights and transforms were checked identical. This corroborates matching compiled layouts, not matching physical trajectories. The runs used one development seed and changed material from initialization, so they do not establish biological friction or performance on other surfaces, grasping, takeoff, flight, landing or unused layouts.
