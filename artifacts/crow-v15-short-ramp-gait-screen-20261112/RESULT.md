# Shorter gait target ramps: no candidate qualified

The three interventions were evaluated against the exact-parity retained sham, on eight dependent lanes from the same used development seed and the same two-second walking window.

* Two substeps: forward travel was 100.1%–106.4% of matched control, with no both-feet-unloaded substeps versus 30 in control. The left/right knee boundary proxies were 84.6%/89.4% of control, failing the required 80% threshold; ankles passed that one metric. Post-hoc across all substeps, the ankle 95th-percentile proxy increased to 120% and 149% of control. This may move sharp motion within the interval rather than solve it.
* Four substeps: all four boundary proxies passed, but there were 452 both-feet-unloaded substeps versus 30 in the control, and three lanes fell below 90% of control forward travel.
* Eight substeps: all four boundary proxies passed and none of the recorded substeps had both feet unloaded, but travel was only 23.1%–41.1% of control.

All settings fail at least one predeclared gate and are rejected. A reporting-script variable shadow initially wrote the two-substep comparison under the wrong filename; the original JSON is retained as ramp2-comparison-pre-fix.json, the corrected script was rerun, and the result is identical. The measures are simulation proxies, not biological validation or continuous foot-clearance proof. The three-substep follow-up was selected after these development results and is not held out.
