# Extended staged recovery: no stable final landing

The requested 6000-step diagnostic used an internal 5000-step episode timeout. Eight first episodes reached all five waypoint criteria with teacher off and no host-requested reset. Six timed out at step 5000 and two physically failed at steps 3919 and 4610. The early-failure states show a backward tip while feet and toes were in perch contact. Observer step and replay indexing differ; retained first-episode records are authoritative. The extended run exactly matched the earlier staged run in logged accepted q/v, policy outputs and endpoint contacts through their common first-episode prefix. This is not parity of unlogged solver internals.

No first episode met even the final 25-interval bilateral opposed half-weight support screen, let alone the added five-second screen. Lane 6 returned near the perch, developed transient opposed support, triggered recovery again and had four 12 rad/s toe-cap substeps after step 500, first at 4882; toe/perch contacts were recorded in that substep, without establishing causality. Lane 7 had 118 intervals of left-side zero normal impulse while right-side opposed support met the load screen, but clearance and subsequent regrip are not established by that observation. Every first-episode contact substep was present, without reported overflow or substep errors; no nonfoot perch impulse or post-step-400 ground impulse was reported.

Lane 5 center-of-mass estimates moved behind the perch as pitch became more negative; they are model-derived associations, not proof of torque cause. The final five-second screen, COM estimates and contact logs do not qualify continuous clearance, biological realism, stable walking, successful landing or unused layouts.
