# Passive yaw hypothesis: insufficient and uncalibrated

The 0 m lanes exactly matched the control in all recorded flight samples, contact and toe substeps, and modeled wing/root kernel wrenches; the comparison checks full logged records, including records after any episode boundary. The independent scalar-law check found non-positive air-relative drag power over 10,000 generated inputs. It tests the formula, not numerical convergence or biological accuracy of the whole model.

All six first episodes reached the 800-interval horizon without a reported physical termination, missing contact substep or overflow. At the final sampled endpoint, the 0.03 m settings reduced absolute heading error from 1.311–1.318 to 1.241–1.257 rad; 0.06 m reduced it to 1.173–1.195 rad. At 0.06 m the last sampled flight-path direction still differed from body heading by +1.87/-2.57 rad, at horizontal speeds 2.78/2.67 m/s. The setting is insufficient for a 90-degree heading demand and is not a qualified turn, route or biological model. The nonzero-distance lanes first differed at the first sampled state because the experimental airframe force law acts throughout the run, including the originally assisted launch. Changes cannot be attributed solely to post-9-second maneuvering. There was no toe speed cap or ground contact from interval 400; earlier toe caps still occurred in two lanes.

The rearward side-force points are unmeasured engineering hypotheses; the scalar side drag and remaining aerodynamic closures also remain uncalibrated. Related-species observations justify investigating passive realignment, not copying numerical values to a crow. Only sparse endpoints assess posture and trajectory; no continuous clearance, independently perturbed starts, supported landing or unused layout was tested. Do not promote this force law or freeze the controller. The raw matched control, candidate, hashes, source, analytic check and analysis remain here.
