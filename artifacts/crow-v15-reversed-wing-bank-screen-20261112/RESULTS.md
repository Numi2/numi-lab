# Reversed wingbeat steering: not qualified

This obstacle-free v15 development screen used six previously used starts and alternating +90/-90 degree demands. The wing-flap residual signs were reversed from the earlier bank screen; normalized strengths were 0.36, 0.60 and 0.90. The two identified direct body moments were disabled after 9 s; earlier launch assistance and uncalibrated aerodynamic closures remained. The retained observer reproduced all 48 sparse pre-intervention q/v/action samples from the previous reference; one reconstructed replay state agrees in body/wing position within 5.2e-7 m. The recorded per-wing/root kernel wrenches exclude tail and other forces.

Both 0.36 lanes reached the 16 s horizon without a reported physical termination. Their final sampled yaw changes were +0.259 and -0.253 rad, in the requested directions but still 1.312/1.318 rad from the target. Their velocity directions differ from body heading by approximately +2.11/-2.74 rad at that last sample, at horizontal speeds of 3.10/3.18 m/s. Body heading and flight-path heading need not coincide during a real turn; these few samples establish neither a safe bounded maneuver nor a biological sideslip limit. Four stronger lanes failed on the tilt criterion: 0.60 at interval 535, and 0.90 at interval 495. In the 0.60 pair the last sample before failure had roll around -0.735/+0.739 rad and horizontal speed around 2.53/2.55 m/s. No lane completes a route here.

During intervals 451–500 the 0.36 pair had mean world-axis wing-plus-root kernel roll moments -0.173/+0.180 N m and yaw moments +0.0215/-0.0232 N m; the 0.60 pair had -0.568/+0.572 N m roll and +0.0522/-0.0568 N m yaw. These values omit tail and other wrenches and constraint effects, so they are not total aerodynamic moments. Their scale and the tilt failures show why simply increasing flap asymmetry is unsuitable. One initial analysis invocation omitted its required arm argument and raised IndexError; the recorded simulation completed successfully and the analysis was rerun with `bank`.

The relation between heading and trajectory needs a more careful test: [Ros et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3250151/) observed changing flight trajectory followed by body realignment in slow pigeon turns, whereas [Ortega-Jimenez et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6660782/) observed passive yaw realignment in lovebirds under gusts. These are not crow measurements and do not justify copying their coefficients. The current simulated large heading/track mismatch and sparse observations motivate testing a body-and-wing-specific restoring mechanism as a separate explicit hypothesis, with force/moment consistency and matched zero-strength controls; no change is established by these references alone. No flight controller is frozen.
