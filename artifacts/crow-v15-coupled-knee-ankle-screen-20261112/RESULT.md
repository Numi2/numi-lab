# Coupled knee and ankle screen: failed

The same-binary matched experiment completed all 300 steps without episode resets, physical failure flags, nonfoot contacts or toe velocity caps, and had exact prewalk parity including step 200. It used eight lanes of previously used development seed 20261127. Against the no-extra-stroke held control, the candidate moved backward in every lane (forward displacement -0.0061 to -0.0433 m, versus +0.0251 to +0.0297 m for the control). Three supported-clearance bouts per foot were not achieved in six lanes. Both-foot-zero-normal substeps increased from zero to 37–362 per lane. Sole-clear/toe-low endpoint counts increased from 49 to 175. Median loaded contact-point speed increased from 0.00276 to 0.00463 m/s and p95 from 0.03484 to 0.05764 m/s, both over the prespecified limits. The candidate fails and is not promoted.

At high positive carrier, the actual knee-angle median difference from the paired small-stroke sham was +0.076 rad left and +0.128 rad right, and ankle differences were -0.086 and -0.143 rad, respectively. These are differences between diverged trajectories, not isolated tracking accuracy. At that phase, both feet had positive normal impulse in 27% of left-swing and 11% of right-swing candidate substeps, while both had zero normal impulse in 14% and 8.5% respectively. These descriptive correlations do not establish the cause of backward movement or support loss; the controller uses accepted-state support flags rather than those exact substep impulses.

Endpoint clearances and point velocities are sampled measures, not integrated slip; zero-normal thresholds do not alone prove a fall. This is a short development screen, not biological gait, takeoff, flight, landing, or full autonomous qualification.
