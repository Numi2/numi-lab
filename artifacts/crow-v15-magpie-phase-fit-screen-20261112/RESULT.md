# Related-corvid phase screen: rejected

The related-magpie observation that knee extension precedes ankle extension motivated experimental ankle-carrier delays of 0.3, 0.6 and 0.9 rad at the 0.5 s gait period. These are hypotheses, not measured crow timings. Both eight-lane previously used development seeds completed 6 s. The zero-delay lanes reproduced their matched baseline exactly across 300 endpoints and 4,800 substeps each, confirming the screening comparison for those controls.

Under the existing 20 ms endpoint observer, 0.3 rad yielded five knee-first proxy recovery windows per seed, versus predominantly ankle-first or unresolved in the baseline. However, 0.3 rad more than doubled each affected foot's sampled p95 contact-point speed on the first seed, with comparable degradation on the second. For 0.6 rad, two lanes per seed had 7–9 both-feet-unloaded substeps and 20–21 toe-speed-cap substeps each; at 0.9 rad, two lanes per seed had 42–51 unloaded and 22–27 capped substeps each. Baseline had none in the 4–6 s window. No nonfoot ground impulse was recorded there. Pooled across the mixed-delay arms, p95 point speed rose from 0.0430 to 0.1042 m/s on seed 20261127 and 0.0447 to 0.1089 m/s on seed 20261126. Point speed is not integrated slip.

The order observer uses an unvalidated ankle landmark proxy, 3D model angles versus side-view observations, incomplete and fragmented contacts, 20 ms endpoints, and a different surface, speed and species. It rejects many contact fragments, especially at larger lags, so the order counts are not evidence of whole-gait biological validity. The contact regression prevents promotion even where the proxy order improves. Smaller delays are a separate diagnostic, not evidence that an accurate biological fit exists.
