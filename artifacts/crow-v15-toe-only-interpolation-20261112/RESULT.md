# Rear-toe target interpolation: rejected for walking

Interpolating only the four rear-toe position targets across each 16-substep interval reduced the first recorded right-rear-proximal speed in lane 0, interval 215, from -0.271 to -0.182 rad/s. Both same-binary arms used the same physical -0.02 rad contact-switched swing command. In eight lanes of one used development seed, the smoothed version retained three supported clearance bouts per foot, no unloaded substeps, nonfoot contact, toe-speed caps, overflows or logged solver errors, and comparable loaded-contact point speeds (median 0.00231 versus 0.00235 m/s; p95 0.02909 versus 0.02935 m/s). These are endpoint-sampled proxies, not integrated slip or biological measurements.

Smoothing did not restore the lost whole-body travel: the candidate moved 21.19–21.88 mm over the two-second window, compared with 25.07–29.71 mm in the earlier no-extra-toe control. Every lane fell below the predeclared 90% benchmark, ranging from 71.8–85.8%. The same-binary unsmoothed toe arm moved 21.26–21.81 mm. Pre-walk q/v/action/contact and all contact substeps matched both arms and the no-toe reference; the same-binary unsmoothed log was byte-identical to the earlier tiny-toe run. The candidate is rejected and is not integrated. Reducing the first velocity spike alone is insufficient to recover travel; the experiment does not isolate the precise cause.

The first compiled prototype extended history but did not yet extend physics interpolation to toe coordinates; it was inspected and corrected before any trial. The retained build and trial use the corrected shader. This research prototype assumes chunk-one execution. One short development trial cannot qualify sustained walking, biological gait, takeoff, landing, or the full autonomous sequence.
