# Whole-gait reanalysis: retain the earlier development baseline

Both seeds and all eight lanes per seed are exposed development cases, not independent biological samples or held-out tests. During control steps 201–300 (2 seconds), all 16 baseline lanes had at least three sample-resolved, support-qualified foot-clearing bouts per side, with left and right bouts alternating. A bout uses the predeclared clearance and support thresholds and at least three consecutive 20 ms endpoints. It does not prove continuous clearance between endpoints, correct stride duty factor, contact-point slip, physiological movement, or a sustainable walk. The earlier coordinated-foot-lift candidate had zero such bouts for either foot in every lane, and produced substantially more endpoint samples in which the sole was clear while at least one toe was not (85 of 100 intervals per lane, vs. 2–5 for the baseline). These are sampled configurations, not time-normalized toe-drag durations.

Baseline forward root displacement was 29–34 mm in the window, versus 15–16 mm for the candidate. Both feet carried positive normal impulse on about 30.4–32.6% of baseline substeps, versus 98.4–98.7% for the candidate. The baseline accumulated 33 substeps in total with neither foot showing positive normal impulse, while the candidate had zero. These fractions depend on the impulse threshold, are whole-window measurements rather than stride duty factors, and do not by themselves determine stability. The baseline's head-center movement relative to torso was about 0.153–0.166 mm in the torso forward coordinate over the window; root pitch span was about 1.01–1.13 degrees. These measures are simulation diagnostics, not American-crow target values.

For each baseline and candidate replay, lane 5's logged coordinates and forward-kinematics foot/toe/head poses were independently compared with the accepted replay over the audit window: maximum body-position difference across the four replay checks was 1.65e-8 m and maximum quaternion difference 3.03e-8. This validates the measurement on that lane only; other lanes use the same instrument and authored ground without independent replay checks. Synthetic controls passed for a supported swing, toe clearance failure, insufficient contralateral support, nonfoot contact and missing samples. An interval ending at the window edge remains a partial candidate and is not called a stride. Touchdown/stride association, cadence, stride length, gait phase, and contact-point slip have not been qualified and are explicitly unreported.

Decision: do not promote the newer coordinated-foot-lift candidate. Preserve the earlier baseline and compare future changes against it. The prior status update that simply characterized the gait work as having no lifted steps omitted the comparison baseline; the result here corrects that incomplete description. No test here demonstrates lifelike crow gait, whole-sequence success, landing, grasp, or reliable takeoff. American-crow whole-body stride calibration and genuinely unused individuals and layouts remain outstanding.
