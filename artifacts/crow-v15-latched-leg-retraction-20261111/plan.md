# Latched retraction for a clean first push-off

The height-only leg retraction met a basic airborne gate only after the bird descended, recontacted the ground and received a large rebound impulse. The threshold also switched back as height fell. Test a latched state transition: after the original contact-gated push starts, the first accepted root height over 0.166, 0.170 or 0.174 m permanently selects the existing crouch joint targets until the end of the flap-suppressed window. Also compare the original 0.04 s push ramp with a 0.02 s ramp. The latch uses measured state, drives only existing bounded leg joints, and introduces no state reset, external force or change to the frozen flight controller. Six variants retain the same eight development starts and passive aerodynamics.

Require exact q/v agreement with the original push through step 248, no failed solver steps or native termination, no non-foot ground contact in the leg-only window, and >=20 mm root rise in every lane. The first interval after step 251 with both feet fully unloaded across all 16 substeps must have upward root speed >0.05 m/s and begin at least two consecutive such rising intervals; thus a later rising rebound after an earlier unloaded/descent interval does not pass. Compare the retained push and held-crouch controls. Preserve every lane and variant. Survivors require physical geometry, unseen starts, temporal refinement, sustained autonomous takeoff-to-flight, routing and landing validation; no reserved layout is used.
