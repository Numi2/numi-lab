# Isolated grip on revised v15 body

Question: can the current eight-toe mechanism generate opposing loads and remain supported without the difficulty of flying into the perch? An earlier isolated grip/release test used a lighter-wing body and could not establish final stable support. This test starts the research wing-inertia body already supported on an isolated box perch; there is no autonomous approach, flight or navigation. A matched control disables toe closure, leaving all contacts and joint drives present. Compare two collision cross-sections: original 0.05 x 0.11 m and experimental 0.06 x 0.06 m, each 0.40 m long with the top at the original ground height. These do not replace actual route scene tests.

Eight paired deterministic initial perturbations per geometry and closure condition, 150 control steps at 0.02 s with four native physics substeps. The closure arm slowly applies the prior contact-triggered toe drive, holds, then ramps release; the control holds toe targets open. Same body, masses, solver and initial state for each matched pair. Record all accepted contacts, state, failure and release events. Prediction: at least 6/8 closure starts per geometry show three consecutive accepted endpoints with opposing front/rear impulses on each foot, retain stable bilateral support for at least 25 steps, release those opposing contacts, and end supported without nonfoot contact or physical/solver failure; controls should not show the same bilateral opposing grip. All conditions and failures are retained. This test cannot establish successful approach, landing, autonomous execution, visual appearance, or biological realism.
