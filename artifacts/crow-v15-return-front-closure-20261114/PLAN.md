# Returning foretoe closure test

In the paired COM-feedback probe, the returning rear toe contacted the perch but the foretoe remained separated by 2.7 mm or more at sampled late intervals, even after its target reached 1.05 rad. Test whether allowing the returning front toes to reach their existing 1.20 rad model limit after release improves physical regrip. Both lanes reproduce the previous COM-feedback candidate; lane 1 alone uses the larger returning front-toe target after `finished`. All other conditions and limits remain unchanged. This is a local hypothesis, not a claim that geometry or force will improve.

Require exact 450-state control parity with the archived COM-feedback lane, exact paired state prefix until the first control target change, ten supported release intervals, 100 consecutive terminal bilateral opposing-grip intervals, bounded tilt and speed, no nonfoot support, complete contact evidence, and no toe speed threshold before failure. Record both feet, geometry and all failures. Pose/contact epoch synchronization remains unverified for this probe. Passing this constructed perch test would not establish walking, flight, landing or mission qualification.
