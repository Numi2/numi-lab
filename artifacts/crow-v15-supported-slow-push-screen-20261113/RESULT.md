# Slower supported leg extension did not pass

Both 400-interval first-episode arms completed, their recorded states/actions/contact prefixes matched through tick 345, trace integrity checks passed and observed working wing efforts remained within the common 3.0 bound. This is one used development seed with correlated lanes, not an independent qualification set. Lanes 0–1 are inherited controls outside the six tested settings and are not evidence for the candidate.

All six candidate push lanes recontact after the first three unloaded intervals; no setting passes the preregistered gates. The 7.16 s end-time lanes have early push-minus-hold impulse deltas 0.069–0.072 N s through tick 355 and 8–13 toe-speed threshold substeps. The 7.24 s pair has only 0.025–0.028 N s early impulse and 12–15 threshold substeps; the 7.32 s pair has 0.050–0.052 N s and 9–15. Some settings show brief support gaps even before the first three-unloaded-interval event. Whole-body center of mass rises only 0.019–0.041 m above tick 350 through tick 400. Root-height increases up to 0.055 m overstate the whole-body result. Peak modeled upward wing force during ticks 348–375 is 6.78–7.03 N; this is a model output, not a calibrated biological force or demonstrated flight.

The inherited substep comparison initially reported a false effort-bound failure for lanes 2–3 because its checker still used the parent trial's 2.5 bound, although the present preregistered shader bound is 3.0. `substep-comparison.inherited.py` preserves that checker; the corrected checker verifies 3.0 for every candidate and completes for both arms. This correction does not change any physical result. The inherited `strict-gates.py` reports the earlier root-height-based gate; all rows already fail it, and the stronger whole-body COM condition in the plan also fails every row.

The longer extension may lower some modeled toe events but loses or marginalizes early push impulse and does not prevent recontact. Do not promote this controller or infer real-bird realism. The retained logs are needed to distinguish changes in toe/ground interaction and later wing/flight transition.
