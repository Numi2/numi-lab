# Wing-wrench audit: asymmetric poses, weak net yaw authority

The corrected observer exactly reproduced all 9,600 endpoint states and 153,600 contact substeps of the previous equal-sign pronation screen. It sampled all 800 substeps per lane during steps 651–700 (13.02–14.00 s), after the physical wing-load kernel, with generalized input states and wrench buffers. See PRELIMINARY.md for the initial wrong-host preflight rejection, which is retained separately.

In the two control lanes, mean left and right wing yaw contributions about the torso were approximately +0.02075 and -0.02087 N m, almost cancelling. Their net mean projected yaw was -0.000020 to -0.000025 N m including torso and tail contributions. At the largest positive and negative pronation command the total mean projected yaw was -0.0000023 and -0.000210 N m respectively. Thus the screen produced measurable differences in net load but very small yaw relative to the opposing wing contributions during this one-second window; it does not establish controllable opposite body turns. Individual wing mean vertical force was about 2.18 N in all lanes. These quantities are model outputs, not measurements of a living crow, and the sample window is not a full flight qualification.

The recorded buffer can include prior physical actuator contributions, fuselage drag/damping, and any enabled root closures; it is not certified as aerodynamic force alone. The loaded research wing kernel includes a strong estimated stroke-dependent closure directed mostly with the airframe, whose magnitude and directional assumptions are not independently calibrated. The diagnostics do not yet decompose that contribution, so they do not establish its share of lift or blame it as the cause. Direct action moments 13 and 14 were disabled in the matched flight; other closures were not removed. Flight remains unqualified, and this controller was not frozen for navigation.
