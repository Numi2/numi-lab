# Support-gated ankle phase: transient unloading

All six candidates reduced each foot's normal-weighted contact-speed integral by at least 25%, progressed .060–.068 m, and avoided observed toe-speed caps or nonfoot ground contact. Their lane medians showed the chosen related-corvid hip/knee/ankle phase directions. However, each experienced 18–19 consecutive substeps of simultaneous foot unloading, a single 22.5–23.75 ms interval beginning at 4.5425 s, during gait transition. Every lane therefore failed the predeclared continuous-contact gate.

These are closely related development starts in a two-second, slow walk; contact-point velocity is a proxy and species/speed differences prevent numerical biological calibration. No controller or full mission is qualified.
