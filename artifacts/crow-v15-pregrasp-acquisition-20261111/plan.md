# Front-toe preshape for initial grip acquisition

Seven of eight perturbed starts failed before post-grip regulation began; four never reached the rear-contact transition and three stalled waiting for combined front/rear contact. Test whether preparing the front toes before or during the rear-contact approach improves acquisition without state resets or artificial force. On four representative previously audited starts (the original passing case plus three failed position/roll combinations), retain the exact existing controller and compare three bounded front-toe preshapes, applied from the first control tick: increments of 0.01, 0.02 or 0.04 rad per tick with respective caps 0.45, 0.65 and 0.85 rad. Rear control and all post-grip feedback remain unchanged. Run 500 ticks per lane, require each reference lane to exactly reproduce its prior 500-state trajectory and every initial shape to match its corresponding separated start. A development candidate must sustain at least 50 consecutive samples of simultaneous bilateral opposed grip and foot support, have no solver or nonfoot-support failure during that interval, bounded tilt/speed, and grip-pose toe overlap at most 0.5 mm. Record all outcomes; any apparent acquisition must later pass 1,000 ticks, all perturbations, release and complete-route checks. No unused layouts are used.
