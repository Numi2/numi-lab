# Comparative avian takeoff evidence

- Provini and Abourachid, “Whole-body 3D kinematics of bird take-off: key role of the legs to propel the trunk,” 2018, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29330588/ . Biplanar fluoroscopy of diamond doves and zebra finches showed a preparatory whole-body crouch and coordinated leg extension. These species and measurements are not American-crow data and do not specify a joint-target trajectory for this model.
- “Transition from leg to wing forces during take-off in birds,” 2012, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22972887/ . Force-plate, kinematic and flow measurements in zebra finches and diamond doves support separately accounting for leg and wing forces and the transition between them. The species, perch protocol and force scaling differ from this simulation.
- Shin et al., “Fast ground-to-air transition with avian-inspired multifunctional legs,” Nature, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08228-9 , and source data https://zenodo.org/records/13326013 . The paper concerns a robotic system, not a validation of this bird model. Its accompanying comparative morphology table contains a carrion-crow row and a common-raven row; a minimal, attributed extract with the original workbook hashes is retained here. Species, original specimen/source definitions, and leg-mass convention must be checked before using these values to change v15 mass or inertia.

These sources motivate measuring whole-body COM, joint movement, ground impulse and separate aerodynamic contribution during matched push and hold. They do not calibrate the current v15 model or prove realistic takeoff. The RAVEN trajectory worksheet examined in the dataset is robot data and must not be presented as crow motion.

A source-constructed v15 corrected-axis mass audit finds 0.45006 kg total and approximately 0.04502 kg (10.0%) in explicitly named hip-roll, thigh, shank, foot and toe bodies. The carrion-crow table row reports 15.76% under a currently unverified leg-mass definition. This discrepancy is a model-calibration lead, not a validated error: confirm the runtime body masses, dissection convention, sex/age/sample and original source, and inertia distributions before changing the body or reusing any policy. The source code, body-level numbers and binary hashes are retained; total mass agrees with the independently used COM probe.

The source paper describes its compiled leg-mass variable as the mass of both legs, drawn from four earlier publications. This narrows the comparison, but the particular carrion-crow row and dissection boundary still need checking against its original source before calibration.
