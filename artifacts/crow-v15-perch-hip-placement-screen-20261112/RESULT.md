# Contact-conditioned hip placement: rejected

The retained negative-pitch start had forward foot placement and failed to develop opposing front-toe contact. Static forward kinematics predicted that an increase in hip position would move the free foot rearward, but could not predict motion with the foot loaded. This native 32-substep experiment compared the original controller and three bounded hip-target offsets (+0.08, +0.16 and +0.24 rad), each from +0.04 and -0.04 rad initial pitch. One lane represented each combination, so this is not an independent multi-seed robustness trial.

All three offsets passed the stationary engineering support and pitch screen from +0.04 rad; the +0.16 rad case ended closer to neutral than the baseline. However, all three failed from -0.04 rad: the root crossed below 0.1 m at intervals 121, 114 and 113, versus 136 in the zero-offset control. All negative-start lanes reached toe speed caps. No tested offset passed both required starts; none is promoted. These results show that static unloaded geometry alone did not predict a successful corrective action under contact, but do not isolate the exact cause of earlier failure.

The first 80 q/v samples matched the retained reference exactly. All lanes completed 550 recorded intervals with full contact and velocity substep coverage, no reported observer errors or overflows; after a fall, continued simulated states do not constitute supported flight or walking. The stationary bench is not a landing or a biological validation. Complete sources, input hashes, logs and contact evidence are retained.
