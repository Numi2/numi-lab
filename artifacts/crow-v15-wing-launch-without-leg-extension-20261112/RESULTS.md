# Matched no-extension-command launch control

The prior early-wing screen is compared with the same eight initial conditions and launch, except that the leg target remains crouched throughout the 6.96–7.40 s push window instead of commanding extension. This comparison disables the same two direct body-moment actuators from 6.30 s; they were available earlier, and the same empirical aerodynamic closures remain. Logged states agree through interval 350 in all lanes, then first diverge at interval 351. Teacher off, 16 substeps, used seed 20261127, 10 s horizon.

All six lanes with early wings are airborne at 8–10 s even without the leg extension command; they reach zero waypoint flags, versus one in the matched extension trials. At 10 s their root x positions are approximately 0.20–0.38 m versus 1.05–1.47 m with extension. The foot-upward impulse during intervals 348–353 is approximately 0.275–0.480 N s versus 0.397–0.589 N s with extension, but contact histories differ and this includes gravity support and passive and aerodynamic effects. These are closed-loop intervention effects, not measured joint work. Wings, holding actuators and passive contacts are still active, and leg commands resume after 7.40 s. Therefore this control cannot identify wing-only flight or establish leg-powered takeoff.

In the two no-early-wing lanes, withholding extension also changes the later controller state; they subsequently become airborne after the push window instead of remaining grounded. This reinforces the need to audit the transition and actual actuator work rather than infer the cause from a final airborne flag. Some early-wing lanes unload and recontact; see the complete transitions. No perturbation, sustained qualified flight, all-waypoint course or supported landing has been established. No controller is promoted.
