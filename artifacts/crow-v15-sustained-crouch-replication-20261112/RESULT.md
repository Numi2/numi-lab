# Uniform crouch continuation: developmental replication

On both used seeds, all eight matched starts exactly matched their respective baseline through 300 accepted state endpoints and 4,800 contact substeps. Across the 4–10 s walking window, every candidate survived, had complete 4,800 contact and toe-velocity substeps, no both-feet-unloaded substeps at the 1e-7 N s detection threshold, no toe caps, overflow, nonfoot ground impulses or reported solver errors, and advanced 0.2612–0.2616 m. Their paired baselines all had six toe-capped substeps and 65–111 unloaded substeps after 6 s. All 16 pass the preregistered engineering screen.

Average ground normal force over the window was approximately body weight, but 9 of 300 twenty-millisecond intervals per lane were below half body-weight impulse; none were below one tenth. A tiny positive contact is not necessarily meaningful weight support. Each lane had 106 intervals with both feet loaded, 105 left-only and 89 right-only by the same detection threshold. These 16 starts converge strongly and are not independent birds. Changing collision-point speed is not material slip; body/wing caps, actual crow gait, speeds and perturbation generalization remain unqualified. A separate 50 Hz accepted-state replay analysis records head and body motion for lane 5 only and uses corrected q[20]/q[21] neck indices. The original v15 body contains pitch and roll hips but not a dedicated hip yaw axis.

The follow-up handoff trial separately shows foot-load gaps after walking ends at 10 s and some toe caps during takeoff; continuous walking alone does not establish a complete walking-to-flight mission.
