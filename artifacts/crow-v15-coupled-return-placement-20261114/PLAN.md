# Coupled return-leg placement experiment

Local oriented-box sensitivity at archived post-release states found that a small positive returning-hip change reduces foretoe/perch gap but tends to increase rear-toe gap; a negative ankle change has the opposite rear-toe effect. Test a coupled, rate-limited target in lane 1: relative to the original controller, add up to +0.03 rad to the returning hip and -0.08 rad to the ankle, ramped with the first 0.20 of return-placement progress, then held. Cap lane 1 placement at 0.20, the exact progress reached by the archived original baseline, and keep the original stricter stance-contact pitch gate and all toe commands. Lane 0 is the unmodified baseline. The increments are a local geometric hypothesis, not a prediction of physically reached joint positions or contact. No external force or state override is added.

Run the same 450 intervals and 32 substeps from identical constructed states. Verify all control and accepted-state parity before the first actual hip or ankle target difference; compare the baseline to the archived run; measure target, placement, position, speed, geometry and full contact substeps; require ten independently checked release intervals followed by 100 final bilateral opposing-grip intervals with established tilt, speed, support and nonfoot-contact gates. Preserve failures. Do not count kinematic gap alone as grip, and report that actuator efforts and per-substep toe speed remain unmeasured. This is a development test, not walking or landing qualification.
