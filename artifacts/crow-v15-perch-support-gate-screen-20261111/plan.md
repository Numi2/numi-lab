# Sustained-contact wing-fold gate screen

The aligned-descent teacher switches to its wing-off landing phase after two foot contact metrics each exceed 0.05 N and the position and speed tests pass. That threshold alone does not establish a stable grasp or weight-bearing support. Hold the frozen flight law active until the same position/speed conditions, low pitch and pitch rate, bilateral toe-contact ramps and a total normal contact magnitude of at least 1 N persist for 15 control intervals. The normal magnitude is only a gating heuristic: it is not vertical support, a force margin, or evidence of opposing grip. This changes the high-level transition only. Compare each of the eight development starts to its own aligned-descent baseline, keep all substep traces, and report first-episode failure or censoring, partial and nominal-weight impulse windows, and toe-speed limits. There is no release or held-out autonomous test.

Biological motivation is general rather than crow-specific: observed parrotlets brake in the air, absorb impact through the legs and adjust foot contact on the perch ([Roderick et al., 2019](https://elifesciences.org/articles/46415)). The simulation is not biologically validated by that comparison.
