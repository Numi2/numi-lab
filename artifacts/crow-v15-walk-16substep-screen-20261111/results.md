# Repaired-toe walking at sixteen substeps

On the same eight development starts, the repaired toe body produced no failed environment steps or native terminations. Across all eight lanes, each interval in the two-second walking window had at least one foot contact and no non-foot ground contact. In replay environment 1, there were four alternating left and right bouts of at least three accepted poses with all five foot/toe boxes more than 1 mm above ground, the lifted foot accumulating at most 1e-7 N s normal impulse over all 16 substeps, the other foot carrying at least half body weight over the interval, and no other-body contact. Left bouts lasted 3–10 samples and right bouts 9–10. The replay lane advanced 2.59 cm, versus 5.58 cm at four substeps. Its maximum sampled root speed was 0.150 m/s and tilt 0.034 rad.

The last-substep contact evidence was checked against the all-substep observer, and replay/observer q alignment was checked before using geometry. The original actors were contract-rebound with identical weights and transformations; no training occurred. This short single-replay geometry observation does not establish 32/64-step convergence, contact-point slip, continuous within-step geometry, stable long walks, takeoff, landing, complete navigation or biological realism. The displacement change demonstrates that time-resolution sensitivity remains relevant.
