# Matched hip timing trial: not admitted

With the same -0.9 rad hip phase and 0.25 lift on all eight development starts, all first episodes completed 300 intervals without logged physical failure, overflow or toe-speed cap. Recorded pre-walk states and contacts exactly matched the preceding screen, and lane 2 reproduced its identical earlier treatment through all samples. Each lane traveled 138–152 mm in two seconds versus 25–30 mm for its paired held reference. All had at least three recorded supported-clearance bouts per foot. This remains slower than the related-magpie trial speeds and is not a biological match.

Two lanes each had six both-feet-unloaded 1.25 ms substeps; held had none. Pooled endpoint contact-point speed median/p95 was 0.00460/0.08317 m/s versus 0.00276/0.03484 held. Those are sampled instantaneous velocities, not proven integrated sliding, but exceed the predeclared screening gate. Of 40 complete toe-group contact runs, all had rear-toe-first touchdown and release, while 52 other complete runs lacked a toe group under the two-substep gap rule. Earlier gap-sensitivity analysis shows such counts can include fragments; neither all fragments nor all toe groups can be assumed correct without further study.

Of 573 low-toe geometry samples, 553 had toe loading during the same 20 ms interval; 20 had no foot normal load throughout that interval. The held trial had 35 entirely unloaded out of 49. Endpoint geometry and interval contacts are not a continuous synchronized drag measurement. The old low-toe count by itself is not an appropriate clearance failure criterion. No result here establishes real-crow gait, robust walking under perturbations, leg-powered takeoff, physical flight or supported landing. This candidate remains experimental and has not been promoted.
