# Phase-aligned knee and ankle proxy audit

For recovery windows bracketed by accepted front-toe release and the next rear-toe touchdown, the held run produced 24 measurable windows (8 ankle-proxy minima before knee minima, 16 same sampled endpoint), the matched hip-phase candidate 16 (all same endpoint), and the mixed crouch screen 20 (9 ankle-first, 11 same endpoint). No measured window showed the knee minimum earlier than the ankle-proxy minimum. This is not evidence that no such motion occurred: endpoint samples are 20 ms apart, same endpoint is unresolved, data were not filtered like the video, and the anatomical third-digit mapping of the modeled second front-toe base is unverified. The ankle is a geometric proxy, not a validated avian tarsometatarsal angle.

Segmentation excludes partial contact runs, missing toe groups, contacts shorter than 40 ms, and recoveries with fewer than three endpoint samples; its two-substep gap rule and duration threshold are engineering choices. Many pairs were excluded, so these are conditional observations, not population estimates or proof of a whole gait. Knee and ankle anchor reconstructions agree within 2.2e-9 m. See analysis.json for individual event boundaries, angle sequences and rejected counts.

The magpie reference reports knee extension beginning before ankle extension during recovery, but the present observer cannot establish whether the model matches. Increase temporal resolution and validate digit/joint mappings before fitting phase-specific movement; do not infer an American-crow joint trajectory from this proxy. Neither geometry nor timing analysis establishes stable walking or full autonomous execution.
