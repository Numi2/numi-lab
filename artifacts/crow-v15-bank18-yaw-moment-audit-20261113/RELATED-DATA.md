# Relevant bird-flight data and implications

Quinn et al., *How lovebirds maneuver through lateral gusts with minimal visual information* (PNAS 2019), https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1903422116, combined live lovebird gust flights with separate yaw-constrained flapping ornithopter experiments. Their model distinguishes a passive slip-reducing moment from a passive yaw-rate damping moment and includes active control. The ornithopter trials isolate the wing contribution under a particular setup; neither experiment measures an American crow's coefficients, our model, or this simulated body's response. Their reported parameter ratios must not be copied into the crow as calibration.

This motivates measuring the simulated model's yaw response to controlled sideslip and yaw-rate disturbances independently, with physically reached matched starting states, logging applied wind, actual accepted aerodynamics and damping, before considering any model change. A nondimensional comparison may inform hypotheses but would not establish crow-specific realism. As of this note no such new controlled sideslip experiment has been completed, and the exact runtime injection and matching mechanism still need verification.
