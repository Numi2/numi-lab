# Smaller related-corvid phase screen: not promoted

The 0.05, 0.10 and 0.15 rad ankle delays were compared to the same-lane 0.05-rad toe-preload baseline on two previously used development seeds. The two zero-delay lanes in each seed matched all 300 endpoints and 4,800 substeps exactly. Across both seeds every lane completed six seconds without reported physical termination or trace error. During 4–6 s neither the baseline nor these candidates hit the toe-speed cap or recorded nonfoot ground impulse.

The delayed lanes did have 14–31 substeps per lane with neither foot loaded, versus zero in the matched baseline. Point-speed p95 pooled over the mixed-delay lanes rose from 0.0447 to 0.0679 m/s for seed 20261126 and from 0.0430 to 0.0671 m/s for 20261127. The 0.15-rad lanes produced three or four knee-first ankle-proxy recovery windows per seed, whereas the 0.05 and 0.10-rad lanes mostly had unresolved same-endpoint minima. These shifts are not sufficient to outweigh the contact regression, and a brief unloaded interval is not by itself proof of a fall. The comparison is a development screen, not held-out validation.

The anatomical landmark, surface, speed, species and 20 ms time-resolution limitations described in the larger phase screen still apply. Contact segmentation excluded more incomplete groups in the delayed cases, and point speed is not integrated slip. The simplistic delayed-ankle command is therefore not a validated whole-corvid gait fit; no candidate is promoted. More accurate synchronized joint and contact observation and physically coupled support control are needed.
