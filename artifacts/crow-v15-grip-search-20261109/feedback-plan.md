# Exploratory force-gated toe coordination

The synchronized resident observer was exactly q/v identical to the corresponding monolithic runs on all four 60-step arms, and no combined arm showed simultaneous opposing horizontal endpoint impulses. In separate sustained commands, rear-only yielded positive-x normal impulse while front-only yielded negative-x; simultaneous closure shifted the body and generally lost one side. Hypothesis: waiting for actual rear toe contact before slowly closing the front toes will retain the first contact while engaging the opposing side.

On the same development-exposed [-0.020,0.040] m box and estimated armature body, run four 150-step arms initialized identically: (0) force-gated controller, (1) rear-only throughout, (2) controller with force observations clamped to zero while all physical collisions remain enabled, and (3) controller with toe collisions disabled. Every step is executed by the native resident GPU solver; the research host supplies bounded target commands based only on the last accepted contact endpoint. Rest for 10 steps, then drive the rear toes to 0.85 rad. After two consecutive positive-x toe/perch normal impulse sums greater than 0.00005 N s, start to increase front targets by at most 0.025 rad per step while that rear load remains above threshold; if it drops below threshold, reduce the front target by 0.015 rad per step. Keep rear at 0.85 rad. If the front and rear horizontal impulses both exceed 0.00005 N s for three consecutive endpoints, hold 10 steps then release both targets to zero. To bound the test, release from step 120 if no such event; stop at step 149.

Predict the live arm will reach and maintain the opposing-load criterion for three accepted boundaries and, after release, lose the opposing load while the clamped-sensor and collision-off arms never enter front-closure phase. Record every control, raw force components, toe q, root state, and failures. This is development/exploration only; even a positive result is not proof of stable grasp under disturbances, physically reached landing, realistic parameter calibration, or full autonomous sequence.
