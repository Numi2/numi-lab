# Exploratory toe/perch contact geometry screen

The previously tested 0.35 and 0.85 rad closures on an 80 mm wide rectangular perch did not yield simultaneous opposing loaded horizontal toe normals. Question: is an opposing contact pair achievable with the existing eight-hinge v15 toe prototype by moving the perch edges within the swept reach of the toe tips? This is an exploratory geometry/command screen, not landing, held-out validation, or evidence of crow anatomy.

Model: archived armature prototype from parent commit 24c9bc4933bfb929127c7de1ef5fc8202984c955; toe geometry and generalized inertia are unchanged, v15 flight source untouched. Single lane, fixed initial posture at original ground height, gravity and 50 control steps of 20 ms with four substeps. A static box perch spans y=+-0.20 m with its top at z=0 and one of three x boundaries: [-0.030,0.050] m (repeated reference), [-0.030,0.038] m, [-0.020,0.040] m. For each, test toe-position targets 0.35 and 0.85 rad after step 10, paired against target zero and against identical commanded toes with their collisions disabled. Runs are separately initialized from the same q/v, not treated as population samples.

Record all final accepted toe/perch contact normals and impulses, root position and speed, toe q/v, and accepted/failed status. Endpoint candidate criterion is a positive upward support contact and simultaneous toe/perch impulses with normal x > 0.5 and x < -0.5, each summed above 0.00005 N s, while the collision-disabled control has no toe impulse. Contact normals are reoriented consistently to toe as A. This is a minimal geometry screen only, not sufficient for sustained grip: a genuine claim would require independently observed time-resolved loads, collision-off and release controls, perturbation resistance, and physically reached starts. Count every requested condition and preserve failures; do not silently rerun or drop them. Stop after 18 runs; later model changes require a new plan.
