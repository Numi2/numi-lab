# Timing mismatch: invalid as a synchronized frequency test

The intended frequency comparison is invalid: the hip, knee and ankle clocks were changed, but the toe clock remained at 2 Hz. This mistake was identified before analyzing the results. Retain the run as a leg/toe timing-mismatch experiment only; it cannot establish the effect of synchronized walking frequency or qualify a gait.

The two controls matched their full six-second baselines, and all lanes matched through four seconds, including 1,600 states and 25,600 contact substeps. Experimental lanes showed 9–43 both-feet-unloaded substeps; two had toe speed-cap events. Although several had lower integrated contact-point speed, all had less forward displacement than the baseline. No tested lane pair satisfied the stated combined conditions. The substep metric is not tracked material-point slip.

A separately fingerprinted synchronized repeat corrects the toe clock. Both tests use development starts and uncalibrated mechanics.
