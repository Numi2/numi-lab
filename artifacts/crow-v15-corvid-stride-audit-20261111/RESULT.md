# Retained v15 walking: articulated joint and toe observations

This is an exploratory analysis of two exposed development seeds and their eight lanes each, not new tests or independent American-crow measurements. The physical model descriptor and compileable inspector are retained. Its hip, knee, ankle, individual proximal/distal toe and hip-roll joints are explicit. Projected sagittal internal angles were measured from native forward-kinematics joint anchors, not inferred from the rendered mesh. Parent and child anchor positions agree within 1 micrometre for every measured point; synthetic 90-degree, 180-degree and degenerate projection checks pass. Prior work cross-checked logged endpoint states and body poses against the replay for lane 5 of each input, not all lanes.

In the 201–300 control-step window (2 seconds), 95 previously identified support-qualified, sampled clearance bouts contain 5–9 endpoint samples each. The sampled knee and ankle internal-angle minima fall at the same 20 ms endpoint in 94 bouts; the ankle minimum occurs earlier in one. This is descriptive of the sampled cleared portion, not proof that the actual biological swing minima coincide: liftoff and touchdown are not defined by these clearance bounds, extrema can fall outside the window, and true ordering within a 20 ms interval is unknown. Endpoint knee angles at the beginning or end of these bouts are 151.1–162.1 degrees, using this model and angle definition; the model geometry and slow development gait differ from the magpie reference and these are not validated crow angles.

Across a full 2-second window, projected spans were 10.31–11.02 degrees for hip, 13.34–13.37 for knee, and 4.39–4.44 for ankle. These are window spans, not stride-normalized amplitudes and not biological targets. At the existing impulse threshold and 20 ms aggregation, rear-toe segments carry normal impulse in 57–67 of 100 intervals per foot; middle front-toe segments do so in 5–24, and the foot group as a whole in 69–72. This establishes uneven modeled contact contribution under these conditions, not biological grip or toe-slip performance. Digit impulse is a sum over the whole interval, not an endpoint contact state; no within-interval ordering is inferred. The separately retained native contact observer maps these segment indices to left/right toe bodies.

The related black-billed magpie study describes sequential knee, ankle and hip reversals during recovery. The near-coincident v15 samples justify higher-resolution contact and angle measurement and a physical controller experiment, but do not prove the exact sequence is wrong or establish American-crow target phases. The current model also has a simplified foot and neck. No reliable stride duty factor, touchdown-to-liftoff interval, calibrated species gait, sustained walking qualification or complete autonomous journey follows from this audit.
