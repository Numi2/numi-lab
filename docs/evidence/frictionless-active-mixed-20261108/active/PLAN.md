# Active frictionless-contact normal equation and impulse

Use the retained isolated frictionless candidate and baseline with the same integrated runtime and 1 kg tilted box-plane fixture. Maintain zero friction, zero gravity and damping, a 0.1 mm positive initial gap, 1 ms step, no CCD, no final velocity iterations and no applied effort. Test existing angle 0.3 radians and an unused angle -0.41 radians; for each use normal velocity -1 m/s and tangential x velocity -1, 0, +1 m/s, and test configured velocity iteration counts 2, 8 and 32 as a diagnostic sweep, not as a production fix. Each arm runs twice. Save initial and final q/v, full active contact witnesses, normal impulses, evaluated target and regularization, and solver residual.

Independently reconstruct each normal velocity from final translational/angular velocity and the initial body pose and captured contact point. Evaluate the unilateral normal residual for each evaluated constraint: abs(vn-target+regularization*lambda) when lambda>1e-8, otherwise max(target-vn,0). Check it against the solver-reported residual within 2e-5 m/s, and verify total normal impulse against unit-mass momentum change within 2e-5 N s. Since both contacts have the same normal and the scene is static, zero friction implies no tangential linear impulse. Compare final full velocity and impulses across the three tangential velocities for each angle and iteration count: the normal and angular response should be independent of tangential translation to 2e-5. Check kinetic energy relative to the incoming state; any energy rise is diagnostic, not a pass unless the discrete target and contact energy explain it. Require the original 0.002 m/s contact residual gate on all candidate 32-iteration cases as a narrow diagnostic, and retain failures at all budgets. No collision or force-calibration qualification follows.
