# Straight axial rod: rotated and multi-support reference

**Research only; no production change or promotion.** The retained production solver can publish inaccurate axial motion and support forces. In the new five- and eight-node fixtures, all 12 five-node production cases rejected at status 4; all 12 eight-node cases published but failed the stated position, velocity, force or work gates. The largest eight-node position error was about 0.695 mm and velocity error about 0.695 m/s. These are bounded synthetic cases, not a claim about every rod state.

The separately compiled vector-axis candidate extends the prior x-only research solver using a projected local axial coordinate, with strict straight-chain, no-contact and other eligibility checks. At offsets 0 and 0.5 m, its first matrix passed all 36 three-node cases across six orientations. At 100 m it passed 4, accepted 8 that failed the unchanged gates, and rejected 6. That result rules out broad promotion. Two clearly bent and transversely moving controls matched the previous guarded general-solver outcomes, including rejection; they do not establish a complete admission proof.

A revised candidate rejects geometrically eligible states when an FP32 coordinate-scale estimate exceeds the configured positional tolerance. In the replicated and unused-rotation follow-up, it passed all 54 cases at offsets 0 and 0.5 m, including 18 cases on three unused axes, and rejected all 27 at 100 m. Rejection avoids the observed false publication but is not useful completion at that offset. The estimate and the 32-epsilon collinearity allowance are screening heuristics, not rigorous error bounds for forces, orientation, cancellation, or general geometry.

The independent FP64 dense reference assembled from the energy gradient agrees with a separate three-node closed form; it also verifies local free-node equations and total linear momentum balance. The guarded candidate passed all 24 predeclared five- and eight-node combinations with two fixed supports, a moving endpoint, or a moving endpoint plus an internal support. Worst Euclidean node position error was 3.62e-8 m, velocity error 2.81e-5 m/s, force error 2.21e-6 N, and error in force-on-target dotted with prescribed support displacement 6.33e-11 J. Force gates were max(1 µN, 1% of reference), not a flat 1 µN. The work quantity is the rod force on the target dotted with target displacement; actuator work has the opposite sign under an ideal balance. This does not establish a complete energy balance.

All declared paired executions repeated identically in captured stdout. Inputs, source, data, analysis, fixed gates, and native artifact hashes are retained. The metal compilation commands are in build JSON files. The two native probes used clang++ C++20 with the integration headers/library and Foundation/Metal as shown in `build-v2.json`; the multi-chain probe used the same flags with `multichain.mm` and output `multichain`. The instrument binaries and linked AIR dependencies are identified by hash but are not bundled. The first build script named the vector linked output `candidate.metallib` rather than `vector.metallib` due to a string replacement; it was copied byte-for-byte before the planned runs. The initial incomplete hash listing is retained. No trial ran before that copy.

This is a one-step, straight, synthetic no-contact configuration. Before production use, the method needs rigorous admission bounds including near-collinear and large-coordinate cases, multi-step replay and full rollback, force/work and energy checks, bent/twisted and active-set cases, coupled rigid and deformable ownership, and broader host/device path qualification. Unsupported general-solver cases can still be incorrect; the precision screen is not a global safety repair. Both experimental CCD and augmented-Lagrangian contact options remain disabled. No performance or physical-material claim is made.

The two raw build stdout files contain trailing whitespace and are retained losslessly in `raw-build-logs.tar.gz`; `raw-build-logs.sha256` records the uncompressed bytes. The first publication check detected that formatting and did not commit; no physical run was changed.

**Subsequent finding:** the [local momentum follow-up](../rod-local-momentum-20261108/RESULT.md) reproduced all 24 guarded longer-chain outputs and found that every one exceeds a separate 0.001 m/s local momentum-residual gate. The 24/24 pass above applies only to the originally stated position, velocity, support-force and work gates; it does not establish force consistency.
