# Mixed rod-contact failing-row witness diagnosis

**Result: the failing residual is reproduced by an independent arithmetic reconstruction, and the two rows are strongly shared-node dominated. This does not identify the convergence mechanism or repair it.** The bounded split solver and both original experimental options remain unpromoted and disabled.

On the Apple M6, the retained 20-step sphere/rod/plane fixture and isolated 2e-5 m/s, 128-sweep shader again accept seven steps and reject the first attempted eighth step (zero-based step 7) with status 11. The two capture shaders and their freshly linked control have identical exposed physical outputs and status after excluding the deliberately overwritten diagnostic fields; their own callback-free versus captured final state and repeated captures also agree. Those checks do not establish equality of every hidden cache or complete owner restoration.

Each rod witness has a positive impulse and belongs to a different edge: edge nodes 0–1 with weights 0.011355/0.988645, and edge nodes 1–2 with weights 0.985075/0.014925. Both therefore act primarily on the same central material node, despite distinct surface witnesses. The normal equations at that failed candidate are approximately -8.6451e-5 and +7.1402e-5 m/s. A CPU FP64 calculation reconstructs surface velocity using the captured node velocities, edge vector, radius, radial direction and twist rate, then evaluates the captured normal target and regularization. Across all 40 row observations, including repeated attempts of the same rejected state, the largest differences from the GPU normal velocity and equation are 6.662e-10 and 6.657e-10 m/s. The tool velocity, geometry, targets and impulses are captured from the same runtime, so this independently checks the arithmetic and rod-velocity reconstruction; it does not independently validate their physical correctness or the full tangent operator.

For a limited diagnostic proxy using barycentric nodal interpolation and diagonal inverse masses alone, the two normal rows have normalized cross term 0.998815. This proxy omits the rotating-frame/radius terms, twist, the implicit elastic rod inverse, rigid response and friction coupling. It is evidence of near shared-node alignment, **not** the condition number or spectrum of the actual coupled operator. The capture supports investigating near-colocated row coupling and frictional active-set behavior; it does not justify pruning either contact, raising budgets, accepting the rejected step, or promoting a solver.

The native fixture repeats failed attempts after restoration; the 40 row observations are not 40 independent trajectories.
