# Mixed-contact failing-row witness capture

Question: do the two near-colocated rod contacts at the first rejected step use the same material node, and does an independent FP64 reconstruction of the actual candidate relative normal velocity and normal equation agree with the solver certificate? An alternative explanation is that the apparent plateau is a status or row-observation error; agreement alone cannot establish the cause or convergence of the coupled operator.

Use the retained 20-step mixed sphere/rod/plane fixture and the isolated bounded split solver at 2e-5 m/s and 128 sweeps. This is a replication and instrument diagnosis, not a new production solver or performance test. On Apple M6 compile a fresh control and two isolated post-solve capture variants from the same retained shader, linked to identical current integration AIR and runtime. One variant records rod endpoint indices, interpolation weight, radius, edge vector, radial vector, twist rate and evaluated normal target. The second records the two node velocities, tool velocity, row regularization, and the solver normal equation and relative normal velocity. Store the diagnostics in contact fields only after the last sweep; the probe must record them before returning results.

Validity gates: every traced run must match its paired uninstrumented control on exposed final q/v, scene, rod state, statuses, all contact fields other than deliberately overwritten scratch fields, and each step's q/v, node velocities, impulses, geometry and status. It must repeat exactly. Capture must identify the same rows on both variants. Reject invalid/nonfinite/missing data or a changed first failure; do not use restored q/v as the rejected candidate. Compare the two independently reconstructed rod-surface relative normal velocities and normal equations against the GPU trace within 2e-5 m/s, including the radius/twist term and captured regularization. Report witness identities and barycentric weights without claiming full inverse-operator conditioning; independently compare a nodal inverse-mass-only Gram proxy and identify the missing implicit stiffness, twist and rigid terms.

Run each of the three arms once; the native fixture itself performs callback-free, capture and repeated-capture executions. Analyze all returned steps, with primary focus on the first failed step; preserve failures and stop if equivalence fails. Inputs, source revisions, compiler commands, runtime/shader/probe hashes and outputs will be retained. Existing CCD and AL options remain disabled; this work does not promote the mixed solver or prove complete rollback or physical force accuracy.
