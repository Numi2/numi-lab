# Four-contact candidate: 100-step discrete controls

The retained unpromoted four-contact initializer (`5de692416`, shader SHA-256 `63cd6d443f64f82ed0f5bdb91ed796eade9c8831ae6637ccb7af617740f64be5`) was tested with the same two-sweep resident box fixture used to reject the separate warm-row containment candidate. The runtime and all probe inputs are identified in the accompanying hash files. Warm and cold modes each accepted and reproduced all 100 steps in both impacts, the near miss, stationary support and sliding support. The near miss received no normal impulse. Maximum stationary support impulse error was 2.70e-8 N s; maximum accepted linear momentum mismatch across cases was 2.99e-8 N s. For the zero-gap impact, the second-step normal impulse was 0.001090485 N s and the reported normal residual 2.29e-9 m/s, compared with approximately 0.560104 N s on the previously retained main and warm-row controls. All preregistered gates pass.

A second probe extends the existing detailed ten-step instrument only by changing its loop bound to 100. Both modes again accepted and duplicated all 500 case-steps, with positions, velocities and normal impulses matching the first probe. The existing independent final-velocity local friction checker reports zero failures across 1,428 rows per mode, including 644 sliding rows, under its declared absolute and relative impulse tolerances. The fixture has isotropic friction and one box on a plane. No test in this run rejected a step, so its rejection-restoration check is vacuous. Raw outputs are compressed without changing their contents; their original hashes are in `raw-output.sha256`.

This is evidence for a bounded discrete-contact candidate, not promotion or general physical validation. The older research also reports a scoped FP64 fixed-state comparison and populated-cache rollback, but full reset-buffer restoration, wider geometry, articulated and moving-body contact, anisotropic and capped friction, rod interaction and swept CCD remain open. The impact threshold used here detects the prior gross spurious impulse; it is not a complete force-accuracy certificate. The CPU contact reference overlapped, so no timing or performance claim is made. Both experimental interval-local CCD and augmented-Lagrangian contact remain disabled.
