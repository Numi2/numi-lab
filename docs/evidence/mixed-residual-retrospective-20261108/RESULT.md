# Retrospective: mixed-contact acceptance and tiny impulses

This is an offline analysis of the previously retained rowtrace.json; no simulation was rerun and no production or experimental solver was changed. The source trace is identified by SHA-256 in analysis.json. It records the unpromoted 2e-5 m/s, 128-sweep mixed rod/sphere/plane candidate and contains seven accepted steps, followed by thirteen rejected attempts. Its prior report remains the historical account of what that candidate actually did. The separate production diagnostic repair in commit 4033da73c did not change that candidate's acceptance rule or its historical trace.

For each accepted step, analyze.py reconstructs the prior reported maximum from the signed normal equation and impulse captured for each row. It reproduces all seven reported values within 1e-9 m/s. It then applies the newer strictly-positive-impulse reporting definition to the same captured rows. Six of the seven accepted states exceed 2e-5 m/s by that definition; the largest is 5.34595456e-4 m/s. The row producing that maximum has a normal impulse of only 3.512667316e-14 N s. At the first such state, the strict residual is 2.033370547e-4 m/s, while the prior reported maximum was 1.565739512e-5 m/s; the offending rod contact impulse was 8.476145341e-10 N s.

The retained candidate shader used kConeEpsilon = 1e-7 N s in both its normal stopping test and its reported complementarity residual. Thus its seven accepted steps satisfied that candidate's implemented cutoff-based test, but six did not satisfy the stricter reconstructed row definition. This is a qualification limitation in addition to the already documented force-gate failure. The retained trace supplies the solver-computed normal equation; this analysis independently recomputes the reduction and threshold classification, not the equation itself, and it does not establish exact behavior of a rerun with a changed stopping rule. The prior capture and exposed replay checks do not establish hidden cache restoration.

An active-row residual defined by strict positivity is discontinuous as impulse approaches zero: an extremely small positive impulse on a separating contact can yield a comparatively large velocity residual while the associated impulse is negligible. Consequently, neither the old absolute cutoff nor the strict residual alone is a physically scaled convergence certificate. The examples do not prove a consequential force error, and the reported row equation by itself does not establish an operator defect. A subsequent solver qualification should separately measure a properly scaled projected normal residual, local impulse/force error, geometric clearance, and full transaction restoration; establish the necessary response scale with independently checked operator action; and retain the 1 ms force and impulse gates. It must not interpret a lower residual or more iterations as sufficient evidence of physical accuracy.

The generated MetalWorld collision cones inspected here initialize without a maximum-normal-impulse cap. Although the shared constraint representation supports a cap, an authored generalized mechanism follows a separate path. Therefore the capped-contact corner of the production reporting helper remains a representation-level limitation, not an established failure on these generated contact fixtures.
