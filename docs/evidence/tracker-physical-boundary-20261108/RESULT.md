# Physical rejection and visual tracker history

**Confirmed defect; diagnostic restore only, not a production fix.** On Apple M6, a real mixed-batch MetalWorld failure leaves the failed environment’s private object-track history advanced. The other environment succeeds and must retain its own progress. This establishes a specific gap left open by the earlier host pre-commit lease; it does not qualify or condemn any other sensor or policy state.

A two-environment Franka fixture used raw contact capacity one, 30 control steps of 20 ms, four substeps, camera-depth dropout 0.35 and oracle instance selection. Both environments completed steps 0–12. On step 13 environment 0 had contact-capacity overflow and environment 1 succeeded; the same mixed outcome occurred at step 26 after both succeeded at step 25. These are intentionally constrained diagnostic failures, not normal-capacity reliability or speed measurements. CCD was disabled in this fixture; the experimental CCD and augmented-Lagrangian options remained disabled.

A temporary, read-only accessor exposed the private Metal buffer to a test callback. GPU blits captured each environment’s three track-history records before the observation callback, immediately afterward, and after the final physics substep. At both mixed failures, environment 0’s pre-observation bytes equaled its previous step’s post-physics bytes, changed during observation, and remained changed after the failed physical step. Environment 1 also changed and retained its updated history, consistent with its successful step. These checks inspect the buffer itself before the next automatic task reset.

A separately compiled capture-free control produced byte-identical published q, v, scene, actor and world-status arrays to the captured run. Thus, for these outputs and this fixture, inserting the capture did not alter the observed trajectory. The prototype described below also matched those arrays; that does not prove equivalence of every unobserved GPU resource.

## Diagnostic restore prototype

A scratch executable copied the entire tracker history to a private backup before each observation and ran a small GPU kernel after the final physics substep. For each of two environments it restored the six `float4` records from that backup when either the world or contact status was nonzero, and kept the new records on success. This 2-environment, 3-history-slot, 1-binding specialization is deliberately hard-coded for the diagnostic and is **not suitable for production**.

The retained analyzer verifies all 30 steps: the prototype restored pre-observation object-track bytes for 5/5 failed environment-steps and retained post-observation bytes for 55/55 successful environment-steps. The failure sequence, q, v, scene, actor outputs and published world-status bytes matched the capture control. An independent repeat of the prototype produced byte-identical history and published arrays. The prototype made no change to NumiLab runtime code.

This result does not establish deterministic continuation after a failed step, retry correctness, object identity/topology behavior, masked-depth rollback, task actor-history rollback, recurrent-policy rollback, GPU-command failure recovery, or preservation of sensor time. The renderer still uses a shared host-side sensor sequence, and task logic terminates and resets the failed environment. A per-environment history copy alone is insufficient for the requested end-to-end transaction. Actor outputs and nonzero rewards can still be published for a physics-error transition; the MLX worker rejects packs containing that error flag, but other consumers require separate qualification. No deployment-valid perception or performance claim is made.

## Reproduction and provenance

The source base is `853ee68cb`. The scratch-only `accessor.patch` applies to that source tree and adds a read-only borrowed history handle. Configure a separate build with `cmake -S <tree> -B <build> -G Ninja -DBUILD_TESTING=OFF` and build target `metalrobo`. Apply `capture.patch` to a scratch copy of the base visual probe; its output is the capture source. Apply `control.patch` or `restore.patch` to a copy named `probe.cpp` to produce the two variants. The patches reconstruct the tested source byte-for-byte. Compile each as Objective-C++23 for arm64 with the source `include` and `matter/include`, link the isolated `libmetalrobo.dylib` and Foundation/Metal, and run with `NUMI_TEST_WORLD_TRANSACTION=1` and `NUMI_TEST_RESULT_PREFIX` naming a writable prefix. The scripts save the snapshots and published arrays. `python3 analyze.py` checks the retained evidence.
