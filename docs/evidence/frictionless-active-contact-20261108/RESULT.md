# Frictionless active contact: accepted penetration and isolated candidate

**Decision: the baseline has a confirmed scoped correctness failure. Keep the candidate isolated pending moving-body, mixed-contact, rollback and broader solver qualification.** Neither experimental directional CCD nor AL contact was enabled. This is a discrete contact fixture with CCD explicitly disabled; it does not establish a failure of every CCD mode.

On Apple M6, two tilted 1 kg boxes start 0.1 mm clear of a static plane, with zero gravity and friction. At two closing speeds and both 2 and 32 velocity iterations, adding a particular parallel velocity causes the baseline to accept a step with no change in normal velocity. In four of 12 cases per iteration budget, the reconstructed initial-corner normal speed misses the -0.1 m/s closing bound by 0.3 or 0.9 m/s. For example, at -1 m/s normal speed the accepted baseline has no normal impulse as inferred from unchanged vertical momentum; it finishes approximately 0.9 mm through the plane. This is not just a residual or timing discrepancy.

The isolated normal-only block candidate from [the earlier oblique-contact diagnostic](../frictionless-oblique-contact-20261108/RESULT.md) produced a positive normal response in all 24 cases. Paired runs with different horizontal speeds had identical normal and angular velocities, and preserved horizontal speed. The largest reconstructed closing-bound violation was 0.00000256 m/s; the declared gate was 0.002 m/s. The candidate met every preregistered fixture gate at both iteration counts. The baseline did not. All 48 cases returned success with generated contacts and exactly repeated q/v in their internal repeat.

The paired-velocity difference combines linear and angular components, so it is only a maximum numerical component difference, not a quantity with a single physical unit. `analysis.json` reports each component-derived row in context. The independently reconstructed corner bound uses initial geometry and final body velocity; it is a diagnostic proxy rather than the solver's evaluated-row certificate. After the fixed experiment, `geometry.py` independently transformed all eight corners using the published final quaternion to calculate endpoint clearance. This post-hoc calculation does not certify the trajectory between endpoints or validate force over a longer rollout. The baseline penetration is substantially larger than its numerical resolution.

The two arms share the current runtime, source and all linked shader inputs except `MetalWorldContact.metal`; `candidate.patch` records the entire isolated change. The current contact shader differs from the older study by a failed-island iteration-reporting change, so this builds and tests against current integrated source rather than reusing the older candidate binary. The baseline and candidate source, exact compile/link commands, raw JSON, exit codes, per-case analysis, post-hoc geometry, host information and pretrial fingerprints are retained here. Executables and AIR artifacts remain on `ssh numi` at the paths in `commands.txt` and `build.json`. A checkout can rebuild them while the recorded toolchain and linked dependencies are available; the evidence does not assert a self-contained release bundle.

The correction addresses the specific source mechanism: zeroing normal/tangent effective-block cross terms for an exactly frictionless cone prevents tangential residual from entering the normal unconstrained update. A passing free-box fixture does not establish accurate contact forces, convergence for all manifolds, moving-body or articulated contact, rod coupling, anisotropic friction, complete transaction restoration, or general collision safety. In particular, the earlier closing control with only two iterations had inadequate normal response, and the current fixtures do not supersede the negative mixed-island evidence. The next executable step is a matching moving-body and mixed rod/rigid test with independently reconstructed contact residual and full rejected-state restoration, before any source promotion.
