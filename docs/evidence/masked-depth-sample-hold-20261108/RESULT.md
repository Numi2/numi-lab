# Masked-depth camera sample hold

The masked-depth path now holds the complete corrupted measurement between batch camera captures. Previously, although the renderer retained its last image, the masked-depth kernel redrew dropout, jitter and noise from the current task step on every observation. At 25 Hz camera and 50 Hz control, the native materialized fixture changed 1,849 pixels on its first unsampled observation and 1,814 on the next; the two baseline trials matched. A later sampled frame changed 2,037 pixels. The preregistered hold gate failed, as retained in `baseline-*.log`.

The tracker passes its existing batch sample decision into the kernel. At a fresh, unsampled, non-reset observation, the kernel copies the preceding normalized measurement into the next control-history slot. Reset and invalid-freshness paths continue to produce far/no-return until a new frame; failure restoration remains owned by the existing physical-step callback. The sample decision is still batch-wide. No extra camera render or CPU readback is introduced. The shader test's explicit sample flag is supplied by its direct test harness, and the observation uniform's size is unchanged.

The corrected native fixture changed zero pixels at each hold and 2,064 pixels at the subsequent capture. Sparse prior-frame history also matched the previous control observation. Separate shader checks passed for 4x2 materialized, 20x20 materialized and 4x2 direct-winner inputs, ring capacities one and three, fresh-sample change, an invalidated reset with a continuing neighbor, and recovery. Baseline unit results retain 12 failed hold/neighbor checks; candidate unit results pass. Both repetitions matched. All 24 visual tests pass on the integration checkout; `fingerprint-visual-ctest.log` retains the initial one-test failure caused by an older object-track fingerprint test expecting compatibility for its masked-depth-only case. That test was updated to recognize this additional observation-semantic change, with its non-depth control still required to remain compatible.

A fixed 30-step, two-environment actual MetalWorld fixture with oracle-masked depth and deliberately restricted contact capacity was rerun twice against the candidate. It produced 55 accepted and five rejected environment-steps. All established accepted/rejected output and reset checks passed, both trials matched, and the independent CPU depth-feedback reduction agreed within 1.68e-8. On 44 unsampled observations with an accepted predecessor and no reset, the current depth input exactly matched the previous published depth; 42 of those rows contained visible pixels, 41 current steps succeeded and three were rejected. This confirms the hold across the observed physical transaction boundary, not a no-reset physical retry or private renderer restoration. The retained final capture archive was independently extracted and both analyses reproduced. The earlier pre-fingerprint candidate capture is also retained.

The task observation fingerprint now has an additional masked-depth semantics marker. The frozen pre-change policy fixtures verify that a masked-depth policy is rejected under the corrected contract, while a policy without masked depth remains compatible. Rebinding is only an explicit contract operation; trained policies require separate requalification. Experimental directional CCD and augmented-Lagrangian contact options remain disabled.

This does **not** provide independent accepted-time camera schedules for each environment, correct GPU-command failure recovery, sensor latency or physical camera calibration, deployment-valid object selection, trained policy quality, or end-to-end performance. The instrument uses simulator identity to select the object, and the mixed physical failures are deliberately induced. The next sensing work should establish per-environment accepted-time sampling, random-stream identity and transactional cached-frame ownership, then validate latency, dropout and held-out perception on an actual task.

The first clean main checkout test exposed a wire-format mismatch: fixtures written on the integration branch used policy format 5, while main supports format 4. That failure remains in `wire-compatibility/main-v5-failed-ctest.log`, and the original format-5 fixture is retained there. The replacement fixtures were generated by the pre-change main task compiler and are read by both branches; each accepts the no-depth control and rejects the old masked-depth observation contract. This addresses the test fixture, not the broader branch and release integration problem.
