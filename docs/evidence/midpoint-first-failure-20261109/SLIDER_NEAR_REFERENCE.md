# Reacting slider: initially active contact reference

This adds a second independent FP64 fixture with the same n=2 material, physical contact coefficient, reference-area quadrature, 100 µm activation width and 10 g sliding rigid coordinate as the existing CPU slider comparison. The initial gap is 95 µm, inside the activation band. Both FEM and slider still move according to the two-way equations and gravity. This is a CPU reference only; it does not run a native coupled Matter step or exercise native owner rollback.

Refining RK4 from 0.5 to 0.25 µs passes the existing 1 ms-binned force, sampled peak, impulse and cumulative-impulse limits. The midpoint comparisons at 15.625 and 7.8125 µs also pass those four gates and the clearance gate against the refined reference. Respectively, the binned-force discrepancies are 0.142% and 0.0356%; the sampled-peak discrepancies are 0.375% and 0.0941%. Peak and binned force are distinct measurements. The respective slider work errors are 1.13% and 0.285%; FEM work errors are 0.649% and 0.163%. Work and torque are reported without inventing a new acceptance threshold. See `slider-near-reference-analysis.json` for the complete values and interpolation rule.

The initial contact force is 0.232 N and the initial barrier potential is 3.901e-7 J. At 15.625 µs the calculated contact work on the two-owner pair is positive 3.849e-7 J; adding the contact-potential change gives -6.80e-10 J. The finer midpoint step reduces the corresponding work-plus-potential difference to -1.71e-10 J. Positive contact work alone is therefore not evidence of net energy creation in this fixture: the initial potential contributes, and the discrete integration rule matters.

The `slider-near-*` artifacts retain the two RK4 traces, two midpoint traces, analysis, command/build/test evidence and hashes. The comparison keeps the four pre-existing force/impulse gates and does not claim spatial convergence, a practical speedup, a native physical response, or force/torque/work qualification of actual MetalWorld ownership. Three scoped CPU CTests passed. A repeat with the default initial gap reproduced every field from each earlier corresponding RK4 and midpoint output exactly; the new optional argument and additional energy fields do not change that prior trajectory.
