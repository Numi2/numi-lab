# Inspection of retained force and cumulative-impulse traces

This is exploratory post hoc inspection of existing results, not a new trial or spatial qualification. The previously reported 0.08504% for the native n=2 midpoint fixture is its maximum 1 ms-binned force discrepancy; its sampled peak discrepancy is 0.2584%. At the 15.625 µs timestep the native peak was 0.722801 N at 4.8046875 ms; the independently integrated 0.25 µs reference peak was 0.724674 N at 4.798 ms. The largest cumulative-impulse difference was -2.5853e-7 N·s at 4.882 ms, versus the reference final impulse 5.06575e-4 N·s. The native cumulative series is linearly interpolated to the 2 µs reference output clock. The underlying step samples and physical limits remain as reported in `MIDPOINT_N2_LADDER_RESULT.md`.

For the separately time-qualified n=32 and n=64 CPU mesh references, the 0.25 µs internal-sample peaks are 0.176421 N and 0.122647 N. On the retained 2 µs trace clock, their peaks occur at 4.974 ms and 9.858 ms respectively; each trace-clock peak is close to that mesh’s internal-sample peak. At 5.980 ms the aligned force samples differ by 0.14055 N (0.16652 versus 0.02597 N). The greatest aligned cumulative-impulse difference is 6.2351e-6 N·s at 6.044 ms; the n=64 final impulse is 4.10017e-4 N·s. These observations explain why the 1 ms averages, instantaneous force and cumulative impulse communicate different aspects of this comparison. They do not identify a cause for the mesh discrepancy or establish that n=64 is spatially converged.

`midpoint-n2-curve-inspection.json` is produced by the existing `inspect_force_impulse_curve.py`; `spatial-force-curve-inspection.json` and its input hashes are produced by `inspect_spatial_force_curves.py`. Neither analysis alters a contact law, an acceptance limit or a simulation result. The n=128 reference remains a separate running experiment.
