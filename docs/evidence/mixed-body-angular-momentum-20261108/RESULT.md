# Mixed-body speculative contact angular momentum

A preregistered native single-step test places a free articulated sphere and a dynamic scene sphere in zero gravity, with an approaching and sliding speculative contact. At a 1.4 mm positive gap, the accepted step changes total angular momentum about a fixed origin by -1.979993e-5 N m s. The independently reconstructed impulse applied at the articulated witness and scene midpoint predicts -1.979869e-5 N m s; their difference is 1.25e-9 N m s. The touching and frictionless controls show angular changes below 2e-10 N m s. Linear momentum errors remain below 1.8e-8 N s. Repeated owners produced identical captured final state. Full inputs, contact rows and the calculation are retained here.

Source inspection shows the selected solver uses body-local articulated point queries and the shared midpoint for the scene-body impulse. This fixture corroborates the resulting internal angular impulse. It is a bounded correctness result for this selected contact path, not an accuracy or performance qualification for other solvers or scenes. A correction needs consistent velocity, effective mass and impulse operators, a physically justified treatment of friction while bodies remain separated, and whole-step near-miss, coupled-contact, conservation and rollback tests. Simply switching both bodies to separated witnesses changes the internal couple and is not established as a correct repair. The experiments remain disabled.

The first trial placed the bodies at y=10 m. Its final-position consistency check missed the preregistered 1e-7 N m s threshold by 2.13e-8 N m s, consistent with FP32 position quantization at that coordinate. It is retained as `output-v1.json`, `probe-v1.cpp`, `inputs-v1.sha256`, and `analysis-v1.stderr`; `analysis-v2.stderr` records the subsequently exposed values. The second trial translated both bodies to y=1 m, still far from the plane, and passed the original threshold without changing the contact geometry, velocities or solver. The first compiled executable was inadvertently overwritten; a rebuild from its retained source did not reproduce its recorded executable hash, so the original binary cannot be independently rehashed. The recorded original library and shader hashes agree with the current ones. No such limitation applies to the second run: the native executable, library and shader hashes were verified before this evidence was copied.

The checked-out main revision at execution was `1422a41d9`, with existing runtime binaries identified by `inputs.sha256`. `python3 analyze.py` recomputes the successful analysis from captured output. The source changes between trials and the failed first certificate are evidence, not an adjustment to the tolerance.
