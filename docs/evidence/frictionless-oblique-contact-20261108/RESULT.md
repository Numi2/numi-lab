# Frictionless oblique sliding: accepted spurious impulse

The current integrated MetalWorld solver accepts a frictionless, non-closing rigid-box/plane step that generates a spurious normal response. The box starts with a 0.1 mm positive gap, no gravity, no angular velocity, and velocity exactly parallel to the plane. Two contacts are generated. With tangential velocity `(-1, 0, 0)` m/s the accepted result has vertical velocity `0.33209279` m/s and angular velocity approximately `(0, 0, -4.56603479)` rad/s; the stationary and opposite sliding controls retain their original velocity. Both directions repeat exactly. The predicted free trajectory under these conditions is a parallel translation with no rotation. This is a correctness defect in the declared frictionless contact case, not a force-calibration or general CCD result. CCD is disabled in this fixture.

Source inspection suggested that the 3-by-3 unconstrained block update allowed tangential residual into its normal component, then zero-friction projection discarded the tangential components. An isolated candidate zeroes normal/tangent off-diagonal entries of the local effective matrix when **both** friction coefficients are exactly zero, before the existing inversion. The candidate covers Wave32 packet, distributed Wave32 and ordered rigid blocks; it does not modify production source or enable either experimental CCD or AL contact option. Its exact patch is retained as `candidate.patch`. This is an engineering hypothesis and narrow correction, not proof that all frictional projections are correct.

On the original four-arm fixture, the candidate preserves the stationary and both sliding controls while the closing control produces a normal response. That response is not independently force-qualified: with two configured velocity iterations its final centre velocity is still -0.7462 m/s, so the case does not establish contact complementarity or impact accuracy.

In a preregistered follow-up, 16 unused zero-friction cases cover four other orientations and both signs of each of two tangential velocities. The original runtime changed velocity or spin by more than 1e-6 in 12/16; the isolated candidate preserved the analytic free translation within that threshold in all 16. Two real contact points were present in these cases. Across 16 matched nonzero-friction cases, serialized q/v and status were identical between shaders. Eight positive-clearance no-contact controls also matched. All 40 candidate cases and their baseline counterparts succeeded and repeated their q/v exactly. The public scripts retain individual outcomes and verify these claims. The nonzero-friction cases do not independently establish accurate friction.

The existing native `metalrobo_metal_world_contact_probe` passed under both identical runtime copies, including its existing multi-environment, overflow, contact-tile and async assertions. Its reported timing is not a valid performance comparison. A separate 12-case hybrid CCD regression using production cap 16 and both experimental selectors disabled matched baseline and candidate in every saved output except initialization timing. All four known thin impacts still rejected; four near misses remained force-free; repeats and the fixture-exposed q/v/cache rollback checks passed. These cases use finite friction, so unchanged results were expected and do not establish improved thin impacts.

The first attempt to run this CCD fixture used its old executable with a newer ABI and returned host status 4 before simulation in every case; the resulting failed verification is retained. Rebuilding the unchanged probe source against the current headers and runtime produced the stated results. The first candidate build-script invocation also failed an overly broad source-match assertion; it made no shader and was corrected before compiling the candidate.

## Limits and next work

This is not production-qualified and does not repair the known thin-impact failures. The candidate has not been validated for moving scene bodies, mixed rod contact, all distributed and ordered owner states, one-axis friction, general frictional contact, impulse/work accuracy, or full transactional rollback. The local normal-only change needs an independent active-contact complementarity check and meaningful mixed-contact tests, plus a permanent regression test, before a production commit. No benchmark or speed improvement is claimed. The separate fixed-law FEM–plane reference still fails its 0.25% spatial force gate; this diagnostic cannot qualify it.
