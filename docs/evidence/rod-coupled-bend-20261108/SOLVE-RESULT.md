# Coupled straight-rest rod: FP64 implicit-step diagnostic

The research-only coupled implicit solve converged on all 12 preregistered cases, including the retained three-node zero- and 100 nm-bend controls and ten unused 5-, 8- and 9-node cases with curved positions, twist and moving supports. Each result had a positive scaled tangent eigenvalue; the minimum was 0.0001934. The method took one to four Newton iterations, and every accepted line search used its first trial. No production source was changed.

An independently compiled native DER energy evaluator differentiated stretch, bend and twist at the resulting positions and twists at both declared finite-difference scales. The largest free-node linear momentum residual was 1.306e-7 m/s and the largest edge angular momentum residual was 1.906e-10 rad/s, below the 0.001 gates. The two native estimates differed by at most 1.372e-7 m/s and 4.243e-10 rad/s, below their 0.0001 agreement gates. The largest support-reaction error was 3.816e-11 N, within max(1e-6 N, 1%) in every support. The velocity and twist-rate perturbation controls were detected, and nonfinite and zero-length cases were rejected without a returned state. Anchors matched prescribed positions exactly in the captured FP64 values.

Anchor forces, impulses and the force-on-target dotted with prescribed displacement are recorded per case in `solve-analysis.json`. This work sign convention is not a complete energy-balance claim. Cases, native inputs and outputs, analysis and statuses repeated byte for byte. `solve.sha256` identifies the independent instrument, solver, derivative source, cases, outputs and logs. The native code is an independent energy-difference evaluation of the same discrete DER model, not an external physical calibration.

This is one FP64 CPU step with hard point anchors and no contact. It is not the production rod path, a Metal result, an earned suture continuation, full rollback, a multistep trajectory, a material fit or a performance measurement. The production false-acceptance defect remains unresolved. Next test sustained trajectories and energy/work against an independently stepped reference, then determine whether an FP32 Metal implementation can meet the same published-state and local momentum requirements. Both experimental collision/contact options remain disabled.
