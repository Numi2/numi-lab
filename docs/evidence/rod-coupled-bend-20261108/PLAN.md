# Straight-rest circular rod: bend/twist derivative check

Question: can the actual DER energy of a straight-rest circular rod be expressed locally and differentiated accurately enough to serve as a building block for the coupled stretch/bend/twist solve? In the production model, material frames are parallel transported and scalar bend stiffness is isotropic. For consecutive unit tangents u,v, c=u·v and adjacent twist difference d, predict the bend contribution is `(EI / restVoronoi) * (1-c)/(1+c) * (1+cos(d))`, with the separate existing quadratic twist energy. This depends on straight rest curvature and does not assert a formula for curved rest geometry, anisotropic sections, nearly antiparallel tangents, or general contact.

Before using the expression in any solver, compare it and its analytical position/twist gradients to `evaluateDiscreteElasticRodEnergy` compiled from the pinned native source. Compare energy components and finite differences of each native component at two perturbation sizes. Use 24 independently generated seeded configurations of 3, 5 and 9 nodes, with three-dimensional bending, nonzero twists, varied segment lengths, rotations and world offsets 0 and 0.5 m; reject generated pairs with c < -0.5. Use a straight/twist-zero null and a no-cosine bend-law negative control. Include exact translation and rotation checks. The fixed test set and seed are deterministic; a failure is retained, not excluded or retuned.

Pass requires bend and twist energy agreement within max(1e-18 J, 2e-10 relative), native gradient estimates at two perturbation scales within max(5e-12 N or 1e-15 Nm, 3e-4 relative), and analytic gradients agreeing with the finer native estimates under the same thresholds. Null, invariance and negative controls must behave as declared. Bind source, executable, generated inputs, host, output and analysis by hash; run twice for repeatability. Stop after these 24 cases and report every failure. No Metal rollout, production change, calibrated material, performance or surgical claim is made. Follow-up solver design requires unused cases and independent local-force/torque/work, contact and transaction checks. Directional CCD and augmented-Lagrangian contact stay disabled.
