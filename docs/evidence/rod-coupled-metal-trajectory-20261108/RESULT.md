# Coupled Metal rod: host-carried trajectory comparison

The unchanged compensated-force Metal candidate completed and passed **30/30** preregistered one-millisecond steps across the three-node, five-node moving-support, and eight-node trajectories. The independent native-energy-based FP64 reference advanced its own full state. Each trajectory began with anchors aligned to the actual initial endpoints; the moving support then advanced 1 micrometre per step. Both complete executions and their compressed native/GPU input-output traces were byte-identical.

The largest position, velocity, twist and twist-rate differences were 2.51e-9 m, 4.02e-6 m/s, 2.08e-7 rad and 2.47e-5 rad/s. Largest support-force error was 8.00e-9 N. Independently evaluated local linear and angular momentum residuals were at most 0.000210 m/s and 0.000151 rad/s, below their 0.001 gates; the reference's respective maxima were 0.0000906 m/s and 0.0000842 rad/s, below 0.0001. The moving support accumulated -8.947994317e-7 J of force-on-target work versus -8.947994712e-7 J for the reference; maximum cumulative difference was 3.95e-14 J. The two fixed-support work totals were zero. Maximum kinetic-plus-elastic energy difference was 8.30e-14 J. The full per-step comparison is `trajectory-analysis.json`, with raw inputs and native/GPU outputs in the deterministic compressed trace.

This checks repeated Metal solves with **host-owned** state continuation and prescribed hard supports. It does not test device-resident persistent state, failure rollback, contact, moving rigid-body coupling, larger rotations, curved rest geometry, calibrated materials, performance, or production publication. The trajectory initial states had been used in the one-step holdout, and the independently implemented reference evaluates the same discrete energy; these limits matter for interpreting agreement. Production's false acceptance remains unfixed. Experimental directional CCD and AL contact remain disabled. The next integration step is an authoritative persistent GPU owner with atomic accepted-state commit and a forced-failure restoration/retry test.
