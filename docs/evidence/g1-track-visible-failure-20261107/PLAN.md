# Preregistered visible tracked object and delayed physical failure

The preceding ball-dodge task has no actor object-track bindings and cannot establish visibility. This independent fixture uses the authored G1 ball-disturbance-recovery task, which includes six actor object tracks, with the same isolated, nonmutating private-history snapshot instrumentation and contract-bound zero policy. The camera points along its local +z and a sixth synthetic rigid target is placed above it; target visibility is a hypothesis and must be established by tracker confidence, not inferred from placement. The other five rigid spheres in environment 0 begin spaced on a circle with inward velocities; their convergence is intended to exceed raw-contact capacity eight only after at least one accepted frame. Environment 1 and the whole control arm use the ordinary initial positions. The same task/world contracts, seed, zero policy and asset packs apply to both arms; the distinct task contract is reported at run.

Run instrumented then no-snapshot control, each as one process with a 120-second timeout. Retain exact timestamps, identities, statuses, printed actor values and bitwise history snapshots. Require six real tracker bindings, a target confidence of 1 on an accepted step in both environments before any failure, and an actual later environment-0 capacity rejection while environment 1 succeeds and the control succeeds. Only then compare the immediately prior accepted and rejected environment-0 private histories and batch cadence; report the exact values, not just a status. Track array indexing follows the owner shader and track-to-body order must be verified from the compiled ordered bindings. Actor/status equality with no-snapshot control at printed precision is a diagnostic for instrumentation side effects.

The intended collision dynamics may be rejected too early, may never overflow, may disturb the view, or normal capacity may be insufficient; each outcome is retained and is inconclusive for the requested history gate. A changed history in the failed step is evidence of mutation before acceptance, but does not alone prove replay divergence or determine correct post-reset behavior. This fixture retains production automatic reset and stops at failure; it does not retry or qualify a publication repair. The visual model is synthetic and oracle instance tracking is not deployment-valid perception.
