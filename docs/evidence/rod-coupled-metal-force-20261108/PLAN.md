# Compensated force accumulation in the coupled Metal rod

The centered-angle candidate passed 7/12 exposed cases; the paired-displacement candidate gained one of those rejects but lost two prior passes. Both stalled with small linear momentum residuals, while the latter did not establish that displacement rounding alone was the cause. Hypothesis: in the loaded rod, opposing per-edge stretch forces are individually rounded to FP32 before cancellation, limiting the accuracy of the local momentum residual and line-search merit.

Starting from the retained paired-displacement candidate, preserve its state coordinates, energy, Hessian, Newton solver, line search, iteration limits, initial states, timestep and every physical gate. Carry edge length, normalized edge components, stretch force products and the complete assembled position residual as two-term FP32 values through cancellation and inertia addition. Continue to use FP32 Hessian and twist terms, and explicitly project only the final residual to FP32 for the linear solve. The compensated operations and tested executable are research-only; no existing runtime or physical law changes.

On the same twelve exposed cases, predict that at least two of the five cases rejected by the centered-only candidate now pass every original state, local momentum and force check, and that all seven earlier passes remain passes. Run exactly two complete copies of the fixed case set; retain all rejections, failed builds and any regression without adjusting thresholds. This is an exploratory follow-up, not independent holdout, material calibration, contact, rollback, persistent-state, production integration or performance evidence. Both experimental CCD and AL contact modes remain disabled.
