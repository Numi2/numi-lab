# Coupled contact equations at first rejection

The retained rod–sphere–plane candidate’s first rejected step admits a low-residual solution of its **captured local discrete equations**. This distinguishes a failing iteration from failure to find any solution in this host calculation; it is not a production repair, physical force reference or completed trajectory.

All three post-solve axis captures reproduced the same non-scratch exposed 20-step trace as the unmodified control and the previous sweep study: 12 accepted, eight rejected, with status 11 at step 12. Capture/noncapture and repetition flags passed. The captured regularized 9-by-9 response was finite, had relative maximum asymmetry `2.48e-8`, and a positive symmetric spectrum (smallest eigenvalue about `0.999`). The operator is from the GPU at this state; the earlier independent operator reconstruction was performed at a different state.

A separate FP64 host solve constructed the affine equations from the captured matrix, final impulse and equation RHS. A finite-difference semismooth Newton method with backtracking was run from the captured impulse, half of it and zero. All three seeds reached the same root to the printed precision in one, one and two host iterations. Each root passed the declared `1e-6 m/s` normal and projected-tangent residual gate, normal impulse nonnegativity and friction-disk check; a deliberately perturbed normal impulse failed the same certificate. Computed residuals below `3e-13 m/s` are for the captured, printed-precision affine data only and do not indicate physical precision. Both rod rows lie approximately on their friction boundaries; the plane row lies inside its boundary.

This supports testing a joint normal–tangent solve against the unchanged sustained fixture. It does not prove that the same method will work in FP32, resolve other configurations, produce accurate forces, preserve all hidden state, or satisfy the full coupled mechanics milestone. No candidate was promoted and neither disabled CCD nor augmented-Lagrangian experiment was enabled. The failed split-solver evidence and iteration history remain intact.
