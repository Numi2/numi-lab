# HyperPolicy state at the physical acceptance boundary

**Repaired for committed physical-step outcomes:** MetalWorld now offers an optional GPU action-owner callback after a physical step. HyperPolicy snapshots its complete phase record and previous physical-action row before inference, then restores them per environment if the world status or enabled contact status reports failure. Accepted environments keep their candidate state. Existing stateless action programs need not implement the callback. Wrappers that replace the program context must forward it to the original owner. This adds a field to a C++ structure, so clients must rebuild against the matching headers and library.

On `ssh numi`, a real deterministic, untrained HyperPolicy controlled a two-environment Franka fixture for 30 steps with an intentionally undersized contact capacity. The run produced 55 accepted and five rejected environment-steps. The read-only instrument captured private GPU policy records before inference, after inference, and after each physical step. Before the repair, three failures without an episode reset advanced both phase and the previous action. For example, at step 13 the failed environment changed phase from `0.2599999607` to `0.2799999714` and action from `0.06499999017` to `0.06999999285`, while its neighbor succeeded. Automatic reset on the subsequent step did not make that failed transition transactional.

After the repair, all five rejected rows exactly restored their complete 48-byte phase record and entire nine-action row; all 55 accepted rows retained their full post-inference candidate records. Two independent runs matched. The instrumented and uninstrumented versions produced identical recorded q, v, scene, task observations, statuses, termination flags and policy traces; the repaired noninstrumented outputs also matched the original fixture. This comparison is specific to this run and does not establish the behavior of all later tasks or retry strategies.

The permanent `hyper_policy_physical_rejection` regression uses the real resident and deliberately mixed failure. At the final step it performs a *diagnostic second inference*, with a zero reset mask and a separate action buffer, to observe the retained state. For the rejected environment the action repeats the attempted rate-limited increment when restoration is enabled; disabling the callback in the negative control advances it again. The accepted neighbor advances identically in both controls, and both controls have equal final q/v. This second inference is not a simulated retry or a trained-policy evaluation. The `hyper_policy_restore_statuses` GPU test covers world-only failure, contact-only failure, both failures, stale contact codes when contact is disabled, accepted rows, full records, all actions and canaries. The earlier capacity-growth and host-abort regression also passes. The main build passed those three tests, ten related visual transaction tests and `metalrobo_run_program_check`.

The public rollout trace still records the action proposed for a failed transition; consumers must honor its physics-error flag. This repair does not prove automatic reset or task learning-time semantics, a complete no-reset physical retry, environment-capacity resizing, overlapping submissions, or recovery after an actual GPU command-buffer failure. Owners must serialize a shared HyperPolicy runtime. It also does not qualify force accuracy, contact performance, or policy skill. Experimental directional CCD and augmented-Lagrangian contact remain disabled.
