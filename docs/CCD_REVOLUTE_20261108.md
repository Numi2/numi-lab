# Off-center revolute-link impact

A floating parent of mass 1 kg and a 0.5 kg child share their COM at the revolute pivot; the free child z-axis rotational inertia is 0.05 kg m². A child-mounted sphere is offset 0.2 m along x and contacts a static plane. The collision is frictionless and inelastic, gravity is zero, initial orientation is identity, initial gap -0.1 mm, timestep 10 ms, and production two-sweep count. The contact response is independently `W = 1/1.5 + 0.2²/0.05` per kg; normal impact impulse is `max(-v_contact,0)/W`. Parent velocity changes by impulse / 1.5; child angular velocity changes by 0.2 times impulse / 0.05. Work is the change in translational and rotational kinetic energy; for a closing impact it equals -0.5 times normal impulse times incoming speed.

Two distinct parent/joint velocity splits each yield incoming contact speed -1 m/s. Both were accepted: impulse matches the 0.6818181818 N s reference within 5.5e-8 N s, contact velocity within 6.6e-8 m/s, parent response within 3.2e-8 m/s, and work matches -0.3409090909 J within 2.1e-8 J. Derived child angular impulse agrees with the witness arm times normal impulse within 1.20e-8 N m s. Rest and separating cases have zero impulse and zero work. All four replayed the observed outputs exactly. The angular comparison reconstructs child angular velocity from measured contact and parent velocities, and depends on the stated geometry and axis. This is a frictionless, one-contact, shared-COM diagnostic, not broad multibody or friction qualification.
