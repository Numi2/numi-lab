# Synthetic 59-row mixed-island containment

This isolated research test is deliberately nonphysical: 33 overlapping neighboring rods share a sphere and ground. It checks a larger mixed contact island and rollback, not rod mechanics or useful motion. Both composed world and rod gravity were zero. Contact detection produced one mixed island with 59 constraints and an initially populated manifold; both main and candidate accepted the zero-target control. Queue flags were zero. Source inspection routes rod-bearing islands to a single wave32 owner, including more than 256 rows, rather than the distributed rigid path; this fixture did not directly capture the work packet.

With the ground target at 1 m/s, clean main accepted a state whose reported normal residual was 0.9390781522 m/s. The research candidate rejected both repeated attempts with status 11. After each rejection, the captured q/v, scene, rod nodes/edges and manifold counts/headers/points were byte-equal to the pre-attempt capture. Its subsequent neutral continuation matched a separately initialized resident that received no rejected target. This is evidence for those captured fields and that continuation, not a general hidden-cache or reset guarantee. Rejected trial impulses do not represent accepted motion.

The initial fixture printed only residuals.y; a separately pinned instrumented rerun exposed both status values without changing the ABI: residuals.x = 0.9390781522 m/s, residuals.y = 0.01009999961 m/s. Independent host arithmetic applied to each shader-captured post-solve row normal velocity, target, regularization and impulse found 11 violating rows, with ground row 0 at 0.9390781522 m/s against 0.0101 m/s. The paired candidate and diagnostic shader produced matching common emitted output. This verifies the aggregate against captured rows; it does not independently reconstruct the candidate velocities from the restored state. No production solver or ABI change was made for this diagnostic.
