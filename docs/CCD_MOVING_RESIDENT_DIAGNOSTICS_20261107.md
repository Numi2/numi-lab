# Moving contact and resident rollback diagnostics

The moving-contact fixture uses a kinematic scene body moving upward at 0.25 m/s and measures velocities relative to it. Of 48 cases, 47 were accepted; the 33-contact frictional one-sweep attempt rejected with status 11 and restored its initial state. At two sweeps, both 33- and 257-contact frictionless impacts produced 1 N s normal impulse, zero final relative speed and -0.25 J kinetic work on the dynamic body. The two frictional cases produced the expected approximate 0.5 m/s tangential speed and -0.625 J kinetic work. This qualifies only a prescribed-translation fixture.

The resident diagnostic seeds 33 accepted contact manifolds and then drives a frictional one-sweep rejection using a kinematic target. q, v, manifold counts, headers and points restore exactly, but the scene state does not: the input target changes scene velocity from (0,0) to (-1,+1) m/s and is retained despite status 11. Without explicit target restoration, the next neutral continuation differs from the clean control. After explicitly submitting the previous target in both paths, the continuation matches byte-for-byte across q, v, scene and manifold fields. This follows the current prepare kernel, which applies kinematic targets before checkpointing, but fails the requested pre-step complete-restoration boundary. Two earlier attempts using floating-root efforts did not cause a rejection because those efforts are ignored; they remain retained and make no rollback claim.

No speed claim follows from these diagnostics.
