# Bounded mixed rod priority experiment

This separate research candidate modifies only rod-containing wave islands: for each configured sweep it makes at most eight priority-coordinate updates, recomputing contact velocities after applying each selected correction through the actual factorized rod and articulation response. It retains the existing final normal complementarity certificate, the two configured sweeps and disabled experimental CCD/contact options. The normal-first coordinate update and objective-based priority are not proven globally convergent; acceptance by the runtime certificate does not itself qualify friction or physical accuracy.

The fixed three-case zero-gravity sphere–plane–rod fixture, exact shader and executable were pinned along with `gates.txt` and the independent `check.py` before running. All three candidate cases accept and reproduce observed q/v/rod state on replay. Normal residual is at most 6.24e-8 m/s; each passes the predeclared absolute local normal, friction, impulse-work, total impulse and frozen-operator action checks. Accepted total momentum error is at most 5.18e-8 N s. The matched prior candidate rejects all three with normal residuals 0.45335, 0.11573 and 0.01764 m/s, restoring observed q/v/scene and rod state.

The +1 m/s case remains a material accuracy concern despite passing the predeclared *absolute* thresholds: work is -0.000461335 J versus -0.000553479 J in the rank-deficient FP64 fixed-point reference, a 16.6% difference, and total rod normal impulse differs by 7.01e-5 N s, approximately 11.6% of the reference. The captured matrix has rank 8/9; the observed alternative is consistent with nonunique or ill-conditioned frictional response, but its implications require further study. Matching normal complementarity, cone bounds and global momentum does not establish a unique force distribution or accurate work. The reference solves the frozen read-back operator and does not independently establish general physical rod accuracy. No accepted-step rod torque check, multiple geometries, populated-cache failure path, non-rod regression or total-time comparison has qualified this candidate; it is not promoted.

The frozen Coulomb system exposes the source of this ambiguity. In the +1 m/s case, the floor is inactive and both rod tangent slips are positive. Set the two tangent impulses to -0.5 times their respective nonnegative normal impulses. The two rod normal response rows coincide; prescribing any split of the total rod normal impulse leaves one scalar normal equation for that total. Eleven evenly spaced splits yield total normal impulse from 0.00053420 to 0.00062025 N s and work from -0.00046133 to -0.00057297 J; all sampled normal residuals are below 1.8e-16 m/s, cone violations zero, and rod slips at least 0.4385 m/s. This demonstrates how the discrete rigid-friction formulation can have multiple admissible, observably different solutions at this redundant contact geometry. It does not identify a uniquely correct physical branch or justify arbitrarily choosing one. For the captured +1 m/s fixture, writing rod normal impulses as n₁,n₂ and the sliding tangent impulses as -0.5n₁,-0.5n₂ gives the common rod normal equation approximately
`1759.51648 (n₁+n₂) + 131.15102 (n₁-n₂) = 1.00999993`.
The other rod normal equation is the same to captured precision. The admissible force split is therefore not selected by those normal equations; it changes tangential velocities because the two contacts have different tangential response columns. Collision witness ownership is per rod-collider/rigid-collider pair, so normal-row redundancy alone does not prove the tangential material points or twist states can safely be merged. Local maximum dissipation holds at each sampled fixed point. Selecting whichever has the most negative total work would add an unqualified global selection rule, not follow directly from the tested local Coulomb conditions. Any future merging, contact regularization or force-distribution rule requires an explicit model contract and independent geometry, mesh and physical validation.
